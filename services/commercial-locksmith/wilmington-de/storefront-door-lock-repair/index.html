Storefront Door Lock Repair in Wilmington - common questions

What do you check first when a storefront door in Wilmington won't lock right?

We start with the door alignment, then check the latch, strike, cylinder, and hinge or pivot wear. On aluminum storefront doors, the lock is often only part of the problem. If the door is dragging or sitting out of square, fixing the lock alone usually won't hold.

What should I have ready before you repair my storefront lock on Market Street?

Have the door unlocked if possible, and know whether you're the owner, tenant, or property manager. If there are special access rules, master key concerns, or landlord approvals, that helps us plan the repair without delays. A photo of the door and hardware can also be useful.

Is it a bad sign if the key turns but the storefront still doesn't secure?

Yes, it usually means the lock is not fully engaging or the door isn't lining up with the strike. That can wear down the hardware fast and leave the entrance easier to force. We check both the lock parts and the door fit so the fix lasts.

Should I repair the mortise cylinder or replace the whole storefront lock?

If the body of the lock is solid and the issue is the cylinder, key wear, or minor internal damage, a repair or cylinder replacement may be enough. If the latch, hook bolt, or case is badly worn, replacement can be the better choice. We look at the whole set before recommending either.

What if my storefront door in Wilmington is an older aluminum frame with original hardware?

That's common in older commercial spaces, and we can often still service it. The main concern is whether the parts are worn past safe repair or if the door has been adjusted badly over time. If the original hardware is still usable, we try to keep the system working without changing more than needed.
