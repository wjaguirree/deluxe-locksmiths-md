Signs It's Time to Act

A panic device usually gives you warning before it fails outright. The door may need a hard push to latch, the latch may drag, or the bar may feel loose, stick, or sit crooked after people use it all day. If the exit door on a storefront near Market Street is starting to slam, rattle, or catch at the frame, that's not just wear and tear. It can point to a device that's out of alignment, damaged inside, or no longer matching the door's condition. We also see problems when a building has had repeated lock changes, hinge repairs, or door closers replaced and the exit hardware was never adjusted to match.

Waiting until the device fails is harder on everyone. A stuck bar can slow down employees, interrupt inspections, and turn a normal opening or closing routine into a repair emergency. It can also leave you guessing whether the door will release cleanly when people need it to. Acting on early signs gives us room to fit the right hardware, check the door prep, verify the strike and closer, and make sure the exit path works the way it should. Panic Bar Installation in Wilmington - common questions

How does a panic bar installation usually work on a Wilmington storefront door?

We start by checking the door, frame, and existing hardware so the new exit device fits the opening and the use of the space. Then we prep the door, mount the bar, align the latch, and test the release and closing action. If the frame or door needs adjustment for proper egress, we handle that as part of the job.

What should we have ready before you install a panic bar in our building?

It helps to know the door material, whether it's a single door or a pair, and whether the opening already has a closer, alarm, or access control. If the building has inspection notes or a hardware issue you've noticed, we should see that too. That gives us a clear picture of what the opening needs before installation.

Can an old door near Market Street still take a code-compliant exit device?

Often, yes, but not every old door is ready as-is. Some need reinforcement, new strike prep, or hinge and frame repair before the hardware will function properly. If the door is too damaged, we'll say so. The goal is a safe exit that works every time, not forcing hardware onto a weak opening.

Should we choose a rim panic bar or a vertical rod device for our Wilmington property?

It depends on the door and how it's used. Rim devices are common on many single doors and are often simpler to service. Vertical rod hardware is usually better for certain taller doors or paired openings. We look at the frame, the latch side, and the building layout before recommending one option over the other.

Can a panic bar be installed on a glass storefront or a door tied into access control?

Yes, but the hardware has to be planned around the rest of the system. Glass storefronts, electrified strikes, and access control each change the prep and the wiring or latch setup. We make sure the exit device lets people out cleanly while still working with the rest of the door hardware instead of fighting it.
