A commercial installation starts with how the door gets used, not just what fits the cutout. A homeowner usually wants one solid lock for a front or back entry and a clean match to the door hardware. For a business, we look at who needs access, who should not, and how the lock has to work with keys, master keying, and daily traffic. That can mean storefront cylinders, office levers, back-of-house deadbolts, or heavier hardware for a door that takes a beating all day. In a place like Wilmington, where one building might sit near Rodney Square and another might be part of a busy downtown block, the right lock setup has to match both the door and the way the space is managed.

Commercial Lock Installation in Wilmington - common questions

How do you decide whether my Wilmington storefront needs repair, rekeying, or a full lock replacement?

We look at the lock body, cylinder, strike, and door alignment before recommending a path. If the hardware is solid and the issue is limited, repair or rekeying may be enough. If the lock is worn out, damaged, or wrong for the door, replacement is usually the better option for a business.

What should we have ready before you install a commercial lock at our office near Rodney Square?

It helps to know which door needs the work, who should have access after the job, and whether the door is part of a master key system. If there are old key records, existing hardware, or photos of the door, that can help us choose the right commercial-grade lock and avoid the wrong fit.

Can a bad lock on a Market Street storefront be repaired instead of replaced?

Sometimes, yes. If the problem is a loose part, worn cylinder, or minor alignment issue, repair can make sense. If the lock has been forced, the keyway is badly worn, or the door needs a stronger setup, replacement is usually the safer long-term choice for a busy storefront.

If we change tenants in a Wilmington office building, is rekeying enough?

Rekeying is often the right first step when the existing locks are still in good shape. It changes who can use the keys without changing every piece of hardware. If the locks are old, damaged, or inconsistent across doors, we may recommend replacing some or all of them instead.

What if the door is old and the lockset doesn't match modern commercial hardware?

That's common in older Wilmington buildings. In those cases, we may need to adapt the opening, choose hardware that fits the door thickness and prep, or recommend a different lock style. If the door can't support a proper install, we'll say so rather than force hardware that won't hold up.
