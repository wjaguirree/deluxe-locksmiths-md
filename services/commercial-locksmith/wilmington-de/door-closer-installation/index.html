Door Closer Installation in Wilmington - common questions

How do you decide what kind of door closer fits my Wilmington storefront or office?

We look at the door size, weight, material, and how people use it. A light interior office door and a busy commercial entry door need different hardware. We also check the frame and hinge condition so the closer we install will actually control the door instead of fighting a problem that should be corrected first.

What should we have ready before you install a door closer at our building?

It helps if someone can show us which door is giving trouble and how it has been acting. If the door already has a history of slamming, not latching, or rubbing the frame, tell us that too. We can work with standard commercial openings, but knowing the traffic pattern and access needs helps us set it up right.

Could a closer be causing our door to stay ajar or slam shut hard?

Yes. A worn or poorly adjusted closer can let a door drift open, close too fast, or fail to latch. That can also happen if the hinges are loose or the door is out of alignment. We check the whole opening because fixing only the closer sometimes leaves the real issue untouched.

Should we repair the old closer or replace it on a Wilmington building door?

If the closer is sound and just out of adjustment, repair usually makes sense. If it leaks, has broken parts, or cannot be tuned to hold the door properly, replacement is the better call. We'll tell you which path fits the door after we look at the hardware and the opening.

Can you install a closer on an older door in one of the older downtown buildings?

Usually, yes, but older doors sometimes need extra attention. Screw holes may be worn, the frame may be out of square, or the door may need reinforcement before a closer will hold correctly. We check those issues first so the hardware is mounted securely and the door closes the way it should.
