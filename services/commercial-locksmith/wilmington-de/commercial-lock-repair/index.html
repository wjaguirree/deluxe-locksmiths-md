Commercial Lock Repair in Wilmington - common questions

My office door in Wilmington is hard to lock. What do you check first?

We start with the door alignment, the strike, and the condition of the latch or deadbolt. A lock that feels bad is often being forced by a door that has dropped, a closer that is out of adjustment, or worn internal parts. We look at the whole opening, not just the lock face, so the repair solves the real problem.

What should I have ready before you work on a storefront or office lock?

It helps to know which door is failing, what the lock does when it fails, and whether the problem started after a break-in, a tenant change, or a hardware swap. If there is a master key system or access control in place, let us know that too. Having the working key on hand is useful when the lock still responds to it.

Is a sticking lock a security risk, or just a nuisance?

It can be both. If a lock is hard to turn, people tend to stop using it the right way, which can leave a door not fully secured. A worn latch or damaged strike can also make the door easier to defeat. We repair those issues before they turn into a bigger access problem or a lockout.

Should we repair the existing lock or replace it on our Wilmington property?

Repair makes sense when the hardware is still solid and the wear is limited to a part that can be fixed or adjusted. Replacement is the better move when the lock body is badly worn, parts are discontinued, or the door has been damaged enough that the old hardware won't hold up. We'll explain the difference plainly.

Can you repair a commercial lock if the door has panic hardware or a keypad system?

Yes, but the approach depends on how the hardware is built. Panic devices, electrified parts, and keypad entries can fail in different ways, and sometimes the problem is not the lock itself but the way the door is closing or the power is being delivered. We troubleshoot the opening as a system so the repair fits the setup.
