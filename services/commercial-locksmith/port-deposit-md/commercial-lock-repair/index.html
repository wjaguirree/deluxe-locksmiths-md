Commercial Lock Repair in Port Deposit - common questions

How do you figure out whether my commercial door in Port Deposit needs lock repair or a full replacement?

We look at the lock body, cylinder, strike, hinges, and closer as a system. If the parts are solid and the issue is wear, alignment, or one broken component, repair is usually the better path. If the hardware is bent, badly corroded, or missing key pieces, replacement may be the practical fix.

What should I have ready before you come out to fix a lock on my shop or office?

Have the door open and tell us what the lock is doing: hard key turn, latch not catching, handle sagging, or a door that needs to be pushed to close. If you know the brand or type of hardware, that helps too. Clear access around the door saves time once we're on site.

Can a worn lock on a riverfront business start causing security problems before it fails completely?

Yes. A lock that binds, feels loose, or only catches partway can still let the door look closed when it isn't secure. That's common on doors that get heavy daily use or see moisture and wind. Catching it early can prevent a bigger failure at a bad time.

Should I keep a mortise lock or switch to newer hardware on an older building in Port Deposit?

If the existing lock fits the door well and the frame is still sound, repairing or restoring it can be the cleanest choice. If the door has repeated alignment trouble or the old hardware is no longer dependable, a newer setup may work better. The right answer depends on the door, not just the age of the building.

What if the lock works, but the door only closes correctly when someone slams it shut?

That usually points to an alignment problem, not just a bad lock. The closer, hinges, strike, or frame may be out of line. In that case, repairing the lock alone won't solve it. We'd look at the whole door so it closes and latches without rough handling.
