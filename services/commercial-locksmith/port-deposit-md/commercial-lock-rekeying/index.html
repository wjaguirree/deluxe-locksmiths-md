Commercial Lock Rekeying in Port Deposit - common questions

How does commercial rekeying work for a shop or office in Port Deposit?

We keep the existing lock hardware and change the internal pins or tumblers so old keys no longer work. Then we cut new keys for the people who should still have access. It's a practical option when the locks are in decent shape and the main issue is key control after turnover, a lost master, or a staff change.

What should we have ready before you rekey our building?

It helps to know which doors need the same key, which ones should stay separate, and who needs access to each area. If you have an old master key or a list of current key holders, bring that too. We can sort out the hardware on site, but clear access decisions make the job cleaner.

What's the main risk if we wait after a key is lost?

The risk is that someone still has access when they shouldn't. In a commercial setting, a missing key can affect offices, stock rooms, and exterior doors, not just one entrance. Rekeying resets control without forcing you to replace every lock, which is often the faster way to shut down that risk.

Should we rekey or replace the locks on our Port Deposit property?

If the locks work well and the doors are sound, rekeying is usually enough. If the hardware is worn, damaged, or mismatched from door to door, replacement may make more sense. We'll look at the condition of the locks and talk through which choice fits the building and how it's used.

Can you rekey older locks on a stone building or an unusual door setup?

Often, yes. Port Deposit has a lot of older construction and nonstandard openings, so we're used to checking the lock type, the door condition, and whether parts are still serviceable. If a lock can be rekeyed safely, we'll do that. If not, we'll explain the limits and what would need to change.
