Storefront Door Lock Repair in Churchville - common questions

My Churchville storefront door closes, but the lock won't catch. What's usually wrong?

That usually means the door is out of alignment, the strike is worn, or the latch or hook bolt isn't reaching far enough. On aluminum storefront doors, a pivot issue or threshold wear is common too. The repair often involves adjustment first, then replacing the worn part if the door still won't secure correctly.

What should I do before a lock repair on my shop door near MD-22?

If the door still secures, leave it closed and don't keep testing the key. Clear access around the door, and note whether the problem is with the key, the latch, the handle, or the bottom edge dragging. If there's another secure entrance, use that until the storefront hardware is sorted out.

Could forcing a stiff storefront lock damage the door?

Yes. Forcing a bind can break the key, damage the cylinder, or bend the latch, strike, or handle parts inside the door. On aluminum storefront doors, repeated force can also throw the door out of alignment. It's better to stop using pressure and have the hardware checked before more parts get affected.

Do you always replace the whole lock, or can just one part be fixed?

Not always the whole lock. A lot depends on what failed. Sometimes a mortise cylinder, hook bolt, pivot set, or threshold adjustment is enough. If the body of the lock is worn out or the internal parts are damaged, replacing the full assembly makes more sense than piecing together a weak repair.

What if my storefront door at a small business is sagging and rubbing the floor?

That's often a pivot, hinge, or threshold problem, and the lock can be fine while the door itself is out of square. If the door is sagging, the latch may miss the strike even though the key works. The fix usually starts with getting the door hung correctly, then adjusting the lock to match.
