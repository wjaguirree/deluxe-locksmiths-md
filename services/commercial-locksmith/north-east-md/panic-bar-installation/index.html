Panic Bar Installation in North East - common questions

How does a panic bar installation work on my exit door in North East?

We remove the old hardware if there is any, measure the door, and install the panic device that fits the opening. Then we adjust the strike, latch, and closer so the door opens with a push and still shuts securely. If the door needs extra prep, we handle that on site.

What should I have ready before you install a panic bar at my shop or restaurant?

If possible, have the door open and tell us which door is the required exit. It also helps to know whether the door is wood, metal, or glass, and whether it already has a closer or deadbolt. If there's an inspection note or a failed latch, we want to see that too.

What's the main safety issue if my panic bar is sticking or not latching right?

The biggest concern is a door that won't open cleanly when people need to get out, or one that won't stay secure when it should be closed. A worn bar, bad strike, or misaligned frame can cause both problems. We check the full opening so the hardware works as an exit, not just as a lock.

Should I replace the whole exit device or just the broken parts on my North East building?

If the bar is worn, the latch is damaged, or the device no longer matches the door, a full replacement is usually the cleaner fix. If the problem is limited to the strike, trim, or closer, a repair may be enough. We look at the condition of the door before we recommend either path.

Can you install a panic bar on an older storefront near the water if the door is not standard?

Often, yes. Older doors and unusual frames may need a different style of exit device, extra prep, or added reinforcement. We see that on older commercial openings in North East. If the door is too damaged or out of square, we'll tell you what has to be corrected before the new hardware can work properly.
