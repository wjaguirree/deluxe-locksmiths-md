Commercial Lock Repair in Harrington - common questions

Why does a commercial door in Harrington keep going out of alignment after it's been adjusted?

On a busy door, alignment problems usually come from worn hinges, a tired closer, loose screws, or a strike plate that no longer matches the latch. An adjustment can help for a while, but if the parts are worn or bent, the door will drift again. A proper repair checks the whole opening, not just one piece of hardware.

What should I have ready before you work on a storefront lock or panic bar?

It helps to know which door is acting up, what it's doing, and when the problem shows up. If you have keys, access cards, or any old hardware parts, keep them handy. If the door has been hitting the frame, dragging, or failing to latch, that detail tells us a lot before we start.

Can a quick fix make a commercial door fail again during a busy day?

Yes. A quick fix can hide the symptom without correcting the wear behind it. If a closer is weak, a hinge is loose, or the latch isn't lining up, the door may work for a bit and then start sticking, banging, or refusing to lock again. That's why a real repair is better on high-use doors.

Should I repair the existing lock or replace the whole set on a business door?

If the body of the lock is sound and the issue is wear, misalignment, or a failed part, repair is often the right move. If the hardware is badly damaged, bent, or has been repaired too many times, replacement may make more sense. The choice usually comes down to how much of the door hardware is still in good shape.

What if the door still opens, but the lock feels rough and the latch only catches sometimes?

That's a common edge case and it usually means the problem is starting, not finished. A rough key turn or an inconsistent latch often points to internal wear, debris, or a shifting strike. If you wait too long, the door can stop securing properly or start damaging the frame and hardware.
