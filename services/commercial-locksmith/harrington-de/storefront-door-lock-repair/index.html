Storefront Door Lock Repair in Harrington - common questions

How do you figure out whether my storefront door needs repair or a new lock?

We check the lock, the door alignment, the strike, and the pivots together. If the hardware is worn but still sound, repair is usually enough. If the cylinder is damaged or the internal parts are stripped, replacement is often the better choice. We don't guess from one symptom alone.

What should I have ready before you come look at my shop door in Harrington?

Have the door unlocked if possible, and let us know which door is acting up and what you're seeing, such as dragging, sticking, or a key that won't turn smoothly. If you have working keys, bring them. If the door has a crash bar or previous repair history, that helps too.

Can a worn storefront lock create a safety problem even if the key still works?

Yes. A key can still turn while the latch, hook bolt, or strike is not holding correctly. That can leave the door easy to force or hard to secure at closing time. We look at whether the lock is actually locking the door, not just whether the key moves.

If I lost keys for my storefront, do I need rekeying or replacement?

If the lock hardware is still in good shape, rekeying is usually the first thing to consider. That changes who can use the existing lock without replacing the whole setup. If the cylinder is worn or the door hardware is failing, replacement may make more sense. We'll walk through both options.

Can you work on a storefront door that has an older mortise cylinder and an aluminum frame?

Yes. That's a common setup on commercial doors, and it often needs careful adjustment more than a full swap. We can repair or replace the mortise cylinder, check the hook bolt, and make sure the door closes properly against the threshold and strike.
