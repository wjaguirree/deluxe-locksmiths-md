Panic Bar Installation in Harrington - common questions

How does a panic bar installation usually work on an older commercial door in Harrington?

First the door, frame, hinges, and closer are checked together. On older openings, the installer may need to reinforce the door, patch old holes, or cut new prep for the device. Then the bar, latch, and strike are fitted so the door opens with one motion and latches again without binding.

What should I have ready before you install exit hardware at my building?

It helps to know which door is the required exit, whether the door is steel, wood, or glass-and-frame, and whether there's an existing closer or lockset on it. If you have any inspection notes or photos of the door edge and frame, bring those along. That helps avoid unnecessary changes.

Can a panic bar be installed if the door already has a deadbolt or other lock?

Sometimes, but the locking setup has to be legal for the way the door is used. On an exit door, the inside must release in one motion. A deadbolt, surface bolt, or extra security lock can create a problem if it blocks egress. The hardware has to be chosen and placed so the door still functions as an exit.

Should I choose a rim device or a vertical rod device for my store or shop?

A rim device is common on many single doors and is often the simplest choice. Vertical rod hardware can make sense on taller doors or double doors that need latching at more than one point. The right option depends on the door construction, opening size, and how the exit is used day to day.

What if my door swings too hard or doesn't close all the way after the panic bar is installed?

That usually points to a closer, hinge, or frame issue, not just the bar itself. If the door won't close fully, the latch won't engage the way it should. The fix may involve adjusting the closer, repairing the frame, or changing strike placement so the whole opening works as one system.
