What a Proper Repair Looks Like

A proper storefront lock repair starts with the door itself, not just the cylinder. Aluminum doors move, settle, and get knocked out of line, especially on busy stretches near State Street or in buildings that see a steady stream of foot traffic. If the latch or hook bolt is being forced to work against a bad pivot, a worn threshold, or a sagging frame, swapping parts alone won't hold up. We check the full opening, find the bind point, and make the lock, door, and hardware work together again instead of masking the problem.

The difference shows up right away. After a real repair, the door should close cleanly without a shove, the key should turn without grinding, and the hook bolt or latch should engage fully every time. Storefront Door Lock Repair in Dover - common questions

What parts of a storefront door usually need repair in a Dover shop?

The common trouble spots are hook bolts, mortise cylinders, pivots, strikes, and thresholds. On aluminum storefront doors, one worn part can make the whole door hard to lock. We check how the door hangs, how the latch meets the strike, and whether the lock itself is worn or just out of alignment.

What should our staff have ready before you come out?

It helps to know which door is acting up, whether the problem is latching, locking, or dragging, and whether anyone has forced the door recently. If you can keep the area clear and have someone who can let us in, that makes the work smoother. A quick description of what changed is useful too.

Could a storefront door that still opens be a security risk?

Yes. A door can look fine and still not lock properly if the strike is worn, the pivot is off, or the mortise lock isn't catching. That leaves the entrance easier to defeat and can also cause the door to keep working itself looser. We treat that as a real security problem, not just a nuisance.

Should we repair the lock body or replace the whole storefront door hardware?

It depends on what failed. If the lock case is worn beyond repair or the cylinder is damaged, replacement may make more sense. If the real problem is alignment, a loose pivot, or a threshold issue, repair is often the better path. We look at the whole door before recommending either option.

Can you fix a storefront door that has been rubbing since the building settled?

Often, yes. Settling can shift the door just enough to throw off the latch and wear the hardware unevenly. That may call for pivot adjustment, threshold work, or a mortise lock repair. If the frame itself is badly damaged, we'll say so, but a lot of these doors can be brought back into line.
