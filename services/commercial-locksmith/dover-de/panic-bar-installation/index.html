Signs It's Time to Replace It

The trouble usually starts small. A door that needs a hard push to latch, a bar that sticks after a cold snap, or a lever that feels loose at the spindle can all point to worn internal parts. In a place like Dover, where one building may see state workers, delivery traffic, and event crowds all in the same week, that kind of wear shows up fast. We also get calls when the exit device no longer opens cleanly after the door has been painted, patched, or adjusted for a closer fit. If the hardware has been bent, the latch keeps rubbing, or the door has to be slammed to close, the problem is already past a minor nuisance.

Acting on those signs is easier than waiting for a failure because a failing exit device tends to create a second problem before it stops working completely. People start propping doors, using makeshift fixes, or avoiding a rear exit because it feels unreliable. That's how inspection issues and daily headaches build at the same time. We see this often on older commercial doors along US-13 and in busy properties near Downtown Dover, where heavy use exposes weak hardware quickly. Replacing a worn device before it seizes gives you a clean, code-ready setup instead of a door that only works when someone is holding it just right.

If the bar is dragging, the latch is inconsistent, or the door has started to shift out of alignment, those are the signs to address now. It's simpler to repair or swap the hardware under normal conditions than to deal with a locked exit, a failed inspection, or a door that won't release when people need it most.

Panic Bar Installation in Dover - common questions

What do we need to know before you install a panic bar on our Dover storefront door?

The main things are the door material, the frame, the way the door swings, and whether it's a primary exit or a back exit. Photos help, and so does knowing if the door already has hardware on it. If the opening has been repaired before, tell us that too. It can change the prep work.

Can a panic bar be installed on an older commercial door in downtown Dover?

Often, yes, but older doors may need extra prep first. We check for warping, hinge wear, damaged latch areas, and past patchwork. If the door or frame is too far out of square, the hardware won't latch correctly until those issues are addressed. A clean fit is what makes the exit device dependable.

What problems can make a panic bar fail inspection or not work right?

Common issues are a door that doesn't close fully, a strike that's out of line, the wrong hardware for the door type, or blocked exit travel. Fire-rated openings also have to stay matched to approved components. If the device was installed on a door that drags or rubs, it may not release and latch the way it should.

Should we choose a panic bar, a push pad, or another exit device for our Dover office?

That depends on the door, the occupancy, and how people use the opening. A simple push bar is common on many exits, while some doors need a different trim or a fire-rated device. If the opening also needs outside access control, we'll look at the full setup so the exit hardware and entry hardware work together.

What if our door already has an old exit device that keeps sticking open?

That can often be repaired, but sometimes replacement is the better call. If the body is worn out, the latch is damaged, or the device no longer fits the door correctly, a new installation is usually cleaner. We check the door, the frame, and the existing prep before deciding whether repair or replacement makes more sense.
