Final Checks Matter

After the hardware is mounted and the system is programmed, we do a full walk-through with the doors open and closed, then with the door set in normal use. That tells us whether the latch lines up cleanly, whether the strike is holding the bolt where it should, and whether the keypad, fob, or access panel is reading every user the way it should. In a town like this, where salt air, wind off the water, and older door frames can all show up in the same job, those checks are what keep a system from acting stubborn a week later.

We also test the lock under real conditions: forced door movement, repeated entry attempts, battery backup if the system has it, and any exit hardware tied into the opening. That matters because a keyless system has to do two jobs at once. It needs to let the right people in without delay, and it needs to keep the door secure when it should be shut. We verify the schedule settings, user codes, and fob access levels so staff can come and go without handing around keys or leaving old credentials active after someone leaves.

Before we finish, we confirm that the finish is clean, the wiring is protected, and the device sits solid on the door or frame. On commercial openings near US-40 or down by the Promenade, that last check is important because vibration, traffic, and weather can work on loose parts fast. We leave only after the system responds the way it should and the door closes, locks, and reopens in a predictable way. That is how we know the job is ready for daily use.

Keyless Entry Systems in Havre de Grace - common questions

Can a keypad system work on the front door of my Havre de Grace shop without replacing the whole lock?

Often, yes. A keypad can sometimes be added to the existing door hardware, but it depends on the lock body, the door material, and how the latch is built. If the current setup is worn out or mismatched, replacing the lockset or adding electric strike hardware may be the cleaner fix.

What should I have ready when I call about keyless entry for my restaurant or gallery?

Photos of the door, the lock, and the inside side of the opening help a lot. It also helps to know how many people need access, whether you want codes or fobs, and whether the door already has a closer, alarm contact, or electric hardware. That gives a clearer picture of what will fit.

What usually goes wrong with commercial keyless entry systems near the water in Havre de Grace?

The common problems are worn buttons, weak batteries, reader issues, and hardware that drifts out of alignment. Moisture and exposure can also shorten the life of exterior parts. If the door slams hard or drags, that can cause false reads or a latch that won't release cleanly.

Should I choose keypad access or fobs for staff turnover at a downtown business?

If you want simple access changes, fobs are handy because you can add or remove them without touching the door hardware. Keypads are good when you want one code for a team or separate codes for shifts. The better choice depends on how many people need access and how tightly you want to track entry.

Can a keyless entry system be set up on an older door with original hardware?

Sometimes it can, but older doors can be tricky. If the frame is uneven, the latch is tired, or the lock parts don't line up well, the system may need a different strike, a new mortise body, or a full hardware change. The goal is to make the door work reliably, not force a modern system onto a bad fit.
