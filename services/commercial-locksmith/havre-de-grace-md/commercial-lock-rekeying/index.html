Commercial Lock Rekeying in Havre de Grace - common questions

How does commercial rekeying work for a storefront in Havre de Grace?

The locksmith removes the lock cylinder, changes the internal pin pattern, and cuts new keys to match that new setup. Old keys stop working. The lock body stays on the door, so this is different from replacing the whole lock. It's a common fix when a business wants to reset access after a staff change or a missing master key.

What should I have ready before I call about rekeying my business doors?

It helps to know how many doors need attention, whether any of them are keyed alike now, and if there's a master key involved. If you've had a turnover issue or a key went missing, mention that. If the hardware is sticking or the key has been hard to turn, say that too, since wear can change the plan.

Can rekeying fix a lock that already feels loose or hard to turn?

Sometimes, but not always. Rekeying changes who can open the lock; it doesn't rebuild worn parts. If the cylinder is sloppy, the key won't turn cleanly, or the door has alignment problems, the better answer may be a repair or replacement along with the rekey. The condition of the hardware decides that.

Should I rekey or replace the locks after an employee leaves with an old key?

If the hardware is in good shape, rekeying is usually the first thing to look at. It resets access without changing the whole lock. Replacement makes more sense if the lock is damaged, outdated, or not suited to the kind of control you want. A locksmith can look at the door and tell you which path fits the setup.

Can one key still open several doors in my Havre de Grace shop or restaurant?

Often yes. If the locks are compatible and the system is set up for it, they can be keyed alike again during the rekey. That can be useful for a small business that wants simpler access for managers while still keeping former keys out. The hardware has to be checked first to make sure it can be arranged that way.
