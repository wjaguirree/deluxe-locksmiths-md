Final Checks After Rekeying

After a commercial rekey, we do more than hand over new keys and move on. We test every cylinder we touched with the new key and with the old one that should no longer work. That check tells us the bitting is set correctly and that no one can still open the door with a key that was supposed to be retired after turnover, a lost master, or a lockout issue. We also run the key through several times to catch sticky wafers, worn pins, or a binding plug before the job is considered done.

We also verify how each lock behaves with the rest of the hardware on the door. A rekey can be correct inside the cylinder and still leave you with trouble if the latch drags, the deadbolt doesn't throw cleanly, or the door is out of alignment. That matters in places around the Route 40 corridor, where exterior doors take constant use and a small mechanical issue can turn into a daily headache for staff. If your building uses master keying, we confirm the hierarchy too, so the right keys open the right doors and no one gains access where they shouldn't.

Before we wrap up, we label or document key changes so your team knows what was updated and what still needs to be controlled. That helps property managers, office leads, and maintenance crews keep track of access after a change in staff or tenants. We also look at any related hardware that affects security, such as loose strikes, worn keyways, or damaged escutcheons, because a clean rekey should leave the whole opening working the way it should, not just the cylinder on its own.

Commercial Lock Rekeying in Edgewood - common questions

How does commercial rekeying work for a storefront in Edgewood?

We remove the cylinder, change the internal pins or parts that match the key, and issue new keys that fit the updated setup. The outside hardware can stay in place if it's in good shape. That lets you reset access after turnover, a lost key, or a master key issue without changing every lock on the property.

What should we have ready before you rekey our office or shop?

Have the doors identified that need the change, plus any existing keys you still have on hand. If there's a master key system, old tenant keys, or a list of which doors should match, bring that too. The clearer the access plan, the easier it is to set the locks up the way your business needs.

What's the risk if we keep using a lock after an employee leaves?

If old keys are still out there, anyone who kept a copy could still get in. That's the main reason businesses rekey after turnover or a lost master key. Rekeying resets who can open the door while keeping the existing hardware, which is usually the cleaner move when the lock itself still works.

Should we rekey the locks or replace them on our building near Pulaski Highway?

If the hardware is solid and the problem is access control, rekeying usually makes more sense. If the lock is damaged, worn out, or not holding the door properly, replacement may be the better answer. We can tell you which doors just need a new key setup and which ones need more than that.

Can you rekey a few doors but keep the rest of the business on the same key?

Yes, in many cases. That's common for offices, stockrooms, and side entries where you want tighter control on only part of the building. We can also tell you when a lock doesn't match the rest of the system, or when older hardware makes a clean match difficult.
