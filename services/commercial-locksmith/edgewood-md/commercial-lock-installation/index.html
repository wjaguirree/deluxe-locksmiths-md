What a Proper Install Shows

A proper install starts before the lock ever goes on the door. We match the hardware to the opening, the door material, and the way the building is used. A storefront on US-40, an office door, and a back-of-house steel door all need different hardware and different prep. We cut the mortise or prep cleanly, set the strike so the latch meets it square, and make sure the door closes without dragging, binding, or needing a hard shove. On a good job, the key turns smoothly, the latch throws fully, and the trim sits tight instead of wobbling or pulling away.

What separates real work from a bodge is the fit and the finish. Bad installs leave oversized holes, loose screws, crooked cylinders, or a lock that only works when the door is held a certain way. We also see makeshift fixes that ignore the frame or the closer, which leads to repeated problems after a few cycles of use. In busy parts of Edgewood, where commercial doors see steady traffic and weather, that kind of shortcut shows up fast. The lock should be aligned, anchored properly, and matched to the security level the door actually needs.

After we finish, you should be able to look for a few simple signs: the latch catches cleanly, the deadbolt throws all the way, the key operates without sticking, and the hardware doesn't shift when you use it. The door should close and lock the same way every time. If you're checking a new install near the Route 40 corridor or anywhere else in town, those are the details that tell you the work was done right.

Older properties around the Route 40 corridor often have doors that have been repaired more than once. Newer storefronts and office suites near the Edgewood Area of APG tend to have cleaner door prep and more standardized hardware. Whether the building is older or newer, the goal is the same: a lock that fits the door, lines up with the frame, and holds up under daily use.

Commercial Lock Installation in Edgewood - common questions

How do you decide whether my Edgewood storefront needs a new lock or just a repair?

We start by checking how the door closes, how the key turns, and whether the strike lines up with the latch. If the hardware is worn out, we replace it. If the door or frame is out of line, a new lock alone will not solve the problem. The right fix depends on what is causing the failure.

What should I have ready before you install a commercial lock at my shop on Pulaski Highway?

It helps to know which door needs work, who should have access, and whether the old hardware is still on the door. If you want keyed alike locks or separate keys for different doors, have that decided before we start. That keeps the installation focused on the opening that matters most.

My office door locks, but it still feels loose. Is that a sign the lock is failing?

Sometimes, but not always. A loose feel can come from worn hardware, stripped screws, or a frame that has shifted. If the latch is catching only part of the time, the problem may be the strike rather than the lock itself. We check the whole setup before recommending a replacement.

Should I choose a key-in-knob lock, deadbolt, or lever lock for a small business in Edgewood?

It depends on the door and how the space is used. A lever is common for offices and easier daily traffic, while a deadbolt can be a good fit for a door that needs stronger secure closure. We look at the type of door, the frame, and the existing prep before suggesting hardware that fits.

Can you install new locks on a door that was altered by a previous landlord or tenant?

Often yes, but we need to see what was changed first. Extra holes, patched hardware, or a moved strike can affect the new installation. Sometimes the door can still take standard commercial hardware. Other times we need to correct the prep so the new lock sits solid and works the way it should.
