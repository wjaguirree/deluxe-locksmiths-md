Commercial Lock Repair in Edgewood - common questions

My shop door still locks, so why would it need a repair instead of just leaving it alone?

If the lock only works when you pull or lift the door a certain way, something is wearing or shifting. In Edgewood, that usually shows up on busy entrances and rear doors that get used all day. Leaving it alone can lead to a latch that stops catching, a key that binds, or hardware that fails under normal use.

What should we have ready before you come out to repair a commercial lock?

It helps to know which door is acting up, what it's doing, and whether the problem is with the key, the latch, the strike, or the panic hardware. If you have an access key or building contact for the door, keep that handy. We can work from the symptoms and inspect the hardware on site.

Is it a bad sign if the lock only sticks when the door is busy or being slammed shut?

Yes, that usually points to wear, misalignment, or a closer problem. A lock can seem fine when the door is used gently, then act up under normal traffic. That's common on commercial doors in Edgewood, especially where customers, staff, or deliveries are using the same entrance all day.

Should we repair the existing lock or replace it with a new one?

If the body of the lock is still sound and the issue is alignment, loose parts, or a worn component, repair is often the right path. If the cylinder, latch, or housing is badly worn or damaged, replacement may make more sense. We'll look at the whole door and explain which option fits the hardware.

Can you fix a rear entrance lock that's been damaged by repeated use or by someone forcing it?

Sometimes, yes. If the frame, strike, and lock body are still usable, we can repair and realign the hardware. If the forced entry bent the door or broke the locking parts beyond a reliable repair, replacement may be needed. We'll tell you plainly what can be restored and what can't.
