A proper storefront repair starts with finding what's actually out of line, not just swapping parts and hoping it holds. On aluminum doors, the lock, closer, pivots, threshold, and frame all work together. If one piece is sagging or rubbing, the cylinder can feel stiff, the hook bolt can miss its keeper, or the door can drag so hard that the latch never seats cleanly. Storefront Door Lock Repair in Edgewood - common questions

My Edgewood shop door closes, but the key won't lock it. What usually causes that?

That usually means the door and frame aren't lining up cleanly, or the lock hardware is worn inside the door. On aluminum storefront doors, a small shift at the pivot or threshold can keep the latch or hook bolt from seating. We check the full door set so we fix the real cause, not just the symptom.

What should I have ready when I call about a storefront lock repair in Edgewood?

It helps to know whether the door is aluminum or steel, what the key is doing, and whether the problem is at one door or a pair of doors. If there's a tenant or manager on site, that helps too. A quick photo of the door edge and handle area can also make the conversation more useful.

Can a damaged storefront lock leave my shop easier to force open?

Yes. A loose latch, worn cylinder, or misaligned hook bolt can make the door less secure and easier to defeat. We look at how the lock meets the frame, whether the strike is holding properly, and whether the door is sitting square. If something is compromised, we'll recommend the repair that restores the door's function and security.

Should I repair the mortise cylinder or replace the whole lock on my Edgewood storefront?

It depends on what failed. If the cylinder is the issue and the body of the lock is still solid, a cylinder replacement may be enough. If the internal parts are worn, the bolt is damaged, or the lock has been forced, a larger repair makes more sense. We'll inspect it and tell you which option fits the door.

What if my storefront door in Edgewood keeps sticking after it's unlocked?

That's often a sign of hinge, pivot, or threshold trouble rather than a bad key. The lock may be fine, but the door may be rubbing, sagging, or hitting the frame off-center. We can adjust the hardware and check the lock together so the door opens, closes, and secures the way it should.
