Panic Bar Installation in Edgewood - common questions

How is a panic bar installed on a storefront door in Edgewood?

The door and frame are checked first so the device fits the opening and the latch lines up. Then the hardware is mounted, the strike is set, and the closer is adjusted so the door opens easily from inside and shuts without dragging. If the frame or door edge is worn, that has to be corrected too.

What should I have ready before you install exit hardware at my business?

Have the door used for exit access available, along with any inspection notes, building rules, or old hardware you want kept or replaced. It also helps to know whether the door is metal, aluminum, or wood, since that changes the mounting and reinforcement work.

What if my door frame is old or damaged

That's common on older commercial spaces. A panic bar won't work right if the frame is loose, bent, or patched badly. In that case, the frame may need repair, reinforcement, or new strike work before the device can latch the way it should.

Should I choose a rim device or a vertical rod setup for my building?

It depends on the door and the use of the opening. A single storefront door often uses a rim style device. Some wider or heavier openings may call for a different setup. The right choice comes from the door type, traffic level, and whether the opening needs extra hardware support.

Can you replace a locked-up push bar that's already on the door?

Yes, if the door and frame are still usable. Sometimes the device is worn out, sometimes the strike or closer is the real problem. If the existing hardware is beyond repair or no longer matches the opening, replacing it is the cleaner path.
