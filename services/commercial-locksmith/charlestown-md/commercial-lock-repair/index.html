Commercial Lock Repair in Charlestown - common questions

What usually gets repaired on a commercial lock in a waterfront business?

Most often it's the latch, deadbolt, cylinder, strike, or the way the door lines up with the frame. In Charlestown, salt air and heavy use can wear those parts faster than people expect. The fix depends on what's failing, but the goal is always the same: make the door lock and release smoothly again.

What should I do before someone comes out to fix our lock?

Keep the door in the safest position you can, and don't force the key or slam the door. If there's another key that normally works better, have it ready. It also helps to know whether the problem is at the latch, the deadbolt, or the strike plate, since that points the repair in the right direction.

Can salt air around the marinas really cause lock problems?

Yes. Salt, wind, and repeated use can wear down moving parts, corrode the cylinder, and make the door stop lining up with the frame. You may notice the key dragging, the latch sticking, or the bolt not fully catching. That doesn't always mean the lock has to be replaced, but it does need attention before it fails completely.

Is it better to repair the lock or replace it?

If the lock body is sound and the issue is wear, misalignment, or a tired cylinder, repair often makes sense. If the keyway is damaged, the housing is cracked, or parts don't hold adjustment, replacement may be the better call. The right choice depends on the hardware, the door, and how much use it gets each day.

What if the lock only acts up after the door has been used all day?

That's common on busy commercial doors. Heat, humidity, and repeated use can shift the door just enough to make the latch drag or the bolt miss the strike. In that case, the repair may involve both the lock and the door alignment. If it keeps changing through the day, mention that when you call.
