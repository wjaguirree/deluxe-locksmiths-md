Storefront Door Lock Repair in Charlestown - common questions

How do you decide whether my Charlestown storefront needs repair, rekeying, or a full lock replacement?

We look at both the lock and the door. If the hardware is intact but out of adjustment, repair is often enough. If you need a new key setup but the lock still works well, rekeying may be the right move. If the cylinder, hook bolt, or mortise body is worn or damaged, replacement is usually the better choice.

What should I have ready before you work on my storefront door near the water?

Have the current key, any spare keys, and a clear idea of what the door is doing, such as sticking, not latching, or turning hard. If other people use the entry, tell us who should keep access after the work is done. That helps us match the repair or rekeying to the way the door is actually used.

Can a bad storefront lock let someone force the door open more easily?

Yes. A misaligned hook bolt, loose strike, or worn pivot can leave the door easier to pry or shake open than it should be. We check the lock and the fit of the door together, because a strong lock on a poorly hanging door still leaves a weak point. Fixing the alignment is part of making the entry secure.

If my key still turns, why would I need anything besides a rekey?

Because rekeying only changes the key pattern inside the lock. If the door drags, the latch misses the strike, or the cylinder feels gritty, the problem is mechanical. In that case, we may repair the hardware or replace the worn part first, then rekey if you also need to control who has access.

Can you work on an older aluminum storefront door that has been repaired before?

Usually, yes. Older doors often have mixed parts, patched holes, or hardware that has been adjusted more than once. We inspect the pivots, threshold, cylinder, and locking points to see what can still be saved. If the door body is sound, we can often repair it without replacing the whole opening.
