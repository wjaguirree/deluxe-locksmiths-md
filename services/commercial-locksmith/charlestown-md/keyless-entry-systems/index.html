Keyless Entry Systems in Charlestown - common questions

How does a keyless entry system work for a marina gate or service door in Charlestown?

A keypad, fob reader, or access-control system checks whether a person has permission, then releases the lock if they do. For a marina or restaurant, that can mean different access for staff, managers, and vendors. The setup can be tied to one door or several points, depending on how much control you need.

What should I have ready before someone comes out to look at the system?

Have the brand name, model number if you can find it, and a short description of what the system is doing now. If you've got old codes, fobs, or paperwork from the original install, keep those together. It also helps to know whether the issue began after a power problem, battery change, or staff turnover.

I'm worried a former employee still knows the code. Is that a real problem?

Yes. If a code has been shared too widely, the clean fix is usually to change the code and review who still needs access. In some cases, moving to fobs or a managed access system makes more sense because credentials can be added or removed without giving out a door code that people can pass along.

Should I keep the keypad system I have, or switch to fobs or access control?

If you want something simple for a small staff, a keypad may be enough. If people come and go often, fobs or a managed access-control setup are easier to update when someone leaves. The right choice depends on how many users you have, how many doors you need to control, and how much record-keeping you want.

What if the power went out and now the door won't respond?

That can mean the battery backup is weak, the power supply needs attention, or the lock hardware didn't reset cleanly. Don't keep cycling the keypad or forcing the door. Note what changed after the outage and whether other devices on the same circuit are working, then have the system checked as a whole.
