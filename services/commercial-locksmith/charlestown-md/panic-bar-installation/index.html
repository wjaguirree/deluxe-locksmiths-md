A lot of people think a panic bar is just a door handle with a push plate. It's not. On a commercial door, the device has to let people get out cleanly under stress and still meet the code requirements that apply to the building. If the bar is wrong for the door, mounted poorly, or tied to the wrong latch and closer, it can create trouble during an inspection and even bigger trouble when people need the exit to work.

That's the kind of job we handle on site, because these doors usually can't come apart and go to a shop. For marinas, boat yards, and the restaurants on the water in Charlestown, the hardware has to stand up to heavy use, weather, and the way staff actually move through the building. We install panic and exit devices that fit the door, align with the frame, and support safe egress without making the door a nuisance to use. If you've got a door that sticks, won't unlatch cleanly, or needs to be brought up to code, we can take a look and get the right hardware in place.

Panic bar installation has to start with the door, not the hardware box. A lot of bad jobs happen when someone bolts on an exit device that doesn't match the door thickness, latch style, swing, or frame condition. On a marina-side building, that shows up fast: a bar that drags, a latch that doesn't fully catch, or a door that opens but won't close and stay secure. The right result is a device matched to the opening, aligned cleanly, and tested so the door opens with a firm push and still shuts the way it should.

The other common mistake is treating a panic bar like generic hardware. That leads to cut corners on dogging, trim, mounting height, strikes, or fire-rating needs. For restaurants on the water and other public-facing spaces, the exit device has to support real egress and hold up to steady use, not just pass a quick visual check. A proper installation means the door opens smoothly, the latch seats correctly, and the hardware fits the use of the building instead of fighting it.

In Charlestown, that matters in older buildings as much as newer ones. Some doors need reinforcement, closer adjustment, or frame repair before the bar goes on. If the opening is already damaged or out of square, the fix should address that first. The goal is a code-compliant exit door that works the same way for staff, guests, and inspectors, without sticking, rattling, or becoming a weak point at the first busy night.

Panic Bar Installation in Charlestown - common questions

How is a panic bar installed on an older Charlestown storefront door?

The door and frame get checked first for fit, swing, latch depth, and any damage. If the opening is out of square, the hardware won't work right no matter how new it is. After that, the correct exit device is mounted, aligned, and tested so the door opens easily and closes securely.

What should I have ready before you install an exit device at my marina or restaurant?

It helps to know the door material, whether it's a fire door, and what the door is used for day to day. If there's an existing closer, lock, or deadbolt, that matters too. Clear access to the door and the surrounding frame lets the work start with a full check of the opening.

What's the biggest risk if the panic bar is installed wrong?

The biggest problem is a door that opens but doesn't latch, or a latch that won't release cleanly under use. That can create a life-safety issue and a failed inspection. Bad mounting can also damage the door over time, especially on openings that see steady traffic.

Should I choose a rim device or another type of exit hardware for a building near the waterfront?

It depends on the door construction, the frame, and what the opening needs to do. A simple single door often uses a rim device, but some doors call for different hardware because of their size or existing lock setup. The right choice is the one that fits the door and the code requirements together.

Can you add a panic bar if the door already has a lock or closer on it?

Sometimes yes, but not always without changes. Existing locks, closers, and strikes can conflict with exit hardware if they're not matched correctly. The door has to be looked at as a whole. In some cases, parts need to be removed or adjusted so the exit device works properly and the door still shuts and secures.
