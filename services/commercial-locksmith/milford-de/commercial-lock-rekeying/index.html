Commercial Lock Rekeying in Milford - common questions

How does commercial rekeying work for a shop or office in Milford?

The lock is taken apart, the internal pins are changed, and new keys are cut to match the new setup. The outside hardware usually stays in place. That makes rekeying a smart option when you want to change access without replacing every lock on the building.

What should I have ready before you rekey my business doors?

Have a list of which doors need to match, any master key setup you use, and the keys you still want to keep active. If you know which keys are missing or who no longer needs access, that helps the job go cleanly and keeps the new key system organized.

Is rekeying enough if a former employee still has an old key?

Usually, yes, if the locks themselves are still in good shape. Rekeying makes the old key useless. If the hardware is worn, damaged, or not matching the rest of your doors, then a lock repair or replacement may be the better long-term fix.

Should I rekey or replace the locks on my Milford storefront?

If the locks work well and you mainly need to control who has access, rekeying is usually the first thing to look at. If the lock sticks, the key is hard to turn, or the parts are worn out, replacing the hardware can save you from chasing the same issue again.

Can you rekey old commercial locks if the building still has the original hardware?

Sometimes, yes. It depends on the lock type and whether the parts are still in usable condition. Older hardware can often be rekeyed, but if the cylinders are badly worn or the keyway has been altered over the years, the lock may need more than a pin change.
