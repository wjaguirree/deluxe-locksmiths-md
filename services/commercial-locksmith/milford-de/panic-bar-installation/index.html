Panic Bar Installation in Milford - common questions

How does a panic bar installation work for a shop or office in Milford?

We look at the door, frame, and existing lock hardware first, then choose a panic or exit device that fits the opening. After that, we mount the bar, set the strike, test the latch, and make sure the door still closes and releases the way it should. If the outside needs keyed access, we handle that side too.

What should I have ready before you install a panic bar at my Milford location?

It helps to know which door is the main exit, whether anyone needs key access from the outside, and whether the door is solid wood, metal, or glass-and-frame. If the opening has given you trouble before, point out the sticking spot or broken part. That gives us a clear path before we start the work.

I'm worried the panic bar on my door won't pass an inspection. What do you check?

We check that the door releases from the inside without extra steps, that the latch fully clears, and that the hardware matches the door and frame. We also look for bad alignment, worn parts, and any condition that could keep the exit from working as intended. If something needs correction, we'll address it before wrapping up.

Should I repair, rekey, or replace the panic bar on my building?

If the device is mechanically sound but has a small failure, repair is often enough. If the outside cylinder is the issue and the rest of the hardware is solid, rekeying can make sense. If the bar is the wrong style, badly worn, or no longer dependable, replacement is usually the better long-term choice.

Can a panic bar be installed on a side door or a back entrance that gets used after hours?

Yes, as long as the door and frame can support the right device and the opening is set up for safe exit. That matters for side and rear entrances that staff use when the main entrance is closed. We look at how the door is used, then choose hardware that gives controlled entry from outside and clear egress from inside.
