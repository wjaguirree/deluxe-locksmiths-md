Storefront Door Lock Repair in Milford - common questions

My Milford storefront key turns, but the door still won't lock. What are you checking first?

We start with the latch or hook bolt, then check the alignment of the door, pivots, and strike. On aluminum storefront doors, the lock can seem bad when the real problem is a door that has dropped or shifted. We test the full assembly before replacing parts so the fix matches the actual failure.

What should I have ready before you work on my Walnut Street shop door?

Have the key that normally works, and be ready to describe what the door is doing: sticking, dragging, not latching, or locking only partway. If you know whether the door was hit, slammed, or worked on before, tell us that too. Those details save time when we open the hardware and inspect it.

Can a broken storefront lock leave my Milford office more exposed than I realize?

Yes. A lock that still turns can give a false sense of security if the hook bolt isn't fully engaging or the strike is worn. A loose pivot or sagging door can make the problem worse. We check both the lock and the door fit so the door closes securely, not just temporarily.

Should I repair the mortise cylinder or replace the whole storefront lock?

If the cylinder is the only worn part, repair or replacement of the cylinder may be enough. If the hook bolt, internal body, or strike is damaged, replacing the whole lock set can be the better option. We look at how the door is built and what failed before we recommend one path over the other.

What if the storefront door on my Milford building keeps rubbing the threshold?

That usually points to a door alignment problem, not just a lock problem. A rubbing threshold can wear down the latch, bend the strike, or make the bolt miss its opening. We can adjust the hardware and replace worn parts so the door closes squarely and the lock works without forcing it.
