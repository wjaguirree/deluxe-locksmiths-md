Storefront Door Lock Repair in Forest Hill - common questions

Why won't my aluminum storefront door lock even though the key turns?

On storefront doors, the key turning doesn't always mean the lock is lining up. The mortise cylinder may be fine, but the hook bolt, strike, pivot, or threshold could be out of position. If the door has dropped or the closer is pulling it unevenly, the lock may not catch cleanly even though the cylinder works.

What should I have ready before someone looks at the door in Forest Hill?

Have the door propped open and closed, if possible, and be ready to describe exactly what it's doing: sticking, scraping, failing to latch, or locking only when lifted. A photo of the edge hardware and the face of the door helps. If there's a panic bar or patch hardware, mention that up front.

Is a misaligned storefront lock a security risk for a shop on MD-24?

Yes. If the hook bolt or latch isn't fully engaging, the door may look shut while still being easy to force or rattle loose. Even a small alignment issue can wear the hardware faster and leave the entry unreliable after closing. A repair should restore full engagement, not just make the key turn again.

Should I repair the mortise cylinder or replace the whole storefront lock set?

It depends on what's worn. If the cylinder is the only problem, replacing just that part may be enough. If the hook bolt, keeper, pivot, or internal mechanism is worn, a broader repair makes more sense. The best choice is the one that matches the door's condition instead of swapping parts one by one.

What if the storefront door was damaged by a delivery cart or forced open?

That can bend the edge of the door, shift the pivot, or throw the strike out of line. In that case, the lock may be only part of the problem. The door should be checked for frame damage, closer strain, and whether the hardware still sits square. Sometimes the lock can be repaired, but sometimes the opening needs adjustment first.
