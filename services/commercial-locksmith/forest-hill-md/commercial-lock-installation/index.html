Commercial Lock Installation in Forest Hill - common questions

How does a commercial lock installation usually work on a storefront or office door in Forest Hill?

The first step is checking the door, frame, and existing cutout so the new lock fits the opening the right way. From there, the hardware is installed, aligned, and tested for smooth latching and locking. If the door already has a setup in place, that can affect what parts are needed and whether anything has to be adjusted.

What should I have ready before you come out to install a new business lock?

Have the door open if possible, and know which door needs the work. If there are existing keys, hardware pieces, or paperwork from the door manufacturer, keep those together. It also helps to know who should hold the final keys, since commercial installs often depend on who needs access after the work is done.

Can a new commercial lock fix a door that keeps sticking on the frame?

Sometimes, but not always. A sticking door can be caused by hinge wear, frame movement, or the door itself sagging. A new lock may not solve that on its own. The lock should be installed after the door closes properly, or the hardware can end up binding and wearing out sooner than it should.

Should I rekey the new lock to match the rest of my building or start with a separate key?

That depends on how you use the space. If you want one key to open several doors, rekeying can make daily access simpler. If the new door is for back-of-house access, storage, or a tenant space, a separate key may be the better choice. The right setup depends on who needs access and how tightly you want it controlled.

What if the door is new construction and the builder already left hardware on it?

That happens a lot in Forest Hill. Builder-grade hardware may work for a while, but it does not always match the way the business will actually use the door. In some cases the lock can be replaced cleanly; in others the door prep or the frame needs a closer look before new hardware goes in.
