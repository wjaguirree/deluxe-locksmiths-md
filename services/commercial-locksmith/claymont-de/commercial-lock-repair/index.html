Commercial Lock Repair in Claymont - common questions

Why does the storefront lock on Philadelphia Pike keep sticking even though the key still turns?

That usually means the problem is not just the key or the cylinder. The latch may be worn, the strike may be out of line, or the door may be sagging a little and throwing everything off. We check the full door setup, since a lock that turns but does not catch cleanly is often a fit or hardware issue.

What should we have ready before you come repair a commercial lock in Claymont?

It helps to know which door is acting up, when the problem started, and whether the lock is keyed alike with other doors on the property. If there is a key that still works, have it handy. If the door has a closer, panic bar, or access control parts, let us know so we can inspect the whole assembly.

Can a damaged lock be repaired, or does it usually need to be replaced?

Either can be right depending on the wear. If the body, cylinder, or internal parts are still sound, repair may be enough. If the hardware is cracked, badly worn, or no longer matches the door properly, replacement is the better path. We look at what is actually failing instead of assuming one answer.

Is it a problem if one employee keeps forcing the key to get the door open?

Yes. Forcing a key can bend parts inside the lock, worsen alignment problems, or break the key off in the cylinder. That can turn a manageable repair into a bigger one. If a door needs extra muscle to open or latch, it is better to stop using it that way and have us inspect it.

What if the lock works, but the door does not shut smoothly after the hardware was repaired?

That can happen when the lock is only part of the issue. The hinges, closer, strike plate, or door alignment may still be off. On commercial doors, everything has to work together. We can check the door as a system and correct the part that is keeping it from closing and locking properly.
