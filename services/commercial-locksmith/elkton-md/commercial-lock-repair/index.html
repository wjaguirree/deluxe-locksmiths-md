If the key sticks, the latch drags, the lever feels loose, or the door only catches when it's pushed hard, those details tell us a lot about whether the problem is the lock body, the strike, the closer, or the hinge side of the opening. Commercial Lock Repair in Elkton - common questions

What usually gets repaired on a commercial lock in Elkton?

Most repairs involve the lock body, latch, cylinder, strike plate, or the way the door and frame meet. If the hardware is worn or out of alignment, the door may not secure cleanly even if the key still fits. We look at the whole setup, not just one part.

What should I have ready before calling about a broken storefront lock?

It helps to know which door is acting up, what it does when you try to lock or unlock it, and whether the issue started after wear, a jam, or a door adjustment. If other staff use the same key or the problem happens at certain times of day, that's useful too.

Is a sticky lock a security problem or just a nuisance?

It can be both. A sticky lock often means parts are wearing out or the door is shifting, and that can get worse until the door won't secure properly. If staff start forcing the key or slamming the door, the hardware can fail faster.

Should I repair the lock or replace it for my Elkton office door?

If the hardware is structurally sound, repair is often the better first step. If the cylinder is badly worn, the latch is damaged, or parts no longer line up correctly, replacement may make more sense. The right choice depends on the condition of the door, not just the lock name on the faceplate.

Can you fix a commercial lock if the door itself is sagging or rubbing?

Yes, sometimes the lock problem is really a door alignment issue. If the door drags, shifts, or rubs the frame, the latch may miss the strike and the lock will feel bad even if the mechanism is fine. In those cases, the repair has to account for the door and hardware together.
