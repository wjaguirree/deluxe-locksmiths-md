Storefront Door Lock Repair in Elkton - common questions

Why does my aluminum storefront door lock feel tight but still not catch right?

That usually means the lock is only part of the problem. The door may be sagging on its pivots, the strike may be off, or the threshold may be worn. We check the full door setup so the repair matches the way the opening actually hangs and closes, not just the symptom at the key.

What should our staff do before you get to the storefront on East Main?

Leave the door as close to its normal locked or unlocked position as you safely can, and keep any spare keys or loose hardware nearby. Don't force the key, slam the door, or keep trying to twist a jammed lock. Those steps can turn a repairable issue into broken internal parts or a damaged cylinder.

Could a worn hook bolt or pivot turn into a bigger security problem?

Yes. If the door is dragging or not fully seating, the lock may look engaged even when it isn't holding correctly. That can leave gaps at the latch side or make the door easier to force. We check for wear in the hardware and the frame so the fix actually restores the secure close.

Should we repair the mortise cylinder or replace the whole storefront lock body?

It depends on what is worn. If the cylinder is the only bad part, a cylinder change may be enough. If the internal lock body is worn, bent, or damaged by repeated forcing, replacement makes more sense. We'll inspect the hardware and tell you which path fits the condition of the door.

What if the door only binds when it's hot in the afternoon or after the building sits closed?

That can happen when the frame, threshold, or pivots shift under changing conditions. The lock may be fine, but the door is no longer lining up the same way all day. We can check the fit under the conditions you're seeing and adjust the hardware so the door closes more consistently.
