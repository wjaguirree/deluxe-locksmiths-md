Storefront Door Lock Repair in Aberdeen - common questions

My storefront door in Aberdeen is hard to lock. How do you tell if it's the lock or the door itself?

We check how the key turns, how the latch or hook bolt lines up, and whether the door is hanging square in the frame. A hard lock can come from worn internal parts, but it can also come from a pivot, threshold, or strike that's no longer lined up. The fix depends on which part is actually failing.

What should we have ready before you work on our shop door?

Have the building key, any current key control info, and the name of the person who can approve the repair. If the door has been sticking, it helps to know when it started and whether anyone has already adjusted the closers or hardware. That saves time and helps us focus on the right cause.

Can a bad storefront lock become a safety problem for customers near the Aberdeen Proving Ground area?

Yes. If the door doesn't latch the way it should, the entrance can be left unsecured or can jam when people need to use it. That's a security concern and a practical one. We check the lock, the door alignment, and the closing hardware so the entrance works the way it should for daily use.

Should we repair the mortise cylinder or replace the whole storefront lock?

If the cylinder is the only worn part, repairing or replacing that part may be enough. If the hook bolt, internal case, or several moving parts are worn, a fuller replacement may make more sense. We look at the condition of the door and hardware first so you're not replacing parts that still have usable life.

Our tenant door on a Route 40 storefront was forced and now drags. Can that still be repaired?

Often it can. A forced door may leave the pivot bent, the strike damaged, or the frame out of line. We inspect the hardware and the door swing to see what can be adjusted and what needs replacement. If the frame is damaged, we'll say so plainly before any work moves ahead.
