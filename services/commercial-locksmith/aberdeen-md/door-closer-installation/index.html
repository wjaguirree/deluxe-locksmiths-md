Door Closer Installation in Aberdeen - common questions

How does a commercial door closer installation work on my Aberdeen storefront?

We look at the door, frame, hinges, and latch first, then choose a closer that matches the door's use. After mounting it, we set the closing speed, latch speed, and backcheck if needed. The final step is testing the door several times so it closes fully without slamming or stopping short.

What should we have ready before you install a closer on our office door?

Please make sure the door opens and closes normally and that the frame area around the top of the door is accessible. If there's already a closer, tell us whether it's leaking, loose, or leaving the door ajar. If the door is fire-rated or tied to access control, let us know that up front.

What can go wrong if a door closer is installed wrong?

A bad install can make the door slam, stop before latching, or drag hard enough to wear out hinges and locks. On a commercial door, that can also mean people prop it open because it's annoying to use. We correct that by matching the closer to the door and setting it up so it actually supports the opening.

Should we replace the closer we have or switch to a different style?

If the current closer is only out of adjustment, we can often save it. If the body is worn out, leaking, or too small for the door, replacement makes more sense. For some doors, a surface-mounted closer is the cleanest option; for others, the frame or traffic pattern points to a heavier-duty setup.

Can you install a closer on a fire door or a rear entrance with heavy daily use?

Yes, as long as the door and hardware are suitable for that use. Fire doors need the right closer and must latch properly every time. Rear entrances that see deliveries or staff traffic often need a sturdier closer and tighter adjustment. We check the door as a whole so the installation fits the job, not just the hardware.
