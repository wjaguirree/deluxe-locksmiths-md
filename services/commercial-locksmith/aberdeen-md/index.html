Commercial locksmith in Aberdeen - common questions

How does a commercial rekey work if the lock itself is still in decent shape?

The lock stays on the door, but the internal pins are changed so the old key no longer works. That's useful after employee turnover, a lost key, or a move into a space where you don't know who still has copies. If the hardware is worn out, rekeying won't fix that, and replacement may be the better call.

What should I have ready before you come out to a shop or office in Aberdeen?

It helps to know which doors are involved, who needs access, and whether you want the same key to open more than one lock. If you have a tenant mix, a manager key, or a panic device that has been giving trouble, point that out. Clear access to the doors and any existing key list makes the job cleaner.

If a storefront lock is sticking, what's the risk of waiting on it?

A sticking lock can turn into a door that won't secure fully or won't open smoothly for staff. That creates two problems at once: weaker protection after closing and more wear on the door, frame, and hardware. If people start forcing the handle or slamming the door, the damage usually spreads fast.

Should I choose a master key system or keyless entry for a small business?

A master key system is a good fit when you still want physical keys but need access organized by role. Keyless entry works better when you want to cut down on key control, track access changes more easily, or avoid rekeying every time staffing changes. The right choice depends on the door count and how people move through the building.

Can you fix a panic bar that looks fine but won't latch right every time?

Sometimes, yes. The trouble may be in the bar itself, the closer, the strike, or the way the door and frame line up. If the hardware is bent, worn, or missing parts, repair may not hold. On a commercial door, a panic device has to function reliably, not just look intact.
