Panic Bar Installation in Aberdeen - common questions

How do you decide which panic bar my Aberdeen storefront needs?

We start with the door material, width, swing, and how the exit is used. A rear service door, a glass storefront, and a steel employee exit don't all take the same hardware. We match the device to the opening so it latches cleanly and releases the way the door is supposed to.

What should I have ready before you install a panic bar at my shop?

If possible, have the door area open, know which door is being worked on, and share any building or inspection requirements you've been given. If the door also works with a closer, alarm, or access control, let us know. That helps us set the hardware up as one working system instead of a single part.

What if the exit door in my Aberdeen office is already sticking or scraping?

That's important to mention before the install. A panic bar can't do its job well on a door that's out of alignment or dragging badly. We may need to correct the fit, adjust the frame, or address the closer first so the new hardware latches and releases properly.

Should I keep my existing lock or switch to a new exit device?

It depends on how the door is used. If the current lock conflicts with safe egress or doesn't meet the opening's needs, a different exit device may be the better choice. If the lock is part of a working setup, we can often integrate it. We'll look at the opening and explain the options plainly.

Can you install a panic bar on a door near Aberdeen Proving Ground with restricted access?

Yes, as long as we can access the door and the hardware choice fits the building's rules. Restricted sites often need careful coordination with management or security, and sometimes the device has to work with an alarm or controlled entry system. We'll work from the site requirements, not guess at them.
