A building with older door hardware, patched cylinders, or long-used master key systems can usually be rekeyed without disturbing every lock on the property, but the work has to be done carefully so the new key pattern matches what's already there. We see that a lot in older offices, small warehouses, and mixed-use spaces where the hardware has been serviced over the years and not always by the same person. In those cases, we inspect the cylinders, check how the keys are cut, and rekey the locks so former keys stop working while the usable hardware stays in place.

Newer buildings and newer hardware bring a different set of issues. Commercial Lock Rekeying in Aberdeen - common questions

How does a commercial rekey work in an Aberdeen office or shop?

We remove the cylinder, reset the pins so the old key no longer fits, and cut new keys for the people who should still have access. If several doors should match, we line them up that way. The goal is to keep the locks you already have and change who can use them.

What should we have ready before you come to rekey our building?

Have the doors that need service identified and know who should keep access after the change. If there's a master key system, bring any key records you have. It also helps to tell us about any sticking locks, broken keys, or doors that were recently repaired so we can plan the work in the right order.

What if a master key was lost at our Aberdeen Proving Ground contract office?

That's a strong reason to rekey quickly, because the old master can still open every lock it was made for. We can reset the affected cylinders so the lost key no longer works. If the building uses multiple access levels, we'll keep the key hierarchy in mind so the right people still get in.

Should we rekey the locks or replace them after staff turnover?

Rekeying is usually the better choice when the hardware is in good shape and the problem is access control. Replacement makes more sense if the lock is damaged, heavily worn, or no longer fits the door properly. We can look at the setup on arrival and tell you which route makes sense for each door.

Can you rekey every door if our building has a mix of older and newer locks?

Often, yes, but not every lock system is identical. Older hardware may use different cylinders or keyways than newer doors, and some locks may need separate treatment. We'll check each one and let you know what can be matched together and what needs to stay on its own key.
