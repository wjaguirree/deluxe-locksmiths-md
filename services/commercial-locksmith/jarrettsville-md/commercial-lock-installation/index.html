Commercial Lock Installation in Jarrettsville - common questions

How does a commercial lock installation usually work on a shop or office in Jarrettsville?

We start by looking at the door, frame, and how the space gets used. Older buildings sometimes need the opening checked first so the new lock lines up correctly. Then we install the right commercial hardware, test the latch and strike, and make sure the door closes and secures the way it should.

What should I have ready before we install new locks on my business doors?

It helps to know which doors need new hardware, which ones are staff-only, and whether the same key should work across more than one entrance. If the building has older doors or past repairs, tell us that too. That helps us bring the right hardware and plan for any frame or hole corrections.

My building is old and the door has been patched before. Is that a problem?

Not necessarily, but it changes the job. Old patches, worn frames, and shifted doors can affect how a lock sits and how well the latch catches. We can often work with existing openings, but if the door is badly out of line, we'll point that out before installing hardware that won't hold up.

Should I choose the same lock for my front office and my back door?

Not always. A front office door may need different hardware than a rear service entrance or storage room. We look at traffic, privacy, and who needs access. In many buildings, the best setup is a mix of lock types that still keeps key control simple for your staff.

Can you install commercial locks on a detached garage or equipment shed at my property?

Yes. Those outbuildings often need a different lock than the main office because the doors, frames, and use are different. We can install commercial-grade hardware on garage doors, utility doors, and storage spaces, as long as the door itself is in usable condition or can be corrected on site.
