Repair, Rekey, or Replace

When we look at a storefront door in Jarrettsville, we start with the hardware that's actually failing. On aluminum doors, the problem is often not the whole door but one worn part: a hook bolt that won't catch, a pivot that's gone out of line, a threshold that's no longer supporting the sweep, or a mortise cylinder that turns rough or sticks. If the door is sound and the issue is isolated, repair is usually the right move. We adjust, rebuild, or replace the failed part and get the door closing and locking the way it should.

Rekeying makes sense when the lock still works but control needs to change. If a tenant moved out, a key was lost, or too many copies have been handed around, we can rekey the cylinder so old keys no longer work while keeping the rest of the door hardware in place. That's often the best choice for shops along MD-23 where the door is in decent shape and the security concern is really about who has access, not whether the lock body is worn out.

Replacement is the call when repair won't hold. If the mortise lock is stripped inside, the cylinder is damaged beyond clean rekeying, or the door has taken enough wear that the hardware no longer lines up, we'll replace the failing parts with hardware that matches the door and the opening. We also look at the door frame, the pivot set, and the threshold together, because on a storefront those pieces work as one system. Out by the Jarrettsville crossroads, with weather and daily use beating on the same entrance, a lock fix has to be chosen for how the whole door performs, not just for the symptom you notice first.

Storefront Door Lock Repair in Jarrettsville - common questions

My aluminum storefront door locks, but the handle feels rough. What usually causes that?

That usually points to alignment, worn pivots, or a latch that isn't meeting the strike cleanly. The cylinder may be fine. We check the whole door first, because a rough handle often means the door is hanging slightly off square or the threshold and strike need adjustment.

What should I have ready before you come out to repair my shop door in Jarrettsville?

Have the door usable enough for us to inspect it safely, and tell us what it's doing: not latching, key sticking, handle loose, or the door rubbing. If you know whether the hardware is original or has been changed before, that helps us narrow the repair.

Can a bad repair leave my storefront easier to force open?

Yes. If the lock is replaced without fixing a sagging door or a loose strike, the hardware can end up weaker than before. We try to avoid that by checking the pivots, threshold, and frame first, then repairing the lock so the whole setup closes and catches the way it should.

Should I repair the mortise cylinder or replace the whole lock on my storefront door?

If the body of the lock is sound and the trouble is limited to the cylinder, repair or replacement of that part may be enough. If the hook bolt, internal parts, or mounting are worn out, replacing the full assembly can be the better call. We'll look at the door and tell you which path makes sense.

My building is older and the storefront door has been worked on before. Is that a problem?

Not necessarily, but older doors often have mixed parts and past repairs that don't line up well. That can make the job more involved. We sort through what's still usable, what's worn out, and what needs to be adjusted so the repair fits the door instead of fighting it.
