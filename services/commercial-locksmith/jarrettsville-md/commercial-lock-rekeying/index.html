Commercial Lock Rekeying in Jarrettsville - common questions

How does commercial rekeying work for a small business in Jarrettsville?

The lock cylinders are taken apart and reset so old keys stop working and new keys take over. If the hardware is in decent shape, the door stays in place and only the keying changes. That's useful for offices, shops, and storage rooms where access needs to be tightened without replacing every lock.

What should I have ready before a rekey at my shop or farm business?

Have the doors or rooms you want included identified, along with any keys you still want to keep in service. It also helps to know whether there's a master key system, a lost key, or a staff change behind the job. That lets the rekey match how the building is actually used.

Can rekeying fix a lock that's already worn out from years of use?

Sometimes, but not always. If the cylinder is worn, the key turns rough, or parts are damaged, rekeying may not solve the whole problem. In older Jarrettsville buildings, it's common to find hardware that needs repair or replacement after the lock is opened up.

Should I rekey or replace the lock after an employee leaves?

If the lock body is sound, rekeying is usually the cleaner move because it changes access without changing the whole door set. Replacement makes more sense when the lock is damaged, outdated, or no longer fits the security you want. The right choice depends on the condition of the hardware and the kind of access control you need.

What if my building has old keys mixed with newer ones from an expansion?

That's common in rural commercial properties. The older doors may use a different keyway or a previous master system, while the newer doors follow something else. In that case, the job may involve rekeying some cylinders, leaving others alone, or setting up a cleaner key plan so one building doesn't depend on a patchwork of old keys.
