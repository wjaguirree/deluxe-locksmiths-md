Commercial Lock Repair in Jarrettsville - common questions

My shop door in Jarrettsville won't latch unless I lift it. What are you checking first?

We'd start with the door alignment, hinges, strike plate, and latch before we blame the lock itself. A lot of commercial doors don't actually have a bad lock; they have a door that has dropped, shifted, or started rubbing. If the hardware is worn too far, we'll repair or replace the part that's failing.

What should we have ready before you come out to repair a commercial lock?

It helps to know which door is acting up, what the lock is doing, and whether the issue started after a hard close, weather change, or break-in attempt. If you've got matching keys, keep them handy. If the door has panic hardware or a closer, letting us know that upfront helps us bring the right parts.

I'm worried the door will still be easy to force open after the repair. Can that happen?

It can if the lock is fixed but the door or frame is still damaged. That's why we look at the whole setup: lock, strike, frame, hinges, and closer. If something is weak, we'll point it out and repair what's needed so the hardware is actually holding the door the way it should.

Do you try to repair the existing lock, or do you replace it right away?

We try to repair what's still in good shape. If the cylinder, latch, trim, or internal parts are worn out, replacement makes more sense than patching a failing part. On commercial doors, we usually aim to keep the usable hardware and replace only the part that's causing the trouble.

What if the lock is on an older building near the Deer Creek side of town and the hardware is outdated?

That's common, and it doesn't automatically mean the door needs a full overhaul. We can often work with older mortise or rim-style hardware by replacing the worn pieces, matching the function, and keeping the door secure. If parts are no longer serviceable, we'll explain the options plainly before any replacement goes in.
