Panic Bar Installation in Jarrettsville - common questions

How does a panic bar installation work on a business door in Jarrettsville?

We inspect the door, frame, latch, and closer first, then match the device to the opening. After that we mount the hardware, check the push action, verify the latch catches cleanly, and make sure the door closes the way it should. If the door needs adjustment, we handle that as part of the job.

What should we have ready before you install an exit device at our shop near the Jarrettsville crossroads?

It helps to know what kind of door you have, whether it's wood, metal, or aluminum, and whether the door already has a closer or deadbolt hardware. If there's an existing problem, tell us what it's doing so we can bring the right parts. Clear access to the door and frame also helps.

Our back door opens, but it won't stay latched. Is that the panic bar or the door?

It could be either one. A worn latch, a misaligned strike, a sagging door, or a bad closer can all cause that problem. We look at the full opening so we don't replace the wrong part. In a lot of cases, a simple adjustment fixes it; in others, the device has reached the point where replacement makes more sense.

Should we choose a rim panic device or a vertical rod setup for our building?

That depends on the door, the traffic, and how the opening is built. Rim devices are common on simpler single doors. Vertical rod hardware is better when the door needs top-and-bottom locking points. We'll look at the opening and explain which style fits the door and the way your business uses it.

Can you install a panic bar on a metal side door that's used for deliveries, even if it's not a main entrance?

Yes, if the door and frame can support the right hardware and the opening is suitable for an exit device. Side and rear doors often need just as much attention as the front door, especially if staff use them every day. We check the door construction, the latch area, and the closing action before we install anything.
