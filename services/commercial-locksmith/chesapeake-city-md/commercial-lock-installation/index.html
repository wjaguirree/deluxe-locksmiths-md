Commercial Lock Installation in Chesapeake City - common questions

How do you decide which commercial lock makes sense for my Chesapeake City storefront?

We look at the door type, how often it opens, who needs access, and whether the door is for customers, staff, or storage. A front entry, a rear service door, and an office door often need different hardware. We match the lock to the way the business operates so it is secure without being awkward for daily use.

What should I have ready before you install a new lock on my building?

It helps to know which door needs attention, who should have access, and whether any old hardware is being replaced or reused. If there are keys, a code list, or a broken part from the old lock, have that handy. We can work from there and sort out the rest on site.

Should I be worried about using the same key on the front and back doors of my business?

Sometimes that is convenient, but it is not always the best setup. If too many people can open too many doors, control gets messy fast. We can set up matching keys where it makes sense, or separate access where staff-only areas need tighter control. The right answer depends on how your building is used.

Is a standard deadbolt enough for a restaurant door, or do I need something heavier duty?

For many commercial doors, a standard residential lock is not the right fit. Restaurants and inns usually need hardware made for repeated use, tighter closing, and better resistance to wear. We look at the frame, the door thickness, and the traffic on that entry before recommending a lock style that will hold up.

Can you install a commercial lock if the door is older and the frame is not perfect?

Yes, but the condition of the door and frame matters a lot. Older trim, worn strike areas, or warped wood can affect how well a lock sits and latches. We can often make the hardware work, but sometimes the door needs adjustment first. If the frame is badly damaged, we will say so plainly.
