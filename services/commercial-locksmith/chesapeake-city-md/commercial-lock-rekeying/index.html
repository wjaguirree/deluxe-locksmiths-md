Commercial Lock Rekeying in Chesapeake City - common questions

How does commercial rekeying work in a place like Chesapeake City?

We change the inner pinning of the lock cylinders so the old keys no longer work. The hardware stays in place, which is useful for a business that wants to keep using its current doors, deadbolts, and locksets. We can also set up the locks to work with a new master key plan if your building needs that.

What should I have ready before we rekey my shop or inn?

It helps to know which doors should stay on the same key and which ones should be separated. If you have old keys, a list of employees who still need access, or notes about doors that act up, that helps us plan the work. We also need someone who can identify the spaces that should be opened and tested.

What if a former employee still has a key to our building?

That's one of the main reasons businesses rekey. If the old key is still out there, rekeying stops it from working without forcing you to replace every lock. For a place with staff turnover or seasonal help, it's a straightforward way to reset access and keep control of the doors that matter.

Should I rekey everything to one key or keep different doors separate?

It depends on how the building is used. Many owners want one key for the main entrances and office, but separate keys for storage, liquor, or staff-only areas. We can talk through how the property is used day to day and build a key plan that fits the way your team actually works.

Can you rekey older locks in the historic part of town, or do they need to be replaced?

Some older locks can be rekeyed and kept in service, while others are too worn or too unusual to support a clean rekey. We look at the lock first and tell you what makes sense. If the hardware is still solid, rekeying is often the best way to keep the look of the door while changing who has access.
