Storefront Door Lock Repair in Camden - common questions

Why won't my Camden storefront door latch unless I push it hard?

That usually points to alignment, not just the lock. On aluminum storefront doors, the pivot, threshold, hook bolt, or strike can drift out of position and keep the door from seating properly. We check the full setup so the repair addresses the real cause instead of treating only the symptom.

What should we have ready before you work on our storefront lock in Camden?

Have the door accessible, the current keys on hand, and any key instructions or past service notes if you have them. If the lock is tied to other doors or staff entries, it helps to know that before we start. Clear access around the door also makes the work easier and safer.

Is it risky to keep using a storefront lock that sticks sometimes?

Yes, because sticking often gets worse under daily use. A lock that only catches part of the time can leave the door unsecured or can trap staff outside or inside at the wrong moment. We look at whether the issue is wear, misalignment, or internal failure and recommend the fix that matches the condition of the hardware.

Should we rekey the mortise cylinder or replace the whole storefront lock?

If the lock body is still solid and the issue is control over existing keys, rekeying is usually the right move. If the cylinder is worn, the hook bolt is damaged, or the mortise case is failing, replacement makes more sense. We compare the condition of each part before recommending one path.

Can you repair an aluminum storefront door on the Route 13 side if the frame looks bent?

Sometimes, yes. A bent frame, bad pivot, or worn threshold can often be adjusted or corrected enough to restore proper locking. If the damage is severe, the lock may never line up well until the door or frame is repaired first. We can tell you when the hardware fix is enough and when it isn't.
