A lot of business owners think rekeying means swapping out every lock on the property, or that it only makes sense after a break-in. That's not how it works. When keys are lost, staff changes, or a master key has been copied too widely, we can change what the existing locks accept and leave the hardware in place. The lock bodies stay on the doors, but the old keys stop working. That matters for offices, storefronts, and mixed-use buildings along US-13 where you may have several entrances, storage rooms, and back doors to keep track of.

The other common mistake is assuming every lock in a building has to be handled the same way. In practice, we look at the actual setup and rekey the cylinders that need control restored. If one door is worn out, damaged, or not worth keeping, we'll say so plainly. If the hardware is in good shape, there's no reason to replace it just because access changed. That's the practical side of the job, and it's usually the better fit for properties in Camden, from the commercial stretch near the Route 13 corridor to quieter buildings tucked back from the road.

Commercial Lock Rekeying in Camden - common questions

How does commercial rekeying work for a business in Camden?

We keep the lock hardware in place and change the internal pins so a new key works and the old one doesn't. If your building has several doors, we can usually set them up to use one key or separate them by area, depending on how you want access managed.

What should I have ready before rekeying the locks at my Camden shop or office?

Have the doors opened and tell us which keys still need to work for managers or staff. If you have a master key system, bring any old keys you still have and describe which doors each one should open. That helps us avoid extra trips and keeps the key plan organized.

Can rekeying cause problems with a storefront lock that already feels sticky?

Yes, if the lock is already worn or binding, rekeying alone may not solve the issue. We'll check whether the problem is in the cylinder, the door alignment, or the lock body itself. If the hardware is failing, repair or replacement may be the better choice.

Should I rekey or replace the locks after a manager leaves with a master key?

If the hardware is in good shape, rekeying is usually the right move because it makes the old key useless without changing every lock. If the system is old, mixed, or already giving you trouble, replacement can be smarter. The condition of the hardware should decide it, not just the lost key.

Can you rekey older commercial locks in Camden, or do some need to be replaced?

Older locks can often be rekeyed if the cylinders are still serviceable and parts are available. Some very old or worn models are a poor fit for rekeying because the keys and internal parts are too damaged or uncommon. In those cases, replacement is the practical fix.
