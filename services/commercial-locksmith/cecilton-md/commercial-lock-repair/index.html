Commercial Lock Repair in Cecilton - common questions

How do you figure out whether the lock is bad or the door is out of line?

We test the lock with the door open and closed, then check the strike, hinges, closer, and latch. If the lock works when the door is open but fails when it is shut, alignment is usually part of the problem. That keeps us from replacing hardware that is still usable.

What should we have ready before you come to a commercial lock repair in Cecilton?

It helps to know which door is acting up, what the lock is doing, and whether the problem is constant or only happens at certain times. If the building uses master keys or restricted access, have that information ready too. A quick walkthrough of the issue usually saves time on site.

Could a sticking lock mean the door will stop locking completely?

It can, especially if the latch is grinding or the key is wearing the cylinder. A door that gets harder to lock usually puts extra stress on the parts until something gives. It's better to repair it while it still works than wait for the hardware to fail shut or fail open.

Should we repair the lock or replace the whole commercial door hardware set?

If the cylinder, latch, or strike is the only worn part, repair is often enough. If the door has repeated damage, bent hardware, or parts that no longer match up well, replacement may make more sense. We look at the condition of the full opening before recommending either option.

What if the lock works on the back door but not on the front door of our Cecilton business?

That usually means the front opening has its own issue, such as heavier use, a closer that is pulling too hard, or frame movement. Two doors can have the same lock style and still need different repairs. We treat each opening on its own instead of assuming the problem is the same.
