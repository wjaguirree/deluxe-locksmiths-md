Commercial Lock Rekeying in Cecilton - common questions

How does commercial rekeying work for an older building in Cecilton?

We remove the cylinder from each lock, change the pins inside, and cut new keys so the old ones no longer open the doors. If the lock body is sound, it usually stays in place. Older buildings sometimes need a mix of rekeying and parts replacement when the hardware has been altered over the years.

What should I have ready before I ask for a rekey at my business?

Have the doors you want changed identified, along with any master key system details if you use one. It also helps to know whether keys are missing, an employee left, or you want a full reset after a tenant change. If there are doors that should stay on a separate key, say that up front.

I lost a master key at my shop. Is rekeying enough, or do I need new locks?

If the locks are in decent shape, rekeying is often enough to protect the building again. The answer depends on how the system was set up and whether the master key opened all areas or only some of them. If the hardware is badly worn or mixed with unknown copies, replacement may be the better call.

Should I rekey all the doors at once or only the ones employees use?

That depends on how the building is used. Many owners rekey every exterior door so one old key can't still reach the property, while leaving some interior locks alone. If you have storage, office, and customer areas on different keys, it makes sense to map the whole setup before changing anything.

Can you rekey the lock if the door has older commercial hardware or mixed parts?

Often, yes. Older commercial hardware can usually be rekeyed if the cylinder and keyway are still in workable shape. The problem is usually worn parts, stripped screws, or a lock that has been changed by several owners. In those cases, the lock may need repair or replacement before it can be keyed properly.
