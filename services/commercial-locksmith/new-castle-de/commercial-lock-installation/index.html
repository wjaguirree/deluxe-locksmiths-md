Commercial Lock Installation in New Castle - common questions

Can you install a commercial lock on an older door in New Castle's historic district?

Yes, but older doors often need careful measuring and fitting. We look at the door thickness, existing holes, frame wear, and whether the latch lines up cleanly. In older buildings, the door may have settled over time, so a straight hardware swap is not always the best approach.

What should we have ready before a commercial lock installation?

It helps to know who needs access, what the door is used for, and whether the lock is for a front entrance, office door, or back entry. If you already have hardware on hand, we can check whether it matches the door and frame before installing it. A clear description of the problem also helps us bring the right parts.

I'm worried the new lock won't line up with our frame. Can that be fixed?

Usually, yes. Misalignment is common on commercial doors, especially where the frame has shifted or the old hardware was installed years ago. We check the strike, latch, hinges, and door swing, then adjust the setup so the lock operates smoothly. If the frame is damaged, that may need repair before the hardware will work right.

Should we choose a keyed lock or a push-button style for a small business in New Castle?

It depends on how the door is used. A keyed lock is a good fit when you want simple control and familiar hardware. Push-button or other access styles can make sense when staff changes often or several people need entry. We'll look at the door, the level of use, and how much control you want over access.

Can you install a new lock if the door already has old mortise hardware?

Sometimes, yes, but it depends on the door and what the old hardware leaves behind. Mortise lock openings don't always match modern commercial locks, so we may need to adapt the setup or choose a replacement that fits the existing cavity. If the door is too worn or altered, a different hardware plan may work better.
