Storefront Door Lock Repair in New Castle - common questions

My storefront door in New Castle locks sometimes but not every time. What are you checking first?

We start with alignment. On aluminum storefront doors, a lock that works one day and misses the next is often a door-fit problem, not just a bad keyway. We check the pivot, threshold, strike, hook bolt, and closer so we can find what's preventing the latch from meeting the frame cleanly.

What should I do before you come look at a stuck storefront lock?

Don't force the key or keep cycling the handle. If the door is binding, that can break the cylinder or damage the edge of the door. Keep any broken key pieces, and if possible, note whether the problem is locking, unlocking, latching, or the door dragging on the floor.

Is a worn mortise cylinder always the problem on a storefront door?

Not always. A cylinder can wear out, but a lot of storefront lock trouble comes from the door no longer lining up with the strike. If the door sags, the hook bolt may scrape or miss even when the cylinder is fine. We look at the full door system before deciding what needs repair.

Should I replace the whole storefront lock or just repair the broken part?

If the body of the lock is sound, repair is often the better first move. We may be able to correct the cylinder, strike, pivots, or threshold and keep the existing hardware in service. If the lock case is damaged or parts are too worn, then replacement makes more sense.

Can you work on an old storefront door in New Castle's historic district without changing its look?

Often, yes. Older doors may have original hardware or older-style parts that need careful fitting. We try to repair what can be saved and match the existing setup as closely as possible. If a door has settled for years, the goal is a working lock and a clean fit without unnecessary changes.
