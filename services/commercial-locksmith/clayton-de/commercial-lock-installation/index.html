Commercial Lock Installation in Clayton - common questions

How does a commercial lock installation on my Clayton storefront usually work?

We look at the door, the frame, and the current hardware first. Then we match the lock to the way the space is used, whether that's customer entry, staff access, or a back door. If anything is out of line, we correct that before setting the new lock so it closes and latches the way it should.

What should I have ready before we replace the lock on my office or shop in Clayton?

It helps to know which doors need work, who should have keys afterward, and whether the old hardware has been acting up in any specific way. If you have building rules or access needs for employees or vendors, bring those up too. That gives us a clear picture before we start.

I'm worried about my back door not lining up right. Can a new lock still be installed?

Sometimes, but not always on its own. If the door is sagging, the frame is damaged, or the strike is too far off, the lock may not work right until the opening is corrected. We can tell you whether the issue is the lock itself or the door setup around it.

Should I rekey the lock or replace it for my small shop near Route 300?

Rekeying works when the lock is in good shape and you mainly need different keys. Replacement makes more sense if the hardware is worn, outdated, or not secure enough for the door. We can usually tell which choice fits better once we see the opening and the condition of the parts.

Can you install commercial locks on older railroad-town buildings in Clayton without changing the whole door?

Often, yes. Older doors can sometimes take modern commercial hardware with the right prep and fitting. If the door is heavily worn or not built for the lock type you want, some adjustments may be needed. We look at the existing door first and work from there.
