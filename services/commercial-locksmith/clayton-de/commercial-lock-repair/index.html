Commercial Lock Repair in Clayton - common questions

Why does our commercial door in Clayton keep needing the lock adjusted?

When a door gets used hard, the problem is often alignment, not the key itself. The latch may be rubbing the strike, the door may be sagging, or the closer may not be pulling it shut evenly. Repairing the worn or shifted parts usually works better than just forcing the lock harder.

What should I check before someone comes out to repair the lock?

See whether the key turns smoothly, whether the door closes fully without lifting or pushing, and whether the same problem happens from both sides. If you have a spare key, bring it out. Don't keep testing a lock that's sticking, because that can break a key or damage the cylinder further.

Could a bad lock leave our shop or garage easy to force open?

Yes. A loose cylinder, damaged latch, or worn strike can make a door less secure even if it still seems to close. If the hardware is loose, misaligned, or bent, it may not be grabbing the frame the way it should. That's worth fixing before the door is left shut for the night.

Is it better to repair the existing lock or replace it on a commercial door?

If the body of the lock is solid and the wear is in parts like the cylinder, latch, or strike, repair is often the right call. If the hardware is badly worn, damaged, or no longer matches the door, replacement may make more sense. The condition of the door itself matters too.

What if the lock works, but only when someone lifts the door a little?

That usually points to a door alignment problem, not just a lock problem. The hinges, closer, or frame may be out of line, so the latch only catches when the door is forced into position. The fix may involve the lock hardware and the door hardware together, since both affect how well it shuts.
