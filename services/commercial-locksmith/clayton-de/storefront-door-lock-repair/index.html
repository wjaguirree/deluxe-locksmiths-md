Storefront Door Lock Repair in Clayton - common questions

My aluminum storefront door in Clayton locks, but the key feels gritty. Is that a lock issue or just the door being out of line?

It can be either, and sometimes both. A gritty key feel often comes from worn parts inside the mortise cylinder, but an out-of-line door can make the bolt bind and feel rough too. We check the key action, the latch position, and the door alignment before deciding whether repair, rekeying, or replacement makes sense.

What should I have ready when I call about a storefront door lock on Route 300?

Have the door type in mind, if you know it, and tell us whether the issue is with locking, unlocking, latching, or access by staff. It also helps to know if the key is broken, missing, or still working. If the door has panic hardware or a hook bolt, mention that too.

Can a bad threshold or pivot make the lock seem broken on my shop door in Clayton?

Yes. On storefront doors, the lock often gets blamed first, but a worn pivot or bad threshold can drop the door just enough to throw the hook bolt or latch off line. In that case, replacing the cylinder alone won't solve the problem. We look at the door as a system, not just the key side.

Should I rekey or replace the storefront lock if my old employee still has a key?

If the lock body is still in good shape, rekeying is often the cleaner choice because it changes who can open the door without changing all the hardware. If the cylinder is worn, the key is hard to turn, or the lock has already been damaged, replacement may be the better fix. We'll sort out which option fits the door.

My shop door near the Maryland line has a hook bolt that won't catch every time. Can that be repaired, or does it need a new lock?

A hook bolt that misses the strike can sometimes be repaired if the lock is sound and the door is properly aligned. If the bolt is worn, the cylinder is loose, or the internal parts are failing, replacement may be necessary. We check the fit, the strike, and the hardware before recommending the next step.
