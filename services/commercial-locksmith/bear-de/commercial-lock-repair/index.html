Commercial Lock Repair in Bear - common questions

How does commercial lock repair usually work on a storefront door in Bear?

We start by checking the lock, the latch, the strike, and the way the door closes. Then we figure out whether the problem is inside the lock or caused by alignment, wear, or damage. That lets us repair the right part instead of treating a symptom and leaving the real issue in place.

What should I have ready before you come out to repair a business lock?

It helps to know which door is giving trouble, what it's used for, and whether the lock has been acting up for a while or failed all at once. If there are master keys, tenant keys, or access restrictions, have that information ready too. A photo of the door can also be useful.

Is it a problem if our front lock is sticking but still opens most of the time?

Yes, because that kind of wear usually gets worse. A sticking lock can turn into a door that won't latch, won't unlock cleanly, or leaves the building less secure than it should be. It also puts extra wear on the key and the hardware, which can lead to a bigger repair later.

Should we repair the lock or replace it on a high-use door?

If the body of the lock is solid and the issue is wear, alignment, or one damaged part, repair may be enough. If the lock has repeated failures, broken internal parts, or damage from forced entry, replacement can be the better choice. We look at how the door is used before we recommend either option.

Can you fix a lock if the door is part of a multi-tenant building and different people use it every day?

Yes. Those doors often need extra attention because worn keys, heavy traffic, and changing tenant access can all affect how the lock performs. We check whether the problem is the lock itself, the cylinder, or the way the door and frame are wearing together, then repair what actually needs attention.
