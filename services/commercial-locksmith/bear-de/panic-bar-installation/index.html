Panic Bar Installation in Bear - common questions

How does panic bar installation work on a storefront in Bear?

We look at the door, frame, and existing lock setup first, then choose exit hardware that matches the opening. After that, we mount the device, set the strike or latch parts, and test how the door opens and closes. If the closer, hinges, or frame are off, we address those issues so the bar works the way it should.

What should I have ready before you install an exit device at my place on Route 40?

It helps to have the door area clear, the business contact person available, and any old hardware or keys on hand. If there's an inspection note, a failed latch, or a door that has been giving you trouble, share that too. That gives us a clearer picture of whether the issue is the bar itself or the door around it.

My back exit closes but doesn't latch right. Is that a panic bar issue or something else?

It could be the bar, but it might also be the strike, closer, hinges, or even a warped door. A panic device has to line up with the rest of the opening. If the door has been slamming, sagging, or rubbing, we'll check those parts before replacing hardware that may not be the real problem.

Should I choose a rim panic device or a vertical rod setup for my Bear business?

That depends on the door style and how the exit is used. Rim devices are common on many single doors. Vertical rod hardware can make sense on taller doors or certain openings that need top and bottom latching. We look at the door in place and recommend the option that fits the opening and the way your business operates.

Can you install panic hardware on an older door that was patched before we took over the lease?

Often, yes, but older patchwork can limit what will work well. If the door has extra holes, damaged edges, or mismatched parts, we may need to repair or rework the opening before the new hardware goes on. The goal is a clean, secure install that doesn't depend on old shortcuts.
