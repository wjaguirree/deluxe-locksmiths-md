Storefront Door Lock Repair in Bear - common questions

How do you figure out whether my storefront problem is the lock, the door, or the frame?

We start by testing how the door closes, latches, and locks from both sides. If the key turns but the door won't secure, that often means alignment. If the handle is loose or the key binds only in one position, the cylinder or mortise parts may be worn. The goal is to fix the real fault, not cover it up.

What should I have ready before you come look at my Bear storefront door?

If you can, tell us the door brand or bring the old key, any broken parts, and a quick note about when the problem started. It also helps to know whether the door has been hard to close, hard to lock, or damaged by forcing. That gives us a better starting point when we inspect the hardware.

Can a worn storefront lock leave my business more exposed even if the key still works?

Yes. A key that still turns doesn't always mean the door is secure. If the latch doesn't fully catch, the strike is loose, or the cylinder is worn, the door may not hold the way it should. We check the full setup so the repair restores real security, not just basic function.

Should I replace the whole lock or just repair the broken part on my aluminum door?

That depends on the condition of the mortise case, cylinder, latch, and door frame. If only one part is worn or damaged, repair is often the right move. If the hardware is badly worn or the parts no longer fit together correctly, replacement makes more sense. We'll explain which option fits the door.

My storefront door opens fine from the inside but the outside key is hard to use. What does that usually mean?

That often points to the mortise cylinder, not the whole door. It can also mean the latch isn't lining up cleanly with the strike, which puts extra stress on the key and the lock body. We can check both sides of the door and narrow it down before anything gets replaced.
