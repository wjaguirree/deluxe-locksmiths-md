Door Closer Installation in Bear - common questions

How does a commercial door closer installation usually work on a storefront in Bear?

We check the door size, weight, swing, and existing hardware first, then choose a closer that matches the opening. After mounting it, we adjust closing speed, latching force, and sweep so the door shuts fully without banging. If the frame or hinges are off, we'll point that out before the final setup.

What should I have ready before you install a closer on my business door?

It helps to know the door material, whether it's a metal storefront or a hollow metal commercial door, and if the opening has panic hardware, a lock, or a fire rating. If the door is already rubbing or sagging, tell us that too. Those details help us bring the right closer and avoid surprises on site.

Can a door closer fix a door that keeps slamming in my Bear shop?

Sometimes, but not always by itself. A slamming door can come from a worn closer, bad adjustment, loose hinges, or a latch that doesn't line up. We'll inspect the full opening and correct the cause we can see. If the door is damaged or out of square, that has to be addressed for the closer to work right.

Should I choose a surface-mounted closer or a concealed one for my business door?

Surface-mounted closers are common, easy to service, and a good fit for most commercial entries. Concealed closers are cleaner-looking, but they're not right for every door and can be more involved to service. We look at the door style, traffic, and hardware already in place before recommending one.

What if my door is fire-rated or has a panic bar already on it?

That changes the hardware choice and how it gets installed. A fire-rated door needs closer settings that let it close and latch properly, and a panic bar door has its own clearance and function requirements. We work with the hardware already on the opening and make sure the closer supports it instead of getting in the way.
