Commercial Lock Installation in Wyoming - common questions

How does a commercial lock installation usually work on a storefront in Wyoming?

First, the door, frame, and existing hardware are checked together. Then the right lock type is chosen for that opening, whether it's a keyed lever, deadbolt, or panic hardware. If the old prep is worn or off-center, the door or frame may need adjustment so the new hardware lines up and closes properly.

What should I have ready before you install new locks on my shop door?

It helps to know who needs access, whether the door is an entry, office, or back door, and whether the hardware needs to match existing keys or operate on a new key. If you have the old lock, key, or lease notes, bring those details. That makes it easier to choose the right setup for the opening.

My lock keeps sticking on an older door near DE-10. Do I need a whole new lock or just a repair?

Not always a full replacement. If the lock is fine but the door has sagged or the strike is out of line, the issue may be alignment. If the cylinder is worn, the latch is broken, or the body is damaged, replacement makes more sense. The door and frame should be checked before any decision is made.

Should I keep the old hardware or switch to something heavier for a business door?

If the old hardware is light-duty, loose, or already repaired several times, heavier commercial-grade hardware is usually the better call. It holds up better under repeated use and gives a cleaner fit on business doors. The right choice depends on the door material, how often it's used, and whether the opening needs special exit hardware.

Can you install a commercial lock if the door has been patched or bored before?

Often, yes. Patched or reworked doors are common on older buildings, and the install can usually be adapted to that opening. The important part is whether the door and frame are still sound enough to hold the new hardware. If the old holes are too damaged, reinforcement or fresh prep may be needed first.
