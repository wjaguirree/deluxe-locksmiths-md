Commercial Lock Repair in Wyoming - common questions

Why does a commercial door in Wyoming keep sticking even after someone already tried to fix it?

A quick fix can hide the real problem. The latch may be worn, the strike may be out of line, or the door may have shifted in the frame. On busy doors, those issues usually keep coming back unless the lock, hinges, and closer are checked together and adjusted as one system.

What should I have ready before you come out to repair a store or office lock?

Have a key that normally works, know which door is acting up, and be ready to point out what changed, such as dragging, sticking, or a key that suddenly feels rough. If the door has a closer, panic bar, or keyed entry hardware, mentioning that helps narrow down the repair.

Can a bad repair make the door less secure on a business in Wyoming?

Yes. If the latch doesn't fully catch or the strike is loose, the door may look closed but not actually secure. A patch that only gets the door moving again can leave weak points behind. A proper repair should make the door close cleanly and hold shut the way it should.

Should I repair the lock or replace the whole commercial door hardware?

If the problem is wear in one part, repair is often the better first step. If the body is cracked, the keyway is badly worn, or the hardware has been forced enough times that several parts are failing, replacement may make more sense. The choice depends on how the door is built and what's actually worn out.

What if the lock works, but only when the door is pushed hard from one side?

That usually points to alignment, not just the lock itself. The strike may be off, the hinges may have sagged, or the closer may be pulling the door out of line. In that case, repairing only the lock won't solve it. The door and hardware need to be lined up together so it latches normally.
