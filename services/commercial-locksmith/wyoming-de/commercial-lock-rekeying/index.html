Commercial Lock Rekeying in Wyoming - common questions

How does commercial rekeying work for a shop or office in Wyoming?

The lock cylinder is opened, the pins inside are changed, and new keys are cut to match the updated setup. The old keys stop working. If the hardware is in good shape, the door keeps its existing lock body, which lets you control access without changing every lock on the property.

What should I have ready before rekeying the doors at my business?

Have the doors you want changed identified, along with any master key information you already use. It also helps to know which employees or managers should keep access after the change. If a key was lost, tell us which lock or locks might be affected so the right cylinders can be addressed.

When is rekeying not enough and the lock should be replaced instead?

If the lock is cracked, binds badly, has worn-out parts, or no longer works smoothly after repair, replacement is usually the better choice. Rekeying only changes key access; it doesn't fix damaged hardware. For older storefront and office doors, replacement can also be the better option if the lock no longer matches the security needs of the space.

Can you rekey one lost master key without changing every lock in the building?

Often, yes. If the system is still in workable condition, the cylinders can be rekeyed so the lost master no longer operates the locks. The exact method depends on how the existing key system was built, so it's important to look at the whole setup before deciding whether to keep, adjust, or replace parts of it.

What if my building on Railroad Avenue has old hardware that still matches the doors?

Old hardware can often be kept if it's solid and the internal parts are still usable. That matters in older Wyoming buildings where the door style and lock style were built to work together. If the hardware is worn out or not secure enough, replacing it may be the cleaner long-term choice.
