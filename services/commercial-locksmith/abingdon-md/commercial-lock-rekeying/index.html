When a commercial rekey is handled poorly, the whole building pays for it. Old keys keep working longer than they should, master keys stop matching the way they're supposed to, and staff turnover turns into a security headache. For offices, retail spaces, and service businesses along MD-24, that can mean chasing down who still has access instead of getting back to work. Rekeying lets you change who can open the lock without replacing every piece of hardware.

Deluxe Locksmith handles commercial rekeys on site, where the lock is actually installed. That keeps the job tied to the doors you use every day, whether it's a storefront, a suite door, or a back entrance that sees constant traffic. If you need access changed after an employee leaves, a key goes missing, or a master key needs to be reset, the cylinders can be reworked so the old keys no longer operate them. The goal is simple: restore control without disrupting the rest of the building.

Commercial rekeying changes the key that opens a lock without changing the lock body. Inside a standard cylinder are pins matched to the old key cuts. When a key is lost, copied, or no longer belongs in the hands of former staff, those pins are rearranged and a new key is cut to the new pattern. The outer hardware stays in place, which matters on storefronts, offices, and back doors that already fit the door and latch well.

The work is simple in concept but depends on the hardware. Some locks use removable cylinders, others need the cylinder pulled and serviced on the bench, and some older units have worn plugs, bent springs, or damaged keyways that make rekeying a poor fix. If a lock is loose, sticky, or badly worn, a rekey can expose the problem rather than solve it. In those cases, the right answer may be new cylinders, new keyed alike locks, or a partial hardware change so the door still closes and latches cleanly.

For businesses along Emmorton Road and near I-95 exit 77, the goal is control: keep the working hardware, reset who can open what, and leave the building with a cleaner key system than before. That can include door hardware at employee entrances, storage rooms, rear service doors, and suites that changed hands after turnover or a master key issue.

Commercial Lock Rekeying in Abingdon - common questions

How does a commercial rekey work on my Abingdon storefront or office?

The lock cylinder is reset so the old key no longer works and a new key does. The door hardware stays in place unless a part is worn or damaged. For many commercial doors, that means the key change can be handled without replacing every lock in the building.

What should I have ready before you rekey our doors near Emmorton Road?

Have access to the doors, any current keys you still control, and a list of which locks should match. It also helps to know whether you lost a master key, had staff turnover, or want separate keys for offices, storage, and exterior entrances.

What can go wrong if we just keep using the old keys after an employee leaves?

If an old key is still out there, anyone with it may be able to open the door. That creates a real access risk for stock rooms, offices, and rear entrances. Rekeying resets that access without forcing you to replace good hardware that still works.

Should I rekey the locks or replace them on a building in Abingdon?

Rekeying is the better choice when the lock body is sound and you just need new control over the keys. Replacement makes more sense if the cylinder is worn out, the key turns rough, parts are broken, or the lock type no longer fits how the door is being used.

Can you rekey every exterior and interior commercial lock to one key?

Often, yes, if the hardware is compatible and the doors are meant to share access. Some locks can be set up to work on one key while others stay separate. If a few doors use different brands or older cylinders, they may need a different plan.
