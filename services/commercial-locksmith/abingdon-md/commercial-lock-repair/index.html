Commercial Lock Repair in Abingdon - common questions

My shop door in Abingdon locks, but it doesn't latch cleanly. Is that a lock problem or a door problem?

It could be either, and on commercial doors it's often both. If the bolt or latch is fine but the strike is off, the door may need alignment. If the lock feels rough, binds, or won't retract properly, the lock body may need repair. A proper check looks at the whole door set, not just the key cylinder.

What should I have ready before someone comes to repair a commercial lock at my business?

Have the door used most often unlocked and accessible, and know which door has the issue. If there's a master key system, key card setup, or a history of past repairs, that helps too. If the door has been sticking, note when it happens and whether it's worse at certain times or in certain weather.

Can a worn commercial lock in a retail space become a security risk even if it still turns?

Yes. A lock can still turn and still fail to secure the door fully if the latch doesn't seat right or the hardware is loose. That can leave the door easier to force or make it seem shut when it isn't fully engaged. If staff have to jiggle it, slam it, or pull hard to get it to close, it needs attention.

Should I repair the existing lock or replace it on my Abingdon storefront?

Repair makes sense when the core hardware is sound and the issue is wear, adjustment, or a single failed part. Replacement is better when the lock is badly worn, damaged, or no longer matches the door well. On older commercial doors, the frame and strike condition often decide which option will hold up best.

What if my building has older hardware that doesn't match anything standard anymore?

That happens a lot in older commercial spaces. The job may involve identifying the lock type, sourcing compatible parts, or repairing what can be saved while keeping the door usable. If exact matches aren't available, a good repair plan works with the existing door and frame instead of forcing in the wrong hardware.
