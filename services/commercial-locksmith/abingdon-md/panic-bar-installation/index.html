Panic Bar Installation in Abingdon - common questions

How is a panic bar installation handled on a storefront door in Abingdon?

We inspect the door, frame, latch side, and closer first, then choose the right exit device for that opening. After installation, we test the swing, latching, and release so the door opens easily and still closes securely. If the frame or door needs reinforcement, we handle that as part of the job.

What should our staff have ready before you install a panic bar?

It helps to know which door is the main exit, whether the building has a fire alarm tie-in, and if the door is wood, hollow metal, or aluminum. We also like to see any inspection notes or past hardware problems. That gives us a clear picture before we start cutting or drilling.

Can a bad panic bar become a safety or inspection problem?

Yes. If the device sticks, is mounted wrong, or doesn't latch correctly, the door may not meet the building's egress needs. A loose or patched-up bar can also fail under heavy use. We fix the hardware so it works as an exit device, not just as a door handle.

Should we repair the old bar or replace it with a new one?

If the body of the device is sound and the issue is a worn part or a simple adjustment, repair can make sense. If the door was forced, the parts are bent, or the hardware no longer matches the opening, replacement is usually the better choice. We look at the door as a whole before we recommend either path.

Can you install a panic bar on a door that was changed before we took over the space?

Usually, yes, but it depends on what was changed. Old holes, patchwork repairs, or a nonstandard frame can affect the fit. We can work with many older or altered doors in Abingdon, but if the opening is badly damaged, we may need to rebuild part of it before the new device goes on.
