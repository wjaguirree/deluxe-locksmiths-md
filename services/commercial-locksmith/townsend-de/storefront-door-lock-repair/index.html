Storefront Door Lock Repair in Townsend - common questions

Why does my storefront door in Townsend only lock if I lift the handle?

That usually means the door is out of alignment. The pivot, threshold, or closer may be letting the door sag so the hook bolt or latch doesn't meet the strike cleanly. We check the full door assembly, not just the lock, and repair the part that's keeping the door from closing square.

What should I have ready before you come to repair my shop door?

Have the door unlocked, the problem hardware visible if possible, and a quick idea of what changed: sticking, spinning key, broken latch, or a door that won't fully close. If the storefront has multiple keyed doors, it helps to know which one is acting up so we can focus the repair.

Can a worn storefront lock on Main Street be repaired, or does it have to be replaced?

It depends on what failed. A rough cylinder, loose strike, or bad adjustment can often be repaired. If the mortise lock body, hook bolt, or pivot hardware is worn out, replacement may be the better fix. We'll look at the door and tell you which parts are still worth keeping.

Will a damaged threshold or pivot ruin the new lock I already have?

It can. If the door sags or drags, even a good lock won't line up right and may wear out early. That's why we check the threshold, pivots, and closer first. Fixing the alignment usually protects the lock and keeps the door easier to use day to day.

Can you work on an aluminum storefront door at a small garage or farm business near Blackbird State Forest?

Yes. Those doors often have the same hardware as a town storefront, just in a different setting. We repair hook bolts, mortise cylinders, pivots, and thresholds on site, even when the door sits on a less formal property or sees heavier dirt and use than a retail entry.
