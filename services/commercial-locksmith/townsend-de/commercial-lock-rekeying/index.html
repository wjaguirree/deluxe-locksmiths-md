When a commercial rekey is done wrong, the problem shows up fast: the old key still works somewhere it shouldn't, a former employee can still get in, or one office door ends up keyed differently from the rest and slows everyone down. For farms, garages, and small businesses along Route 71, that kind of mistake can turn into lost time, missing equipment, and a building that never feels secure again.

We rekey commercial locks so you can keep the hardware that still does its job and change who has access. That's the right move after staff turnover, a lost master key, or when a property has been handed off and you want a clean start without replacing every lock. We work on site at your place, which matters when the locks are spread across a shop, an office, storage rooms, or outbuildings with different uses. We'll set things up so the right keys open the right doors and the wrong ones don't.

Commercial rekeying changes the pins inside the cylinder so old keys stop working and the lock can take a new key. The body of the lock, the latch, and the door hardware stay in place. That matters for shops, garages, farm offices, and outbuildings along Route 71, where the issue is often control, not a full hardware swap. When staff changes, a master key gets lost, or a key shows up in the wrong hands, rekeying lets us reset access without tearing out every lock on the property.

Most commercial cylinders wear out in the keyway, not the whole lock. Keys get rounded off, pins stick from dirt or moisture, and old cylinders start to turn rough or bind. In those cases we inspect the lock, measure the cylinder type, and replace only the worn core, pins, springs, or tailpiece parts that no longer match the door. If the hardware is too worn, we can move to a new commercial cylinder that fits the existing setup so the door still works the way it should. For properties near Blackbird State Forest with sheds, gate locks, and storage rooms, that keeps the job focused on the hardware that actually controls access.

Final Checks After Rekeying

After we rekey a commercial building, we don't stop at the cylinder work. We test each lock with the new keys to make sure the plug turns smoothly, the latch throws cleanly, and the old key no longer operates it. That check matters because a rekey only helps if the lock still works the way your staff expects when they arrive at a side door, front entrance, or equipment shed. In a town like Townsend, where properties can spread out along Route 71 and outbuildings often carry their own locks, we also confirm that each location was matched to the right keying plan so there's no confusion later.

We also verify any master keying or keyed-alike setup against the access list you want to keep. That means checking which doors should open with one key, which should stay restricted, and whether a lost master changed the way the system needs to be organized. We do that so your managers keep control without giving every employee more access than they need. If there are file rooms, storage cages, or sheds near Green Spring or off Blackbird Forest Road, we pay extra attention to those because a lock that looks fine can still be miskeyed or left on the wrong pattern if nobody confirms it at the end.

Before we leave, we make sure the door hardware still lines up correctly, the key code record reflects the new setup, and the customer who receives the keys understands which copies were issued. Those final checks exist to close the gap between "rekeyed" and "ready to use."

Commercial Lock Rekeying in Townsend - common questions

How does commercial rekeying work on the shop and storage doors in Townsend?

We open each lock cylinder, remove the old key pins, and set new pins to match a different key. The lock body stays on the door. That lets us stop old keys from working while keeping the existing hardware in place. It's a good fit for office doors, shop entries, and storage buildings.

What should we have ready before you rekey our building near Route 71?

Have the doors accessible, know which locks should match the same key, and bring any working keys you still have. If you use a master key system, tell us that up front. That helps us avoid changing one lock in a way that breaks the rest of the setup.

Can rekeying fix a lock that feels rough or sticks when we turn the key?

Sometimes. If the issue is worn pins or a tired cylinder, rekeying and replacing the worn parts can help. If the lock body, latch, or keyway is badly worn, rekeying alone won't solve it. In that case, replacement of the cylinder or hardware is the better answer.

Should we rekey or replace the locks after an employee leaves with a key?

If the hardware is in good shape, rekeying is usually the right move because it resets access without changing every lock. If the lock is already worn, mismatched, or part of a damaged door, replacement may make more sense. We can look at the hardware and talk through both options.

Can you rekey gate locks and outbuilding locks on a farm property outside Townsend?

Yes, as long as the lock type can be serviced and the cylinder is in usable shape. We handle the same access issues on gates, sheds, and equipment buildings as on office doors. If a lock is sealed, rusted solid, or too damaged to service, we'll let you know that it needs replacement.
