Clean Commercial Lock Work

A proper commercial lock job starts with the door, not the hardware box. We check the slab, frame, strike, hinges, closer, and prep so the lock lines up the way it should and the latch actually seats. On a storefront near Main Street or a back door on a newer build, the difference between a clean install and a bodge usually shows up fast: no rubbing, no forced throw, no loose trim, and no screws fighting soft wood or thin metal. We match the lock grade to the door and the way it gets used, then install it so the bolt, latch, and strike all work as one setup instead of separate parts held together by luck.

What you should look for afterward is simple. The key should turn without binding. The door should close and lock without a shove. The latch should catch fully every time, and the strike plate should sit tight without gaps or shims stacked like a patch job. If it's a panic device, mortise lock, deadbolt, or lever set, the hardware should look aligned and feel solid, not wobbly or over-tightened. We also make sure the finish work is neat, because sloppy drilling, mismatched holes, and crooked trim tell their own story.

Commercial Lock Installation in Smyrna - common questions

Can you install a new commercial lock on my storefront without replacing the whole door?

Often, yes. If the door and frame are still in good condition, the new lock can usually be fitted to the existing opening or adjusted with modest prep. We look at the door type, the latch position, and the strike alignment first, then match the hardware to what the door can support.

What should I have ready before you come out to install a lock at my Smyrna business?

It helps to know which door needs the work, what kind of business it is, and whether the existing lock is sticking, loose, or missing parts. If you have the old hardware or a key that still works partway, that can help with identification. Clear access to the door makes the job easier.

My back door was forced before. Should I choose a stronger lock or replace the frame too?

If the frame, strike, or door edge is bent or split, a stronger lock alone may not solve the problem. In those cases, the hardware and the opening both need to be checked. Sometimes the right fix is new commercial hardware plus repair to the damaged parts around it.

I'm torn between a keyed deadbolt and a commercial lever lock. How do I choose?

It comes down to how the door is used. A storefront or office entry often needs hardware that is easy for staff and still secure for the owner. A back-of-house door may call for a different setup. We look at traffic, security needs, and the type of door before recommending one.

Can you work with older locks in the historic part of town, or do they all need to be changed out?

Some older mortise-style locks can be serviced or replaced with compatible hardware, but not all can be reused safely. If the lock body is worn out or parts are no longer dependable, new commercial hardware is the better call. The door has to be checked in person to know which path makes sense.
