Storefront Door Lock Repair in Smyrna - common questions

How do you figure out whether my storefront lock in Smyrna needs repair or replacement?

We start by checking how the door sits, then we test the cylinder, mortise case, hook bolt, pivots, and strike alignment. If the part is repairable and the rest of the door is sound, we repair it. If the hardware is worn past a reliable fix, we explain that and replace only what needs to be replaced.

What should I have ready before you work on my front door?

Have the door unlocked if possible, and let us know whether the issue is sticking, not latching, dragging, or not turning. If you have the old key, that can help with testing the cylinder. It also helps to tell us if the door has been repaired before, since mixed parts can change the way the hardware fits.

Can a bad pivot make the lock seem broken?

Yes. On storefront doors, a worn pivot or loose threshold can let the door sag enough that the hook bolt misses its strike. That can feel like a lock failure even when the cylinder is fine. We check the full door setup first so we do not replace good lock parts when the real issue is the door alignment.

Should I repair the mortise cylinder or replace the whole storefront lock?

If the cylinder is the only failed part, a cylinder change can be the right answer. If the mortise case, hook bolt, or internal parts are worn, repair may not hold up. We look at how the door functions as a whole and help you choose the option that fits the hardware already on the door.

My storefront door was patched by another company and now it binds. Can that be fixed?

Often, yes. We see doors with replacement parts that do not match the original hardware or the door's alignment. That can cause rubbing, weak latching, or a loose feel at the handle. We sort out what was installed, what still fits, and what needs to be adjusted or replaced so the door works as intended.
