Panic Bar Installation in Fallston - common questions

How does a panic bar installation usually work on a business door in Fallston?

We start by checking the door, frame, and swing direction, then we match the exit device to the opening. If the door already has hardware, we see whether it can be adjusted or needs to be replaced. After installation, we test the latch, release, and closure so the door opens easily and secures properly again.

What should I have ready before we replace the panic bar at my office or shop?

It helps to know which door is involved, whether it's wood, metal, or aluminum, and whether the opening has been giving you trouble with sticking or failing to latch. If there was an inspection note, keep that handy. We'll handle the measurements and hardware match once we're on site.

My door opens, but it takes extra force to get it to latch. Is that the panic bar or something else?

It could be either, but often it's not the bar alone. A misaligned strike, a weak closer, or a warped door can make a working exit device seem bad. We check the whole opening so we're fixing the real cause instead of swapping parts that were fine.

Should we repair the old exit device or install a new one?

If the bar is worn, missing parts, or no longer matches the door, replacement is usually the cleaner path. If the hardware is solid but the problem is alignment or a closer issue, repair may make sense. We'll look at the whole setup and tell you which option fits the door best.

Can you install a panic bar on an exterior side door or a back exit at a small business?

Yes, if the door and frame can support the right hardware and the opening can be made code-compliant. Some back exits need a different device style or extra trim work. We'll inspect the opening first and let you know if that door is a good candidate or if another solution is better.
