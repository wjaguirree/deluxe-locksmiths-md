Commercial locksmith in Fallston - common questions

How do you set up a master key system for a small business in Fallston?

It starts with a door-by-door plan. Some doors can be keyed alike, while others stay separate for storage, offices, or restricted rooms. The goal is to give managers the access they need without putting every door on the same key. On-site work keeps the setup matched to the building and the way your staff actually uses it.

What should I have ready before you rekey our office or shop?

Have a list of the doors that matter, who should keep access, and whether any locks already have trouble turning or latching. If you know which keys are still in circulation, that helps too. It's also useful to point out any deadbolts, storefront locks, panic hardware, or door closers that should be checked at the same visit.

What's the risk if our panic bar or exit hardware isn't working right?

A bad panic bar can slow people down when they need to leave quickly, and it can also create code issues if the door doesn't release the way it should. Sometimes the problem is the bar itself; other times it's the latch, closer, or alignment. It should be corrected before it turns into a safety or inspection problem.

Should we repair the storefront lock or replace it with keyless entry?

If the door and frame are solid, a repair or rekey may be all you need. Keyless entry makes sense when staff changes often or you want to stop handing out keys. The right choice depends on the door, how many people need access, and whether you want a simple mechanical setup or a system with codes or cards.

Can you help if an old back door in our building won't line up anymore?

Yes. Older commercial doors often shift, especially on properties with heavy use or settling. The issue may be the lock, strike, hinge side, or door closer. Fixing the alignment can bring the latch back into place and make the door work the way it should without forcing the hardware.
