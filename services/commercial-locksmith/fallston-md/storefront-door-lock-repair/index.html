Storefront Door Lock Repair in Fallston - common questions

Why is my aluminum storefront door not latching even though the lock turns?

That usually means the latch or hook bolt isn't meeting the strike the way it should. On aluminum storefront doors, the problem is often a worn pivot, a sagging door, or a threshold that's out of line. We check the full door movement, not just the cylinder, so we can fix the part that's actually causing the miss.

What should I have ready before we come out to repair my storefront door in Fallston?

If you can, keep the door area clear and let us know what the door is doing: sticking, not locking, dragging, or key turning but no catch. If there's an existing key that still works part of the time, have it handy. Knowing whether the door is an older storefront unit or a newer aluminum system also helps.

Can a bad storefront lock become a safety problem for my staff or customers?

Yes. A door that doesn't close fully can leave the opening unsecured, and a door that binds can be hard to open in a hurry. In a small office or veterinary practice, that matters because people are coming and going all day. We focus on making the door operate smoothly and latch the way it should.

Should we repair the mortise cylinder or replace the whole storefront lock?

It depends on what failed. If the cylinder is worn but the rest of the hardware is sound, repair or replacement of that part may be enough. If the hook bolt, faceplate, or internal parts are damaged, a larger replacement can make more sense. We inspect the door first so the fix matches the condition of the hardware.

My storefront door was repaired before, but it's rubbing again. Is that normal in older Fallston buildings?

It can happen, especially in older openings where the frame, pivot, or threshold has shifted over time. A previous repair may have solved the lock issue without correcting the door alignment. We look at the whole door assembly so we can tell whether the problem is wear, settling, or a part that was never set right.
