Commercial Lock Repair in Fallston - common questions

My office door in Fallston locks, but it doesn't close right. Is that a lock problem or a door problem?

It can be either, and often it's both. If the latch and strike are not meeting cleanly, the door may be out of alignment even when the lock is worn too. We check the full opening so the repair matches the real cause instead of masking it.

What should we have ready before you come out to repair a commercial lock?

It helps to know which door is acting up, when the problem happens, and whether the key still works. If the building has more than one staff key or a restricted opening schedule, having that information on hand helps us test the lock the right way without guessing.

Can a broken lock on a veterinary clinic door be repaired, or does it usually need replacement?

Many can be repaired if the issue is wear, loose parts, or alignment. If the cylinder, latch, or internal body is damaged beyond a reliable repair, replacement makes more sense. We look at the hardware in place and explain the practical options for that door.

If one employee has been forcing the key, could that have damaged more than the lock?

Yes. A hard turn can damage the cylinder, the latch, or the door hardware around it, and repeated forcing can bend parts that were already starting to fail. We inspect the door and frame too, because a smooth key turn does not always mean the opening is healthy.

We have an older storefront near the Fallston crossroads. Can you repair the existing hardware instead of changing the whole door?

Often, yes. If the frame and door are sound, we can usually work with the hardware already there and replace only the worn parts. That keeps the opening closer to how it was built and avoids unnecessary changes to a door that still has good life left in it.
