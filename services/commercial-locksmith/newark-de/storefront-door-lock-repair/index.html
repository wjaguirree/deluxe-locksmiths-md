Storefront Door Lock Repair in Newark - common questions

Why does my storefront lock keep sticking when the door shuts fine by hand?

That usually means the lock is not the only issue. On a storefront door, the strike, threshold, hinges, or pivot can be out of line, so the bolt or hook has to fight the door every time it closes. We check the full opening and repair the parts that are causing the bind.

What should I have ready before you come out to fix a storefront door in Newark?

If you can, have someone who can open the door, a key that still works if there is one, and a quick note about what the door has been doing. It also helps to know whether the problem started after a hard close, a break-in attempt, or normal wear. That points us to the right parts faster.

My shop on Main Street has a loose lock and a sagging door. Is that a security risk?

Yes. A sagging door can keep the lock from engaging fully, which means the door may look shut while the hardware is not doing its job. That can lead to more wear, a jammed lock, or a door that won't secure at closing. We check the alignment first so the repair protects the opening, not just the cylinder.

Should I repair the mortise cylinder or replace the whole storefront lock assembly?

If the problem is mainly the cylinder, a repair or replacement of that part may be enough. If the hook bolt, case, or other internal parts are worn out, replacing the whole assembly can make more sense. We look at how the door is operating and tell you which fix fits the condition of the hardware.

Can you fix a storefront door that was damaged after hours and now will not latch right?

Often, yes. After-hours damage can bend the frame, shift the strike, or knock the pivot out of line, and those issues can keep the door from latching even when the lock itself is still working. We inspect the door, identify what moved, and repair the parts that need to be corrected.
