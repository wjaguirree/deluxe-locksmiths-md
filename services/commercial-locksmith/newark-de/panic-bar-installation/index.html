Panic Bar Installation in Newark - common questions

What happens during a panic bar installation on my Newark storefront door?

We inspect the door, frame, and existing hardware, then fit an exit device that lets people leave easily and keeps the opening secure from the outside. If the door needs extra repair before the bar can be set correctly, we'll point that out first. The goal is a clean installation that works with the way the space is used.

What should I have ready before you come out to install a panic bar?

Have the door area clear and know whether the opening is for staff only, customers, or both. If there's an old lock, closer, alarm contact, or prior hardware issue, keep that information handy. For leased spaces in Newark, it also helps to have landlord or property manager approval before work starts.

My exit door sticks and doesn't close right. Can a panic bar still be installed safely?

Sometimes, but not always as-is. A panic bar depends on a door that closes and latches correctly. If the frame is out of line, the door drags, or the opening doesn't catch properly, we may need to correct that first. Otherwise the device can fail when it's needed most.

Should I choose a rim panic bar or another style for my business near the University of Delaware?

It depends on the door and how it's built. A rim device is common on many exit doors, while other openings may need different hardware to match the frame, fire rating, or security setup. We look at the actual opening instead of guessing from the outside.

Can you install a panic bar on a glass door or a narrow rear exit door?

In some cases, yes, but the door has to support the hardware and meet the right use and code requirements. Glass doors, narrow frames, and older exits can limit the options. If the opening can't take a standard device, we'll explain what is and isn't possible and what changes would be needed.
