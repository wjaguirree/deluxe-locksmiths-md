Installed Right the First Time

A proper commercial lock installation starts with the door, not the hardware box. We look at the condition of the frame, the bore, the closers, hinges, and how the door sits when it shuts. If a storefront door on Broad Street is sagging, or a back door near the riverfront has been forced out of square, a lock that is simply screwed in place can bind, latch poorly, or wear out fast. The right job lines up the strike, sets the latch depth correctly, and leaves the door closing cleanly without drag or rattle.

What separates solid work from a bodge is how the lock behaves after we leave. The key should turn without scraping, the deadbolt should throw fully, and the latch should meet the strike without needing a shove. We also check that the hardware matches the door material and use, whether that means a glass storefront, an office entry, or a back-of-house door that gets heavy use. If a lock sits crooked, the trim plates are loose, or the cylinder feels gritty on day one, those are warning signs that the job was rushed.

Commercial Lock Installation in Perryville - common questions

How does a commercial lock installation work for a shop on Route 40 in Perryville?

We come to the property, look at the door and frame, and install hardware that fits the opening. If the existing door has wear, misalignment, or old parts, we work around that so the new lock sits right and latches cleanly. The process is straightforward, but the details matter on a busy storefront.

What should I have ready before you install a new lock at my office?

It helps to know which door needs the work, who should have access, and whether you want the same key system used on other doors. If there's old hardware on the opening, we'll want to see that too. The more we know about how the door is used, the better we can match the lock to it.

I'm worried the new lock won't line up with my old door. Can that be fixed?

That's a common issue, especially on older commercial doors. If the frame, latch area, or door edge is off, we can often adjust the setup or choose hardware that fits the opening better. If the door is badly worn or damaged, we'll tell you that before anything is installed.

Should I choose a keyed lock or another commercial setup for my Perryville office?

It depends on how many people need access and how often the door is used. A small office may do well with a simple keyed lock, while a shared space or customer-facing entry may need a different setup. We look at the door and the way the building runs before recommending hardware.

Can you install a new lock if my back door is used for deliveries and staff only?

Yes. Back doors often need a different setup than the front entry because they take more wear and usually serve a narrower group of people. We install hardware that fits that role, and we can make sure the lock supports daily use without making the door hard to operate.
