Commercial Lock Repair in Perryville - common questions

If my storefront lock on US-40 is sticking, what should I check before I call?

See whether the door is rubbing, whether the latch lines up with the strike plate, and whether the key is the issue or the whole door is binding. Don't force the hardware. A sticking commercial lock is often a mix of wear and door alignment, and that changes the repair.

What should I have ready for a lock repair on a business door in Perryville?

It helps to know which door is acting up, what it does when it fails, and whether anyone has tried to fix it already. If you have the key that normally works, bring it. Photos of the inside and outside hardware can also help if the problem only shows up when the door is closed.

Can a worn lock still be used for a while, or is that risky for my shop?

Sometimes it still works, but that doesn't mean it's safe to ignore. A lock that binds, skips, or only catches partway can fail at the wrong time or make it easier for someone to force the door. For a business, especially one with daily foot traffic, that's not something to gamble on.

How do you decide whether to repair the lock or replace it on a commercial door?

If the core problem is wear, alignment, a loose part, or a damaged strike, repair may be enough. If the housing is cracked, the keyway is badly damaged, or the hardware has failed repeatedly, replacement can make more sense. The door condition matters too, since a bad frame can ruin a good lock.

What if the lock problem only shows up after closing time near the MARC station?

That usually points to a door that settles differently when the building empties out, or hardware that only binds when the door is fully latched. The useful thing to note is exactly what changes at closing: does the key stop turning, does the latch miss the strike, or does the closer pull too hard. That tells the repair technician where to start.
