Repair, Rekey, or Replace

When a door in Felton won't open the way it should, we start by looking at the whole opening, not just the panic bar itself. If the device is solid but binding because of loose hardware, a shifted latch, or worn trim, repair is usually the first path. If the bar works but the key control or outside trim no longer matches who should have access, rekeying can restore control without changing every part on the door. If the hardware is damaged, out of code, or simply not suited to the traffic and layout, replacement is the better call. That choice matters on older buildings in the town grid and on shop doors that see hard use from day to day.

We make that decision by checking how the door closes, how the latch engages, and whether the opening still gives safe exit under load. A panic device has to do its job when people are pushing through it, not just when it looks fine. If a repair will leave the door dependable and inspection-ready, we'll say so. If rekeying is enough to bring access back under control, we'll take that route. If the body, trim, or strike has worn past what makes sense to keep, we'll recommend a replacement that fits the opening and the code requirements.

Panic Bar Installation in Felton - common questions

How does panic bar installation usually work on a business door in Felton?

We start by checking the door, frame, swing direction, and existing hardware. Then we fit the exit device so the latch, closer, and trim all work together. If the opening has old holes or uneven prep, we account for that before mounting the new hardware so the door functions cleanly and stays inspection-ready.

What should I have ready before you install a panic bar on my shop or garage door?

It helps to know the door's use, whether it swings inward or outward, and if anything is already mounted on it. If there's a closer, alarm contact, or old lock hardware, mention that too. Clear information up front helps us bring the right device and avoid surprises once we're at the opening.

Can a panic bar fix a door that doesn't close right now?

Not by itself. If the frame is bent, the door is rubbing, or the closer is worn out, the exit device may not work correctly until those issues are handled. We can point out what needs attention, but a panic bar only does its job when the rest of the opening is working the way it should.

Should my building use a rim device or a vertical rod panic bar?

It depends on the door and how the opening is built. A single door often uses a rim device, while some wider or specific doors call for a vertical rod setup. We look at the door type, frame, and use of the space before recommending hardware, since the wrong style can create trouble later.

What if my door has old holes from a removed lock or a past repair?

That happens a lot, especially on older commercial doors. Old holes can usually be patched, covered, or worked around depending on the device and the door material. We check the condition of the opening first so the new hardware is mounted securely and the finished door looks and works the way it should.
