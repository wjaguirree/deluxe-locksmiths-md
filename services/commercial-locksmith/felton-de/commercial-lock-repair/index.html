Commercial Lock Repair in Felton - common questions

My shop door in Felton still opens, but the lock feels loose. Is that a repair job or a replacement job?

If the door, frame, and latch are basically sound, it's often a repair job. Loose set screws, a worn strike, or a misaligned latch can make the lock feel bad even when the hardware is still usable. If the cylinder or internal parts are worn out, replacement may be the better answer.

What should I have ready before you come out to repair a commercial lock?

Have the key that normally works, and let the door stay in the condition it fails in if you can safely do that. It also helps to know whether the problem is with the key, the latch, the deadbolt, or the door closing. If there's a second entrance using the same hardware, mention that too.

If my back door at a Felton business is sticking, what's the risk if I keep using it?

The main risk is that the problem gets worse and the hardware starts damaging the frame or lock body. A sticking door can also leave people forcing the latch, which wears out the mechanism faster. In some cases the door stops locking cleanly before anyone notices it has shifted.

Should I repair the existing lock or switch to new commercial hardware?

If the lock is a standard commercial set and the body is still solid, repair is often enough. If the hardware is obsolete, badly corroded, or mismatched with the door, replacement can be the cleaner fix. The decision usually comes down to whether the part can hold up after the repair and whether replacement parts are still practical.

Can you fix a lock on an older storefront door without rebuilding the whole door?

Often, yes. Older doors around town may need strike adjustment, hinge correction, or fresh prep work so the lock lines up again. If the frame is badly warped or the door edge is too worn, more work may be needed. But a lot of older commercial doors can be brought back into service without replacing everything.
