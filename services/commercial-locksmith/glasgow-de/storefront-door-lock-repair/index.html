What to Check First

Before we touch the door, we want you to gather a few basics. If the lock is sticking, check whether the door is rubbing at the top or dragging on the threshold, because an aluminum storefront door can go out of alignment and make a good lock seem bad. If the key turns but nothing happens, note whether the inside lever, hook bolt, or deadlatch feels loose, gritty, or hard to reset. It also helps to have the key that still works, even if only part of the time, and to tell us whether the issue started after a break-in attempt, a hard slam, or a recent weather change.

What you should leave alone is the adjusting and disassembly. Storefront hardware is tied together through the mortise case, cylinder, pivots, and door frame, so forcing the key, prying the door, or loosening hardware without the right setup can bend parts that were still usable. Storefront Door Lock Repair in Glasgow - common questions

My storefront door in Glasgow closes, but the lock still won't catch. What are you checking first?

We start with the door alignment, then check the strike, the latch or hook bolt, and the pivot hardware. A door can look fine at a glance and still miss the lock by a small amount. On aluminum storefront doors, that small gap is often enough to stop the lock from engaging cleanly.

What should I have ready before you come out to look at a storefront lock near Glasgow?

If you have the key that currently works, bring it. If the door has multiple problems, tell us what happens in order: does it drag, stick, fail to latch, or not unlock cleanly. That helps us separate a lock issue from a door alignment issue before we start working.

Could a loose storefront handle be a sign the lock is failing, not just the handle?

Yes. On commercial aluminum doors, a loose handle can point to a worn spindle, loose mounting hardware, or a problem inside the lock case. The handle may be the first thing you notice, but the real failure can be deeper in the door. We check both the trim and the lock body.

If my shop door around People's Plaza is hard to lock, is repair better than replacing the whole door hardware?

Often, yes. If the problem is a worn cylinder, a misaligned strike, or a pivot that needs adjustment, repair can solve it without changing the whole assembly. If the lock body is damaged or the door hardware is too worn to hold adjustment, replacement may be the cleaner fix.

Can you fix a storefront door if the bottom drags on the threshold but the lock itself still works?

Yes. That's a common edge case on storefronts. If the door drags, the issue may be the pivot, threshold, or door alignment rather than the lock. We can correct the hardware so the door swings properly again, which usually helps the latch and lock line up the way they should.
