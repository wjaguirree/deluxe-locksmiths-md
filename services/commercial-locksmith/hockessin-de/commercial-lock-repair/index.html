Commercial Lock Repair in Hockessin - common questions

How do you figure out whether my commercial lock in Hockessin needs repair or replacement?

We look at the full door set, not just the lock face. If the cylinder, latch, and strike are sound, repair is often enough. If the keyway is worn out, the lock body is damaged, or the hardware no longer matches the door's use, replacement may be the better option. We'll explain what's worn and what still has life left.

What should we have ready before you come out to our office near Lancaster Pike?

Have the door unlocked if possible, and make sure someone can show which lock is acting up. It helps to know whether staff are having trouble with one key, every key, or only when the door is shut. If there's been a break-in attempt or recent door work, let us know that too, since it can change what we check first.

Is a loose handle or a sticky latch a sign the lock is failing?

Sometimes, but not always. A loose handle can come from worn hardware or mounting screws, while a sticky latch can be caused by dirt, a bad spring, or the door not lining up with the strike. We test each part so we're not fixing the wrong thing. That matters on busy commercial doors, where several small issues can feel like one big one.

Should we repair the existing lock or switch to a different commercial lock style?

If the existing lock is a good fit for the door and the problem is limited to wear or alignment, repair usually makes sense. If staff keep fighting the same hardware, or the lock is not suited to how the door gets used, a different style can be the better long-term choice. We can walk through the tradeoffs without guessing.

Can you still repair a lock if the key turns but the door won't open?

Yes, that can still be a repair job. The issue may be the latch not retracting, a broken internal part, or the strike catching the bolt. Sometimes the lock is fine and the door is out of alignment. We check the whole setup before deciding whether the lock itself needs work.
