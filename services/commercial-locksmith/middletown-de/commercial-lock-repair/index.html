Before we come out, it helps to look at how the door is acting and gather a few simple details. Note whether the problem is the latch, the deadbolt, the closer, the handle, or the key cylinder, and whether the door is dragging, sticking, or not lining up with the strike. What we want you to leave alone is just as important. Don't force a stiff key, pry on a jammed latch, or keep cycling a commercial lever that's binding. That can bend parts that were still usable and turn a repair into a bigger job. If the door is not closing right, avoid slamming it or propping it open with pressure on the hardware. For electric strikes, panic hardware, and keyed systems that multiple employees use, it's best not to take covers off or move internal parts unless you already know the setup.

Commercial Lock Repair in Middletown - common questions

What usually goes wrong with a commercial lock on a store or office door in Middletown?

The most common issues are a latch that won't catch, a deadbolt that feels tight, a key that turns but doesn't release smoothly, or a door that has dropped out of alignment. Heavy daily use, loose hardware, and frame movement can all cause the same door to start acting up.

What should we have ready before you come out to repair the lock?

It helps to know which door is acting up, what the problem looks like, and whether the lock has been repaired before. If the building has a master key system, let us know that too. Having access to the door and someone who can approve the work makes the process smoother.

Is a sticking lock something we can keep using for a while, or is that a risk?

It's a risk. A sticking lock often gets worse as the parts wear against each other. The key can snap, the latch can fail, or the door can stop securing properly. If the door is used by staff or customers all day, it's better to deal with it before it turns into a lockout or a broken part.

Should we repair the existing hardware or replace the lock entirely?

That depends on what is worn out and whether the rest of the door hardware is still sound. If the body of the lock is solid and the problem is alignment, a repair may be enough. If the parts are stripped, damaged, or the hardware is low-grade and failing repeatedly, replacement can make more sense.

What if the lock is on a glass storefront or a door with special hardware?

That still may be repairable, but it needs the right parts and a careful approach. Commercial doors with panic hardware, mortise locks, or storefront hardware can fail in ways that look simple but aren't. We check the full assembly so we're fixing the cause, not just the symptom.
