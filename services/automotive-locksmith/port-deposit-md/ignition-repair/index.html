Ignition Repair in Port Deposit - common questions

My key turns rough in Port Deposit. How do you tell if it's the ignition or the key?

We look at the wear pattern on the key, test how the cylinder feels with the correct key, and check whether the problem changes when the wheel is loaded or the shifter is in different positions. A worn key can mimic a bad ignition, but if the cylinder has damaged internal parts, a new key alone won't solve it.

What should I have ready before you work on my ignition?

Have the vehicle year, make, and model handy, along with any signs you've noticed, like sticking, jamming, or a key that only works sometimes. If the steering wheel is locked or the vehicle won't shift normally, mention that too. Those details help narrow down whether it's the cylinder, the key, or another part of the starting system.

Is it safe to keep forcing the key if it only sticks once in a while?

No. Forcing a rough ignition can bend the key, damage the cylinder further, or break the key off inside. Once that happens, the repair usually gets more involved. If the ignition is already showing resistance, it's better to stop using extra force and have it checked before it locks up completely.

Should I repair the ignition cylinder or replace it if it's worn?

If the wear is limited and the housing is still sound, a repair may be enough. If the cylinder is badly worn, broken internally, or has been damaged by a snapped key, replacement may be the better choice. The right answer depends on the condition of the parts, not just on whether the car still starts sometimes.

Can you help if the key is stuck and the vehicle is parked near the Susquehanna riverfront?

Yes. That kind of situation often involves more than one problem, such as a locked steering wheel, a worn key, or an ignition cylinder that's failing. We can sort through what's actually binding before anything gets damaged. If the key is trapped in the cylinder, don't pry on it or twist harder, because that can make the repair harder.
