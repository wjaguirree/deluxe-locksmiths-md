Ignition Replacement in Port Deposit - common questions

How do you tell if my problem is the ignition switch, the lock cylinder, or just a bad key in Port Deposit?

We check how the key behaves in the door and in the ignition, then watch for signs like a stuck wheel, a key that turns partway, or a dash that acts odd. A worn key can copy the symptoms of a bad ignition. We sort out the source first so we replace the part that actually failed.

What should I have ready before you come out to replace my ignition?

Have the vehicle registration and proof that it's yours or that you're allowed to approve the work. If you have a spare key, bring it. If the vehicle has ever had a different ignition or a remote-start add-on, tell us that up front. Those details help us plan the repair and avoid surprises once we're on site.

Can a worn ignition damage my steering wheel or leave me stranded in Port Deposit?

Yes. A failing ignition can bind the wheel, trap the key, or keep the vehicle from starting even when everything else seems fine. If the problem is ignored, the cylinder can seize harder or the key can break off inside. We'd rather replace the worn part before it turns into a bigger lockout or towing problem.

Should I replace the whole ignition or just rekey it to my current car key?

If the hardware is worn out, replacement is usually the right fix. If the rest of the system is sound, we can often key the new ignition to the key you already carry. That keeps one key working across the vehicle instead of adding another one to the ring. The best choice depends on the condition of the cylinder and the vehicle's setup.

What if my truck is stuck with the key inside near the riverfront or I can't move it at all?

We can work on the vehicle where it sits in many cases, including jobs where the keys are locked in the truck and the ignition problem is part of the issue. If the vehicle can't be made safe or the repair calls for a different setup, we'll tell you that plainly. We don't guess on ignition work.
