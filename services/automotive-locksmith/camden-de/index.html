Automotive locksmith in Camden - common questions

My car is stuck in a lot off Route 13 in Camden. How do you figure out if I need a new key, a fob reset, or ignition work?

We start with what the car is doing, not with a guess. If the doors unlock but the engine won't start, that often points to a transponder or fob issue. If the key won't turn or feels gritty, the ignition may be worn. If the blade is bent or damaged, a replacement key may solve it.

What should I have ready before you come out for a car key problem in Camden?

Have your ID, the vehicle registration, and proof that you can use the car. If you still have one working key or fob, bring that too. It also helps to know the year, make, and model, because the key type and programming steps can vary a lot from one vehicle to the next.

Will trying to force a key that's stuck in the ignition make things worse?

It can. Forcing it can bend the key, break off the blade, or damage the cylinder farther. If the key is stuck, stop turning it and don't yank on it. In a lot of cases we can extract the broken piece and check whether the ignition still works before any bigger repair is needed.

If I have one working car key, is it better to make a duplicate or get a new fob made for my Camden car?

If the current key is still in good shape, duplication is usually the simpler path. If the fob buttons are failing, the shell is cracked, or the vehicle uses a smart key system, a new fob may be the better choice. The right answer depends on what still works and what type of system your car uses.

My key was lost at a business near Camden-Wyoming Avenue and I only have the vehicle with me. Can the work still be done there?

Yes, if the vehicle is accessible and you can prove you're allowed to use it. That's a common on-site job for automotive locksmith work. We can make and program the needed key or fob at the vehicle's location, which keeps you from having to tow it elsewhere first.
