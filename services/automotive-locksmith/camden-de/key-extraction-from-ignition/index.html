Key Extraction From Ignition in Camden - common questions

How do you remove a broken key from the ignition without wrecking the column?

The first step is figuring out whether the broken piece is loose, wedged, or sitting against worn parts inside the cylinder. From there, the fragment is removed with tools meant for the lock, not with anything that can scratch the housing or push debris deeper. If the cylinder is damaged too, that gets addressed separately.

What should I have ready before someone comes out for a snapped ignition key in Camden?

Have the vehicle make, model, and year handy, plus whether the engine turns, the key is stuck in one spot, or part of the key is still visible. If you have a spare key, bring that too. It helps to know whether the car is parked at home, at work, or in a lot off Route 13.

Could trying to pull the broken key myself make it worse?

Yes. Tweezers, blades, or bent wire can push the fragment deeper or scratch the cylinder pins. Once that happens, the ignition may stop turning cleanly even after the broken piece is out. If the key is snapped flush or the slot feels tight, it's better to leave it alone and avoid turning the problem into a cylinder repair.

How do I know if I need key extraction or a new ignition?

If the key broke but the cylinder still turns smoothly once the piece is removed, extraction may be enough. If the key was already hard to turn, wouldn't release, or now spins oddly after the break, the ignition itself may be worn. The difference shows up in how the lock behaves before and after the fragment is out.

What if my key broke in the ignition at a parking lot in Camden and the doors are locked too?

That can be handled as two separate problems: getting into the car and removing the broken key from the ignition. The order matters, because the car can be opened without touching the ignition, and then the broken piece can be addressed without forcing the lock. That avoids extra damage and keeps the repair focused.
