When a key snaps in the ignition, the first job is to get the broken piece out without scarring the cylinder or steering column. After that, we look at what actually failed. Key Extraction From Ignition in Middletown - common questions

How do you get a broken key out of my ignition without damaging the car?

We start by checking how much of the key is exposed and whether the cylinder still turns. Then we use extraction tools that grab the broken blade without digging into the lock. If the ignition is worn or sticky, we work more slowly so the steering column and surrounding parts stay intact.

What should I have ready when you come out for a broken ignition key in Middletown?

Have the vehicle nearby, the key if part of it is still with you, and proof that the car is yours. If the key broke while the wheel was locked or the car is parked in a busy lot, let us know the exact spot and whether the shifter is in park. That helps us plan the right tools.

Can trying to turn the key again make the ignition worse?

Yes. If the blade is cracked or half-broken, more force can snap it deeper into the cylinder or bend the ignition parts inside. If the key is already stuck, it's better to leave it alone and have us work on it as-is. That usually gives us a cleaner repair path.

Should I replace the ignition or just extract the broken key?

That depends on the condition of the lock. If the break was caused by a worn key and the ignition still works smoothly, extraction and a new key may be enough. If the cylinder is rough, loose, or hard to turn, replacement can be the better call. We'll tell you what we see before any extra work is done.

My key broke in the ignition after a school drop-off and the car is stuck in a lot. Can you still help?

Yes. We handle roadside and parking-lot calls like that all the time. If the broken piece is still in the ignition, we can remove it on site. If the car won't shift or the wheel is locked, we work around that without forcing the column, so the car can be driven or towed after the repair.
