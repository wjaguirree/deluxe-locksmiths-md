Ignition Replacement in Wilmington - common questions

How do you decide whether my ignition needs repair, rekeying, or replacement?

We start by checking what the key is doing in the cylinder and whether the problem follows the key or stays with the car. If the ignition is worn but still structurally sound, rekeying or repair may work. If the internal parts are damaged, the switch is failing, or the cylinder has been forced, replacement is usually the better path.

What should I have ready when we call about an ignition problem in Wilmington?

Have the vehicle year, make, and model ready, along with your location and a clear description of what the key does. If you have the spare key, keep that nearby too. Knowing whether the key turns at all, sticks, or won't come out helps us plan the job before we arrive.

Could forcing a stuck key make the ignition worse?

Yes. Forcing it can bend the key, damage the wafers inside the cylinder, or break off a piece that makes the problem harder to solve. If the key is fighting you, stop turning it and don't try to pry it loose. A careful inspection is better than making a small issue into a bigger one.

If I still have the original car key, can the new ignition be matched to it?

Often, yes. On many vehicles we can key the replacement ignition to the key you already use, which keeps things simple. Some cars and some types of damage make that harder, especially if the original parts are too worn or the vehicle has had prior work done. We check that before we proceed.

What if my car is stuck in a garage near Market Street and the key won't turn at all?

We can still evaluate the ignition where the car is parked and see whether the issue is with the key, the cylinder, or a broken component inside the column. Tight spaces can make the work slower, but that doesn't change the approach. If the car can't be shifted or steered safely, we'll talk through the practical options first.
