Key Extraction From Ignition in Wilmington - common questions

How do you get a broken key out of an ignition without damaging the column?

We start by checking how the key broke and how far the piece sits inside the cylinder. Then we use extraction tools sized for the lock, not makeshift picks or screwdrivers. The goal is to remove the fragment through the keyway and inspect the cylinder so we can tell whether it still turns smoothly.

What should I have ready when you come to my car in Wilmington?

If possible, have the vehicle make, model, and year, plus any spare key or fob you still have. It also helps to know whether the key broke while turning, while inserting, or after the ignition already felt stiff. That tells us a lot about the wear inside the lock.

Can trying to turn it harder make the ignition worse?

Yes. Forcing a stuck key can bend the blade farther, push the broken piece deeper, or score the cylinder wafers. That can turn a simple extraction into a cylinder repair. If the key is resisting, stop using it and let us deal with the lock hardware directly.

Should I replace the ignition cylinder or just get the broken key removed?

If the cylinder still turns smoothly after the broken piece comes out, removal may be all you need. If the key was already sticky, the cylinder may be worn enough that another break is likely. In that case, replacing or rekeying the cylinder is the better call.

What if the key snapped off and my car is parked in a garage downtown?

That's a common situation, and it usually isn't a problem as long as we can reach the vehicle and work safely around it. We can handle the extraction on site, then check whether the ignition accepts a spare key or needs more work. If the car has other lock issues, we'll go through those too.
