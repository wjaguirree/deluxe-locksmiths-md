How We Decide the Fix

When a key snaps off in the ignition, we do not treat every case the same. First we look at what broke: the key blade, the ignition cylinder, or both. If the broken piece is sitting loose and the cylinder still turns smoothly, we can often extract it cleanly and keep the original ignition in place. If the key broke because the cylinder is worn or binding, extraction is only the first step. In that case we check whether the cylinder can be repaired, whether it should be rekeyed to match the rest of the vehicle, or whether replacement is the safer call.

Repair makes sense when the ignition is still mechanically sound and the damage is limited to the key itself. Rekeying is the right move when the ignition works, but the cuts inside the cylinder have worn enough that a fresh key pattern will reduce future sticking and make the new key feel right. Replacement is what we choose when the cylinder is damaged, the wafers are worn out, or the ignition has already been forced and scarred from trying to turn a broken key. We make that call on site, using the condition of the parts in front of us, not a guess.

Key Extraction From Ignition in Cecilton - common questions

How do you get a broken key out of the ignition without damaging the column?

We start by looking at how the key failed and how much of it is visible. From there, we use the right extraction tools to remove the broken piece without forcing the cylinder. If the ignition is already worn or damaged, we work carefully around that so the steering column and surrounding parts stay intact.

What should I have ready before you come out to a broken ignition key in Cecilton?

Have the vehicle year, make, and model ready, and tell us whether the key snapped while turning or while the car was parked. If you have the spare key, keep it nearby. It also helps to know whether the vehicle is at your home, a job site, or a landing near the river, since access can affect how we approach the work.

Can trying to turn the ignition again make the problem worse?

Yes. If part of the key is still lodged in the cylinder, turning it can push the piece deeper or damage the wafers inside the lock. If the steering wheel is locked, forcing it can also make the column feel seized. It's better to leave it alone until the broken piece is removed.

If my key snapped, do I need extraction only, or do I need a new ignition key too?

Sometimes the broken key is the only issue, and once it's removed the original ignition still works. Other times the key failed because the blade was worn or the cylinder is failing. We look at both the key and the lock so you can decide whether to keep using the existing ignition or move to a new key setup.

What if the key broke off in a truck at a Sassafras River landing and I can't get it into neutral?

That can happen if the ignition and shift interlock are tied together on that vehicle. We'll look at the exact setup before touching anything. In some cases the broken key has to come out before the shifter will release normally. The important part is not to force the column or the selector while the key piece is still stuck.
