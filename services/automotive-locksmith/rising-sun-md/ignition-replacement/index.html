Repair, Rekey, or Replace

When the ignition acts up, we start by sorting out what failed and what still works. Sometimes the cylinder is worn but salvageable, and cleaning, rebuilding, or rekeying will get you back on the road with the key you already carry. Other times the problem is deeper, like broken wafers, a jammed switch, or damage from a key that's been forced too many times. In those cases, repair won't hold, and replacement makes more sense than trying to patch a part that's already worn out.

We also look at the rest of the vehicle's lock set before we decide. If your door and trunk keys still match and you want to keep one key for the whole car, we'll try to key the new ignition to that existing key when the parts allow it. Ignition Replacement in Rising Sun - common questions

How do you tell if my ignition needs replacement or if it's just the key acting up?

A worn key can cause a sticky ignition, but not every hard turn means the cylinder is bad. The check is usually based on how the key feels in the lock, whether the steering wheel is binding, and whether the problem shows up every time. If the cylinder, switch, or actuator is the real issue, replacing only the key won't fix it.

What should I have ready if my truck won't start from a bad ignition?

Have the vehicle ready to access, and have the key you normally use, even if it's worn. If you have proof of ownership and the vehicle's make, model, and year, that helps confirm the right parts and keying method. If the truck is loaded down or parked in a tight spot, mention that up front so the work can be planned around it.

Can a new ignition be keyed to my existing key, or will I need a separate key?

On many vehicles, the new ignition cylinder can be keyed to match your current key. That's often the cleaner result because you keep one key for daily use. Some vehicles or damaged systems make that harder, and a separate key may be unavoidable. The exact answer depends on the ignition design and what parts are still usable.

What can go wrong if somebody replaces the ignition the wrong way?

A bad installation can leave you with a key that still sticks, a steering column that isn't assembled right, or a switch that doesn't fully engage. Some jobs fail because the wrong part was used, and others because the real problem was in the switch, not the cylinder. A proper job should turn smoothly and start and shut off reliably.

If my truck is locked up with a load on it near Rising Sun, can the ignition still be replaced?

Often, yes, but the setup matters. If the truck is accessible and the ignition is the problem, the work can usually be handled where it sits. A loaded truck may need extra care so nothing gets disturbed while the column or dash parts are opened up. If the vehicle has other electrical damage, that can change the repair plan.
