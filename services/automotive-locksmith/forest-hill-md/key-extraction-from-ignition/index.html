Key Extraction From Ignition in Forest Hill - common questions

How do you remove a broken key from my ignition without damaging it?

We use extraction tools made for the cylinder, not improvised hardware-store tricks. The broken piece is pulled out from the keyway, then we check how the ignition turns and whether the fragment left any burrs or debris behind. If the cylinder is already worn, we'll explain what we see before anything else gets forced.

What should I do before you come to my car in Forest Hill?

Don't keep turning the key or pushing another key fragment into the ignition. If the steering wheel is locked, leave it alone. Have the vehicle location, make, model, and year ready, and tell us whether the key broke while turning, starting, or coming out. That helps us bring the right tools.

Is it a bad sign if my ignition feels loose after the key broke?

It can be. A loose feel often means the cylinder is worn, not just jammed with a broken key. We can remove the fragment and see whether the lock still catches properly, but if the internal parts are failing, extraction alone won't solve the issue for long. We'll tell you if that's what we find.

Should I try to pull the broken key out myself or just leave it?

If the tip is sticking out, some people try tweezers and make the break worse. If it's flush or buried, stop there. A wrong pull can bend the wafer pins or push the piece deeper. Leaving it alone is usually the safer choice until we can extract it correctly.

What if my key broke off and the car is also locked at a trailhead lot near Rocks State Park?

That happens more often than people think. We can address the ignition issue and, if needed, help with the lockout side of it as well. The main thing is not to keep forcing the key or the ignition once it has broken. The less movement after the break, the better the outcome usually is.
