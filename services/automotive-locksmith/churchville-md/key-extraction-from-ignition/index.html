Key Extraction From Ignition in Churchville - common questions

How do you get a broken key out of an ignition without damaging the column?

The cylinder is worked carefully so the broken piece can be hooked, gripped, or lifted out without forcing the lock. If the key fragment is visible, removal is usually simpler. If it's deeper in the ignition, the trim and cylinder position matter more, and the job has to be done with a lighter touch.

What should I have ready when you come out to Churchville for a broken ignition key?

Have the vehicle make, model, year, and the exact location of the car or truck. If you still have the other half of the key or a spare, keep it handy. It also helps to know whether the steering wheel is locked, because that changes how the ignition can be worked.

Is there a risk the ignition will need replacement after the key breaks off?

Sometimes, yes. If the cylinder is worn, the key is badly bent, or the fragment is jammed hard, the extraction can reveal damage that was already there. In many cases the key can be removed and the ignition kept in service, but a badly worn cylinder may not turn smoothly afterward.

Should I try to pull the broken key out myself before calling?

Usually not. Tweezers, picks, or glue can push the piece deeper or leave residue inside the cylinder. That makes the job harder and can turn a simple extraction into a larger repair. If the fragment is visible, it's still better to leave it alone and have it removed with the right tools.

What if my car is locked and the key broke in the ignition at a park-and-ride lot near Aberdeen?

That can happen when the key breaks after the door is already locked or the steering wheel is under tension. The ignition and the door lock may need separate handling, and the order matters. The broken key has to come out first or the cylinder can get more damaged when the lock is worked.
