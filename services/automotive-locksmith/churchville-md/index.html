Automotive locksmith in Churchville - common questions

Can you make a replacement car key if I lost the only one I had in Churchville?

Usually, yes, if the vehicle information and security system allow it. We can often create a new key, cut it to match the car, and program it if the vehicle uses a chip or fob. Some models need extra steps, but a lost key does not always mean the car has to go to the dealership.

What should I have ready when you come out for a car key or ignition problem?

Have the vehicle close by, your registration if you can get to it, and proof that you're allowed to have the car serviced. If the key still works partway, don't force it, because that can make the damage worse. For a fob issue, bring any spare fob or the old shell if you have it.

Is there a risk if I keep using a key that already sticks in the ignition?

Yes. A sticking key can wear down the key blade and damage the ignition cylinder, and a bad turn can leave the key broken off inside. If that happens, the car may be harder to start and harder to secure. It's better to address it before the cylinder wears farther or seizes up.

How do I know if I need a new fob or just programming for my Churchville vehicle?

If the buttons work sometimes but the car still won't recognize the fob, programming may be the issue. If the shell is cracked, the battery contacts are damaged, or the unit won't respond at all, replacement may make more sense. We look at the vehicle and the fob together before deciding.

Can you extract a broken key from the ignition without replacing the whole ignition?

Often, yes. If the cylinder isn't badly damaged, the broken piece can usually be removed and the ignition checked for wear. If the tumblers or housing are already worn out, replacement may be the cleaner fix. The right answer depends on what the key left behind inside the lock.
