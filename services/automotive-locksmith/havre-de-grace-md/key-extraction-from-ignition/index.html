Key Extraction From Ignition in Havre de Grace - common questions

How do you get a broken key out of an ignition without damaging the cylinder?

The usual approach is to inspect how the key broke, then use extraction tools that grab the fragment without pushing it deeper. If the cylinder is still intact, the piece can often be removed cleanly and the ignition checked for wear before the car is started again.

What should I do before you get to my car in Havre de Grace?

Stop turning the key, don't force the steering wheel, and don't try to dig the piece out with a blade or wire. If you can, have the car's make, model, year, and a clear description of what the key is doing ready. That helps the job start with the right tools.

Is there a risk that the ignition has to be replaced after a key breaks off?

Sometimes, but not always. If the fragment came out cleanly and the cylinder wasn't forced, the ignition often still works. If the key snapped because the lock is worn, or if someone kept twisting after it jammed, the cylinder may be damaged enough that repair or replacement is the better fix.

Should I try a spare key first if the one in the ignition snapped?

Not until the broken piece is removed. A spare can't help if the cylinder is blocked. Once the fragment is out, a spare is a good way to tell whether the problem was just the broken key or whether the ignition itself is wearing out.

What if the key broke off while I was parked near the water and the car is stuck there?

That happens. Don't keep working the ignition in place, and don't leave the fragment rubbing in the cylinder. Stay with the car if you can, note exactly where it's parked, and explain whether the key is broken off, jammed, or both. The cleaner the description, the easier the extraction.
