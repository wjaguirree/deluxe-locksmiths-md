Automotive locksmith in Aberdeen - common questions

My key fob quit working after I parked near Ripken Stadium. What usually fails first?

The battery is only one possibility. The fob case can crack, the internal board can fail, or the vehicle may need the fob programmed again. If the buttons stopped responding and a battery swap didn't help, the next step is usually testing the fob and the car's system to see whether repair, reprogramming, or a new fob is the right move.

What should I have ready when you come out for a car key or ignition job in Aberdeen?

Have the vehicle registration and some form of ID ready if you can. It also helps to know the year, make, model, and whether the key is a standard metal key, transponder key, or fob. If the key is broken or the ignition is stuck, leave the pieces where they are so nothing gets pushed deeper.

Is it risky to keep trying a key that's sticking in the ignition?

Yes. Forcing it can bend the key, damage the cylinder, or leave a broken piece inside. If the key is already hard to turn, it's better to stop and have it checked. Sometimes the fix is a worn key; other times the ignition itself is wearing out and needs repair or replacement.

How do I know if my car needs a new key, a rekey, or ignition replacement?

A new key makes sense when the original is lost or too worn to work well. Rekeying is about changing the lock pattern so an old key no longer fits, which is useful after a key loss or when you want control back. Ignition replacement is for cylinders that are damaged, stripped, or beyond practical repair.

Can you still help if the key broke off inside the ignition or door lock?

Usually, yes. Key extraction is often possible without replacing the whole part. After the broken piece is removed, the next question is whether the lock or ignition still works cleanly. If the break happened because the cylinder is worn, you may need repair or replacement to keep the problem from coming right back.
