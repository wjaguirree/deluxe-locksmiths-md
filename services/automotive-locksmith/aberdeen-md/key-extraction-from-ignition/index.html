How do you get a broken key out of the ignition without damaging the column?

The broken piece is removed with tools made for tight ignition work, not by prying at the plastic trim. The cylinder is checked as the fragment comes out so the keyway, pins, and surrounding parts stay as intact as possible. If the ignition is already worn, that gets noted before anything else is forced.

What should I have ready before you work on my car in Aberdeen?

Have the vehicle make, model, and year ready, along with proof that it's yours. If you have the remaining half of the key, a spare, or a fob that still works, keep those on hand too. Clear out anything blocking access to the ignition area so the job can be done without extra delays.

Could trying to turn the key again make things worse?

Yes. If the key is already bent, cracked, or partly broken, more force can push the fragment farther into the cylinder or damage the wafers inside. If the wheel is locked too, forcing it can add stress to the ignition. It's better to stop and have the broken piece removed cleanly.

If the key broke, do I need a new ignition or just a new key?

Not always. If the cylinder turns smoothly after extraction, a new key may be enough. If the ignition feels gritty, sticks, or keeps catching, the cylinder may be worn and need more than a replacement key. The condition of the ignition tells you which direction to go.
