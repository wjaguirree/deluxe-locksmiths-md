Key Extraction From Ignition in Claymont - common questions

If my key snapped off in the ignition in Claymont, what do we actually do at the car?

We first confirm the fragment's position and the condition of the ignition. Then we use extraction tools to pull the broken piece out without forcing the cylinder. After that, we test the ignition and talk through whether the key itself was worn, the cylinder is rough, or both.

What should I have ready before you come to my car on Philadelphia Pike?

Have the car make, model, and year ready, plus whether the key broke off fully or is still sticking out. If you have any remaining key pieces, keep them. Also let us know if the ignition has been hard to turn lately, because that helps us decide whether there's a deeper issue.

Could trying to pull the broken key out myself make the ignition worse?

Yes. Tweezers, glue, knives, and bent wire can push the fragment deeper or mar the cylinder. That can leave you with a repair that takes more work than a clean extraction would have. If the piece is jammed or the key is worn down, it's better to stop and have it handled carefully.

Is it better to just replace the ignition or try to extract the key first?

If the ignition itself is sound, extraction is usually the first step because it preserves the existing hardware. Replacement only makes sense when the cylinder is already worn, damaged, or failing after the broken key is removed. We can tell you which path fits the condition of the car instead of guessing.

What if my key broke off and the car is still stuck in park near the Claymont rail station?

That can happen if the ignition won't fully release the key or the vehicle's shift interlock is tied to the ignition position. We can still work from the car's location and see whether the broken key, the cylinder, or the locking system is the part holding everything up. Sometimes the issue is more than one thing.
