Automotive locksmith in Claymont - common questions

My car key turns sometimes but sticks other times. Do you repair the ignition or just cut a new key?

We check both, because a new key alone won't solve a worn ignition. If the key is badly worn, duplication or replacement may be the right fix. If the cylinder is damaged, the ignition may need repair or replacement. We test the result in the vehicle so the key and ignition work together instead of masking the real problem.

What should I have ready when you come out to program a fob in Claymont?

Have the vehicle, every working key or fob you still have, and proof that the car is yours. If the old fob was acting up, tell us exactly what it was doing, such as failing to unlock, not starting the car, or only working close to the door. That helps us choose the right programming path and avoid wasted steps.

My transponder key worked until I dropped it. Is there a risk in trying to keep using it?

Yes. A damaged transponder key can become unreliable without warning, and forcing it can leave you stranded or damage the ignition. If the shell cracked or the chip was jarred loose, we may be able to replace the key or program a new one. We'll tell you when the old key is past the point of repair.

Should I ask for a duplicate key or a new programmed key for my car?

If you already have a working key and just want a backup, duplication is usually the right move. If the only key is lost, broken, or no longer communicates with the car, you may need a new programmed key or fob. We'll look at the vehicle's system and tell you which option fits the situation.

Can you help if my key is stuck in the ignition at the Claymont rail station lot?

Yes. A stuck key can come from a worn key, a damaged cylinder, or a steering lock issue. We can extract the key and inspect the ignition for damage. If the cylinder is worn, we can talk through repair or replacement so you don't end up with the same problem again on the next start.
