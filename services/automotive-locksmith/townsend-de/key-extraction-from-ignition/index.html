Key Extraction From Ignition in Townsend - common questions

How do you get a broken key out of my ignition without damaging the steering column?

We start by checking how far the broken piece sits in the cylinder and whether the lock still turns. Then we use extraction tools that reach the key fragment directly, rather than prying on the trim. If the cylinder is already damaged, we'll stop and talk through the next step before more parts get forced.

What should I have ready when you come out for a key stuck in the ignition in Townsend?

Have the vehicle accessible, the registration ready, and proof that you can authorize the work. If you have another working key, bring it. That helps us compare wear and see whether the broken key was copied from a worn original. If the ignition has been acting up, tell us what it did before the break.

Can trying to turn the key again make the ignition worse?

Yes. If the key is bent, cracked, or partly broken, forcing it can push the fragment deeper or damage the wafers inside the cylinder. That can turn a simple extraction into a full ignition repair. If the key won't move normally, it's better not to keep working it.

If the key comes out, do I always need a new ignition cylinder?

Not always. If the lock body is still in good shape, we may be able to extract the piece and make a working replacement key. If the cylinder is worn, loose, or binding, replacement may be the better fix. We'll inspect it first and explain what the hardware is telling us.
