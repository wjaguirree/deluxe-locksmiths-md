When a key snaps off in the ignition, the problem is usually inside the lock cylinder, not the steering column. The cylinder uses a small set of wafers, springs, and a shaped keyway to read the cuts on the key. If the key is worn, bent, or turned under strain, the tip can shear off and leave the blade lodged where the tumblers need to move. If the cylinder is already sticky, that broken piece can jam the whole assembly.

We remove the fragment with tools made for the lock, not with force that chews up the housing. That matters because the ignition cylinder has to keep its shape for the next key to work correctly. After the broken piece is out, we check for bent wafers, worn springs, or damage on the key face. If the lock is still sound, we may be able to make it work with a fresh key cut to the right pattern. If the cylinder itself is worn out, replacement is the better fix.

The replacement part depends on the vehicle and the failure. Some ignitions take a new cylinder keyed to the existing system. Others need the cylinder matched and the key transponder checked so the car will recognize it. We focus on restoring the lock so it turns cleanly and the driver isn't left fighting the same failure again.

If the key broke off, keep every piece you can find. A clean break gives us a better starting point, especially if the tip is still partly visible. Key Extraction From Ignition in Dover - common questions

How do you get a broken key out of my ignition without damaging the lock?

We use extraction tools that grab the broken blade from the keyway and pull it straight out. The goal is to avoid prying on the steering column or scoring the cylinder. After removal, we test the lock so we can tell whether the problem was only the broken key or whether the ignition is worn too.

What should I have ready if my key snapped in the ignition in Dover?

Have the vehicle year, make, and model ready, and keep any remaining part of the key if you still have it. If the key broke while turning, let us know whether the ignition is stuck in one position or the key will still go in and out. That helps us plan the repair and bring the right parts.

Could forcing the key make the ignition worse?

Yes. Forcing a stuck key can bend wafers inside the cylinder, break the remaining key piece deeper in the lock, or damage the face of the ignition. If the key won't move cleanly, it's better to stop and have it handled before the cylinder gets chewed up enough to need a full replacement.

Do I need a new ignition cylinder, or can the broken key just be removed?

If the cylinder is still smooth after extraction, a clean removal may be enough. If the lock is worn, sticky, or has already been damaged by a rough key, replacement is often the better choice. We look at how the cylinder feels and whether a proper key still turns it without binding.

What if my key is a transponder key and part of it is stuck in the ignition?

We can still extract the broken piece, but the repair may also involve checking the chip side of the key. If the blade broke because the key was worn or twisted, the replacement needs to match the vehicle and work with the immobilizer system. We handle both the mechanical lock and the electronic side when needed.
