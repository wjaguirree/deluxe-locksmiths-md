Ignition Repair in Dover - common questions

Why does my key stick in the ignition even though the car still starts?

That usually means the cylinder is worn or the key edges have changed enough that the pins aren't lining up cleanly. It may start out as a small catch, then turn into hard turning or a key that won't come out right. If you keep forcing it, the wear gets worse and the ignition can fail altogether.

What should I have ready before you come look at my ignition in Dover?

Have the vehicle year, make, and model, plus a clear description of what the key is doing. If the issue started after the key was dropped, bent, or duplicated, mention that. If you have a spare key, bring it. That helps separate a key problem from an ignition problem.

Is it risky to keep driving around Dover with a rough ignition?

Yes, because the failure can move from annoying to serious without much warning. The key may stop turning, the car may not shut off cleanly, or the steering lock may act up. That can leave you stuck in a parking lot, in a driveway, or somewhere you weren't planning to be when the part finally gives out.

Do I need a new ignition cylinder, or can the one in my car be repaired?

Not every worn ignition has to be replaced. If the cylinder body is still sound and the wear is limited, repair or rekeying may be the better answer. If the parts inside are damaged, broken, or badly worn, replacement is usually the practical fix. The condition of the key matters too.

What if the key works in the door but not the ignition?

That points to a mismatch between the ignition cylinder and the key, or a cylinder that's worn differently from the door lock. It can happen after a replacement key, a previous repair, or years of use. The ignition needs to be checked on its own, because door operation alone doesn't tell the whole story.
