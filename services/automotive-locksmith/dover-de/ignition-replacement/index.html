Ignition Replacement in Dover - common questions

How do you tell if my ignition is bad or if the key is the problem?

We look at how the key behaves in the cylinder, whether the wheel is locked, and whether the dash powers up normally. A worn key can mimic a bad ignition. So can a binding steering lock or battery trouble. The pattern of the symptom usually points to the right part before anything gets replaced.

What should I have ready when you come to replace the ignition on my car in Dover?

Have the vehicle parked safely, the key you normally use, and any spare keys if you have them. If the car has aftermarket remote start, steering column work, or a history of ignition trouble, mention that up front. That helps avoid guessing and makes it easier to match the new ignition to the existing key.

Could a worn ignition damage my key or leave me stuck in a Dover parking lot?

Yes. A rough cylinder can chew up the key blade, make it harder to turn, and eventually trap the key in the off position. If you keep forcing it, the problem can get worse and may spread to the steering lock or switch. It's better to stop using a key that already feels sticky or sloppy.

If my car has a transponder key, do you replace the whole ignition or just part of it?

That depends on the vehicle. Some cars only need the lock cylinder or switch replaced, while others tie the ignition to the anti-theft system more tightly. In those cases, the new part has to be matched and tested with the key and electronics. The right fix depends on the exact failure, not just the symptom.

Can you still help if the key won't turn at all and the steering wheel is locked?

Usually, yes, but the first step is checking whether the wheel is under pressure, the key is too worn, or the cylinder has failed internally. Sometimes it's a binding issue that can be cleared; other times the ignition has to be replaced. If the key is bent or badly worn, bring that up because it changes the diagnosis.
