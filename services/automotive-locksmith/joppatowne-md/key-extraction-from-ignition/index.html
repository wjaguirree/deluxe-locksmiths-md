Key Extraction From Ignition in Joppatowne - common questions

How do you remove a broken key from my ignition without damaging the steering column?

We start by looking at how the key broke and how far the piece is sitting in the cylinder. Then we use the right extraction tools, not prybars or makeshift picks, so we can lift the fragment out cleanly. After that, we check whether the ignition still turns smoothly or if the cylinder itself needs attention.

What should I have ready when you come out for a snapped ignition key in Joppatowne?

Have the vehicle make, model, and year ready, plus proof that it's your vehicle. If you know whether the key is broken off in the ignition or the car is locked with the key inside, say that up front. If the steering wheel is locked or the battery is weak, mention that too so we know what we're walking into.

Can trying to turn the key or pull it out make the problem worse?

Yes. Forcing a bent or broken key can drive the fragment deeper, score the cylinder, or leave the ignition stuck in a bad position. If the key is already snapped, it's better not to keep twisting it. The less the cylinder gets disturbed, the better the chance of a clean extraction and a usable ignition afterward.

If my key broke in the ignition, do I need extraction only or a new ignition cylinder too?

That depends on what caused the break. If the key snapped because the blade was worn but the cylinder is still smooth, extraction may be enough. If the cylinder is rough, sticky, or hard to turn, we'll let you know that the ignition itself may be part of the problem. We won't guess; we check it first.

What if my truck is locked with the key inside near the water and the tide is going out?

We can handle that kind of call too. First we open the truck without harming the door or seal, then we deal with the key or ignition issue if needed. Salt, sand, and damp hardware can make locks behave differently, so we work carefully and keep the entry clean instead of forcing anything.
