We also verify the steering lock and the shift interlock, when the vehicle has them, because an ignition problem can show up as a key issue when the real trouble is elsewhere in the starting path. Ignition Repair in Fallston - common questions

If my ignition in Fallston is sticking but still starts, do I need repair or replacement?

If it still starts but feels rough, repair may be enough when the cylinder is only worn or dirty. Replacement makes more sense when the key won't turn cleanly, the lock binds after repeated use, or the internal parts are damaged. The deciding factor is whether the ignition is still holding up mechanically.

What should I have ready when I call about an ignition issue from a house off MD-152?

Have the vehicle year, make, and model, plus a clear description of what the key does. It also helps to know whether you have one working key or a spare, and whether the problem is constant or comes and goes. If the car is parked at the end of a driveway or in a detached garage, mention that too.

Can forcing a rough ignition make the problem worse before you get it looked at?

Yes. Forcing a sticky ignition can bend the key, wear the cylinder faster, or leave the lock in a position where it won't release cleanly. If it already feels gritty or catches partway through the turn, stop pushing on it. That's how a repairable issue turns into a full replacement.

Is rekeying ever the right fix for an ignition in Fallston, or is that only for door locks?

Rekeying can make sense if the problem is keyed wrong, such as after a bad duplicate or if you need the ignition matched to an existing key system. It won't fix broken springs, worn wafers, or a cylinder that's physically damaged. If the lock itself is failing, repair or replacement is the better route.

What if my key turns, but the steering wheel lock or fob still acts strange?

That can mean there are two separate issues. The steering lock may be binding, or the key and ignition may be worn even if the fob works. A fob problem doesn't rule out ignition wear, and an ignition issue doesn't always mean the remote is involved. Both need to be checked against how the vehicle is behaving.
