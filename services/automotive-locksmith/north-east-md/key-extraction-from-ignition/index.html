Key Extraction From Ignition in North East - common questions

How do you get a broken key out of the ignition without damaging the column?

We use extraction tools made for broken key removal and work carefully inside the cylinder, not around the steering column trim. The goal is to remove the metal piece intact if possible, then check whether the ignition still turns smoothly. If the cylinder is already worn, we'll say so before anything else gets forced.

What should I have ready before we come out to my car in North East?

Have the vehicle keys, registration, and proof that you're connected to the car ready. If there's a spare key, keep it nearby. If the broken half came from a key ring, glove box, or boat bag, don't toss those items out yet. That can help if the missing piece is elsewhere.

Can trying to start the car again make the broken key situation worse?

Yes. Repeated twisting can push the broken piece deeper or scar the ignition cylinder. That can turn a simple extraction into a larger repair. If the key is stuck or snapped, stop using it and leave the ignition alone until it's checked.

If I have a spare key, should we use that instead of extracting the broken one?

Not right away. First we need the broken piece out, because a spare key won't help if metal is still blocking the cylinder. After extraction, we can tell you whether the spare should work normally or whether the ignition itself needs attention.

What if my key broke after a day at the river and part of it might be in my boat bag?

Keep the bag with you and don't sort through it too fast. If the matching half of the key is there, that can help confirm the cut and avoid confusion. If not, we can still work from the vehicle itself, but it's better to know exactly what was lost before anything is forced.
