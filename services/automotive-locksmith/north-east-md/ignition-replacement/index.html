Ignition Replacement in North East - common questions

How do you know if it's the ignition switch and not the battery?

A dead battery usually takes out the dash, lights, and cranking at once. An ignition problem can show up as a key that won't turn, won't return, or turns but doesn't send power the way it should. If the key feels wrong before anything electrical happens, that points more toward the ignition than the battery.

What should I have ready before you come out for an ignition replacement?

Have the vehicle registration, a photo ID, and every key you still have for that vehicle. If the key is stuck in the ignition, leave it as it is and mention exactly what happened before it failed. That helps sort out whether the issue is the cylinder, the switch, or a related lock part.

Can forcing a stubborn key make the problem worse?

Yes. Pushing harder can snap the key, damage the cylinder pins, or leave part of the blade stuck inside. It can also mask the real cause, like wear on the key itself. If the key binds, stop and let it be checked before the lock or steering column gets hurt.

Can the new ignition be matched to my existing car key?

Often, yes. If the vehicle's parts and the condition of the key allow it, the replacement can be keyed to the key you already carry. That is usually the cleanest option when the old key still works elsewhere on the car and the rest of the lock system is in decent shape.

What if my car key works in the door but not in the ignition?

That usually means the ignition cylinder is more worn than the door lock, or the key itself is worn enough that the tighter ignition won't accept it. In that case, the fix may be a new ignition cylinder or switch, not just another copy of the same worn key.
