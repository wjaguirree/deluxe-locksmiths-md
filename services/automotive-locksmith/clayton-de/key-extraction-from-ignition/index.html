Key Extraction From Ignition in Clayton - common questions

How do you get a broken key out of the ignition without damaging the column?

The broken piece is usually pulled with lock picks, extractors, or fine tools made for that job, while the cylinder is held steady. The point is to catch the key fragment itself, not pry against the housing. If the key is buried deep or the cylinder is already worn, the lock may need to come apart for a cleaner removal.

What should I do before help arrives if my key snapped in the ignition?

Leave the key position alone and don't keep turning or jiggling it. If part of the blade is still sticking out, avoid grabbing it with pliers unless it's loose enough to lift straight out. Have the vehicle make, model, and year ready, because ignition designs vary and that helps match the right tools and replacement parts.

Can the ignition still be used after the broken key piece is removed?

Sometimes, yes. If the cylinder is still smooth and the key was the only problem, it may work after cleaning and testing. If the lock is worn, gritty, or damaged by the break, using it again can cause another jam or a full lockup. In that case, replacing the cylinder is usually the better path.

Do I need a new ignition if the key broke, or can just the key be replaced?

If the break came from a worn key and the cylinder is still healthy, a new key cut from the right code or a good pattern may solve it. If the cylinder is the part that caused the bind, a new key alone won't fix the real issue. The lock has to be inspected before choosing between key replacement and ignition work.

What if the key is broken off and the steering wheel is locked too?

That can happen when the ignition is in a certain position and the wheel latch is engaged. The wheel lock and the key cylinder are separate parts, but they interact. The broken key still has to come out first. After that, the ignition can be tested to see whether the lock is binding, seized, or just set against the steering latch.
