Ignition Repair in Clayton - common questions

My key goes in but won't turn in my car in Clayton. What usually causes that?

Most of the time it's a worn ignition cylinder, a worn key, or a steering wheel that's bound against the lock. Dirt and old debris inside the cylinder can add to it. If the key feels rough instead of smooth, don't force it. That can break the key or chew up the cylinder further.

What should I do before a mobile ignition repair comes out to my place in Clayton?

Leave the key alone if it's stuck, keep the shifter in Park, and don't add oil or spray lubricants into the ignition. If the wheel is locked, try a gentle turn of the wheel while easing the key, but stop if it fights you. Having the vehicle year, make, and model ready helps.

Is it a bad idea to keep trying the key if the ignition is sticking?

Yes, if it's binding hard. Repeated force can snap the key off in the cylinder or make the internal wear worse. If it only turns with a smooth, normal effort, that's different. But when it feels gritty, jams partway, or grinds, it's better to stop and have it checked.

Do I need a full ignition replacement, or can the cylinder be repaired?

Not every ignition problem means a full replacement. If the cylinder is worn but the housing and related parts are still sound, repair may be enough. If the damage is deeper, or the parts are badly worn, replacement can be the better call. The condition of the key and cylinder tells the story.

My car wouldn't turn off all the way after a lockout near the crossing. Can that be tied to the ignition?

It can be. A sticky ignition cylinder can trap the key, keep the switch from returning fully, or leave the wheel locked when you least expect it. That's one of the reasons not to keep twisting on it. If the key is stuck or the cylinder feels rough, it needs a proper look before it gets worse.
