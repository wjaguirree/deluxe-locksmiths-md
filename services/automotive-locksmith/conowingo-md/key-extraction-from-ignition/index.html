How do you get a broken key out of an ignition in Conowingo without damaging the car?

The ignition is checked first so the broken piece isn't shoved farther in. Then the fragment is removed with tools made for the job, not by prying on the steering column. After that, the cylinder is tested with a good key to see whether it still turns smoothly or if the ignition itself is worn.

What should I have ready if my key snapped in the ignition while I was parked near the dam?

Have the vehicle year, make, and model, plus proof that you're allowed to use the car. If you still have the other half of the key, keep it nearby. It also helps to know whether the key broke while turning, while starting, or when you tried to remove it, because that can point to a worn cylinder.

Can trying to pull the broken key out myself make the ignition worse?

Yes. A small fragment can slide deeper or damage the thin parts inside the cylinder if it's picked at the wrong way. That can turn a clean extraction into an ignition repair. If the key is already stuck partway in, it's better to stop and have it removed carefully.

If the key broke off, do I need a new ignition or just the broken piece removed?

Not always. If the cylinder still turns smoothly after extraction, the ignition may be fine with a fresh key cut correctly. If the key broke because the cylinder is worn or rough, the real issue may be the ignition itself. The right call depends on how the cylinder feels once the fragment is out.

What if the car is a work vehicle and the broken key is the only one available in Conowingo?

Then the first job is still the same: remove the broken piece without making the ignition worse. After that, the next step depends on whether there's a way to get a proper replacement key made from the vehicle code or from a working key pattern. If there's no usable key left at all, the solution may take more than extraction alone.
