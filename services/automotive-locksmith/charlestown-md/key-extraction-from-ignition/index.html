Key Extraction From Ignition in Charlestown - common questions

How do you get a snapped key out of my ignition without damaging the column?

We start by checking how the broken piece is sitting in the cylinder, then use extraction tools that reach the key fragment without forcing the lock. If the ignition is still mechanically sound, we work to remove the piece cleanly and test the cylinder afterward so we can see whether it turns normally.

What should I have ready if my key breaks in the ignition in Charlestown?

Have the vehicle parked as safely as you can and keep the area around the steering wheel clear. If the key is still partly in the lock, don't twist or pry it. We'll need the vehicle year, make, and model, and it helps to know whether the key broke while turning, starting, or pulling out.

Could trying to pull the key myself make things worse?

Yes. Pliers, screwdrivers, or sharp picks can push the broken piece deeper or scratch the cylinder pins. That can turn a clean extraction into a damaged ignition. If the key is stuck, it's better to leave it alone until we can work on it with the right tools.

Should I try to start the car again after the key snaps?

No. If part of the key is still in the ignition, another turn can wedge it tighter or break the cylinder. If the vehicle is in a marina lot or another tight spot, we can come to where it sits and handle the extraction before anything else gets damaged.

What if my ignition is worn out, not just the key broken off?

That happens a lot. If the cylinder feels loose, gritty, or keeps catching, the broken key may be a symptom of wear in the lock itself. We can remove the key fragment first, then tell you whether the ignition still looks usable or whether it's starting to fail.
