Ignition Repair in Charlestown - common questions

How does ignition repair usually work when my key is sticking in Charlestown?

We start by checking whether the trouble is the key, the ignition cylinder, or both. If the cylinder is worn but still serviceable, we repair or rebuild the worn parts. If it's badly damaged, replacement may be the better route. The goal is smooth turning, clean key removal, and no force needed.

What should I have ready when I call about a rough ignition?

Have the vehicle year, make, and model handy, plus a clear description of what the key is doing. If the key sticks only in the ignition, or also in the door, say that. If there's a trailer attached or the vehicle is stuck in a lot, mention that too so the job is set up the right way.

What happens if I keep driving with the ignition acting up?

The biggest risk is getting stranded when the cylinder finally gives out. A worn ignition can also damage the key, break part of it off inside, or make the steering and shift controls harder to deal with. If the problem is getting worse, it's better to address it before it turns into a bigger lockout or towing problem.

Should I try to repair the ignition cylinder or replace it?

That depends on the wear and the damage. If the cylinder is only rough from normal wear, repair may be enough. If the key has been forced, the cylinder is damaged inside, or a broken key is trapped in it, replacement can be the cleaner fix. We'll look at what the lock is doing before making that call.

Can you still help if the key broke off and the car is parked near the waterfront?

Yes, if the broken piece is still in the ignition or the cylinder can be accessed safely, that's a common repair. If the lock has been badly damaged by forcing the key, more parts may need attention. Either way, it helps to leave the key where it is and avoid prying at it, since that can make the damage worse.
