Key Extraction From Ignition in Chesapeake City - common questions

How do you remove a broken key from my ignition without damaging it?

We start by checking how the piece is sitting in the cylinder and whether the ignition is still aligned. Then we use extraction tools made for broken keys, not improvised picks or force. The goal is to pull the piece out cleanly and avoid scratching the cylinder, steering column trim, or the keyway.

What should I have ready before you come out to my car in Chesapeake City?

Have the vehicle make, model, and year ready, and tell us whether the key is broken off flush, sticking out, or partly turning. If you have a spare key, keep it with you. It also helps to know if the ignition has been hard to turn lately, since that can point to wear inside the lock.

Can trying to pull the broken piece out myself make the ignition worse?

Yes. A screwdriver, tweezers, or a stiff wire can push the broken piece deeper or bend parts inside the cylinder. If the key is already jammed, more force can turn a simple extraction into an ignition repair. It's better to leave it alone until we can take a careful look.

If the key broke in the ignition, do I need a new ignition or just the broken piece removed?

Not always. Sometimes the key is the only problem and the cylinder still works once the broken piece is out. Other times the ignition is worn, and that's why the key snapped in the first place. We check both so you can decide whether extraction alone is enough or whether the lock needs more work.

What if my key snapped off while the car is parked near the waterfront and I can't move it?

That's a common situation here, and we can work on the vehicle where it sits. If the ignition is locked with part of the key inside, we focus on safe extraction first so the car can be started or shut down properly again. The main thing is not to keep forcing the broken key.
