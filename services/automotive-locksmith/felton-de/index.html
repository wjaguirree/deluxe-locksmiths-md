Automotive locksmith in Felton - common questions

If my car key is lost near the Felton town grid, what do we do first?

We start by confirming the vehicle's make, model, and year, then we identify whether it needs a cut key, a programmed transponder, or a fob. That helps us choose the right equipment before touching the car. If you have registration or proof of ownership handy, it can help us verify the vehicle and move the job along.

What should I have ready if the keys are locked in my truck at a grain elevator?

Have the vehicle location, the year, make, and model, and tell us whether the truck has a standard key, a transponder key, or a push-button fob. If you know the key is still inside, say that up front. We can work with the vehicle as it sits and focus on the access method that fits that truck.

Can forcing the key or trying the ignition over and over make things worse?

Yes. A worn key can twist farther than it should, and repeated force can snap it in the cylinder or damage the ignition parts. If the key is hard to turn, stops halfway, or feels gritty, it's better to stop and have us look at it. That can save the ignition from turning into a larger repair.

If I need a new key for my car in Felton, should we copy the old one or start from scratch?

If you still have a working key, duplication is often the simplest path because it gives you a spare and keeps the original on hand. If the only key is lost, broken, or no longer works, we have to start from the vehicle's lock or security data instead. The right choice depends on what the car will still accept.

What if the key is broken off in the ignition and the car is parked outside my house on US-13?

We can usually handle that on site. First we remove the broken piece without damaging the cylinder. Then we check whether the ignition still works with a proper key or whether the cylinder is worn enough to need repair or replacement. That order prevents us from pulling apart the ignition before we know what failed.
