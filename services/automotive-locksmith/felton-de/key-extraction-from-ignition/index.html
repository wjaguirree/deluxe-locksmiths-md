Key Extraction From Ignition in Felton - common questions

Can you pull a broken key out of my ignition without taking the whole column apart?

Often, yes. If the fragment is accessible, it can usually be removed with the right extraction tools and a careful approach. The main concern is avoiding damage to the cylinder, trim, or wiring. If the key is jammed deep or the ignition is already worn, more disassembly may be needed.

What should I have ready before you come out to my place in Felton?

Have the vehicle make, model, and year ready, plus the exact spot where it's parked. If the keys are locked in the truck or the broken piece is still partly visible, mention that. It also helps to know whether the ignition turns at all, because that changes how the job is handled.

Is there a risk that the ignition will get worse when the key is pulled?

There is if the wrong tool or too much force is used. A proper extraction is meant to remove the broken piece without chewing up the cylinder. If the ignition is already worn, the key may have snapped because of an underlying problem, so it's smart to check for damage before using it again.

If my truck keys are locked inside at a grain elevator, should I try to get the broken key out first or handle the lockout first?

Start with whichever problem keeps you from moving the vehicle safely. If the ignition key is broken and the truck can't be started, the extraction comes first. If the keys are locked inside and the ignition is fine, the lockout comes first. Sometimes both issues need to be handled in sequence.

What if the key broke off because the ignition turns stiff in colder weather?

That can happen, and it usually points to wear inside the cylinder, the key itself, or both. After the broken piece is removed, the ignition should be checked for binding before another key is used. If it still feels rough, a fresh key or repair may be the better next step than forcing the old one.
