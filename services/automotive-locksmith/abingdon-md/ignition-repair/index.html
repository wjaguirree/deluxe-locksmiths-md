Why Ignitions Fail

A lot of drivers try to push through a rough ignition with the wrong shortcut. Some spray in whatever lubricant is handy, then keep forcing the key. That can wash debris deeper into the cylinder and make the tumblers bind harder. Others blame the key and file it down, bend it to "help" the lock, or switch to a copied key that was never cut cleanly in the first place. Those quick fixes can buy a little time, but they also wear the ignition faster and can leave you stuck with a key that won't turn at all. In busy parts of Abingdon, where people are heading on and off I-95 or running errands near Box Hill, that kind of wear shows up fast because the car gets used hard and parked on the move all day.

We see another common shortcut when someone keeps yanking on a key that already feels wrong. That often snaps the tip off inside the cylinder, which turns a repair into a bigger extraction job. Some drivers also swap in used parts from a salvage yard without checking wear patterns or key code match. That can create a mismatch between the cylinder, the key, and the steering lock, so the problem comes right back. If the ignition is sticking, turning rough, or catching at one point in the travel, the smart move is to have us inspect the cylinder, key cut, and related parts together. Ignition Repair in Abingdon - common questions

My key is turning rough in my Abingdon car. How do you tell if it's the key or the ignition?

We look at the wear pattern on the key and the way the cylinder reacts when it's inserted and turned. A worn key can mimic a failing ignition, but if the key catches in the same spot every time, the cylinder is usually involved. Sometimes both are worn, and the fix has to address both sides so the problem doesn't keep coming back.

What should I have ready before you come out to repair the ignition in Harford County?

Have the vehicle year, make, and model, and be ready to describe the symptom as clearly as you can. It helps to know whether the key still turns at all, whether it gets stuck in one position, and whether the steering wheel is locked. If you have a spare key, mention that too.

Is it risky to keep forcing a sticky ignition if my car still starts most of the time?

Yes. Forcing it can bend the key, damage the tumblers, or snap the key off in the cylinder. Once that happens, the repair usually gets more involved. A failing ignition can also leave the car stuck in the off or accessory position, which is a bad place to find out you have a bigger issue.

Do I need a whole new ignition, or can you repair just the cylinder on my Abingdon vehicle?

That depends on the damage. If the cylinder is worn but the housing and related parts are still sound, repair or rekeying may be enough. If internal parts are broken or the mechanism is badly seized, replacement may be the better route. We'll sort that out based on the actual failure, not guesswork.

What if my fob works but the key won't turn, or the key works but the remote doesn't?

Those are separate problems. A working remote doesn't rule out a worn ignition cylinder, and a good ignition doesn't fix a dead fob battery or a failing remote. If the key is rough in the cylinder, the ignition still needs attention even if the locking and unlocking buttons seem fine.
