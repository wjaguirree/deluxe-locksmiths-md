What to Check First

Before we touch the ignition, there are a few things worth checking on your end. Make sure the shifter is fully in park and the steering wheel isn't locked hard against the column. If the key is still partly visible, don't twist it, pull on it, or try to fish it out with pliers, screwdrivers, or magnets. That usually pushes the broken piece deeper or damages the cylinder. If you're stopped near I-95 exit 77 or along MD-24, it also helps to note whether the key snapped during normal use, while turning, or after the battery went low, since that tells us a lot about what's binding inside.

If you can do so safely, gather the vehicle's year, make, model, and whether it's a standard metal key, a chip key, or a fob-style key with a hidden blade. If the key broke because the ignition is worn, we may also want to know whether the key has been sticking lately, turning rough, or needing extra force. What to leave alone is just as important. Don't spray oil, graphite, glue, or random cleaners into the ignition. Don't keep cycling the key to see if it will move. Key Extraction From Ignition in Abingdon - common questions

How do you remove a broken key from my ignition without damaging it?

We inspect how the key failed, then use extraction tools sized for the cylinder. If part of the blade is exposed, we can often grab it directly. If it is buried deeper, we work it out without prying on the steering column. The aim is to remove the broken piece and leave the ignition usable.

What should I have ready before you come to my car in Abingdon?

Have the vehicle parked safely, the transmission in park if it applies, and any remaining key pieces you may have found. If you have a spare key or the fob, keep it handy. It also helps to know whether the key was turning normally before it broke, because that points us toward the right fix.

Could forcing the key make the ignition worse?

Yes. If the blade is already cracked or the cylinder is worn, extra force can snap off more metal or damage the internal wafers. That can turn a simple extraction into a larger repair. If the key feels wrong, stops partway in, or binds, it is better to stop and let us look at it.

How do I know if it is the key, the ignition cylinder, or the fob?

A broken blade means a key extraction job. A key that inserts but will not turn often points to a worn cylinder or steering lock tension. If the key turns but the vehicle does not respond, the issue may be electronic. We check the pattern before we touch anything, so we do not chase the wrong problem.

My key broke in the ignition at a store near I-95 exit 77. Can you still help on site?

Yes. This is the kind of job we usually handle where the vehicle sits, whether that is a driveway, a lot, or a curbside stop. We bring the tools needed for extraction and for the follow-up check, so we can tell whether the ignition is still sound after the broken piece comes out.
