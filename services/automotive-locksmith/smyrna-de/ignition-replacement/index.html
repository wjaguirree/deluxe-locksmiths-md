Ignition Replacement in Smyrna - common questions

How do you tell if the ignition in my Smyrna car needs replacement or just a key adjustment?

A worn key can mimic a bad ignition, so the first step is checking the cut, the cylinder, and how the key moves in the lock. If the key works in the doors but not the ignition, or it turns only when jiggled, the cylinder may be worn. If the issue is electrical, the switch behind the cylinder may be the real problem.

What should I have ready when you come out to replace my ignition?

Have the vehicle year, make, and model, plus proof that it's your car. If you still have a working key, bring it. If the key is stuck in the ignition or locked in the car, say that up front. Those details help determine whether the cylinder can be matched to the key you already use.

Can a bad ignition leave me thinking the starter failed instead?

Yes. A worn ignition switch can keep power from reaching the starter or can make the dash act like the car is dead when the battery is fine. That's why the ignition hardware has to be checked before parts get replaced at random. The symptom may look electrical, but the worn piece can still be the cylinder or switch assembly.

If I want to keep one key for my doors and ignition, is that possible?

Often, yes. On many vehicles the replacement can be keyed to match the key you already carry, or the ignition cylinder can be rebuilt around the existing key code. It depends on the vehicle and on how worn the old parts are. If the cylinder is too damaged, matching may still be possible with the right replacement parts.

What if my key is locked in the car and the ignition won't turn in the same visit?

That can happen, especially when the lock cylinder is worn or the key is bent. The door can usually be opened first so the vehicle can be accessed, then the ignition hardware can be inspected. If the cylinder is beyond repair, it's replaced, and the new part is set up so the keying issue doesn't come right back.
