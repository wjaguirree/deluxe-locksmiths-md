Key Extraction From Ignition in Smyrna - common questions

How do you remove a broken key from my ignition without damaging the car?

We use extraction tools made for the cylinder and work carefully to avoid scratching the housing or forcing the ignition. The goal is to pull the broken piece straight out, then check that the cylinder still turns smoothly. If the key broke because the lock is worn or binding, we'll let you know what else needs attention.

What should I have ready before you come out to my car in Smyrna?

Have the vehicle make, model, and year ready, plus the exact location and a way to prove you're allowed to have the car opened or worked on. If you have the remaining half of the key, keep it with you. It also helps to tell us whether the key is stuck, snapped flush, or already falling into the ignition.

Is there a risk the ignition will stop working after the broken key is removed?

It can happen if the cylinder was already worn or the key broke because the lock was binding. Removal itself doesn't have to ruin the ignition, but a badly damaged cylinder may still need repair or replacement. We check the condition of the lock before we leave so you know what shape it's in.

Should I ask for key extraction only, or do I need a new key too?

If the key broke because it was bent, thin from wear, or hard to turn, a new key is often the better next step. If the broken piece came out cleanly and you have a spare that works, extraction alone may be enough. We can talk through both options after we see how the ignition is behaving.

What if my key snapped off and I'm also locked out near the Duck Creek side of town?

That happens more often than people think. We can handle the lockout and the ignition issue as separate parts of the same job. If the broken key is still in the ignition, we'll address that first so the car can be opened and operated without making the cylinder worse.
