Key Extraction From Ignition in Delaware City - common questions

How do you get a broken key out of an ignition without damaging it?

We use small extraction tools made for ignition work and work from the shape of the broken piece, not brute force. The goal is to catch the fragment and slide it out without scratching the cylinder or bending internal parts. If the key is stuck deeper or the ignition is already worn, we may need to service the cylinder after the piece is removed.

What should I do with my car before you get to Delaware City?

Stop trying to start it and don't keep turning the key. If the engine is already off, leave it off. Keep the shifter in park, gather both pieces of the key if you have them, and make room around the driver's door so we can work. If the car is in a tight spot, like a ferry area or parking lot, tell us that up front.

Is it a bad sign if the key snapped because the ignition felt stiff first?

Yes, that often points to wear inside the key, the cylinder, or both. A stiff ignition can mean the key is starting to catch on worn wafers or a damaged cylinder. If we only remove the broken piece and the underlying problem stays, the next key can break too. That's why we check how the ignition feels after extraction.

Should I ask for key extraction or a full ignition replacement?

If the key broke but the cylinder still turns normally, extraction may be enough. If the key jammed, the cylinder feels rough, or there's obvious damage, a replacement may make more sense. We'll look at the condition of the ignition first and tell you which path is safer for that vehicle instead of guessing.

What if my keys are locked in a truck at the ferry landing and the broken key is in the ignition too?

That can happen. We can deal with both problems, but we need to know exactly what's inside the truck before we start. Don't try to fish keys out through the ignition area or force a door open if the vehicle is locked up tight. Tell us the vehicle make, model, and whether the key is broken, locked in, or both.
