Key Extraction From Ignition in Newark - common questions

What actually happens when you pull a broken key out of an ignition?

We first confirm how the key is broken and whether any part is still holding the cylinder up. Then we choose the safest extraction method for that ignition design. The order matters because rushed pulling can push the fragment deeper or damage the lock face. After removal, we test the ignition and check whether the key or cylinder still has a wear issue.

What should I have ready before you come to my car in Newark?

Have the car parked safely, the steering wheel unlocked if possible, and any remaining key pieces you can find. If the vehicle won't move, tell us that up front. It also helps to know the make, model, and year, since ignition layouts vary a lot. If the problem started after a key was bent or hard to turn, mention that too.

Can trying to dig the broken piece out myself make the ignition worse?

Yes. A paper clip, pick, or screwdriver can scratch the cylinder, bend internal parts, or push the fragment farther in. That can turn a clean extraction into an ignition repair. If the key is already stuck, it's usually better to leave it alone and let us remove it with the right tools and access.

If the key broke, do I need a new ignition or just the broken piece removed?

Not always a new ignition. If the cylinder isn't damaged and the key broke because of wear or a bad angle, extraction may be enough. If the key has been sticking for a while, or the cylinder feels rough after the break, we may need to discuss repair or replacement. We'll look at the actual condition before suggesting the next step.

What if my key broke off and I'm parked near Main Street or on campus?

That's a common call in Newark, especially around student housing and busy parking areas. We can work where the car is as long as it's safe and accessible. If the key broke while the engine was running, or if the ignition won't return to the off position, tell us that right away so we can approach it carefully.
