Ignition Repair in Newark - common questions

Why does my ignition feel rough when I turn the key in Newark?

Usually the cylinder is worn, the key is worn, or both are changing shape together. We check how the key enters, turns, and comes back out, because forcing it can make the damage worse. If the problem is still in the repair range, we work on the cylinder before it turns into a no-start situation.

What should I have ready before you work on my ignition?

Have the vehicle, the key you normally use, and any spare key you still have. If the steering wheel is locked or the key only works part of the time, tell us that up front. Small details help us separate a worn ignition from a key problem or a steering-related issue.

Can spraying lubricant into the ignition make things worse?

Yes. The wrong spray can collect grit, gum up the cylinder, or hide a problem that needs real repair. People often try that first and then wonder why the key feels even rougher. We look at the wear itself, not just the symptom, so the fix actually matches the fault.

If my key is worn, do I need ignition repair or a new key?

Sometimes the key is the main problem, and sometimes the cylinder is worn too. If the key only works after a lot of fiddling, both pieces may be contributing. We check both before deciding, because replacing the wrong part leaves you with the same trouble.

What if my car won't turn at all after sitting near the University of Delaware area?

That can be a worn cylinder, a locked steering wheel, or a key that no longer matches the ignition well enough. Don't keep forcing it. A no-turn ignition can damage the cylinder fast, and once that happens the repair usually gets more involved.
