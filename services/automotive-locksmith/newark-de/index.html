Automotive locksmith in Newark - common questions

How does a lost car key replacement work if my car is parked in Newark?

We identify the vehicle, confirm the key type, and check whether it uses a transponder or fob. From there, we cut and program a replacement where the car is parked. If there's a broken key or an ignition problem too, we handle that first so the new key is matched to a working system.

What should I have ready before you come out to my car?

Have the vehicle make, model, and year ready, plus proof that you're allowed to have the car opened or serviced. If you still have a damaged key or a spare that partially works, keep it with you. That can help us determine whether the issue is the cut, the chip, the fob, or the ignition.

I lost my only key near the University of Delaware. Is the car still recoverable?

In many cases, yes. A lost-only-key situation is common on college streets and parking lots. We can usually make a replacement key or fob from the vehicle itself. If the locks or ignition have been changed before, or if the car has unusual security features, we'll need to work from what the vehicle allows.

Should I replace the ignition or just have the key re-cut?

If the key is worn but the ignition is sound, a new key or duplication may solve the problem. If the cylinder sticks, binds, or only works with force, ignition repair may be the better answer. We check the key, cylinder, and wear together so you're not replacing parts that still work.

Can you help if the key broke off in the ignition or door lock?

Yes. We can extract the broken piece and then test the lock or ignition for damage. If the cylinder is still usable, we can make or program a new key afterward. If the break also damaged the internal parts, we'll talk through the repair or replacement that fits the condition of the vehicle.
