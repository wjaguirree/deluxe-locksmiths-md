Ignition Replacement in Newark - common questions

How do we tell if my car needs an ignition replacement or just a new key?

We look at how the key behaves in the cylinder and whether the problem follows the key or stays with the ignition. If a spare key works better, the key may be the issue. If every key binds in the same spot, the ignition is likely worn or damaged. That check keeps us from replacing the wrong part.

What should I have ready when we come out to my car in Newark?

Have the vehicle accessible, the registration if you have it, and any working keys you still have. If the steering wheel is locked or the key is stuck, don't force it. That can break parts inside the ignition. If the car is in a lot near the University of Delaware or on a side street, make sure we can reach it safely.

Can a bad ignition damage my starter or battery if I keep trying it?

Repeated forcing can cause more than one problem. A worn ignition can keep the key from turning cleanly, and that can lead to extra wear on the cylinder, the key, or the steering lock parts. It can also leave you stuck in a parking spot trying the same key over and over. It's better to stop before the parts bind harder.

Should we replace the ignition or try to keep my existing key?

If the vehicle allows it, we try to key the new ignition to the key you already use. That keeps things simpler for daily driving. In other cases, the lock design or the condition of the old parts means a direct match is not practical. We'll tell you which route makes sense once we see the vehicle.

What if my key turns, but the car still won't start?

That may not be an ignition lock problem at all. The issue could be in the ignition switch, the actuator, the battery, or something else in the starting system. We sort out whether the key and cylinder are doing their job first. If they are, we don't push an unnecessary ignition replacement just because the symptom looks similar.
