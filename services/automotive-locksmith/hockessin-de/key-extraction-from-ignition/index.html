Key Extraction From Ignition in Hockessin - common questions

How do you get a broken key out of my ignition without damaging the car?

We start by checking how the key broke and how much of it is still visible. From there, we use extraction tools made for ignition work, not improvised force. The aim is to pull the broken piece straight out and leave the cylinder, steering column, and trim in place if they're still serviceable.

What should I have ready when I call about a broken ignition key in Hockessin?

Have the vehicle year, make, and model ready, and tell us whether the key is snapped off, stuck, or not turning at all. If it's a fob-equipped vehicle, mention that too. If you can safely get to the car and share whether the key broke on the way home, at work, or in a driveway, that helps us plan the job.

Can trying to turn the key harder make the ignition worse?

Yes. Forcing a stuck key can bend the broken piece deeper into the cylinder or damage the wafers inside the ignition. It can also wear the key harder and make extraction more difficult. If the key is already caught, it's better to leave it alone until it's handled with the right tools.

If my ignition is worn, should I replace it or just have the key extracted?

If the ignition still turns smoothly after the broken key comes out, extraction may be all you need. If it sticks, grinds, or keeps catching, the cylinder may be worn enough that replacement makes more sense. We can usually tell which direction is practical once the key is out and the lock can be tested.

My car uses a fob with a hidden key blade. Is that the same kind of job?

Not exactly. The backup blade can break or stick even when the electronics are the real problem. We handle both the mechanical key side and the ignition or start system side, but the process depends on the vehicle. On some models, the fix is just extraction; on others, we need to look at the fob and the lock together.
