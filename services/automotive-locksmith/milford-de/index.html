Automotive locksmith in Milford - common questions

If my car key stopped working in Milford, how do you figure out whether it's the key, the fob, or the ignition?

We start with the vehicle's symptoms and the type of key it uses. If the door won't unlock, the fob may need programming or a battery replacement. If the key turns in the door but not the ignition, the issue may be the ignition cylinder. If a transponder chip isn't being recognized, the key itself may need to be programmed or replaced.

What should I have ready before you come to my car?

Have the vehicle make, model, and year ready, plus any working key or fob you still have. If the key is broken, keep both pieces. It also helps to know whether the car uses a metal key, chip key, or push-button start. That saves time once the work starts and helps avoid bringing the wrong replacement.

I'm worried about scratching my door or damaging the ignition by trying to fix it myself. What should I not do?

Don't force the key, twist hard on a sticky ignition, or dig around inside the lock with a screwdriver, knife, or pick. Those moves can break off more metal, damage the cylinder, or bend the door hardware. If the key is stuck, leave it where it is and describe exactly what happened so the repair can be handled cleanly.

Do I need a new key, a copied key, or a new fob for my car?

If you still have one working key, duplication may be enough. If the original is missing or broken, car key replacement or new fob creation may be the better path. For vehicles with chips, transponder key programming is often part of the job. The right choice depends on whether the car uses a standard metal key, a chip key, or a smart fob.

Can a key break off in the ignition and still be removed without replacing the whole ignition?

Often, yes. A broken key can sometimes be extracted from the ignition without replacing the cylinder, as long as the lock itself isn't badly damaged. If the ignition is worn, seized, or already failing, ignition repair or ignition replacement may be the better answer. The condition of the cylinder decides that, not guesswork.
