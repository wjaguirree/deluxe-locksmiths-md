Choosing the Right Fix

When an ignition acts up, we don't jump straight to replacement. We look at what's actually failing: a worn key, a sticky cylinder, damaged wafers, or an electrical problem in the switch assembly. If the key is still reading correctly but the cylinder is binding, a repair or rekey may solve it. If the key won't turn, the cylinder has internal wear, or the housing is broken, replacement is usually the better path. We also check whether the issue is mechanical or tied to the vehicle's electrical side, because those are handled differently and the wrong call can leave you with the same problem after the work is done.

Where possible, we key the new ignition to the key you already carry so you're not adding another key to your ring. Ignition Replacement in Milford - common questions

How does ignition replacement usually work on a car in Milford?

First, the vehicle gets checked to confirm the problem is in the ignition assembly and not the battery, starter, or key. Then the failed parts are removed and replaced with hardware that matches the vehicle's setup. If the model allows it, the new ignition can often be keyed to the key you already use.

What should I have ready before you replace my ignition?

Have the vehicle make, model, and year handy, plus any key you still have for it. If the car is stuck at a work lot, apartment, or hospital parking area, make sure you can point out the exact vehicle. If the steering wheel is locked or the key is broken off, mention that up front.

Can a worn key cause the ignition to fail even if the switch itself is still good?

Yes. A worn key can make it seem like the ignition is failing when the real issue is that the key no longer matches the cylinder well. That can lead to hard turning, sticking, or intermittent starting. A proper check should look at both the key and the ignition before replacing parts.

Should I replace the ignition, or can it just be rekeyed?

If the cylinder is worn but still serviceable, rekeying may be enough on some vehicles. If the switch, cylinder, or related parts are damaged, replacement is the better fix. The right choice depends on the vehicle's design and the condition of the parts, especially on newer models with tighter security systems.

What if my car starts only sometimes or the key turns but nothing happens?

That can point to a worn ignition switch, a bad cylinder, or an electrical issue in the starting circuit. A key that turns without starting doesn't always mean the whole ignition has failed. The cause has to be traced before parts are replaced, especially if the vehicle has been acting up off and on.
