How do you remove a broken key from the ignition without damaging the car?

The broken piece is removed with lock tools made for that type of cylinder, not by prying at the steering column. The key is to keep the fragment from shifting deeper while it's lifted out. After that, the ignition is checked for wear, burrs, or damage that caused the break so the next key doesn't fail the same way.

What should I do with my car in Milford before anyone gets there if the key snapped off in the ignition?

Stop turning the key and don't keep testing it. If the broken piece is still partly visible, leave it alone. Have the spare key, vehicle make and model, and any signs of prior sticking ready. If the car is in a tight spot, make sure there's room to work at the driver's door and ignition area.

I'm worried the ignition will be ruined if the broken key comes out the wrong way. Is that a real risk?

Yes. Forcing the fragment with the wrong tool can bend internal parts, scratch the cylinder, or leave metal inside the lock. That's why a careful extraction matters. If the key was already worn or bent, the lock may need follow-up repair after the piece is removed so it doesn't keep catching.

Not always. If the cylinder is still operating smoothly after the fragment is removed, a new key may be enough.
