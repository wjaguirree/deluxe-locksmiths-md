Ignition Repair in Milford - common questions

What actually fails inside an ignition cylinder when the key starts sticking?

The tiny pins, wafers, and springs inside the cylinder wear down or get gummed up. That keeps the key from lining up smoothly, so the turn feels rough or won't complete. Sometimes the key itself is worn too. We check both the key and the cylinder before deciding whether repair or replacement makes sense.

Do I need to bring anything with me before you work on my ignition in Milford?

Bring the vehicle, the key you normally use, and any spare key you have. If the key has been bending or copied from a worn original, that helps explain the problem. If the steering wheel is locked or the key only turns partway, leave it as you found it so we can inspect the parts as they are.

Can forcing a stuck key damage more than the ignition?

Yes. Forcing it can bend the key, break pieces inside the cylinder, or stress the ignition switch and steering lock. Once that happens, the repair can get more involved. If the key is hanging up, it's better to stop and have the hardware checked than to keep cranking on it.

How do you decide between repairing the cylinder and replacing it?

If the cylinder body is still solid and the wear is mostly inside, repair may be enough. If the housing is cracked, the key profile is too worn, or the switch and cylinder are both damaged, replacement is the cleaner fix. We look at how the parts fit together before choosing the route that matches the problem.

What if my car starts sometimes but the key still feels rough when I turn it?

That's often an early warning that the cylinder is wearing out. It may still start today, but the internal parts can keep degrading until the key stops turning at all. In that case, the goal is to repair or replace the worn hardware before it leaves you stuck with a locked ignition.
