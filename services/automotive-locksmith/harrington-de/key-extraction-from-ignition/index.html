Key Extraction From Ignition in Harrington - common questions

If my key snapped in the ignition while I was parked near the fairgrounds, what actually gets done?

The broken piece is removed from the ignition cylinder first. After that, the lock is checked to see whether it still turns cleanly and whether the key broke because of wear or a deeper ignition problem. If the cylinder is still sound, it may only need cleaning or service. If it's damaged, another fix may be needed.

What should I have ready when I call about a broken ignition key in Harrington?

Have the vehicle year, make, and model ready, plus whether the key is broken off flush or still visible. It also helps to know if the steering wheel is locked and whether the ignition has been sticking for a while. That information helps sort out whether extraction alone is likely or if the cylinder may need more work.

Can trying to turn the key make the ignition worse?

Yes. If the key is bent, cracked, or partly broken, extra force can shove the broken piece deeper or damage the cylinder wafers. That can turn a simple extraction into a bigger repair. If the key won't move normally, it's better to stop and have it handled carefully.

How do I know whether my ignition needs repair, rekeying, or replacement?

Repair is for an ignition that still works once the broken key is removed. Rekeying is useful if the locks need to match again after key wear or a lost key issue. Replacement is for cylinders that are worn out, scored, or no longer tracking the key correctly. The condition of the lock decides the path, not just the broken key.

What if the key broke off deep inside and I'm stuck in a lot with a crowd around me?

That's still a normal extraction job, but it has to be done carefully so the broken piece doesn't get shoved farther in. The vehicle should stay off, and nobody should keep fiddling with the ignition. If the cylinder is damaged, the lock may need more than extraction. The main concern is protecting the column and getting the car usable again.
