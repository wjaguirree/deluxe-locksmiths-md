Repair, Rekey, or Replace

When an ignition starts sticking, we first figure out what's actually worn. Sometimes the key is the problem, sometimes the cylinder is dry or dirty, and sometimes the internal wafers are damaged enough that the key won't turn cleanly anymore. If the wear is light and the lock body is still sound, we can repair the ignition and get it turning the way it should again. If the cylinder is still serviceable but the tumblers no longer match your key the way they used to, rekeying can be the better move. If the housing is broken, the key has been getting harder to turn for a while, or the parts are worn past a clean repair, replacement is the safer call.

Ignition Repair in Harrington - common questions

How do you tell whether the ignition in my Harrington car needs repair or replacement?

We look at how the key enters, turns, and returns, then check the wear inside the cylinder and on the key itself. If the issue is dirt, worn parts, or a sticking cylinder that can still be serviced, repair may be enough. If the internal damage is too severe, replacement is the better call.

What should I have ready when I call about a stuck ignition near the fairgrounds?

Have the vehicle year, make, and model ready, along with whether the key is sticking, not turning at all, or coming out rough. If the car is parked in a tight spot, mention that too. It also helps to know if the steering wheel is locked or if the key was recently copied.

Can forcing the key damage the ignition if it already feels rough?

Yes. Forcing a worn ignition can bend the key, break off a piece inside the cylinder, or wear the internal parts further. Once that happens, a simple repair can turn into a harder job. If the key is fighting you, it's better to stop and have it checked before it seizes.

Should I try a quick fix or go straight to a proper ignition repair?

A quick fix may get the cylinder turning for the moment, but it usually doesn't remove the wear that caused the problem. If the ignition is already sticking in Harrington traffic or in a lot where you can't afford to gamble, a proper repair is the safer choice because it addresses the worn parts directly.

What if my car only sticks when it's parked for a while, not every time?

That can still point to a worn ignition cylinder or a key that's not matching the cylinder well anymore. Intermittent trouble is common with parts that are starting to fail. It may feel minor at first, but if the pattern is already there, the problem usually doesn't fix itself and can get worse without warning.
