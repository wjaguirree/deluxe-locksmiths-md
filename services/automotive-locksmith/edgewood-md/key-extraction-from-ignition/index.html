Key Extraction From Ignition in Edgewood - common questions

How do you get a broken key out of an ignition without damaging it?

The broken piece is removed with extraction tools matched to the lock, not by prying at the steering column. The goal is to lift the fragment straight out and leave the ignition usable if the cylinder is still in good shape. If the lock is bent, worn, or already failing, that gets checked before anyone tries to turn it again.

What should I have ready before help comes out for a stuck key in Edgewood?

Have the vehicle accessible, the doors unlocked if you can do that safely, and the make, model, and year ready to share. If the key broke, keep any remaining pieces. If the car is in a parking lot near the Edgewood MARC station or elsewhere in town, make sure it's in a spot where work can be done safely.

Can forcing the key make the ignition worse?

Yes. Turning, wiggling, or pulling hard can bend the broken piece deeper into the cylinder or damage the wafers inside the lock. That can turn a straightforward extraction into a larger ignition problem. If the key is already stuck, it's better to stop and have it handled carefully.

If the key comes out, do I always need a new ignition?

No. If the cylinder is still working smoothly, extraction may be the only repair needed. But if the key snapped because the lock is worn, sticky, or hard to turn, there may be a bigger issue behind the break. The next step depends on what the lock looks and feels like after the piece is removed.

What if the key broke off while I was locked out in a parking lot after the train left?

That happens, and it changes the job a bit because the vehicle may be stuck where it sits. The focus is still on getting the broken piece out cleanly and opening the car without adding damage. Once the ignition is cleared, the lock can be checked for wear so you know whether the same key can keep being used.
