Ignition Repair in Elkton - common questions

How do you tell if the ignition cylinder is worn out or if it's something else?

We look at how the key behaves in the cylinder, whether the key itself is damaged, and whether the problem shows up in the switch side of the ignition. A key that rocks, binds, or only works after a lot of wiggling usually points to worn internal parts. If the key turns but the vehicle still won't start, the issue may be deeper in the ignition system.

What should I have ready before you work on my ignition in Elkton?

Have the vehicle make, model, year, and the key you normally use if you still have it. If the car has an aftermarket remote or push-button add-on, mention that too. It also helps to know whether the key has been hard to insert, hard to turn, or hard to remove, because that tells us what part of the ignition is failing.

Can forcing a sticky key make the ignition worse?

Yes. Forcing a rough key can bend the key blade, damage the wafers inside the cylinder, or break the lock body further. Once that happens, the repair can become more involved. If the key starts hanging up, it's better to stop using force and have the ignition looked at before the parts chew themselves up.

Should the ignition cylinder be repaired or replaced?

If the wear is light and the cylinder still matches the key correctly, repair may be enough. If the tumblers, housing, or switch components are badly worn, replacement is usually the better choice. We'll look at the condition of the parts and the way the key fits, then explain which fix makes sense for that vehicle.

What if my key turns but the car still won't start?

That can happen when the ignition cylinder is working but the electrical switch behind it is failing. It can also be a sign of a different issue, like a worn key, a starter problem, or a vehicle security fault. We sort out where the failure is before replacing parts that don't need to be replaced.
