Ignition Repair in Bear - common questions

How do you figure out whether my ignition is bad or the key is the problem?

We start by checking how the key turns, whether the cylinder binds, and whether the issue changes with a spare key. If the spare works better, the key may be worn. If both keys act the same, the ignition cylinder or a related part is more likely the cause. That keeps us from replacing the wrong part.

What should I have ready before you work on my car ignition in Bear?

Have the vehicle's make, model, and year ready, plus any working keys or fobs you still have. If the steering wheel is locked, if the shifter is stuck, or if the key won't come out, mention that too. Those details help us know whether the problem is in the cylinder, the key, or another lock-related part.

Can a bad ignition damage my key or leave me stuck with the wheel locked?

Yes. A worn cylinder can chew up a key, make the wheel feel locked, or keep the key from turning all the way. Forcing it can make the wear worse. We look for the cause before it turns into a bigger no-start problem or leaves you unable to shut the vehicle off cleanly.

Is it better to repair the ignition or replace it on a newer vehicle?

That depends on what failed. If the cylinder is worn but still repairable, repair can be the right call. If the internal parts are too damaged or the ignition system is tied to an electronic issue, replacement may be needed. We explain the difference so you can make the call based on the actual condition.

What if my fob worked yesterday but now the ignition won't respond at all?

That can happen on push-start and key-based systems alike. The issue may be the fob, the key itself, the ignition switch, or the vehicle's immobilizer system. We check the parts in order so we don't chase the wrong problem. If the fob is the source, replacing the ignition won't fix it.
