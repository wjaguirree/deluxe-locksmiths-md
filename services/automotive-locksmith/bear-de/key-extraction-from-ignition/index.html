Key Extraction From Ignition in Bear - common questions

How do you remove a broken key from my ignition without damaging the car?

We use ignition-safe extraction tools to grip the broken piece and pull it out without prying on the steering column. If the key is still partly visible, that can help. If the cylinder is already worn or the key is twisted, we stop before forcing it and talk through the next step.

What should I have ready before you come out for a broken ignition key in Bear?

Have the vehicle make, model, and year handy, plus any signs you noticed before the key broke. If you've got the spare key, that helps us compare it. It's also useful to know whether the wheel is locked, the key was hard to turn, or the break happened while starting or shutting the car off.

Can trying to turn the key harder make the ignition worse?

Yes. Forcing it can bend the broken piece deeper into the cylinder or damage the tumblers inside. That can turn a simple extraction into a larger ignition repair. If the key feels stuck or gritty, stop using it and let us inspect the cylinder before more parts get damaged.

If my key broke, do I need a new ignition or just the broken piece removed?

Not always a new ignition. If the cylinder is otherwise healthy, extraction may be enough. If the key was already sticking, wobbling, or hard to remove, the ignition may be worn and need more work. We look at how the key failed and tell you which option fits the problem.

My fob died in a Fox Run parking lot and I had to use the metal key. What if that key is stuck too?

That can happen when the emergency blade is worn or the ignition is already finicky. We can work on the stuck key and check whether the blade needs replacement or the ignition has an internal wear issue. If the fob battery failed first, it's smart to replace that too so you don't get stuck again.
