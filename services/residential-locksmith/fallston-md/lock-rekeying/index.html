Lock Rekeying in Fallston - common questions

How does rekeying a house in Fallston work?

The lock is taken apart, and the pins inside the cylinder are changed so the old key no longer works. You keep the existing hardware on the door, but you get a new key setup for the home. It's a practical choice when the lock body is sound and you just want to control access again.

What should I have ready before I book a rekey at my place?

Have the doors you want rekeyed identified ahead of time, including any detached garage, barn, or shed. It also helps to gather the current keys you still have and let the locksmith know if any locks are sticky, painted over, or not turning well. That saves time sorting out the job on site.

I just moved into a house in Fallston. Is rekeying enough, or should the locks be replaced?

If the locks are solid and you mainly want to make old keys useless, rekeying is usually the right first step. If the hardware is worn out, missing parts, or damaged, replacement may make more sense. A technician can look at each door and tell you which way fits the condition of the lock.

Can one key be set up for the house and the outbuildings?

Often, yes. If the locks are compatible, the cylinders can be rekeyed to the same key so you're not carrying separate keys for the home, garage, and other structures. If some locks are a different type or in rough shape, they may need a different approach, but it's worth asking for a matching set.

What if I lost the only key to an outside door on my property?

That can still be handled, but the door has to be opened first if it's locked and no working key is available. Once access is restored, the lock can be rekeyed so the missing key won't matter anymore. If the lock is badly worn or not cooperating, replacing it may be the better option.
