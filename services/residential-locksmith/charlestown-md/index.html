Residential locksmith in Charlestown - common questions

If I just moved into a house in Charlestown, should I rekey the locks or replace them?

If the locks are in decent shape, rekeying is usually the cleanest way to take back control of who has a working key. If the hardware is worn, mismatched, corroded, or damaged, replacement may be the better choice. The right answer depends on the condition of each door, not just the number of keys you have.

What should I have ready before you come to rekey or repair my door locks?

It helps to know which doors are giving trouble, whether the problem is with turning the key, closing the door, or latching it, and whether the lock has been changed before. If you have spare keys, keep them handy. For smart locks, have the brand and model if you know it, since that helps match the right parts.

What's the risk if a deadbolt feels loose but still works?

A loose deadbolt can give a false sense of security. The door may seem locked even if the bolt isn't fully catching the strike plate. Over time, that can leave the door easier to force and can also make the lock wear out faster. It's better to fix the alignment or hardware before the problem gets worse.

How do I decide between rekeying and installing a high-security lock?

Rekeying changes who can use the lock you already have. A high-security lock changes the hardware itself and can add better resistance to picking, bumping, and forced entry. If your current lock works well and you just want a new key setup, rekeying may be enough. If the door has had repeated issues or you want stronger hardware, upgrade it.

Can you help if the mailbox lock on a seasonal home is stuck or the key is missing?

Yes. Mailbox locks often fail from wear, corrosion, or keys that were never duplicated properly. If the key is missing, the lock may need to be replaced rather than repaired. It's a common issue on properties that sit unused part of the year, and it's worth fixing so mail and deliveries aren't left exposed.
