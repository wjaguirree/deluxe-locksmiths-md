Lock Replacement in Charlestown - common questions

How does lock replacement work on older Charlestown homes with wood doors?

Older wood doors often need more than a simple swap. The lock body, strike plate, and bolt all have to line up with the existing bore and the door edge. If the wood is worn or the holes are stretched, the replacement has to be fitted so the hardware sits solid and the door closes cleanly.

What should I have ready before replacing a lock on a waterfront house?

It helps to know which door is having trouble, whether the lock is keyed on one side or both, and whether you want to keep the same key or change it. If the door is painted, swollen, or exposed to salt air, mention that too. Those details can affect which lock fits best.

Can a damaged lock be replaced if the door was forced open?

Often yes, but the door and frame need to be checked first. If the hardware was torn out or the strike area is split, the new lock may not hold right until the damaged wood or metal is repaired. A fresh lock on a broken mount can fail again quickly.

Should I replace the whole lock or just rekey it?

If the lock is worn, rusted, loose, or doesn't match the door well, replacement is the better choice. Rekeying keeps the same hardware and only changes the key pattern inside the cylinder. If the hardware itself is the problem, rekeying won't fix the sticking or sloppy fit.

What if my Charlestown door has an unusual old lock or odd door thickness?

That happens in older homes and on custom doors. Some hardware can be adapted, but not every lock is a direct fit. The door thickness, backset, and bore size all matter. If the existing setup is unusual, the best option may be a replacement that fits the door without forcing it.
