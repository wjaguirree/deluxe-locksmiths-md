Lock Repair in Charlestown - common questions

My front lock sticks when the door swells after damp weather. Do you repair the lock or the door first?

Usually both get checked together. A lock can seem bad when the real issue is door movement, a shifted strike plate, or worn hinges. If the hardware is still in good shape, a repair and alignment can solve it. If the cylinder or latch is damaged, the lock itself may need work as well.

What should I have ready before you come out to fix a lock on my Charlestown house?

Have the door open if you can, and know which lock is acting up: deadbolt, knob, or a secondary lock. If you have the key that still works, bring it. It also helps to mention whether the issue started after a move, weather change, or a door repair, since that points to alignment problems.

I'm worried the lock will fail after you repair it. How do you tell if it's too worn to trust?

The lock gets judged by the parts that matter: cylinder wear, latch condition, body damage, and how well it lines up with the frame. If the parts are sound, repair can be the right answer. If the metal is cracked, badly corroded, or the mechanism is slipping, replacement is safer.

Should I rekey the lock instead of repairing it after I lost a house key near the water?

If the lock still works well, rekeying is often the right move after a lost key. It changes which key opens the lock without changing the whole assembly. Repair is for a lock that sticks, binds, or loosens. Replacement is for hardware that is worn out or damaged beyond a solid fix.

Can an old mortise lock in an older Charlestown home still be repaired, or does it need replacing?

Many old mortise locks can still be repaired if the case, latch, and key parts are intact. Those locks are often worth saving when the door is original and the hardware still fits the opening. If the case is broken or the parts are no longer holding alignment, replacement may be the practical choice.
