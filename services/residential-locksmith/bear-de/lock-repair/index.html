Lock Repair in Bear - common questions

How do you figure out whether my Bear front door lock needs repair or replacement?

We look at the cylinder, latch, strike alignment, and the way the key turns in the lock. If the body is still sound and the problem is wear, misalignment, or loose hardware, repair is often the better path. If the internal parts are broken beyond a solid fix, we'll say so and explain why.

What should I have ready before you come to repair my apartment lock in Bear?

Please make sure we can reach the door and that someone on site can show us the problem lock. If it's a rental or townhouse unit, it helps to know whether the property manager needs to approve the work. If you have the key that's been sticking, keep it handy so we can test it.

My lock still works, but it feels loose. Is that a risk or just normal wear?

A loose lock is usually a sign that something is shifting or wearing out, not just harmless play. The screws may be backing out, the cylinder may be worn, or the door and frame may not be meeting right. We can tighten, realign, or repair the right part before the problem gets worse.

If my townhouse lock in Bear keeps sticking, should I repair the deadbolt or replace the whole set?

If only one part is worn, repairing the deadbolt or adjusting the strike may solve it. If the knob set and deadbolt are both old, mismatched, or damaged, a full set replacement can make more sense. We'll look at how the door closes and which parts are actually causing the trouble.

Can you repair a lock that was damaged after a tenant move-out or key change?

Yes, as long as the damage is limited to the lock hardware or the door-side fit. Rekeying, rough use, or rushed turnover can leave parts loose, bent, or misaligned. We can often restore the lock without replacing the entire door setup, but we'll be upfront if the damage is too severe.
