Door Lock Repair in Bear - common questions

Why does my front door latch catch only if I shove it shut?

That usually means the lock and strike are out of alignment, or the door has shifted on its hinges. We check whether the latch is scraping, whether the strike plate needs to be reset, and whether worn parts in the lock are adding resistance. The goal is a door that closes with normal pressure, not a hard push.

What should I have ready before you repair the lock at my Bear rental?

It helps to know which door is acting up, whether the issue is new or long-running, and whether the door was recently painted, repaired, or rekeyed. If you're a tenant, it also helps to have the property owner or manager aware of the repair. That keeps the work simple and avoids confusion if parts need to be replaced.

Could a sticky lock turn into a bigger problem if I ignore it?

Yes. A lock that keeps binding can wear out the latch, loosen the strike, or damage the door edge. In multi-family buildings, repeated slamming can make the problem worse. If the door is still closing only when forced, it's better to correct the alignment and hardware before the repair becomes more involved.

Should I repair the lock or replace it if the key still works?

If the lock body is sound and the trouble is alignment, loose hardware, or a worn strike, repair is often the right move. If the internal parts are badly worn, broken, or damaged from repeated forcing, replacement may make more sense. We look at how the whole door is performing before we recommend one path over the other.

Can you fix a door that was painted shut around the lock area?

Sometimes, yes. Paint buildup can keep the latch from moving freely or prevent the strike from lining up. If the lock itself is still in good shape, we can clear the obstruction, reset the hardware, and check the door for any hidden binding. If the paint has damaged parts underneath, we'll work from there.
