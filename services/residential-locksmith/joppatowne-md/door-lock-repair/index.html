Door Lock Repair in Joppatowne - common questions

My front door lock in Joppatowne turns, but the latch still won't catch. What usually causes that?

That usually means the lock isn't lining up with the strike plate, or the latch is worn and not extending cleanly. On waterfront homes, corrosion can also slow the spring inside the latch. We check the lock body, the strike, and the door fit before deciding whether a repair or replacement will hold up.

What should I tell you when I call about a sticking lock on my house near the water?

Tell us which door it is, whether it's a knob, lever, or deadbolt, and what it's doing: hard to turn, loose, slipping, or not catching. If it changes with weather or after the door was slammed, say that too. Those details help narrow down whether the problem is wear, corrosion, or alignment.

Is it safe to keep using a lock that only works if I lift the door a little first?

It usually means the door or strike has shifted, so the lock is being forced to line up. That can wear out the latch, damage the strike, and eventually leave you locked out. It's better to have it checked before the hardware gets bent or the screws pull out of the frame.

Should I repair the existing lock or replace it when the mechanism keeps binding?

If the issue is alignment, loose mounting, or a single worn part, repair is often the right move. If the inside of the lock is corroded, gritty, or failing in more than one spot, replacement usually makes more sense. We look at the condition of the hardware, not just the symptom, before recommending either path.

What if my deadbolt works, but the knob lock on the same door is failing?

That happens a lot. The deadbolt and knob are separate mechanisms, so one can fail while the other still works. It may be a worn latch, a bad cylinder, or a strike that's shifted just enough to affect one lock more than the other. We can repair the problem side without changing hardware that's still sound.
