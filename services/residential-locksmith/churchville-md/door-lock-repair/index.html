Door Lock Repair in Churchville - common questions

My front door near Churchville crossroads won't latch unless I lift it. Is that a lock problem or a door problem?

It can be either, but lifting the door usually points to alignment. The hinges may have dropped, the strike may be out of place, or the latch may be hitting the frame. We check the whole setup so we're not only fixing the lock while the real issue stays in the door or frame.

What should I have ready before you come out to repair a door lock in Churchville?

If you have the key, keep it handy, and show us which door is acting up. It also helps to know whether the problem is new, after a repair, or after weather shifted the door. If the door has been forced, tell us that too, because it can change what parts need attention.

Can a worn lock be repaired, or does it have to be replaced?

If the body is still solid and the failure is from a loose part, misalignment, or a sticky latch, repair may be enough. If the lock is cracked, badly worn, or has internal damage, replacement makes more sense. We look at the hardware first and explain which path fits the condition of the door.

If I just moved into a house off MD-22, should I rekey the locks or repair them?

Those solve different problems. Rekeying changes who can use the existing lock; repair fixes a lock that isn't working right. If the lock works well, rekeying is often the cleanest first step after a move. If it sticks or won't catch, we need to repair the hardware before talking about new keys.

What if the deadbolt works but the latch keeps sticking on my old farmhouse door?

That's common on older doors with seasonal movement. The deadbolt can still be fine while the latch rubs because the door has shifted, the strike is worn, or the frame needs a small correction. We can usually narrow it down by checking how the door closes and where the latch is contacting.
