Lock Repair in Churchville - common questions

How do you decide whether my Churchville front door needs lock repair or replacement?

We look at what failed inside the lock, how the door is hanging, and whether the key and bolt are still serviceable. If the issue is wear, loose hardware, or alignment, repair is often the right move. If the cylinder, spring work, or body is badly worn, replacement can make more sense.

What should I have ready before you come out to repair the lock on my house near MD-22?

It helps to know which door is acting up, what the lock is doing, and whether it started after weather, a repair, or a door adjustment. If you have a spare key or know the lock brand, that can help too. Clear access to the door is useful so we can check both sides and the frame.

If my deadbolt keeps sticking, is that a lock problem or a door problem?

It can be either, and often it's both. A deadbolt that sticks is commonly caused by a shifted strike plate, a warped door, or screws that have loosened over time. We check the door and frame first, because fixing only the lock body won't solve a bolt that's hitting the frame wrong.

Should I repair the lock on my farmhouse door or replace it with a new one?

If the lock still has solid parts and the problem is mainly alignment or wear in one section, repair is usually worth doing. If the body is cracked, the cylinder is badly worn, or the hardware no longer fits the door well, replacement may be the better long-term fix. We'll explain what each path means before any work starts.

Can you fix a lock that works from the inside but fights me from the outside?

Yes, and that symptom usually tells us the cylinder or key side is worn, dirty, or slightly out of line. It can also happen when the door is under pressure and the bolt isn't seating evenly. We test both sides so we can tell whether the problem is in the lock body, the keyway, or the door fit.
