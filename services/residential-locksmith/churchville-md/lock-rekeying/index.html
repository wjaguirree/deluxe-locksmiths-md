What Good Rekeying Looks Like

A proper rekeying job leaves the hardware solid and the keying cleaned up. We remove the cylinders, set the pins to the new key, and test every lock by hand before we call it done. In a place like Churchville, where old farmhouses, newer homes on larger lots, and the buildings around the crossroads all use different door hardware, that care matters. A rushed job can leave a key that drags, a cylinder that sticks, or a lock that works from one side but feels rough from the other. It can also leave the old key still able to work because a pin stack wasn't changed out right.

When the work is done well, you should notice a few clear things. Your new key should turn smoothly without forcing it. The lock should latch cleanly, the deadbolt should line up without extra pressure on the door, and every key in the set should behave the same way. We also check that the old key no longer works, which is the whole point after a move or after someone stops needing access. If we're rekeying a home near MD-22 or off a quieter side road, we also pay attention to door fit, worn knobs, and older strike plates, because a lock can be keyed right and still feel wrong if the rest of the door is out of line.

A bodge stands out fast. The key catches, the bolt has to be wiggled into place, or the lock sounds loose inside. A proper job feels plain and dependable, with no guesswork each time you turn the key.

Lock Rekeying in Churchville - common questions

How does rekeying work on my Churchville house doors?

We take the lock cylinder apart, change the internal pins to match a new key, and put it back together. The outside hardware stays on the door. If the lock is in good condition, this is a clean way to control who can open it without replacing the whole set.

What should I have ready before you come rekey the locks?

Have the doors and keys you want addressed ready, and let us know if any keys are missing. If there are multiple entrances, it helps to know which ones should work on the same key. If there's a deadbolt and a knob on the same door, tell us whether you want them keyed alike.

How do I know if I need rekeying or if the lock is actually broken?

If the key still turns smoothly and the door hardware is solid, rekeying may be enough. If the key sticks, the bolt binds, parts feel loose, or the lock has been forced, we may need to repair or replace hardware instead. We can look at it on site and explain the difference in plain terms.

Should I rekey all the doors at once, or just the front door?

That depends on how the home is set up. In Churchville, it's common to have a front entry, a side door, and a garage or mudroom door. If different people have had keys over time, rekeying every exterior entry usually makes more sense than leaving one door on an old key.

Can you rekey older farmhouse locks or outbuilding doors on acreage?

Often, yes, if the lock is a standard rekeyable cylinder and the parts are still in usable shape. Some older or specialty locks can't be rekeyed cleanly, or they may need rebuilding first. We'll check the hardware and tell you whether rekeying is practical or if a replacement would be the better path.
