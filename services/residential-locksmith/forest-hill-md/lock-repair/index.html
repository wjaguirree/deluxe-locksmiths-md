Repair, Rekey, or Replace

When a lock starts sticking, feels loose in the door, or turns hard with the key, we don't jump straight to replacement. We look at what's actually failing. A door that has shifted, worn hardware, a dry cylinder, or a bent latch can often be repaired and kept in service. If the lock body is sound but the key control is the problem, rekeying may make more sense, especially in newer homes around MD-24 where builder-grade hardware and builder-supplied keys are common. And if the mechanism is cracked, stripped, corroded, or no longer holding the door the way it should, replacement is the better call.

That decision matters because not every problem is a lock problem. We check the alignment between the lock and strike, the condition of the screws and internal parts, and whether the key itself is worn. Lock Repair in Forest Hill - common questions

Why does my front door lock keep sticking in my Forest Hill house?

Sticking usually means the lock is out of alignment, the latch is worn, or the door has shifted enough that the bolt and strike no longer meet cleanly. In Forest Hill, that can happen in newer homes as framing settles or in older homes where hinges and hardware have seen years of use. We check the whole door, not just the key cylinder.

What should I have ready before you come repair a lock?

Have the door unlocked if possible, and know whether the problem is with the key, the deadbolt, or the handle set. If it's a rental or a business entry, it helps to know who handles approval for the work. If the lock has been painted, bent, or previously tampered with, say so up front.

Can a loose lock damage my door if I keep using it?

Yes. A loose lock can chew up the strike plate, stress the latch, and make the door harder to close. If the knob or deadbolt is shifting in the door, the screw holes or mounting points may already be worn. Repairing it early usually saves the door and keeps the hardware from failing at the wrong time.

Should I repair the lock or replace it if the key is hard to turn?

If the problem is wear, misalignment, or a part that can still be corrected, repair is often the right move. If the lock is badly damaged, corroded, or missing internal parts, replacement may make more sense. We look at how the door fits, how the latch engages, and whether the hardware still has a solid base.

Can you fix a lock on a new-build home if the builder's key was used by several people?

Yes. That's a common reason homeowners call. If the lock is still in good shape, we can often repair or rekey it instead of changing the whole set. That gives you better control over who can open the door without starting over with new hardware unless the lock itself is worn or damaged.
