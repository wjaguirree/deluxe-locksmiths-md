Lock Rekeying in Conowingo - common questions

If we just bought a house in Conowingo, should we rekey the locks first?

Yes, that's usually the first thing we suggest. Rekeying lets you keep the existing hardware while making old keys useless. It's a practical reset after a sale, especially when you don't know who still has copies from the previous owner, contractors, or relatives.

What should I have ready before you rekey my doors?

Have the doors and locks accessible, and gather every key you already have for those locks. If you know which doors should share one key and which should stay separate, tell us up front. If there are stuck locks, broken keys, or a door that won't close cleanly, let us know so we can account for it.

Can rekeying fix a lock that feels loose or hard to turn?

Sometimes, but not always. Rekeying changes the key pattern inside the cylinder. If the problem is worn parts, a bent key, or a latch that's out of line, the lock may still need repair or replacement. We'll check the condition first and explain what makes sense.

Should I rekey or replace the locks on an older house near the river?

If the hardware is solid and you mainly want to control who has access, rekeying is often the better move. If the lock is corroded, mismatched, or missing parts, replacement may be the cleaner choice. Older houses sometimes need a mix of both, depending on the door and the condition of the lock.

What if one door has a different lock than the rest of the house?

That happens a lot. We can often make separate locks work with one key if the hardware allows it, but not every lock can be matched that way. If one cylinder is an odd style or badly worn, we'll tell you whether rekeying it is possible or whether that door needs different hardware.
