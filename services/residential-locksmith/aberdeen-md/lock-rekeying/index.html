Lock Rekeying in Aberdeen - common questions

If I just moved into a house in Aberdeen, do I need to rekey every exterior lock?

Usually, yes. If the front door, back door, garage entry, or basement door all use different keys, it's smart to reset the ones you'll actually use. That way you know exactly which keys open the home. If one lock is damaged or mismatched, it may need repair instead of just a rekey.

What should I have ready before a rekeying visit?

Have the doors unlocked if you can, gather every key you were given, and point out any locks that stick or feel loose. If you're a landlord, bring any details you know about which doors should match. That helps sort out whether the job is simple rekeying or whether one lock needs more work first.

Can rekeying fix a lock that's hard to turn?

Not always. If the problem is old pins, a worn cylinder, or a key that no longer matches the lock well, rekeying can help. If the door is sagging, the strike is off, or the hardware is failing, rekeying alone won't cure it. In that case, the lock may need adjustment or repair.

Should I rekey or replace the lock after a tenant moves out?

If the hardware is in good shape, rekeying is usually the better move because it keeps the existing lock in service and changes access control. If the lock is damaged, mixed with odd parts, or missing key pieces, replacement may be the cleaner choice. The goal is a lock that works smoothly and only opens for the new key.

Can you rekey a lock if the key is lost and I don't have the original?

Often, yes. Many residential locks can still be reset without the original key, as long as the hardware is usable and the lock can be taken apart safely. If the lock has been altered, forced, or is badly worn, the better option may be repair or replacement so the door ends up secure and dependable.
