Lock Replacement in Aberdeen - common questions

When should a lock be replaced instead of rekeyed on a house in Aberdeen?

Rekeying is fine when the lock is in decent shape and you only need new keys. Replace it when the hardware is worn, the key sticks, the deadbolt doesn't throw cleanly, the lock was damaged, or the door has been patched too many times. If the parts are failing, new hardware is the better fix.

What should I have ready before you replace locks at my Aberdeen home or rental?

It helps to know which doors need new hardware, whether you want one key for all the locks, and whether the door has had problems latching or dragging. If it's a rental, having access to the property and any existing key information helps. The main thing is making sure the door itself is ready to be worked on.

Can a new lock still work if the door frame or strike plate is damaged?

Sometimes, but not well unless the fit is corrected. A new lock on a bad frame can still bind, scrape, or fail to latch properly. In those cases, the lock replacement should include checking the strike, the alignment, and the way the door closes. Otherwise the new hardware inherits the old problem.

Should I replace all the locks on a rental unit in Aberdeen after tenants move out?

Often, yes, if the hardware is old, mismatched, or the keys are a mess. For rental turnover, replacement gives you a clean starting point and avoids handing the next tenant a patchwork of worn parts. If the locks are in good shape, rekeying may be enough, but damaged or shaky hardware should go.

What if my lock is old but the door itself is still solid?

That's a common situation. A good door can still have a bad lock. If the latch, cylinder, or deadbolt is worn out, replacing just the lock hardware is often the right move. The door doesn't need to be replaced just because the lock has reached the end of its useful life.
