Door Lock Repair in Wilmington - common questions

Why does my front door in Wilmington need a hard push before it latches?

That usually means the latch and strike are no longer lining up, often because the door or frame has shifted. We check the hinges, latch, strike plate, and door edge together. If the lock hardware is worn, we repair or replace the part that's causing the bind instead of just making the door close by force.

What should I have ready before we repair my door lock?

Please make sure we can reach the door, and tell us whether the problem is with locking, latching, key turning, or the door rubbing the frame. If there's a spare key that normally works better, have that handy. On older Wilmington homes, it also helps to know whether the lock is original hardware or a later replacement.

Is it a bad sign if my lock was already adjusted once and still sticks?

It can be. A one-time adjustment sometimes only covers up a deeper issue, like worn parts, loose hinges, or a strike that no longer matches the latch path. If the door keeps needing extra force, we look for the root cause so the repair holds up instead of turning into another temporary fix.

Should I repair the old mortise lock or replace it on my Wilmington rowhome?

If the body of the lock is still sound and the problem is wear, misalignment, or a damaged part, repair is often the better route. If the mechanism is broken beyond practical repair or parts can't be matched, replacement may make more sense. We'll look at the door and hardware before recommending either path.

My key turns, but the door still won't catch right. Can that be fixed without changing the whole lock?

Often, yes. That symptom can come from the latch not fully engaging, a weak spring, or the strike being off by a small amount. We inspect the moving parts and the door alignment first. If the lock body still has life in it, we can usually repair the issue without replacing the entire set.
