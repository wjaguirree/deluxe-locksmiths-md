Lock Replacement in Wilmington - common questions

My front door in Wilmington still has an old mortise lock. Can you replace it without changing the whole door?

Often, yes. Old mortise locks can sometimes be replaced with a new mortise-style unit that fits the existing pocket in the door. The main things we need to check are the door thickness, the size of the case, the backset, and how the trim lines up. If the door has been repaired before, we look closely at that too.

What should I have ready before we replace a lock at my house near Rodney Square?

If you have a working key, keep it handy. It also helps to know whether the lock has been sticking, whether anyone forced it, and whether the door itself closes and latches normally. If there's an old receipt, a spare key, or any note about past lock changes, that can help us match the right hardware faster.

I'm worried the new lock won't line up with my old door. Is that a real problem?

It can be, especially on older Wilmington homes with worn doors or prior patchwork repairs. A lock that doesn't line up can bind, scrape, or fail to latch cleanly. We check the strike, latch position, and door movement before installing anything so the new lock works with the door instead of against it.

Should I replace the deadbolt, the knob lock, or both on my Wilmington rowhouse?

That depends on what's worn and what you want the door to do. If one part is failing and the other is still sound, replacing just the problem lock may be enough. If the hardware is old, mismatched, or both pieces are showing wear, replacing both can give you a cleaner, more reliable result.

Can you replace a lock if the key is lost and the door is already unlocked?

Yes. That's a common situation. We can replace the hardware whether the key is missing, broken, or no longer trusted. If the lock is damaged from a lost key situation or from someone trying to force entry, we'll inspect the latch, strike, and door edge so the new lock goes on a solid setup.
