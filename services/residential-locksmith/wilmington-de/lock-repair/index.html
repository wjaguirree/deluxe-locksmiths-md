What to Check First

Before we open anything up, there are a few simple things you can check that help us diagnose the problem faster. Try the key from both sides of the door and note whether it binds, turns partway, or works only when the door is pulled or pushed. If the lock is sticking, look at the edge of the door and the strike plate for rubbing, loose screws, or a latch that looks like it's hitting too high or too low. In older homes around Trolley Square and Little Italy, we often find the issue is not the cylinder itself but a door that has settled, weathered, or drifted out of alignment. If you can tell us when the problem started and whether it's worse in humid weather, after use, or only with one key, that gives us a better starting point.

Lock Repair in Wilmington - common questions

Why does my front door lock in Wilmington turn hard but still lock?

That usually means the lock is fighting the door, not just wearing out. The latch may be rubbing the strike, the door may have shifted, or the internal parts may be dry or worn. We check the door, the alignment, and the lock itself in that order so we fix the cause instead of guessing.

What should I have ready before you come to repair a lock on my house?

If you have the key that works best, keep it handy. It also helps to know whether the lock is the same one that came with the door or a later replacement. If the door has been sticking, swollen, or repaired recently, that detail matters because the lock problem may start with the door, not the hardware.

Could a loose deadbolt on my Wilmington rowhome be something bigger than the lock itself?

Yes. A loose deadbolt can be caused by worn screw holes, a shifted strike, or door movement from age and use. In older homes, the lock may still be fine but the door has settled enough to throw everything off. We check the full setup before deciding whether the lock needs repair or a different fix.

Should I repair the old mortise lock in my house or replace it?

If the body of the lock is still sound, repair is often the better first step. Many older mortise locks can be cleaned, adjusted, and brought back into regular use. Replacement makes more sense when the case is damaged beyond practical repair, parts are missing, or the lock no longer matches the door well enough to work reliably.

Can you repair a lock for a tenant if the landlord handles the property in Wilmington?

We can service the lock, but permission matters. If the lock is part of the rental property, the landlord or property manager may need to approve the work or the hardware change. We can still identify what's wrong so the right person can make an informed decision.
