Door Lock Repair in Jarrettsville - common questions

What do you usually fix on a door lock that won't latch in Jarrettsville?

We check the latch, strike plate, screws, hinges, and the lock body itself. A lot of latch problems come from misalignment rather than a dead lock. If the hardware is worn out, we replace the part that's failing instead of forcing the whole door to keep working the wrong way.

Do I need to have anything ready before you repair my front door lock?

It helps to know which door is acting up and whether the problem is new or has been getting worse. If the key still turns but the door won't catch, that tells us something different than a broken lever or a jammed deadbolt. Clear access to the door is helpful, but that's usually all we need.

Is a sticking lock a security problem or just an annoyance?

It can be both. A lock that's hard to latch may leave the door not fully secured, even when it looks shut. A loose strike plate, worn latch, or sagging door can let the hardware miss just enough to cause trouble. We treat it as a real repair, not just a small nuisance.

Should I repair the lock or replace the whole lockset?

If the body of the lock is still sound, repair is often the better path. If the latch is worn, the bore is damaged, or the parts don't match the door anymore, replacement makes more sense. We look at the condition of the hardware and the door prep before recommending either option.

Can you fix a shed or tack room door lock that's been sitting in the weather?

Yes, if the door and hardware are still serviceable. Weather exposure can rust springs, seize latches, and loosen fasteners. In those cases we may clean up the fit, replace worn hardware, and correct the alignment so the door closes and locks without a fight. If the door itself is too far gone, we'll say so.
