Deadbolt Installation in Dover - common questions

How does a deadbolt installation on my Dover house actually work?

We check the door, measure the existing bore, and make sure the bolt, strike plate, and screw length match the frame. Then we fit the new deadbolt so the latch throws cleanly and the door closes without binding. If the door is already cut for a deadbolt, the work is usually straightforward; if not, we lay it out carefully before drilling.

What should I have ready before you come install a deadbolt in Dover?

It helps to know whether the door is wood, metal, or fiberglass, and whether there's already a lock in that spot. If you want keys to match other doors, have those keys handy. A photo of the door edge, the inside side of the lock, and the strike area can also help us bring the right hardware and avoid guesswork.

My front door closes, but the lock feels weak. Is that a deadbolt problem or a door problem?

It could be either, and often it's both. A deadbolt won't hold well if the strike plate is loose, the screws are short, or the door is sagging. We look at the whole setup so the new lock isn't relying on a weak frame. That matters on older Dover homes where the door and trim have settled over time.

Should I choose a keyed deadbolt or a thumbturn deadbolt for my house near The Green?

A keyed deadbolt on both sides gives more control, but it also means a key is needed to unlock it from the inside. A thumbturn is easier to use in daily life and during an emergency. We help you weigh that based on the door's location, who uses it, and whether security or convenience matters more for that entry.

Can you install a deadbolt on an older door with odd spacing or old hardware holes?

Often, yes. Older doors sometimes have patched holes, oversized openings, or hardware that doesn't match current lock sizes. We check the existing layout first and see whether the door can be fitted cleanly without making it look rough. If the door is badly damaged or warped, we'll tell you what the limits are before starting.
