Repair, Rekey, or Replace

When we look at a lock in Felton, we don't start by assuming it needs to be swapped out. We check how the key turns, whether the cylinder is worn, if the hardware is loose in the door, and whether the problem is the lock body itself or just the keyway. On older houses in the town grid, a lock can often be repaired if the mechanism is still solid and the issue is a spring, latch, or misalignment from years of use. If the lock works but you need new key control after a move, a rekey may be the better answer because it keeps the hardware in place and changes which key operates it. We only recommend replacement when the lock is damaged, outdated, corroded, or no longer giving the level of security the door should have.

That judgment matters even more around the older homes and farm properties outside town, where doors may have seen weather, dust, and hard daily use. A lock that's been forced, painted over, or patched too many times can keep failing even after a repair, and a rekey won't solve worn parts that no longer hold up. In those cases, replacing the lock gives you a fresh start with hardware that fits the door properly and matches how you actually use the house. We explain the tradeoffs plainly so you can decide whether to keep the existing hardware, change the keys, or move to a new lock that's a better fit for the door and the way it's used.

In older Felton houses, the usual problems are loose knobs, shallow strike plates, and deadbolts that don't line up with the frame anymore. Those doors can still close, but they may not resist forced entry the way a sound lock should. On newer property, the issue is more often worn keyways, builder-grade hardware, or a lock that never quite fit the door right in the first place.

Vehicles and outbuildings around town bring their own differences. A car or shop door may use hardware that's more exposed to weather and dirt, so the lock wears differently than a front door in the grid of town streets. For farm lanes and equipment sheds, replacement is often about getting back to a lock that turns cleanly, seals the opening better, and holds up to daily use without fighting you.

Lock Replacement in Felton - common questions

How does a lock replacement usually work at my house in Felton?

The old lock is removed, the door and frame are checked for wear, and a new lock is fitted so it lines up correctly. If needed, the strike plate or latch opening is adjusted so the door closes and locks cleanly. The goal is simple: solid hardware that works the way it should, without making the door hard to use.

What should I have ready before you replace the lock?

Have the door open if possible, and make sure someone who can approve the work is there. It helps to know whether you want one key to operate multiple locks or just want each door handled separately. If the door has been sticking or the frame looks worn, mention that up front so the replacement can be matched to the condition of the door.

What's the risk of keeping a worn lock on an older Felton home?

A tired lock can fail in ways that are easy to miss. The key may start sticking, the bolt may not fully extend, or the hardware may be easier to force than you think. In some cases, the door looks secure from the inside but leaves a weak point outside. Replacing it removes that weak link before it becomes a problem.

Should I replace the whole lock or just rekey it?

Rekeying makes sense when the hardware is still in good shape and you just want different keys. Replacement is better when the lock is worn, damaged, outdated, or not holding the door securely. If the knob wobbles, the deadbolt feels sloppy, or the parts don't match the door anymore, replacement is usually the smarter fix.

Can you replace a lock on a farmhouse or outbuilding off Sandtown Road if the door is old or uneven?

Often, yes. Older doors and sheds can still take new hardware, but they sometimes need adjustment so the latch and strike line up properly. If the door is badly warped, cracked, or missing solid wood where the lock mounts, a replacement may need repair work first. The condition of the door matters as much as the lock itself.
