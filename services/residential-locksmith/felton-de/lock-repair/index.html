Lock Repair in Felton - common questions

How do you decide whether a lock on my Felton house can be repaired or needs replacement?

We look at the cylinder, latch, strike plate, screws, and the way the door sits in the frame. If the body of the lock is still solid and the problem is wear, misalignment, or a failed internal part, repair usually makes sense. If the mechanism is broken down or the door prep is too damaged, replacement is the cleaner fix.

What should I have ready before you come out to repair a lock in town?

Have the door unlocked if possible, and make sure we can reach the door area and the hardware on both sides. If the lock has been acting up for a while, it helps to know whether it sticks, turns roughly, or only fails when the door is closed. The old key, if you have it, can also help us compare wear.

My deadbolt feels loose. Is that a sign the lock is failing or just the door?

It can be either. A loose deadbolt may mean the mounting screws have backed out, the strike is off, or the door has shifted and stopped lining up with the frame. If the lock body still works correctly, we can usually tighten, realign, or rebuild the setup instead of replacing the whole unit.

Should I repair the old lock on my older Felton home or switch to new hardware?

If the lock is original and the door still fits well, repair can preserve the existing look and avoid extra holes or trim work. New hardware makes more sense when the parts are worn out, mismatched, or no longer dependable. We'll look at the door and the lock together, not just the face of the hardware.

What if the lock on my farmhouse door is painted over or keeps catching on the frame?

Paint buildup and seasonal movement can both cause trouble. We can clean and free up the moving parts, check the strike, and see whether the door needs a small adjustment. If the paint has damaged the mechanism or the lock was already worn out, we'll explain whether repair is still a good option.
