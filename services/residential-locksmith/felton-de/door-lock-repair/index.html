Door Lock Repair in Felton - common questions

Why does my door lock in Felton feel stiff only when the door is shut?

That usually means the lock is fine by itself, but the latch isn't meeting the strike plate cleanly when the door closes. A shifted frame, loose hinges, or a swollen door can cause that. The repair usually focuses on alignment first, then the lock parts if they're worn or damaged.

What should I have ready before you come look at the lock?

Have the door unlocked if possible, and keep the key or thumbturn you normally use nearby. It also helps to know whether the problem started suddenly or slowly got worse. If there's a second door with the same style of hardware, that can be a useful comparison for how the latch should feel.

Is it a bad idea to keep forcing a sticky lock on an older house?

Yes. Forcing it can bend the latch, chew up the strike plate, or damage the keyway inside the lock. On older doors, the real issue is often that the door has shifted a little, so extra pressure only makes the hardware wear faster. Gentle use is safer until it's checked.

Should I repair the lock or replace the whole knob and deadbolt?

If the body of the lock is still sound, repair can be the right choice, especially when the main issue is alignment or a worn strike. If the internal parts are loose, cracked, or failing, replacement may make more sense. The decision comes down to the condition of the hardware and the door itself.

Can a lock on a farm door or side entry be repaired if the wood is worn out?

Often, yes. Worn screw holes, soft wood around the strike, or a sagging door can sometimes be corrected with reinforcement and adjustment. If the frame is badly damaged, the lock may not stay aligned until that's addressed. The lock repair and the door condition have to be looked at together.
