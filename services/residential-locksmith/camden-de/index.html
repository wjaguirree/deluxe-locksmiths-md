Residential locksmith in Camden - common questions

If I just moved into a house in Camden, do I need to replace every lock?

Not always. If the locks are in good shape, rekeying usually gives you new keys without changing the whole set. Replacement is better when the hardware is worn, mismatched, or damaged. The right choice depends on how the door closes, how the lock turns, and whether the parts still fit together cleanly.

What should I have ready before a locksmith works on my front door?

It helps to know which door is giving you trouble, whether the key turns but won't latch, and whether the lock was ever changed before. If you're a tenant, bring any permission your landlord expects. If there's a brand name on the lock, that can help too, but it's not required.

My deadbolt sticks partway in Camden's older homes. Is that the lock or the door?

It can be either. In older houses, the door or frame may have shifted, so the bolt and strike no longer line up well. Sometimes the lock parts are worn too. A proper repair looks at the full door, not just the cylinder, because a fresh part won't help much if the alignment is still off.

Should I choose a smart lock or stick with a keyed deadbolt on my home?

A keyed deadbolt is simple and dependable. A smart lock can be useful if you want keypad entry or remote control, but it needs the right door setup and good installation. If your door is already loose, warped, or hard to latch, that should be corrected first so the new hardware has a solid base.

Can a mailbox lock be changed if the key broke off inside it?

Often, yes. If the key fragment is still inside, the lock may be opened, cleaned out, and checked for damage. If the cylinder is bent or badly worn, replacement is usually the better route. The important thing is not forcing it, since that can crack the housing and make the mailbox harder to secure.
