How the latch should work

Inside most door locks, the latch is the small spring-loaded piece that slides into the strike plate on the frame. When the door is shut, that latch should line up cleanly with the opening in the frame and catch without extra force. When it starts to wear out, the signs usually show up from the outside first: the door has to be pushed hard, the knob feels loose, the lever doesn't return the same way, or the latch only catches if you lift, pull, or shoulder the door into place. That's not just annoyance. It usually means the lock body, latch, or strike plate is out of alignment, worn down, or both.

Door Lock Repair in Camden - common questions

Why does my Camden front door lock only catch if I pull the door toward me?

That usually means the latch and strike are no longer meeting cleanly. The door may have shifted, the hinges may be loose, or the strike plate may be out of position. We check the full door alignment, not just the lock body, so the latch can enter the opening without binding or scraping.

What should I have ready before you repair a sticky door lock in my house?

Please have the door accessible and the key or lockset available if it uses one. If the door has been giving trouble for a while, it helps to know whether it sticks in hot weather, after heavy rain, or all the time. That gives us a better read on whether the issue is the lock, the frame, or both.

Is it a bad idea to keep forcing a lock that doesn't line up right?

Yes. Forcing it can round off the latch, widen screw holes, and make the strike plate loosen further. It can also leave the door harder to secure. If the lock is rubbing, sticking, or only closing under pressure, it's better to repair the alignment before the damage spreads.

Should I repair the lock or replace it if the latch won't catch smoothly?

If the lock is otherwise in good shape, repair is often the better choice. We can realign the strike, adjust the hinges, and rebuild worn mounting points if needed. Replacement makes more sense when the internal parts are broken, the cylinder is failing, or the hardware is too worn to hold adjustment.

Can you fix a door that used to close fine but started failing after the house settled?

Usually, yes. Settlement can move the frame just enough to throw the latch off. That's common on residential doors, especially when the screws have loosened over time. We look at the door's fit first, then repair the hardware so it closes without needing extra force.
