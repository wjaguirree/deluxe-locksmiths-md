Lock Replacement in Abingdon - common questions

How does a residential lock replacement usually work on a townhouse door in Abingdon?

The old lock is removed, the door and latch area are checked for wear, and new hardware is installed to fit the opening. If the strike plate, screws, or latch are part of the issue, those get adjusted too. The goal is a lock that turns cleanly, latches properly, and matches how the door is actually used.

What should I have ready before having my lock replaced at my house?

It helps to know which door is giving trouble, whether you want the same type of lock or something different, and whether anyone else still has keys. If the issue started after a move, lost key, or break-in concern, say so up front. That gives the work some context and helps pick the right hardware.

What happens if I keep using a lock that's old but still turns?

A lock can still turn and still be a problem. Wear inside the cylinder, loose mounting, or a weak strike can make it easier to force or easier for old keys to keep working. In a busy housing area, that can leave you with less control over who can get in and out of the home.

Should I replace the whole lock or just the cylinder on my front door?

It depends on what is worn out. If the key issue is limited to the cylinder and the rest of the hardware is solid, a cylinder change may be enough. If the body of the lock, the latch, or the exterior finish is damaged, replacing the whole set makes more sense. The door has to close and lock as one system.

Can you replace a lock if the door was damaged from a forced entry attempt?

Sometimes, yes, but the door and frame have to be checked first. If the wood is split, the strike area is loose, or the deadbolt no longer lines up, the lock alone won't solve it. In those cases, the repair has to include the door hardware around the lock so the new lock has something solid to work with.
