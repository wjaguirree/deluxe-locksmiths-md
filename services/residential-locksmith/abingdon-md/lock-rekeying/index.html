Choosing the Right Fix

When a lock starts acting up after a move, we look at what's actually wrong before deciding anything. If the hardware is solid and the problem is worn keys, old access, or a tenant turnover issue, rekeying is usually the clean answer. That keeps the lock in place and changes which key works. If the lock is sticking because of bent parts, loose hardware, or a tired cylinder that still has life left, we may repair it first and rekey it if the core is still sound. If the lock body is damaged, the keyway is badly worn, or the hardware no longer gives the level of security you want, replacement makes more sense. We don't push one choice for every door; we match the fix to the condition of the lock and the way the home is used.

Replacement is best when the lock has been forced, corroded, or built so lightly that it isn't worth putting more work into. Lock Rekeying in Abingdon - common questions

How does rekeying work on a house in Abingdon?

We take apart the lock cylinder, reset the internal pins, and make new keys that match the new setup. The hardware stays on the door, but the old keys stop working. It's a good fit after a move, after a tenant leaves, or whenever you want to cut off access without replacing the whole lock.

What should I have ready before you rekey my locks?

It helps to know which doors you want changed and whether there are any keys still floating around with roommates, contractors, or former tenants. If you have the current key, bring it. If the lock has been sticking or feels loose, mention that so we can check whether rekeying is the right fix.

I just moved into a townhouse off MD-24. What's the risk if I wait?

The risk is that you don't know how many copies of the old key exist. Previous owners, renters, cleaners, or maintenance people may still have one. Rekeying closes that gap. Waiting leaves the door open to avoidable access, and that's a bad trade in a neighborhood where keys change hands often.

Should I rekey or replace the lock on my front door?

Rekeying is the better choice when the lock works well and you mainly need new access control. Replacement makes more sense if the hardware is damaged, outdated, or not secure enough for the door. We can look at the lock and tell you which option fits the condition of the hardware.

Can you rekey if I lost all the keys to my lock?

Sometimes, yes, but it depends on the lock and whether we can access and reset the cylinder safely. In some cases, we may need to remove the lock, pick it, or replace it if the mechanism is too worn or the keyway is too damaged. We'll explain the workable options on site.
