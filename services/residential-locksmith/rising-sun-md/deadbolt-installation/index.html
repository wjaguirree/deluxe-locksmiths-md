Deadbolt Installation in Rising Sun - common questions

How is a deadbolt installed on an older Rising Sun farmhouse door?

The door is checked for thickness, alignment, and solid material around the lock area. Then the deadbolt is fitted, the bore is set or adjusted if needed, and the strike is mounted so the bolt throws cleanly into the jamb. On older doors, the reinforcement matters as much as the lock itself.

What should I tell you before you come out to install a deadbolt on my property?

Tell us which door needs the lock, whether it's a front door, side entry, tenant house, or outbuilding, and if the door is wood, steel, or something older. It also helps to know if the door sticks, the frame has been repaired, or there's already a deadbolt in place.

Can a weak strike area be fixed so the new deadbolt actually holds?

Yes, if the door and frame are still workable. A deadbolt only helps if the strike screws anchor into solid material, so a loose or split jamb may need reinforcement. If the framing is badly damaged, the hardware won't hold the way it should until that part is corrected.

Should I choose a single-cylinder deadbolt or a different style for a barn office or side door?

A single-cylinder deadbolt is common on doors where a key on the outside and thumbturn on the inside makes sense. Some doors, especially around outbuildings or unusual entry points, may call for a different setup. The right choice depends on how the door is used and whether there's safe inside access.

What if my Rising Sun door already has a deadbolt, but it doesn't line up right anymore?

That happens a lot on older doors and repaired frames. The lock may be reused if the problem is minor, but if the bolt is scraping, the strike is off, or the screw holes are worn out, a reset or replacement is usually the better fix. A proper fit matters more than keeping old hardware.
