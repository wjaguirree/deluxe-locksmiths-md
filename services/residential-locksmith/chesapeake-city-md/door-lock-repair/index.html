Door Lock Repair in Chesapeake City - common questions

My front door in Chesapeake City latches only if I lift it a little. What does that usually mean?

That usually points to a door or frame alignment problem, not just a bad lock. The hinges may be sagging, the strike plate may be off, or the wood may have shifted with age and weather. We check the full setup so the latch meets the strike cleanly instead of being forced into place.

What should I have ready before you repair a lock on my house?

If you can, know which door is giving trouble and what it's doing: sticking, rubbing, not turning, or not catching. It also helps to tell us whether it's a knob lock, deadbolt, or both. That lets us come prepared to work on the hardware that's actually causing the problem.

Is it a security risk if my deadbolt turns but the door still feels loose?

Yes. If the deadbolt isn't seating fully or the door isn't closing squarely, the lock may not be doing its full job. A loose-feeling door can also hide wear in the strike area or hinges. We repair the hardware and the fit so the door secures the way it should.

How do you decide whether to repair the lock or replace the hardware on an older Chesapeake City house?

We look at the condition of the latch, cylinder, strike, and door fit. If the lock is sound and the problem is alignment, repair is usually the better path. If internal parts are worn out or the hardware has failed repeatedly, replacement may make more sense. We'll explain what we find before work starts.

Can you fix a lock on a historic door without changing how it looks?

Often, yes. Many older doors just need careful adjustment, cleaned-up hardware, or small repairs to get them working again. If a part has to be replaced, we try to match the existing setup as closely as possible. The goal is to keep the door working without losing its character.
