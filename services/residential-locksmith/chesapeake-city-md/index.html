Residential locksmith in Chesapeake City - common questions

If I just moved into a house in Chesapeake City, should we rekey the locks or replace them?

Rekeying is a good choice when the locks still work well and you just want old keys to stop working. Replacement makes more sense if the hardware is worn, damaged, or outdated. We look at the door, the lock body, and the way it closes before we recommend one path over the other.

What should we have ready before a locksmith comes to our house near the historic district?

Have the door unlocked if possible, and make sure someone can point out which lock is giving trouble. If you want rekeying, bring any keys you have so we can see what's already in use. If the issue involves a smart lock or mailbox lock, it helps to know the model or show us the hardware.

What's the risk if a front door lock keeps sticking but still works for now?

A sticking lock often means worn parts, poor alignment, or a cylinder that's starting to fail. That can leave you locked out, or it can make the door easier to force. If the deadbolt doesn't throw fully, the door may look secured when it really isn't. It's worth fixing before it stops working altogether.

Should we choose a deadbolt upgrade or a high-security lock for an older Chesapeake City home?

A good deadbolt is the basic step if your door just needs stronger hardware. High-security locks make more sense when you want better key control and added resistance to picking, drilling, or unauthorized key copies. For older doors, we also check whether the frame and strike area need attention so the upgrade actually helps.

Can you replace a mailbox lock if the key is gone and the box is still on the house?

Yes. If the lock is accessible, we can usually replace the mailbox lock without changing the whole box. If the lock is damaged, painted over, or jammed from age, replacement may be the cleanest fix. We'll confirm what the mailbox needs once we see the hardware in person.
