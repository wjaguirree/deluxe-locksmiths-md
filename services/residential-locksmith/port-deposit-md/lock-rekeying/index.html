Lock Rekeying in Port Deposit - common questions

How does rekeying work on an older house in Port Deposit?

The lock cylinder is taken apart, the pins are changed to match a new key, and the old key stops working. On older houses, the lock should also be checked for wear, loose parts, and a door that no longer lines up cleanly. If the hardware is too worn, rekeying alone may not give a reliable result.

What should I have ready before you rekey my home locks?

Have the keys you still have, know which doors you want matched to one key, and be ready to point out any locks that stick or feel rough. If the home is older, it helps to mention which doors have been hard to close. That saves time troubleshooting once the work starts.

Can rekeying fix a lock that already feels loose or sticky?

Sometimes it can be serviced at the same visit, but rekeying does not magically repair broken parts. If the cylinder is worn, the latch is failing, or the door is out of alignment, the lock may still misbehave after the key change. A good result depends on the hardware being sound enough to keep using.

Should I rekey the whole house or just the doors with outside access?

Usually the doors that matter most are the exterior ones, since that's where control and security matter most. Some homeowners want all keyed locks on one key, while others only want the entry doors changed. The right choice depends on how many doors you use and whether the current keys were shared too widely.

What if I still have one old key but not all of them?

That's still workable in many cases. A locksmith can often rekey to a new key even if you only have one working old key, as long as the lock itself is in decent shape. If a lock has been rekeyed before and parts don't match well, you may need the hardware checked before the new keying is set.
