Door Lock Repair in Port Deposit - common questions

Why does my door latch at home but still need a shove to close?

That usually means the latch and strike are not lined up anymore. The door may have sagged, the frame may have shifted, or the strike plate may be worn. We check the full door setup, not just the lock body, so the latch can catch without the extra force.

What should I have ready before you come look at my lock?

If the door has been sticking, it helps to know when it started and whether the issue is with the knob, deadbolt, or both. If any keys are acting up, have those ready too. We can work with the hardware already on the door and decide what actually needs attention.

Could forcing the key or handle make the problem worse?

Yes. Forcing a sticky lock can bend parts inside the cylinder, wear the latch, or damage the strike and hinges. That can turn a small alignment issue into a bigger repair. If the lock is resisting, it's better to stop and have it checked before more parts get stressed.

Should I repair the existing lock or replace it on my house in Port Deposit?

If the body of the lock is still sound, repair is often the better first step. If the internal parts are worn, the key is badly chewed up, or the hardware no longer lines up even after adjustment, replacement may make more sense. We'll tell you which path fits the door.

What if my old stone house door has a warped frame and the deadbolt never lines up right?

That's common in older homes, and it does not always mean the lock itself is bad. We look at hinge wear, strike placement, and how the door sits in the opening. Sometimes the fix is an adjustment. Sometimes the hardware needs to be reworked so the deadbolt can seat correctly.
