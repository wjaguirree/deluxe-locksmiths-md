Lock Repair in Port Deposit - common questions

Why does my lock on a Port Deposit stone house keep sticking when the door looks fine?

Old doors can look straight and still be slightly out of line. In a house that has settled over time, the bolt may be rubbing the strike or the latch may not be centered anymore. We check the lock and the door together, then repair the part that's causing the bind instead of guessing.

What should I have ready before you repair my front door lock?

Have the door unlocked if you can, and make sure we can reach both sides of the door. If the lock has been acting up only at certain times, tell us what you've noticed. That helps us separate a lock problem from a frame or door alignment issue.

Can a worn lock be fixed without changing the whole handle set?

Often, yes. If the housing, cylinder, and latch are still usable, we can repair the failing part and keep the rest in place. If the body is cracked, stripped, or badly worn inside, replacement is the safer call. We'll tell you which parts are worth keeping.

Should I rekey or repair if I just got a house near Main Street?

If the lock works well and you only want new keys to work, rekeying is the cleaner choice. If the key is hard to turn, the bolt drags, or the latch is loose, repair comes first. Sometimes both are needed: fix the hardware, then rekey it so the old keys no longer work.

What if my deadbolt only fails when the weather changes along the river?

That can happen when wood swells, frames move a little, or screws loosen over time. We can often correct the alignment and tune the lock so it catches properly again. If the bolt or cylinder is worn out as well, we'll explain whether repair still makes sense or if replacement is the better fix.
