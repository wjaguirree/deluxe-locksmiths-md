Deadbolt Installation in Port Deposit - common questions

How do you install a deadbolt on an older house in Port Deposit?

We start by checking the door, the frame, and where the bolt will actually land. On older homes, the opening may be out of square or the strike area may be worn. We fit the deadbolt so it engages cleanly, then secure the strike so the lock has something solid to hold against.

What should I have ready before you install a new deadbolt?

It helps to know which door you want secured and whether you want to keep the same key as your other locks. If there's an old deadbolt or a damaged strike plate, we can work with that. If the door has been sticking or shifting, let us know before we start.

Why is my deadbolt hard to throw even though the lock is new?

That usually means the lock is fine but the alignment isn't. The bolt may be hitting the strike off-center, or the frame may have moved. In Port Deposit, that's common on older doors and stone homes. If the bolt doesn't seat fully, the lock won't protect the way it should.

Should I replace the whole lock or just add a deadbolt?

If the existing handle set works and the door is in decent shape, adding a properly fitted deadbolt may be enough. If the hardware is worn, the bore is damaged, or the door doesn't close well, replacing more than one part can make sense. We'll look at the door first and explain the options plainly.

Can a deadbolt be added if my door is narrow or the frame is uneven?

Often, yes. Narrow doors and uneven frames need careful measuring and the right hardware, but that doesn't automatically rule out a deadbolt. The key is whether the door has enough solid material and whether the strike can be anchored properly. If not, we'll say so and explain what can be done instead.
