Lock Rekeying in New Castle - common questions

How does lock rekeying work for an older house in New Castle?

We take the lock apart, change the internal pins or parts that match the key, and put it back together so old keys stop working. On older houses, we first check whether the lock is sound enough to keep. If the hardware is worn or damaged, rekeying may not be the right fix.

What should I have ready before you come rekey my house?

Have the doors you want changed unlocked and make sure we can reach the locks safely. It helps to know which doors should work on one key and which should stay separate. If there are old keys, deadbolts, or extra locks on the property, bring them out so we can match everything the right way.

Can rekeying stop someone who still has an old key from getting in?

Yes, that is one of the main reasons people rekey after a move or a breakup. Once the lock is rekeyed, the old key no longer fits the new key pattern. If someone may have copied the key, rekeying is a practical way to take back control without replacing every lock.

Should I rekey or replace the lock on a New Castle row house?

If the lock is solid and the door fits well, rekeying is usually the first choice. If the hardware is cracked, loose, badly rusted, or not operating smoothly, replacement may make more sense. We look at the lock you already have and give a plain answer based on that hardware, not on guesswork.

Can you rekey every door in my house to one key, even if the locks are different?

Often, yes, as long as the lock types are compatible and the hardware is in usable shape. Some older locks in historic homes can be trickier, while newer standard locks are easier to match. We check each door and let you know which ones can be grouped together and which ones need a different setup.
