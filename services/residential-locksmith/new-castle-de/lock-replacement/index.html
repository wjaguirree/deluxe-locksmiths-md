Lock Replacement in New Castle - common questions

Can you replace an old lock on a historic New Castle door without ruining the look?

Often, yes. We match the new hardware to the door as closely as we can and keep the work clean and practical. On older doors, the bigger issue is usually fit and function, so we choose replacement parts that work with the existing opening instead of forcing the door to accept the wrong lock.

What should I have ready before you replace the locks on my house in New Castle?

Have the doors you want changed accessible and know which locks matter most, like front, side, or back entries. If the home is rented, it helps to have the landlord's approval or the property manager's instructions. If you want all the locks keyed alike, say that up front so we can plan the hardware accordingly.

My lock still works, but the key sticks and the deadbolt feels rough. Does that mean it needs replacing?

Not always, but it can. Sometimes the issue is the strike alignment or a dirty cylinder. If the lock body is worn, the keyway is damaged, or the bolt doesn't travel smoothly anymore, replacement is usually the better call. We look at the whole setup before deciding whether repair or replacement makes more sense.

Should I choose a deadbolt, a keyed knob, or a lever lock for my New Castle door?

It depends on the door and how it's used. A deadbolt gives stronger security on most exterior doors. A keyed knob or lever is often part of a complete entry set, but it shouldn't be the only lock you rely on. We can look at the door, the frame, and the existing holes before recommending what fits best.

Can you replace the lock if the door on my New Castle rental has been altered by past tenants?

Usually, yes, but the condition of the door matters. Extra holes, mismatched hardware, or damaged strike areas can limit what will fit cleanly. In those cases, we may need to use hardware that covers old work or make small adjustments so the new lock fits securely and operates the way it should.
