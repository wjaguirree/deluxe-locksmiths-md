Rekeying changes which key works in your locks without replacing the hardware. For a move-in, a lost key, or a tenant turnover, that's usually the first step. The job should be more than swapping pins around and handing over a new key. A proper rekey means each cylinder is opened, inspected, repinned to the new key, and checked so the old key no longer works. Signs It's Time to Rekey

A lot of people wait until a lock quits before they call, but rekeying usually shows its need earlier. If your key starts hanging up, if you have to lift or wiggle the knob to turn it, or if the deadbolt only works when you pull the door hard against the frame, the problem may be inside the cylinder, not the whole lock. In older homes around downtown Harrington, that kind of wear is common, especially after years of the same doors getting used hard in a place that stays quiet most of the year and then gets heavy traffic during fair week.

Another sign is lost control of the keys. If a tenant moved out, a room key vanished, or you bought a home and don't know who else still has a copy, rekeying is the clean way to reset access without changing good hardware. We also recommend it after a breakup, a roommate change, or any move where you can't be sure every old key is accounted for. On roads like US-13, people often come and go with work, rentals, and seasonal stays, so it's easy for keys to spread farther than you think.

Acting on those warning signs is easier than waiting for a failure because the lock is still working enough for us to service it cleanly. Once it stops turning, starts binding badly, or a key breaks off inside, the job can get more involved. Rekeying gives you a fresh key set, clears out old access, and helps keep a small issue from turning into a door that won't open when you need it to.

For an owner, rekeying is about control. If you bought the house, lost track of old copies, or had work done by several different people, you want to know who can get in now. For a tenant, the decision depends on the lease and the landlord's rules. In many cases the landlord handles it or has to approve it, especially if the locks need to be kept keyed alike for the property. For a business, the goal is usually tighter key control for employees, managers, and service doors without replacing every lockset.

The job also plays out differently by building. A single-family house may need all exterior doors keyed to one key. A rental might only need certain locks changed, with the originals saved for move-out. A small office or shop near the Delaware State Fairgrounds may need the front entry, back door, and storage areas treated as separate access points. The right setup is the one that matches how the property is actually used, not a one-size-fits-all key plan.

Lock Rekeying in Harrington - common questions

How does a residential rekey work in a Harrington house?

The lock cylinders are taken apart, the pins are reset to a new key, and the old key is retired. The hardware stays in place unless a lock is too worn or damaged. Afterward, each door should be tested so you know the new key works smoothly and the old one doesn't.

What should I have ready before rekeying after a move?

Have every exterior door key, any deadbolt or knob you want included, and a clear idea of which doors should match. If you're renting, bring the landlord's approval if that's part of your lease. It also helps to point out any sticky locks, loose hardware, or doors that have been giving you trouble.

Can a rekey fix a lock that feels worn out or unreliable?

Sometimes, but not always. Rekeying changes the keying; it does not repair broken springs, stripped parts, or a cylinder that's too far gone. If the lock is worn or damaged, the honest answer may be that it needs repair or replacement instead of a rekey.

Is rekeying better than changing every lock on the house?

If the hardware is in good shape, rekeying is usually the cleaner choice because it keeps the existing locks and changes the key that operates them. Full replacement makes more sense when the lockset is damaged, mismatched, or you want a different style. The right answer depends on the condition of the doors.

What if my Harrington home has different brands of locks on each door?

That's common in older homes and isn't a problem by itself. Different brands can often still be rekeyed, but not every lock can be made to match every other one. If one door uses a different system, you may end up with separate keys unless the hardware is changed or adjusted.
