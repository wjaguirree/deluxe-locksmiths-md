Residential locksmith in Harrington - common questions

If I just bought a house in Harrington, do I need to change every lock or can you rekey them?

Rekeying is often the right first step if the locks are in good shape. It lets us make the old keys stop working while keeping the same hardware on the doors. If a lock is worn out, mismatched, or damaged, replacement can make more sense than trying to save it.

What should I have ready before you come out to rekey my doors?

If you can, gather every key you still have, note which doors are giving you trouble, and tell us whether the locks are all supposed to work with one key. It also helps to mention any past repairs, sticky doors, or hardware that was installed by a previous owner.

My front door sticks and the deadbolt is hard to turn. Is that a lock problem or a door problem?

It can be either, and sometimes it's both. In older Harrington homes, a shifted door frame or loose strike plate can make a good lock feel bad. We check the door alignment, latch, and deadbolt together so we fix the cause instead of only treating the symptom.

Should I choose a smart lock or a regular deadbolt for my house near the fairgrounds?

It depends on how you use the door. A regular deadbolt is straightforward and reliable. A smart lock is useful if you want keypad access or want to avoid carrying keys. We can look at the door, the existing hardware, and how the home is used before recommending one over the other.

Can you replace a mailbox lock if the old key is lost and the door is bent?

Sometimes, yes. If the mailbox body and lock area are still usable, we can usually replace the lock. If the box is bent badly or damaged beyond a clean repair, replacement of the mailbox unit may be needed instead. We'll tell you which option fits the hardware you have.
