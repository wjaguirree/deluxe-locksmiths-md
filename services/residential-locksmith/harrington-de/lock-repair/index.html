Lock Repair in Harrington - common questions

Why does my front door lock in Harrington keep sticking even when the key looks fine?

That usually means the problem is in the door, frame, or lock hardware rather than the key itself. In Harrington homes, settling and seasonal changes can shift the latch out of line. A repair may involve adjusting the strike, tightening loose parts, cleaning worn components, or rebuilding the lock so it operates smoothly again.

What should I have ready when you come to repair a house lock?

Have the key that normally works, and be ready to show which door is giving trouble and what it's doing. It also helps to point out whether the issue is with locking, unlocking, or the door closing. If the door has been forced or the hardware has broken, that information helps narrow down the repair.

Is a loose deadbolt on an older house a safety problem or just wear?

It can be both. A loose deadbolt may still function, but it can also mean the screws are pulling out, the strike is shifting, or the lock body is wearing down. On older houses, that kind of looseness deserves attention because it can keep getting worse until the door no longer secures the way it should.

How do you decide whether a lock should be repaired or replaced?

If the lock body is still sound and the problem is alignment, loose parts, or normal wear, repair is usually the first choice. If the mechanism is broken beyond practical repair, the keyway is badly damaged, or parts are missing, replacement may make more sense. The decision comes from what the lock is actually doing, not from guessing.

Can a farmhouse door out by Route 14 be repaired if the frame has shifted?

Often, yes. A shifted frame can throw the latch and deadbolt out of line, which makes the lock feel bad even when the hardware itself is still usable. In those cases, the repair may involve realigning the strike, correcting loose hinges, or adjusting the lock so the door closes and secures properly again.
