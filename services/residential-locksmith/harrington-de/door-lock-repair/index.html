Door Lock Repair in Harrington - common questions

My front door in Harrington closes, but the latch won't catch right. What do you check first?

We start with the latch and strike plate, then check whether the door has dropped on its hinges or shifted in the frame. A lot of the time the lock isn't broken at all; it's just out of alignment. If the hardware is worn or damaged, we repair or replace the affected parts so the door closes without force.

What should I have ready before you come out to repair a door lock?

It helps to know which door is giving you trouble, what it does when you try to lock it, and whether the issue changes with weather or use. If the key is involved, have it nearby. If the door has been forced, painted over, or previously repaired, that's useful to know too because it can change the fix.

Could forcing the door shut make the lock problem worse?

Yes. If the latch is already scraping or the deadbolt is binding, forcing the door can chew up the strike plate, bend the bolt, or loosen the frame. That turns a small alignment issue into a bigger repair. It's better to stop using force and have the door hardware checked before it wears further.

Should I repair the lock or replace it if it's on an older house near the town center?

If the body of the lock is still solid, repair is often the better first step. Older homes usually need the door and strike adjusted along with the lock itself. Replacement makes more sense when the hardware is cracked, stripped, or no longer holds up to normal use. We'll point out which option fits the condition of the door.

My deadbolt works, but the knob lock sticks on my house off Route 14. Is that a different problem?

It can be. The deadbolt and knob lock don't fail in exactly the same way. One may be fine while the other is rubbing, loose, or misaligned. We inspect both so we can tell whether the issue is in the lock, the door, or the frame. That keeps the repair focused instead of guessing at it.
