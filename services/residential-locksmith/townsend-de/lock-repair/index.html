Lock Repair in Townsend - common questions

How do you figure out whether my Townsend lock needs repair or replacement?

We look at the keyway, the cylinder, the latch, the strike, and how the door sits in the frame. If the lock body is sound, we usually try repair first. If the housing is cracked, the core is badly worn, or the parts no longer hold alignment, replacement makes more sense.

What should I have ready before you come out to repair the lock on my house or shed?

It helps to know which door is acting up, whether the key is hard to turn or the bolt won't throw cleanly, and if anything changed recently, like a door that started dragging. If you have the working key, keep it handy. That lets us test the hardware as we go.

Is it a problem if the lock sticks only when the weather changes in Townsend?

That's common with wood doors and older frames. Humidity can swell the door and throw the latch out of line. In those cases, the lock may not be the only issue. We check the fit of the door, the strike, and the lock together so we fix the real cause instead of treating the symptom.

Should I repair the old deadbolt or switch to new hardware on my Townsend home?

If the lock is still solid and the problem is wear or misalignment, repair is often the better move. If the keyway is chewed up, the internal parts are failing, or the hardware is too light for the door, a new deadbolt is the smarter choice. We'll explain both options clearly.

Can you repair a lock on a detached shed or farm building that gets used hard?

Yes. Those locks often fail from dust, moisture, and rough use, especially on side doors and outbuildings. We can service the cylinder, replace worn parts, or fit hardware that stands up better to the way the door is used. If the door itself is warped, we'll say so.
