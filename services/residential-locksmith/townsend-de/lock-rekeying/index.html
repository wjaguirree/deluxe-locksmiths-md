Lock Rekeying in Townsend - common questions

How does a house rekey work if the locks are still in good shape?

We take apart the cylinder, change the pin setup inside, and make a new key that fits the new combination. The outer hardware stays in place. It's a good choice when the lock body is solid but you want to stop old keys from working.

What should we have ready before you come rekey the doors?

Have every lock you want matched or changed ready to point out, along with any working keys you still have. If there's a shed, side door, or detached garage on the property, tell us about that too so we can plan the keying the right way.

What can go wrong if someone tries to rekey an older Townsend house lock by hand?

The most common problem is uneven pinning, which makes the key bind or fail partway in. Another is missing worn parts that should've been replaced. When that happens, the lock may seem fixed but still act sticky or unreliable. A proper rekey should feel smooth and deliberate.

Should we rekey the locks or replace them after a move?

If the locks are solid, rekeying is usually the cleaner move because it keeps the hardware and changes the key. If a lock is cracked, badly worn, or damaged from forced entry, replacement may make more sense. We'll look at each lock and tell you which path fits it.

Can you rekey a shed or outbuilding lock that's separate from the house?

Yes, if the lock is the type that can be rekeyed and the hardware is still in usable shape. That's a common request on properties with detached sheds, workshops, or equipment storage. We can also match it to the house if you want one key to cover both.
