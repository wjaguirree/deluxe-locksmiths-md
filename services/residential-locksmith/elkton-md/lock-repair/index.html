Lock Repair in Elkton - common questions

My front door lock sticks in my Elkton house. What do you check first?

We start with the lock, then check the door and frame together. A sticky lock is often caused by misalignment, worn internal parts, or a latch that no longer meets the strike cleanly. If the door has shifted or swollen, fixing only the lock won't solve it. We look for the cause before deciding on repair.

What should I have ready before we come out to repair my lock?

Please have the door unlocked if possible, and let us know whether the lock has been hard to turn, loose, or damaged by a forced entry. If you have a spare key, keep it handy. It also helps to tell us if the door has been repaired before or if it only acts up in certain weather.

Can a worn lock be repaired, or does it usually need to be replaced?

It depends on what's worn out. If the issue is alignment, a loose part, or a key that no longer works smoothly, repair may be enough. If the internal parts are badly damaged, the cylinder is failing, or the lock has been forced, replacement may be the better call. We'll explain what makes sense before doing the work.

I'm worried the door won't latch right after the repair. Is that something you can fix too?

Yes. A lock repair often includes checking how the latch meets the strike and whether the door is hanging square. If the door sags, the lock can feel broken even when the hardware is still usable. We'd rather correct the alignment than leave you with a lock that still binds or doesn't secure the door well.

My lock works, but the key is getting harder to turn. Is that an early warning sign?

Usually, yes. That can point to wear inside the cylinder, dirt buildup, or a door that's putting side pressure on the bolt. It's better to address it before the key stops turning or breaks in the lock. In many cases, a repair and adjustment can restore smooth use without replacing everything.
