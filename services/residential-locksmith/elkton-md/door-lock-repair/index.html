Door Lock Repair in Elkton - common questions

My front door in Elkton latches only if I lift the handle. Is that a lock problem or a door problem?

Usually it's a door and hardware alignment issue, not just the lock itself. The latch may be rubbing because the door has settled, the hinges have loosened, or the strike plate is slightly off. We check both the lock and the way the door sits so the fix addresses the real cause.

What should I have ready before you come repair a lock on my house?

It helps to know which door is acting up, what it does when it fails, and whether the problem is with a key, knob, lever, or deadbolt. If you still have the key that normally works, bring it along. Photos of the door edge, strike, and hardware can also help explain the issue before the work starts.

Can a loose strike plate damage the lock if I keep using the door?

Yes. If the latch keeps missing the strike or hitting it wrong, the hardware wears faster and the door frame can take the abuse. You may also end up forcing the knob or deadbolt, which can bend parts inside. Getting the alignment corrected early usually prevents a bigger repair later.

Should I repair the existing lock or replace it on an older Main Street house?

If the hardware is solid and only out of line, repair is often enough. If the cylinder is worn, the latch spring is weak, or parts are missing from a previous repair, replacement can make more sense. We look at the condition of the whole setup, not just the symptom, before recommending either path.

My deadbolt works with the door open but sticks when the door is shut. What does that usually mean?

That usually means the bolt and strike are not lining up cleanly. The door may be sagging, the strike may be shifted, or the frame may have changed shape over time. In some cases the bolt itself is worn. A proper repair checks all of those points before anything gets replaced.
