Lock Repair in Perryville - common questions

How do you figure out whether my lock needs repair or replacement?

We look at how the lock works on the door, not just at the broken part. If the problem is wear, loose hardware, or bad alignment, repair usually makes sense. If the body is cracked, seized, or badly worn inside, replacement may be the better option.

What should I have ready before you come to my home in Perryville?

Please make sure we can reach the door and that any pets are secured. If the lock has been acting up for a while, it helps to know whether the key turns, sticks, or only fails when the door is shut. That detail often points us to the right fix sooner.

My deadbolt works only when I lift the door a little. Is that a lock problem?

Sometimes it is, but often the lock is only showing a door or frame alignment problem. We check the hinges, strike, latch, and bolt together. If the door has settled, repairing the lock alone won't hold. We fix the full setup so the bolt lines up the way it should.

Should I repair the lock or switch to a new one if the key is hard to turn?

If the issue is dirt, worn springs, loose mounting, or a strike that is slightly off, repair is often enough. If the cylinder is badly worn or the key profile no longer matches cleanly, a new lock can be the better call. We'll explain what we find before doing the work.

Can you repair a lock that was damaged after a break-in attempt?

In some cases, yes. If the lock body, strike, or door edge is only bent or pulled loose, we can often rebuild the setup. If the cylinder, housing, or door hardware is badly damaged, repair may not hold. We'll inspect the whole door and tell you what's still usable.
