Why the Install Matters

The shortcuts we see most often are simple, and they cause the same problems over and over. Some locks get mounted with short strike screws that only bite into the trim, not the framing. Others use a heavy deadbolt but leave the hole slightly off, so the bolt drags or barely catches the strike. We also run into doors where someone reused old hardware on a warped slab and called it done. That can leave the lock looking solid while the door still gives under pressure. On a side street off Broad Street or in an older house near the riverfront, that kind of quick fix can mean a door that won't latch cleanly when the weather shifts or when the frame settles.

Another shortcut is skipping the fit check. A deadbolt needs the right backset, clean alignment, and a strike plate that matches the bolt throw. If those details are off, the key can turn hard today and fail altogether later. We've also seen people keep the old screws, which is a bad habit. A deadbolt is only as strong as the screws holding the strike and the screws anchoring the lock body. If those fasteners don't reach solid wood, the lock can pop loose under a hard shove, and the whole point is lost.

The other mistake is choosing hardware for the label instead of the door. A grade-rated deadbolt still needs to be fitted properly to the door thickness, edge bore, and frame condition. Deadbolt Installation in Perryville - common questions

How does a deadbolt installation usually work on my Perryville front door?

We check the door, frame, and existing holes first, then fit the deadbolt so the latch and strike line up correctly. If the door already has a lock in place, we see whether it can be reused or if the opening needs to be adjusted. The goal is a lock that turns smoothly and holds the door solidly.

What should I have ready before you install a deadbolt at my house?

It helps to know what door needs the lock, whether it's wood, metal, or fiberglass, and if you want matching keys for other doors. If there's an old lock on the door, leave it in place unless it's loose or damaged. We can work through the rest on site.

Can a deadbolt help if my door feels solid but the frame looks worn?

Yes, but the frame matters just as much as the lock. A strong deadbolt can only do its job if the strike is anchored into sound framing. If the wood around the strike is split or weak, we'll point that out and make the adjustments needed so the lock has something dependable to bite into.

Should I choose a single-cylinder deadbolt or a double-cylinder deadbolt for my Perryville home?

Most homes do well with a single-cylinder deadbolt, which uses a key outside and a thumb turn inside. A double-cylinder model uses a key on both sides and can make sense in some cases, but it can also be less convenient in daily use. We'll look at the door and talk through what fits the layout.

What if my door is old and the new deadbolt won't line up with the strike?

That's common on older doors, especially where the frame has settled or the door has been replaced at some point. We can often move the strike, adjust the bore, or correct the alignment so the deadbolt seats properly. If the door itself is too far gone, we'll say so plainly.
