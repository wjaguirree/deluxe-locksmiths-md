Residential locksmith in Perryville - common questions

If I just moved into a Perryville house, should I rekey the locks or replace them?

Rekeying is usually the first choice if the locks are in good shape and you just want new keys to work. Replace the lock if it's worn, mismatched, damaged, or the hardware doesn't hold the door securely. A look at the door and lock body usually makes the answer clear.

What should I have ready before you come out to work on my home locks?

Have the doors unlocked if possible, and know which doors need attention: front, back, garage entry, or mailbox. If the home is a rental or part of an estate, it helps to have the owner's approval or any paperwork showing you're allowed to request the work.

Can a new lock damage an older door near the riverfront part of Perryville?

It shouldn't. Older doors can be softer, uneven, or already patched from past hardware changes, so the work needs to be done carefully. The right approach is to fit the lock to the door instead of forcing the door to fit the lock.

Should I choose a smart lock or a high-security deadbolt for a Perryville home?

A smart lock works well if you want keypad access or phone control and the door is in good shape. A high-security deadbolt is better if you want a more traditional lock with stronger resistance to picking or forced entry. Some homes do well with both on different doors.

What if my mailbox lock or a side door lock is the only thing giving me trouble?

That can often be repaired or replaced without changing the rest of the house locks. Mailbox locks, side entries, and utility doors often wear out at different speeds. If the key still turns but the mechanism binds, repair may be enough; if the parts are worn out, replacement is the better fix.
