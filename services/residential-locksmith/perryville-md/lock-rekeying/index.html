How Rekeying Works

Inside the lock, the key doesn't move the latch by itself. It lines up a small stack of pins or wafers inside the cylinder, and that alignment lets the plug turn. When we rekey, we change that internal pattern so the old key no longer lines up, while a new key does. The hardware on the door stays in place, but the part that recognizes the key gets reset. That's why rekeying is usually the first move after a change in tenants, a move into a new house, or when you've lost track of who has a copy.

From the outside, worn parts usually show up as a key that needs to be wiggled, a lock that feels gritty, or a cylinder that turns only if you push harder than usual. Sometimes the key works when the door is open but binds when the door is closed and the latch is under pressure. Other times, a key that used to slide in cleanly starts catching on the way in. Those are signs the pins, springs, or plug are tired, dirty, or mismatched from past use. We see that often in older homes near Downtown Perryville and in busy entry doors along US-40, where family members, landlords, and tenants have passed keys around for years.

When we rekey, we inspect the cylinder, reset the internal parts, and make sure the new key matches the lock the way it should. That gives you control over who can open the door without changing the whole handle or deadbolt. For many homeowners, that means the front door, back door, and garage entry can all be brought under one new keying setup, with the old keys left out of the picture.

Most rekeying jobs in Perryville come down to one of three setups. First is a move-in rekey, where we change the pinning so old keys stop working and the new owner controls access. Second is a same-house rekey, where a homeowner wants to keep the hardware but update who can enter after a lost key, a breakup, or a change in household access. Third is a multi-lock setup, where we rekey several doors to the same key so the front, side, and back doors all match.

The details matter in practice. A newer deadbolt may be straightforward to rekey if the brand and cylinder are standard. An older lock may need disassembly and a closer look for worn parts, stripped screws, or past repairs that weren't done cleanly. If one door is a better candidate for replacement than rekeying, we'll say so. The goal is simple: keep the hardware that still has life in it and reset the keying so the right people can get in.

Lock Rekeying in Perryville - common questions

How does lock rekeying work on a house in Perryville?

We take the lock cylinder apart, change the internal pins or tumblers, and set it up for a new key. The outside hardware stays on the door. That's a good fit after a move, after keys are lost, or when you want one key to work several doors in the house.

What should I have ready before you rekey my home locks?

Have the doors unlocked if possible and know which locks you want to change. It also helps to gather any working keys you already have, including copies. If you want the same key on multiple doors, tell us that up front so we can set the locks to match.

If I just moved near the Perryville MARC station, is rekeying enough or should I replace the locks?

Rekeying is usually enough if the hardware is in good shape and you only need to control who has access. Replacement makes more sense when the lock is badly worn, damaged, or doesn't work smoothly. We can look at the door and tell you which option fits the hardware you already have.

Can all the locks in my Perryville house be made to use the same key?

Often, yes. If the lock brands and cylinder types are compatible, we can rekey several doors to one key for easier use. If a lock is a different style or isn't a good match, we'll explain that before we do anything so you know what can be tied together and what can't.

What if my lock is old or has been worked on before?

That doesn't rule out rekeying, but it can change the job. Older cylinders may have worn parts, and previous repairs can leave mixed components inside. We inspect the lock first, then decide whether rekeying will hold up or whether a replacement is the better fix for that door.
