Lock Repair in Clayton - common questions

How do you repair a house lock in Clayton without replacing the whole thing?

We inspect the lock, the strike, and the way the door closes, then fix the part that is causing the trouble. That might mean tightening hardware, correcting alignment, cleaning the cylinder, or replacing worn internal parts. If the lock body is still sound, there's no reason to replace more than needed.

What should I have ready before you come to fix my front door lock?

Please make sure the door is accessible and let us know which lock is acting up, when it started, and whether the key still works. If you've noticed the door rubbing, sticking, or needing to be lifted, that helps us narrow it down. Having a working key on hand is also useful.

Can a misaligned lock damage the door or frame if I keep using it?

It can. When the bolt or latch keeps scraping the strike, it puts extra wear on the lock and can chew up the door edge or frame over time. If the door is already sitting off line, the problem usually gets worse gradually. Fixing the alignment early is the better move.

Should I repair a loose deadbolt or just replace it with a new one?

If the deadbolt body is still solid, repair is often the right choice. Loose mounting screws, a shifted strike, or a door that has settled can make a good deadbolt feel bad. We only suggest replacement when the lock is worn out, damaged inside, or no longer worth putting back into service.

My back door lock sticks only when the weather changes. Is that still a lock problem?

Sometimes it is, and sometimes the door itself is moving with the season. Wood doors can swell or shrink, which changes how the latch meets the strike. We check both the lock and the door fit so we can tell whether the issue is inside the hardware or in the way the door is hanging.
