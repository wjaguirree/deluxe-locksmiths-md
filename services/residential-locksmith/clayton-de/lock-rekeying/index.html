Lock Rekeying in Clayton - common questions

How does a house rekey actually work in Clayton?

The lock stays on the door, but the inside pins or tumblers are changed so the old key no longer works. A new key is then cut to match the updated lock. It's a practical choice for Clayton homes because it keeps the existing hardware in place while changing access control.

What should I have ready before you rekey my place near Route 300?

Have the doors and locks you want changed identified, and make sure someone with authority over the home is present. If you want several doors matched to one key, point out every lock you want included. It also helps to know whether any keys are missing so the lock plan can be set the way you want.

Can an older Clayton house with old hardware still be rekeyed safely?

Often yes, but not every old lock is worth rekeying as-is. If the cylinder is worn, sticky, or missing parts, the safer move may be to repair or replace that lock first. On older railroad-town houses, the condition of the hardware matters just as much as the keying itself.

Should I rekey or replace the locks after buying a home in Clayton?

If the hardware is solid, rekeying is usually the first thing to do after a move because it changes who can open the doors without changing the whole lock. Replacement makes more sense when the lock is damaged, badly worn, or not the style you want to keep. The right choice depends on the condition of each door.

Can you make my front door, side door, and garage entry all use one key?

Usually, yes, if the lock types are compatible and in good working shape. That's a common request in newer subdivision homes and in older houses that have had several entry points added over time. If one lock is a different style or badly worn, it may need extra work or a different plan.
