Door Lock Repair in Clayton - common questions

My front door in Clayton latches only if I push hard. What usually causes that?

That usually means the lock and strike are no longer lined up. It can come from a loose hinge, a shifted strike plate, a swollen door, or worn latch parts. The fix is to find where the misalignment starts and correct that instead of just forcing the door shut.

What should I check before I call about a sticking door lock?

Check whether the problem happens with the latch, the deadbolt, or both. See if the door rubs the frame, if screws are loose, or if the strike looks shifted. Don't force the key or keep slamming the door. Those habits can bend parts that were still usable.

Can a lock be repaired if the deadbolt works but the latch won't catch in my Clayton house?

Yes, often it can. The latch and deadbolt do different jobs, so one can fail while the other still works. That usually points to alignment, wear at the strike, or a problem with the latch mechanism itself. It does not always mean the whole lock needs to be replaced.

If my rental door in Clayton sticks, should I try to fix it myself first?

Usually no. In a rental, it's better to report the problem and let the owner or manager decide on the repair. You can note whether the issue is at the latch side, the hinge side, or only when the deadbolt is turned. Avoid drilling, filing, or swapping parts without permission.

What if the door frame is the real problem and not the lock?

That happens a lot, especially in older homes. If the frame has shifted or the door has sagged, the lock may be fine but still fail to line up. In that case, the repair has to address the door and strike together. A lock part change alone won't solve it if the frame is out of position.
