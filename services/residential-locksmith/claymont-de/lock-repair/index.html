Lock Repair in Claymont - common questions

Why does my front door lock in Claymont keep sticking even when the key still fits?

Sticking usually comes from wear, paint buildup, a loose latch, or a door that has shifted in the frame. In Claymont, older doors often have a few issues at once, so we check the lock, the strike, and the door alignment before deciding what actually needs repair.

What should I have ready when you come to repair a lock at my house?

If possible, have the door key that normally works, know whether the problem is on the front, back, or side door, and tell us if the hardware has been painted, forced, or recently adjusted. If you're a renter, it also helps to know whether your landlord wants to approve any parts before replacement.

Is a loose lock on an older Claymont house a safety problem or just a nuisance?

It can be both. A loose lock can let the latch fall out of alignment, which makes the door easier to jostle and harder to secure cleanly. On older homes, the issue may start small, but it usually gets worse if the screws, strike, or door edge keep working themselves loose.

Should I repair the lock or replace it on my painted-over door?

If the lock body still works and the trouble is wear, misalignment, or buildup, repair is often the better first step. If the inside parts are broken, the key is badly worn, or the hardware is so damaged that it won't hold adjustment, replacement may make more sense. We'll inspect it and explain what we see.

Can you fix a lock on a tenant door in Claymont without changing the whole set?

Often, yes. If the problem is a stuck latch, a loose knob, or a strike that has drifted out of line, we can usually repair those parts without replacing everything. If the building rules require landlord approval for changes, we'll keep the repair focused on the issue at hand.
