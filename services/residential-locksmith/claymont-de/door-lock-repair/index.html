Door Lock Repair in Claymont - common questions

Why does my front door in Claymont need to be pulled or lifted before the lock catches?

That usually means the door and frame are out of alignment. The latch may be hitting the strike plate instead of entering it cleanly. We look at the hinges, the strike, and the latch together, then repair the part causing the misalignment so the door closes normally again.

What should I have ready before you repair a door lock at my house?

It helps to know which door is acting up, whether the problem is with the knob, deadbolt, or both, and whether the key still turns. If the lock has been painted over, forced, or recently hit, mention that too. That gives us a clear starting point when we arrive.

Could forcing a sticky lock damage the door?

Yes. Forcing a latch or deadbolt can strip screws, crack the strike, bend the bolt, or split the frame edge. If the lock is already sticking, it's better to stop using force and have it checked. We can usually tell whether the problem is wear, alignment, or a failed part.

Is it better to repair my lock or replace it?

If the lock body is sound and the issue is wear, paint buildup, or poor alignment, repair is often the right move. If the cylinder is damaged, the internal parts are failing, or the hardware no longer matches the door well, replacement makes more sense. We'll look at both before deciding.

My old lock in Claymont is painted shut. Can that still be repaired?

Often, yes. Painted-over locks on older homes can sometimes be freed, cleaned, and brought back into service if the internal parts are still usable. If the paint has locked up the mechanism or the hardware is already worn out, we may need to replace the affected parts instead.
