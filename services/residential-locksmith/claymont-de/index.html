Residential locksmith in Claymont - common questions

How does a residential rekey work if I just moved into a house in Claymont?

We change the pins inside the existing lock so the old keys stop working and a new key works instead. If the hardware is in good shape, that's often the cleanest way to secure a move-in. If the lock is worn, painted over, or already damaged, we may recommend repair or replacement instead.

What should I have ready before someone comes out to work on my front door?

It helps to have the door open so we can inspect the latch, deadbolt, and strike plate. If you know the lock brand or have the old key, bring that along. We can usually work from the hardware on site, but details like a sticking key, loose handle, or past repairs help us choose the right fix.

My deadbolt sticks on an older house near Philadelphia Pike. Is that the lock or the door?

It can be either one, and often it's both. The bolt may be worn, but older doors also shift, swell, or get painted in ways that throw off alignment. We check the bolt, cylinder, latch, and strike plate together so we're not replacing good hardware while the real issue is the fit.

Should I rekey the locks or replace them when I buy a home in Claymont?

If the existing locks are solid and you just need new keys, rekeying is usually enough. If the hardware is loose, corroded, outdated, or hard to turn, replacement is the better choice. For doors with mixed old and new parts, we'll look at each opening separately instead of treating the whole house the same way.

Can you work on a smart lock if my storm door or trim makes the fit tight?

Sometimes yes, but the door has to be prepared correctly. Smart locks need proper alignment, enough clearance, and hardware that doesn't bind when the door closes. If the door or trim is out of square, we may need to repair the existing lock set or adjust the door first so the smart lock doesn't fail later.
