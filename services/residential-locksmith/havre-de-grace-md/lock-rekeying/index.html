Lock Rekeying in Havre de Grace - common questions

What actually gets changed when you rekey the locks on a house in Havre de Grace?

The lock cylinder gets reset so the old key no longer matches the pins inside. The outer hardware stays on the door if it's in good shape. You get new working keys, and anyone with an old copy loses access unless they also have a key that matches the new setup.

What should I have ready before someone rekeys the locks after a move?

Have the doors and hardware accessible, and gather every key you already have for the house. It also helps to know whether the locks are all supposed to work with one key or with separate keys. If you're unsure, a quick walk-through of the doors is usually enough to sort it out.

How do I know if my problem is rekeying, or if the lock is actually worn out?

If the lock turns well but you want control over old keys, rekeying fits. If the key drags, the cylinder feels rough, or the latch is off even with the right key, the hardware may be worn or the door may need adjustment. Those are different problems and should be handled differently.

Should I rekey all the doors at once, or just the front door?

If one key opens several doors now, rekeying them together can keep the house simpler. If only one entry changed hands, you may only need that lock reset. The decision usually comes down to how the home is set up and who should still have access to each door.

Can every residential lock in town be rekeyed, or are there exceptions?

Most standard pin-tumbler locks can be rekeyed if the parts are still usable. Some older or specialty hardware may need repair, and some damaged locks are better replaced. If the lock has been forced, rusted, or modified over time, the hardware may not be a good candidate anymore.
