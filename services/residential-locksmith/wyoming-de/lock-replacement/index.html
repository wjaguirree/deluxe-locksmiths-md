Lock Replacement in Wyoming - common questions

My Wyoming house still has the old mortise lock. Can it be replaced without changing the whole door?

Often, yes. Many older doors can keep the same basic opening while the worn mortise parts are swapped or updated. The key is matching the new hardware to the existing pocket, strike, and door thickness. If the door has been repaired before, the fit needs a careful check so the replacement doesn't bind or sit loose.

What should I have ready before you replace a lock on my house in Wyoming?

A clear photo of the front and back of the door, the edge of the door, and the current lock helps a lot. If you still have an operating key, that can help identify the cylinder style. It's also useful to know whether the door has ever been drilled, patched, or reworked, since that can change what fits cleanly.

Is there any risk that a new lock won't line up with my existing strike plate?

Yes, especially on older doors where the frame has shifted or the original hardware has been changed before. A lock can be the right type and still not latch properly if the strike is off. If that happens, the fix is usually adjustment or repair at the same time, not forcing the new lock to work against the door.

Should I replace the whole lock or just the cylinder on my front door?

If the body of the lock is solid and only the key side is worn, a cylinder change may be enough. If the mechanism is loose, damaged, or doesn't latch right, a full replacement is usually the better choice. The right answer depends on the door, the hardware style, and how much of the old lock has worn down.

My key still works, but the lock feels rough. Is that a sign I should replace it now?

Usually, yes. A lock that starts turning hard, sticking, or grinding is often showing wear inside the mechanism. Waiting can leave you with a lock that fails completely or a key that stops turning altogether. On an older Wyoming home, that's especially worth addressing before the problem gets worse.
