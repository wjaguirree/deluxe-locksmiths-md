Door Lock Repair in Cecilton - common questions

My front door in Cecilton latches only if I lift it. What usually needs repair?

That usually points to a door that has dropped a little on the hinge side or a strike plate that no longer lines up with the latch. The lock may still be fine, but the hardware needs to be realigned so the bolt meets the opening cleanly instead of scraping or hanging up.

What should I check before you come look at the door lock?

Please make sure the door can be opened and closed normally from both sides, and tell us if the problem started after weather changes, new paint, or a hard slam. If you know which door acts up most, that helps. We can work with the hardware already on the door and decide what needs attention on site.

Could a sticking lock mean the door frame is the real problem?

Yes. In older Cecilton homes, the frame or hinges can shift enough that the lock gets blamed when the door is really out of alignment. If the latch and strike are fighting each other, forcing the lock can wear both parts faster. We look at the full door set, not just the knob or deadbolt.

Should I repair the existing lock or replace it if it still mostly works?

If the parts are sound and the issue is wear, looseness, or alignment, repair is often enough. If the cylinder is damaged, the bolt is bent, or the hardware is too worn to hold adjustment, replacement may be the better call. The right answer depends on how the door closes and how much life is left in the lock.

What if the lock works, but the door still has to be shoved shut every time?

That's a common edge case and it still counts as a lock repair job. The strike may need to be moved, the latch may need adjustment, or the hinges may need tightening and correction. A door should close with normal pressure. If it needs a shove, something in the setup is out of line.
