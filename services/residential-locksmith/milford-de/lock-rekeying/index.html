If a rekey is done wrong, the problem shows up fast: a key sticks, a cylinder turns rough, or one door opens while another doesn't. After a move, or when a tenant, ex, or contractor no longer should have access, that kind of loose work leaves you guessing about who still has a key. We rekey the locks you already have so the old keys stop working and the right ones do.

That matters in Milford, where older homes near the river often have a mix of hardware that's been changed over the years, and newer houses can still need the locks sorted out after closing day. We handle the work at your place, test each lock before we leave, and make sure the doors line up the way they should. Whether you're just off Walnut Street or farther north in one of the newer developments, we can reset the locks so you know exactly who can get in.

Rekeying changes the pins inside a lock so the old key no longer works and the new key does. We keep the hardware on the door and rebuild the cylinder around a fresh key pattern. That makes sense after a move, after a key goes missing, or when you want one key to fit several doors without swapping every lock. For many Milford homes, especially the older ones near the river and the newer places north of town, rekeying is the clean way to reset access without changing the look of the doors.

The parts that do the work are the key pins, driver pins, springs, and the plug the key turns. When a lock gets worn, the key can start sticking, turning rough, or feeling loose because the pin heights no longer line up as cleanly as they should. We take the cylinder apart, replace the pin set to match the new key, and check the lock body, tailpiece, and strike alignment so the rekey holds up in daily use. If the lock is damaged, cracked, or badly worn, rekeying may not be the right fix, and we'll say so.

This is usually the first thing to do after you move into a house in Milford. It gives you control over who can open the doors without paying to replace hardware that still works.

Lock Rekeying in Milford - common questions

How does lock rekeying work on a house in Milford?

We remove the lock cylinder, change the internal pin setup to match a new key, and put the hardware back on the door. The outside lock stays the same, but the old key no longer opens it. It's a good fit when the lock still works well and you just want to change access.

What should I have ready before you rekey my home?

Have the doors accessible and know which locks you want included. If you want one key for several doors, we can set that up when the hardware allows it. It also helps to know whether any keys are still out there from a previous owner, renter, or contractor so we can plan the work correctly.

Can a rekey fix a lock that feels sticky or hard to turn?

Sometimes, but not always. If the problem is only the key pattern inside the cylinder, rekeying can help. If the lock body is worn, bent, dirty, or damaged, the issue may come back. We check the full lock first and tell you whether rekeying is the right repair or whether another fix makes more sense.

Should I rekey or replace the lock after moving into a Milford house?

If the hardware is solid and you like the style of the lock, rekeying is usually the simpler choice. If the lock is broken, outdated, missing parts, or doesn't feel secure, replacement may be better. We look at the door, the cylinder, and the condition of the hardware before recommending one path over the other.

Can you rekey a lock if I only have one working key left?

Yes, in many cases we can work from a single working key or the lock itself. That said, a badly worn key or a lock that has been altered before can make the job trickier. If the cylinder is in rough shape, we may recommend replacing that part instead of forcing a bad fit.
