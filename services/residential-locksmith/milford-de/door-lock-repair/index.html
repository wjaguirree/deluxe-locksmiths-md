Door Lock Repair in Milford - common questions

My front door on Walnut Street latches only if I pull it hard. What usually causes that?

That usually means the lock and the strike are no longer meeting squarely. The latch may be worn, the strike plate may have shifted, or the door may be sagging on its hinges. A repair starts with checking alignment and the latch mechanism together, because either one can make the door feel stubborn.

What should I have ready before you come look at a broken door lock in Milford?

Have the door open if you can, and know which lock is acting up, such as the front, back, or garage entry. If you have old keys or spare copies, keep them handy. It also helps to mention whether the problem is turning, latching, or locking, since that points to different parts inside the hardware.

Is a sticking lock a security risk, or just an annoyance?

It can be both. A lock that sticks may still be locking, but worn parts can fail without warning or let the latch sit partly engaged. If the door can be forced shut or the bolt doesn't fully seat, the lock is not doing its job the way it should.

Do you repair the lock body or replace the whole set when the mechanism is worn out?

If the cylinder, latch, or strike is the problem and the rest of the set is sound, a repair may be enough. If the inside parts are stripped, broken, or badly worn, replacing the knob or deadbolt set is usually the cleaner fix. The door and the hardware have to work together.

My house in Milford is older, and the door has settled. Can the lock still be repaired?

Often, yes. Older doors usually need a mix of hardware repair and alignment work. The lock may be fine, but the door may have shifted enough that the latch no longer lines up. In those cases, adjusting the strike or hinge side can make the repaired lock work properly again.
