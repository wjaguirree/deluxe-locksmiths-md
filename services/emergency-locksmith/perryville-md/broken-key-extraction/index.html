Broken Key Extraction in Perryville - common questions

How do you get a snapped key out of a lock without wrecking it?

We start by checking how deep the broken blade is seated and whether the cylinder is still lined up. Then we use extraction tools made for the lock type, not makeshift pry tools. The goal is to remove the piece cleanly, protect the pins or wafers, and test the lock before we call it done.

Should I bring the other half of the key if it broke at my place in Perryville?

Yes, if you still have it. The intact half can help us identify the cut pattern and see whether the key was worn before it snapped. If the lock is on a door or vehicle, having the remaining half makes it easier to decide whether a fresh key can be made from it or whether the lock itself needs attention.

What if the broken piece is stuck in the lock and the door is shut near the MARC station?

That's a common lockout problem. We can usually work on the lock with the door closed, as long as the hardware is accessible. The main thing is not to force the key or slam the door, because that can bend the cylinder or trap the fragment tighter. Let us know what kind of lock it is when you call.

Is it better to just replace the lock after a key breaks, or can it be repaired?

If the lock is in good shape and the break was caused by a worn key, extraction and a new key may be enough. If the cylinder is rough, loose, or already failing, replacement can be the better long-term choice. We look at how the lock turns, whether the key blank was worn, and whether the hardware has other damage.

What if the key broke off in a car door or ignition, not a house lock?

That changes the approach because vehicle cylinders have different parts and tighter tolerances. We need to know whether it's the door, trunk, or ignition, and whether the key still turns before it snapped. Some cases can be extracted and restored with a new key, while others also need cylinder repair or replacement.
