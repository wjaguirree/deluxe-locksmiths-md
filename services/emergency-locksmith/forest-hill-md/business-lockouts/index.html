Business Lockouts in Forest Hill - common questions

What should I have ready when I call about a business lockout in Forest Hill?

Have the business name, the exact location, and a way to describe the door or entrance you're locked out of. If there's a rear door, loading area, or shared entry, mention that up front. If you have proof you're authorized to get into the property, keep it handy. That helps avoid delays once we arrive.

Do you open storefront doors differently than office doors?

Usually, yes. A storefront may have panic hardware, glass doors, or a separate deadbolt, while an office door might use a standard knob and deadbolt setup. The goal is the same: open the door without causing unnecessary damage. The exact method depends on the lock, the frame, and how the hardware is installed.

What if the lockout happened because the key snapped off in the lock?

That changes the job. A broken key can block the cylinder and make normal opening impossible until the broken piece is removed. In some cases the lock can still be saved, and in others the hardware is too worn to keep using. We'll look at it on site and decide the cleanest way to get you back in.

Should I try forcing the door or a window if I'm locked out of my shop?

No. Forcing entry can damage the frame, break glass, or make the lock problem worse. It can also turn a simple lockout into a bigger repair. If the building has another legal access point, tell us about it. Otherwise, it's better to leave the door alone and have it opened properly.

What happens if my office is in a newer building with builder-grade keys and hardware?

That often means the lock is standard, but standard doesn't always mean easy if the key is missing or the door is out of alignment. New construction can also have hardware that feels loose or not fully broken in yet. Once the door is open, it's worth checking whether the lock should be rekeyed or adjusted so the problem doesn't repeat.
