Business Lockouts in Havre de Grace - common questions

If my office door in Havre de Grace just latched behind me, do you have to replace the lock?

Not usually. If the hardware is healthy and the key isn't broken inside, the lock can often be opened and left in place. Replacement is more of a last step when the lock is damaged, the cylinder is worn out, or the door has already been forcing the issue.

What should I have ready when I call about a business lockout downtown?

Have the business name, the exact address, and a way to prove you're authorized to open the place. If you know whether the key broke, the lock is sticking, or the door was simply shut behind you, say that too. That helps the job start in the right direction.

Can a broken key in an old storefront lock be repaired, or does it usually need replacement?

It depends on the condition of the lock. If the broken piece comes out cleanly and the cylinder still turns smoothly, repair or rekeying may be enough. If the keyway is chewed up, the springs are failing, or the lock has been hard to use for a while, replacement is often the better call.

If I had employees with old keys, should I rekey after a lockout?

If you're not sure who still has access, rekeying is worth serious consideration. It keeps the existing hardware but changes which keys work. That's useful after staff changes, a lost key, or any lockout where key control is part of the concern.

What if the lockout happened at a marina office and the door hardware is rusted or sticking?

That changes the job a bit. Salt air, weather, and constant use can wear out cylinders and latch parts faster than people expect. If the lock is only stuck, opening and repair may do the job. If corrosion has taken over, replacement may be the more dependable fix.
