Emergency locksmith in Havre de Grace - common questions

My key broke off in the front-door lock on an older house in Havre de Grace. Should I try to pull it out myself?

Not if part of the key is still buried in the cylinder. Tweezers, pliers, or a knife usually push it farther in or scratch the lock face. Leave the key as it is and avoid turning the lock again. A proper extraction can often save the lock body and keep the door hardware usable.

What should I have ready when you come out for a car lockout near the waterfront?

Have your vehicle registration, ID, and any spare key information you may have. If the keys are inside, say so. If they're lost or broken, say that too. It also helps to mention whether the car is parked on a slope, in a tight space, or near traffic so the work area can be handled safely.

I'm worried the lock will get damaged if I try to force the deadbolt. Is that a real risk?

Yes. Forcing a stuck deadbolt can break the key, twist the latch, or damage the door edge and strike plate. If the key won't turn normally, stop and check whether the door is binding, the key is bent, or the lock is simply worn out. Pushing harder almost never solves it.

If I'm locked out of a business, should I try a side door or wait at the front entrance?

Use only the entrances you're authorized to open. Don't test locks that belong to other tenants or try to bypass a secured door. The safest move is to stay with the entrance you normally use, keep your ID or business access info handy, and let the locksmith work from there.

Can a safe be opened if the keypad stopped working after a power issue?

Sometimes, yes, but it depends on the safe type and what failed. Don't keep entering codes if the keypad is glitching, and don't drill it unless you're prepared for permanent damage. If you can, note the brand, model, and what the safe did right before it stopped responding. That helps determine the right approach.
