Broken Key Extraction in Middletown - common questions

My key snapped in the front door lock in Middletown. What happens first?

We start by checking how much of the broken key is still inside the cylinder and whether the lock is otherwise working. If the piece can be extracted cleanly, we do that first, then test the lock before deciding if repair, rekeying, or replacement makes sense.

What should I have ready before we come out to a broken key call?

Have the door unlocked if it still opens, and keep any other keys that worked before the break. If the key snapped in a house you just moved into, tell us that too. That helps us decide whether the lock needs a new key, a rekey, or a new cylinder.

Can trying to pull the broken key out myself make things worse?

It can. If the shard gets pushed deeper or the pins shift, the lock can jam or stop turning cleanly. Lightly working a broken piece loose is one thing, but digging at it with the wrong tool can damage the cylinder and turn a simple extraction into a larger repair.

Do I need a new lock, or can the old one be rekeyed after extraction?

If the lock body is still sound, rekeying is often the better choice. If the key broke because the cylinder is worn, sticking, or already damaged, replacement may be the smarter fix. We look at the hardware itself, not just the broken key, before recommending either option.

What if the key broke in a new build and I'm not sure the hardware is decent?

That happens a lot around newer construction. Builder-grade locks can work fine at first, then start binding as they get used. If the key broke because the lock is rough or inconsistent, we can extract the blade and tell you whether the cylinder can be saved or should be replaced.
