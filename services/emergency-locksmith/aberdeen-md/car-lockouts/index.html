Car Lockouts in Aberdeen - common questions

How do you open a locked car without damaging it?

The goal is to use a non-destructive method that matches the vehicle's lock and trim design. That means working at the door in a way that avoids prying on the glass or bending the frame. If the car has sensitive weatherstripping, side airbags, or tight panel gaps, the approach has to respect that.

What should I have ready when I call about a car lockout in Aberdeen?

Have the vehicle make, model, year, and whether the keys are visible inside or missing altogether. It also helps to know if the car has a traditional key, a fob, or a push-button start. If the car is at Aberdeen Proving Ground or somewhere else with access controls, mention that up front.

Will trying to open the car myself make the lock worse?

It can. Slim tools, screwdrivers, and bent wire can mar trim, distort the door edge, or trigger a bigger mechanical problem. If the lock is already stiff or the key is broken, forcing it can turn a straightforward lockout into a repair job. It's better to stop before the damage spreads.

If the fob is dead, is that a lockout or a repair problem?

It can be both. If the car is locked and the fob won't respond, the immediate issue is access. After that, the real problem may be a battery, a bad fob, or a vehicle-side issue. Opening the car doesn't fix the remote, but it does let you get to the next step safely.

What if my keys are locked in the trunk and the car is parked near US-40?

That's still a lockout, but it may call for a different entry point depending on the car. Some vehicles can be opened through a door and then the trunk released normally; others need a different approach. If the trunk latch is broken or the battery is dead, tell us that when you call.
