Broken Key Extraction in Aberdeen - common questions

Why do keys seem to break more often in older Aberdeen locks?

Older locks can wear unevenly, and the key has to work harder to line up the pins. If the lock has been stiff for a while, the key blade takes the stress. That usually means the break is a symptom, not the whole problem. After extraction, it's worth checking whether the lock turns freely or needs service.

What should I have ready before you come out for a broken key in my car or house?

Have the exact location, a description of the lock, and proof you're allowed to access the property or vehicle. If it's a car, tell us whether it's a door lock or ignition and whether the key is a regular metal key or a transponder style. That helps us bring the right tools for the job.

Could trying to pull the broken piece out myself make it worse?

It can. A bent paper clip, knife blade, or screwdriver can push the fragment deeper or scratch the inside of the lock. That's especially risky if the piece is already seated flush. If you can still see part of the blade, leave it alone and keep the lock from being forced until it's extracted properly.

If my key snapped, do I need a new lock or just a new key?

Not always a new lock. If the broken key was caused by wear in the key itself, a new key may solve it. If the lock is dragging, binding, or has internal damage, a new key won't fix the root issue. The lock needs to turn smoothly after extraction, or the next key can break too.

What if the broken key is in an ignition and the car is at Aberdeen Proving Ground?

That's a common kind of call in this area. The process is the same, but access rules matter because the vehicle may be behind a gate or in a controlled lot. Once access is confirmed, the broken blade is removed first. If the ignition is worn, we'll tell you before you try another key in it.
