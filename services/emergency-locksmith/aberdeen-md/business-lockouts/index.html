What should I have ready when I call about a business lockout in Aberdeen?

Have the exact address, the kind of door or lock you're dealing with, and proof that you're allowed to get in. A lease, business card, ID, or a manager on site can help. If there's a building super or another key holder, it's smart to have that person lined up too.

Can you open a shop or office door without damaging the hardware?

Often, yes. The goal is to open the door cleanly and protect the lock, frame, and closer. That depends on the lock type and the condition of the door. If someone has already forced it or the hardware is failing, the work may shift from opening to repair or replacement.

I'm worried this might be a rekey issue after the lockout. How do I decide?

If the key is simply locked inside, rekeying may not be needed. If the key is missing, copied by the wrong person, or passed around among staff, rekeying makes more sense after the door is open. The key question is whether you still trust every old key that could be out there.

What should I not do while waiting outside my business?

Don't keep forcing the key, prying on the frame, or trying makeshift tools in the lock. That can bend the door, snap hardware, or make the opening problem harder to solve. If it's a storefront or office, keep customers and employees away from the door until it's sorted out.

That still counts as a lockout, but access rules matter more. Be ready to show you belong there, and don't try to bypass a gate or break into a vehicle. If the keys are locked in a truck, the lock can often be opened on site. If access is restricted by the property, the property rules come first.
