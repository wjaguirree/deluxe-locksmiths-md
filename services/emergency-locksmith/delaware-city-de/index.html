Emergency locksmith in Delaware City - common questions

My key broke off in the lock on Clinton Street. Can you still get the door open?

Often, yes. We first look at how much of the key is left in the cylinder and whether the lock is damaged or just jammed. If the broken piece can be extracted cleanly, the lock may still be usable. If the cylinder is bent or worn out, we may need to repair or replace the hardware.

What should I have ready when I call about a car lockout in Delaware City?

Have the vehicle make, model, and year ready, plus whether the key is inside, lost, or broken. If it's a newer car with a chip key or push-button start, say that too. For a lockout, we may also ask where the car is parked so we can bring the right tools and keys for the job.

What's the risk if I keep using a sticky lock on a waterfront property?

A sticky lock usually gets worse, not better. The key can snap, the cylinder can seize, or the door can stop latching the way it should. That leaves the property harder to secure and easier to damage later. If the lock is already binding, it's better to address it before it fails completely.

For a business lockout, should we try the back door or wait for help?

Only use a door if you already know it's supposed to be opened that way and you have authority to do it. Forcing the wrong entrance can damage the lock, frame, or panic hardware. If you're locked out, we can work on the correct entry point and help avoid making the hardware harder to secure afterward.

If my safe won't open, can you get it open without ruining what's inside?

Sometimes, yes, but it depends on the safe type and why it won't open. A forgotten code, dead keypad, or failed lock may be handled differently from a mechanical failure. We work carefully to protect the contents whenever possible, but some safes need invasive methods if the locking mechanism has failed.
