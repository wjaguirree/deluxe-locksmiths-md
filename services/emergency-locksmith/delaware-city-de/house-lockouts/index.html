House Lockouts in Delaware City - common questions

How do you open a house door in Delaware City without breaking it?

We start with the lock and the door, not force. The goal is to open the entry cleanly and avoid damage to the knob, deadbolt, frame, or trim. Once the door is open, we check that the lock lines up and works the way it should before we leave.

What should I have ready if I'm locked out of my house here?

Have a way to show that the home is yours or that you live there, such as an ID or something that links you to the address. If a landlord, family member, or neighbor is involved, let us know before we come out. That keeps the job smooth and avoids delays at the door.

My door is stuck and the key won't turn, is that the same as a lockout?

It can be. In older Delaware City homes, a lockout often starts as a lock that feels rough, sticks, or won't turn all the way. We can look at the lock, the latch, and the door alignment to see whether it's a simple opening job or a hardware problem that needs attention.

Should I try to force the door or take the lock apart first?

No. Forcing the door can damage the lock, the latch, or the door itself. Taking the hardware apart can make it harder to open cleanly and can turn a small problem into a bigger one. If the door won't open, it's better to leave it alone and let us handle it on site.

What if my lockout is at a waterfront house or a place near the marina?

That's common work for us. Salt air, weather, and regular use can make a lock act up or leave a door misaligned. We handle those jobs the same way we handle any house lockout: open the door carefully, check the lock, and make sure everything works before we finish.
