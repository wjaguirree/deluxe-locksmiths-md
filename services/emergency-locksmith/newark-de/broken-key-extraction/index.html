Broken Key Extraction in Newark - common questions

If my key snapped in the front door lock on a Newark rental, can you still get the door working again?

Often, yes. If the broken blade is still removable and the cylinder hasn't been damaged, we can extract it and check whether the lock still turns cleanly. If the lock was forced after the break, the latch or cylinder may need repair or replacement before it's reliable again.

What should I have ready before you come out for a broken key at my place near the University of Delaware?

Have the exact door in mind, a description of the lock if you know it, and proof that you're allowed to enter the property. If it's a rental or student house, having the landlord, leasing office, or property manager information handy can help if approval is needed before work starts.

I tried pulling the broken piece out myself and it moved deeper. Did I make it worse?

Not necessarily, but it can make the extraction more delicate. The main risk is scratching the cylinder or bending pins by using the wrong tool. Stop trying to work it back and forth. Leave the lock alone so we can inspect it cleanly and decide whether the keyway can be saved.

Is it better to extract the broken key or replace the lock on a back door in Newark?

That depends on the condition of the cylinder and how the key broke. If the lock is sound, extraction is usually the first step. If the key broke because the lock is worn, sticky, or damaged, replacement may be the better long-term fix. We'll look at both before doing anything unnecessary.

What if the key broke off in a mailbox lock or a small padlock, not a house door?

Those can be different from a standard door lock, but the same idea applies: don't force the broken piece with another key or a sharp object. Some mailbox and padlock cores are easy to service, while others are better replaced. Once we see the lock, we can tell you which route makes sense.
