Safe Opening Service in Elkton - common questions

If I forgot the combination to my home safe in Elkton, what should I have ready when you get there?

Have the safe brand, model, and any serial number you can find. If there's a backup key, the owner's manual, or old paperwork, keep that handy too. If the safe is part of a larger cabinet or built-in unit, let us know that up front so we can plan the job the right way.

What should I not do before you open my safe?

Don't force the handle, keep spinning the dial, or drill into the door. Those steps can damage the lock, the relocker, or the contents inside. If the safe has a low battery or a jammed latch, leaving it alone usually gives us a better shot at opening it cleanly.

Can you open a safe without ruining the safe or what's inside?

Often, yes. A lot depends on the lock type, the safe's condition, and whether it has already been damaged. We use the least destructive method that fits the situation. If the safe has hidden internal damage or a failed lock, we'll explain what we can do before taking the next step.

Should I try the backup key or keep entering codes if the keypad is acting up?

If you have a known working backup key, that can be worth trying once. If the keypad is flashing, beeping oddly, or not accepting input, stop and leave it alone. Repeated attempts can lock out the electronics or wear down a failing keypad. We can check whether the issue is the lock, the battery, or the keypad itself.

What if the safe is bolted down in my Elkton office and holds records I need to access?

Keep it in place unless moving it is a safety issue. Bolted safes are common in offices, and unbolting them first can create extra problems. Make sure someone authorized to open it is present, gather any paperwork tied to the safe, and tell us whether it's a fire safe, cash safe, or document safe so we can bring the right approach.
