Business Lockouts in Camden - common questions

What should I have ready if my shop in Camden is locked and I need the door opened?

Have your name, the business name, and something that ties you to the address. A key, lease, rent receipt, utility bill, or employee badge can help show you're authorized. If the door has been acting up, tell us that too. That helps us choose the right approach instead of forcing something that can be opened cleanly.

If the front door won't open at my office near Route 13, should I keep trying the key?

No. If the key is binding or the latch feels off, repeated tries can bend the key or damage the cylinder. Stop and leave the lock alone. Keep the area clear and let the door stay in the position it's in. That gives a locksmith a better chance of opening it without creating extra repair work.

Can a tenant call for a business lockout, or does the landlord have to handle it?

It depends on the lease and who controls the lock. A tenant can often call if they're authorized to be there, but some buildings require landlord approval for certain hardware. If you're in a shared property in Camden, it helps to know whether the lock is part of your unit or the building's main access point.

What if my business lockout also left the door damaged or crooked?

Say that up front. A misaligned door, broken latch, or bent strike plate changes the job. Sometimes the lock isn't the real problem; the door or frame is. If we know that before we start, we can avoid forcing parts that are already under stress and decide whether the hardware can be used again.

Can you open the back entrance to my shop if customers still need the front door later?

Yes, if the back entrance is the right access point and it belongs to the business. For some shops, opening the rear door is the cleaner option because it keeps the customer area undisturbed.
