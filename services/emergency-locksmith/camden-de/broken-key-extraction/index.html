How Keys Break

The part that snaps is usually the thin blade, not the plastic head. That blade carries the cut pattern that lifts the pins or wafers inside the lock. Over time, the metal can weaken from bending, corrosion, a worn keyway, or a lock that's already binding. From the outside, it often looks simple: the key turns hard, you give it one more twist, and the tip stays behind in the cylinder. If the break is clean, the key head may still be in your hand while the working end is lodged deep inside the lock.

Broken Key Extraction in Camden - common questions

How do you get a broken key out of a lock without wrecking it?

We inspect the lock first, then use extraction tools that catch the broken blade without pushing it farther in. If the cylinder is still healthy, we remove the piece, test the lock, and cut a working key if needed. If the lock is already damaged, we'll explain that before going further.

What should I have ready when I call about a snapped key in Camden?

Have the door open or closed status ready, and know whether it's a house lock, deadbolt, or vehicle lock. If it's a car, the make and model help. If the key broke because the lock feels stiff, tell us that too. Those details help us bring the right tools for the job.

Is it risky to keep trying the key after part of it breaks off?

Yes. Forcing the key can push the broken piece deeper, bend the plug, or damage the pins inside the cylinder. That can turn a simple extraction into a lock repair or replacement. If the key stopped turning normally, don't keep twisting it. Stop there and leave the lock as-is.

Should I try to extract the broken blade myself or replace the whole lock?

If the key snapped but the lock still seems normal, extraction is usually the first step. Replacing the lock only makes sense when the cylinder is worn, damaged, or already unreliable. A broken key by itself doesn't always mean the whole lock is bad, but a damaged lock shouldn't be forced back into service.

What if the key broke off in a car lock or ignition near Route 13?

That can still be handled on site in many cases. We'll look at whether the broken piece is in the door, trunk, or ignition and whether the cylinder is turning at all. Ignition issues can be more delicate than a door lock, so the condition of the cylinder matters before any extraction is attempted.
