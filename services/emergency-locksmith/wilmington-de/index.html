Emergency locksmith in Wilmington - common questions

My apartment lock in Wilmington still turns, but the key feels rough. Do you repair it or rekey it?

If the lock still functions and the issue is wear, dirt, or a small internal problem, repair may be enough. If the key is lost, copied by the wrong person, or you want old keys to stop working, rekeying is usually the better choice. We look at the hardware first and tell you which option fits the situation.

What should I have ready when you come out for a car lockout near the Riverfront?

Have a way to prove the vehicle belongs to you if you can, plus the make, model, and year if you know them. If the key is broken in the ignition or door, let us know that upfront. Clear details help us bring the right tools and avoid extra damage while we work.

Can trying to open my own door make the problem worse?

Yes. Forcing a stubborn lock, prying a door, or twisting a broken key can damage the cylinder, latch, or frame. What starts as a lockout can turn into a repair or replacement job. If the key is stuck or snapped, stop using it and let us assess the hardware first.

If my office key is lost downtown, do I need every lock replaced?

Not always. If the hardware is in good shape, rekeying may be enough to keep the old key from working while leaving the existing lock in place. Replacement makes more sense when the lock is damaged, outdated, or not holding up. We judge the lock, not just the missing key.

Can you open a safe in Wilmington if I forgot the combination?

Sometimes, yes, but it depends on the safe type and how it's built. Some safes can be opened with non-destructive methods; others need more involved work. We'll inspect the safe, explain the options, and avoid unnecessary damage when possible.
