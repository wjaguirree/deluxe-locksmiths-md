Broken Key Extraction in Wyoming - common questions

How do you get a broken key out of a lock without making the lock worse?

We use extraction tools sized for the lock and the break, then work the fragment out without forcing the cylinder. If the piece is sticking out, the approach is different than if it's buried deep inside. After removal, we check the lock's turn and look for damage before anything gets forced again.

What should I have ready when you come to a house in Wyoming for a snapped key?

Have the door open or closed as it sits, and keep the broken half of the key if you still have it. It also helps to know whether the key broke in the front door, side door, mailbox, or vehicle. If the lock is on a rental or shared property, be ready to show that you're allowed to request the work.

Can I just leave the broken piece in the lock and use another door?

You can, but it's not a good long-term plan. A fragment in the cylinder can keep the lock from latching or make it harder to turn when you really need it. That can leave a door unsecured or push you to use a weaker entrance. We'd rather clear it out and make sure the lock is doing its job.

If my key broke in an older Wyoming door, should I fix the lock or replace it?

That depends on the lock's condition after extraction. If the cylinder turns cleanly and the parts aren't worn, repair may be enough. If the key broke because the lock is loose, stiff, or badly worn, replacement can be the better move. We'll look at the hardware first and explain what makes sense.

What if the key broke in my car ignition and part of it is still sticking out?

Don't twist the ignition or try to dig it out with a knife or paper clip. That can push the piece deeper or damage the cylinder. If part of the blade is visible, that's often the best case for extraction, but we still need to remove it carefully and make sure the ignition will turn normally after.
