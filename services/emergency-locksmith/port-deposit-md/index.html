Emergency locksmith in Port Deposit - common questions

If my key breaks off in the lock in Port Deposit, do you try to save the lock first?

Usually, yes. We start by removing the broken piece and checking whether the lock cylinder still turns smoothly. If the hardware is sound, repair is often the right move. If the lock is worn, bent, or keeps catching, rekeying or replacement may be the safer option so the problem doesn't come right back.

What should I have ready when I call about a house lockout on Main Street?

Have the exact address, a phone number we can reach, and a way to show that you live there or have permission to enter. That helps us avoid delays once we arrive. If the door has a special lock, deadbolt, or older mortise setup, tell us that too so we can bring the right tools.

Can forcing the key damage my old lock or door?

It can. Older locks in Port Deposit often have tighter tolerances, and a bent key or stubborn cylinder can break parts inside the lock or mark up the door edge. If the key is sticking, turning rough, or already snapped, stop using it and let us inspect it before more damage builds up.

Should I rekey or replace the lock after I lost my car key?

For a car, rekeying usually isn't the right fix the way it is on a house. Lost key replacement is often the better path because the vehicle key has to match the vehicle's system, not just the door. If the lock or ignition is damaged, we'll look at whether repair or replacement makes more sense.

Can you open a safe if the dial won't move or the code won't work?

In many cases, yes. The method depends on the type of safe, whether it's mechanical or electronic, and whether the problem is a lock failure or a user issue. We'll inspect the safe first and use the least invasive approach that fits the design. Some safes do need drilling or repair after opening.
