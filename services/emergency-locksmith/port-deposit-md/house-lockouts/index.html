House Lockouts in Port Deposit - common questions

How do you usually open a house when someone in Port Deposit is locked out?

It depends on the door and the lock. A standard deadbolt may open one way, while an older mortise lock or a sticky latch may need a different approach. The aim is to get the door open cleanly and then check that the lock still catches and turns properly before leaving.

What should I have ready before you come out to my house?

Have a photo ID ready if you can, and be ready to show that you live there. It also helps to know whether the door has a deadbolt, knob lock, or older hardware, and whether the key is lost, stuck, or broken off inside the lock.

Is it a bad idea to keep forcing the key when the lock feels stuck?

Yes. Forcing it can snap the key, damage the cylinder, or worsen a latch problem that was already there. If the key is resisting, stop using it. That's especially true with older doors and locks that have settled or been painted over.

Should I try another door first, or wait at the front door?

Only use another door if you know it's safe, unlocked, and won't create a bigger problem. Don't climb, pry, or slip a tool into a window. If there's any doubt, wait at the main entry and leave the hardware alone until help arrives.

What if my key broke in an old lock and the door is still shut?

Don't dig at the broken piece with a knife, paperclip, or drill bit. That usually pushes it deeper or scratches the cylinder. Keep the door closed, and if you have the matching key ring or any spare that belongs to the same lock, bring that along so the hardware can be checked after the opening.
