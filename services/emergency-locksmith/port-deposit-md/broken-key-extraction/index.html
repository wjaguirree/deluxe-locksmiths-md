Broken Key Extraction in Port Deposit - common questions

Can you pull a broken key out of an old Port Deposit mortise lock without replacing the whole lock?

Often, yes. If the broken piece is accessible and the lock internals aren't damaged, extraction can be done without replacing the whole body. Old mortise locks need a careful hand because the keyway can be worn or tight. After the fragment is out, the cylinder and latch should be checked to make sure the lock still operates smoothly.

What should I have ready when the key snaps in my door or car lock?

Have the key fragment if you can remove it safely, plus any spare key, and be ready to show the lock or vehicle so the right tools can be used. If it's a car, the year, make, and model help. Don't keep forcing the broken key or turning the lock, since that can push the piece deeper or damage the cylinder.

Is there a risk that the lock gets ruined during extraction?

There is always some risk, especially if the key is jammed deep, the lock is worn, or a previous repair left damage behind. Good extraction work aims to protect the keyway and cylinder first. If the lock is already failing, you may get the broken piece out but still need repair or replacement because the damage was there before the key snapped.

Should I try to fish the broken key out myself or call for help?

If the fragment is sticking out far enough to grasp cleanly, you might be able to remove it carefully. But if it's flush with the face of the lock, using glue, paper clips, or other makeshift tools usually makes things worse. That can push the piece deeper or scratch the cylinder. For a stubborn break, it's better to leave it alone.

What if the key broke off in a vehicle door versus the ignition?

A door lock is usually more straightforward than an ignition, but both need the right extraction method. Door cylinders can often be serviced in place. Ignitions may need more careful access because of the steering column and the tighter fit of the keyway. Either way, the lock should be tested after extraction to make sure the key turns cleanly.
