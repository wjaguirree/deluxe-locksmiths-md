House Lockouts in Claymont - common questions

How do you open a house door in Claymont without damaging it?

We start by looking at the door, the lock type, and how it's fitting in the frame. The goal is to open it with the least disturbance possible, then check that the latch and deadbolt still work the way they should. If there's wear or misalignment, we'll point it out before we leave.

What should I have ready when I'm locked out of my home?

Have your address, your phone, and some way to show the home is yours if needed. If a neighbor, landlord, or family member has a spare key, let us know. It also helps to mention whether the door is front, side, or rear access, since that affects how we approach the job.

Can you still help if the lockout happened after the door was already sticking?

Yes, but we'll treat it as more than a simple lockout. A sticking door can mean worn hardware, a latch that's not lining up, or a deadbolt that's under stress. We'll open it carefully and then tell you what needs attention so you're not left with the same problem again.

Should I try to pick the lock myself before calling?

It's usually better not to. DIY picking, prying, or forcing the knob can bend parts inside the lock or damage the door edge. That can turn a straightforward lockout into a repair. We can assess the lock and open it with the right tools for the hardware in front of us.

What if my key is inside but the deadbolt is on from the other side?

That's a common house lockout, and the fix depends on the lock setup. Some doors can be opened cleanly without forcing anything, while others need a more careful approach because of the deadbolt or the way the door is built. We'll figure out the least invasive way to get you back in.
