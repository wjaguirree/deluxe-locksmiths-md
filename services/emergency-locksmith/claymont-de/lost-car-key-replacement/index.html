How the Key System Works

When a car has no usable key left, we're dealing with the part that actually tells the vehicle it's allowed to start. On older vehicles, that may be a cut metal key with a chip head or a separate remote. On newer ones, the key and the electronics work together: the blade opens the door and the chip or fob talks to the immobilizer. If that handshake doesn't happen, the engine won't stay running, even if the lock turns. We originate a new key where the car is parked by matching the lock, reading the vehicle's key data, and programming a fresh key so the system recognizes it.

From the outside, worn keys often show the story before they fail completely. Lost Car Key Replacement in Claymont - common questions

If I lost every key to my car in Claymont, what do you actually do?

We identify the vehicle, cut a new key from the lock or the vehicle data, and program it if the car uses a chip, remote, or smart key. The work is done where the car is parked, so you don't need to arrange a tow just because there's no key left to copy.

What should I have ready before you come out to replace my lost car key?

Have the vehicle, the registration if you can reach it, and a way to prove the car belongs to you. If there's a hidden spare at home or with a family member, let us know. The more we know about the make, model, and year, the easier it is to bring the right equipment.

Can a replacement key still work if the original was lost near the Claymont rail station?

Yes, the location where it was lost doesn't change the replacement itself. What matters is the car's key system and whether any old key needs to be removed from the vehicle's memory. If the missing key might be with someone else, that's worth telling us so we can advise on the next step.

Should I replace the old key or have a new one programmed if I still have one working key?

If you still have a working key, making a spare is usually the smarter move because it gives you a backup and is simpler than starting from zero. If every key is gone, we have to originate one from the vehicle. The right choice depends on whether you can still use the car at all.

What if my car is old, imported, or has an unusual key system?

Some older or less common vehicles use a different setup, and some can be more involved than a standard chip key. We'll check the year, make, model, and key type first. If the vehicle needs dealer-only equipment or a method that isn't supported on site, we'll tell you plainly before any work starts.
