Safe Opening Service in Claymont - common questions

How do you open a safe if I lost the combination in Claymont?

We start by identifying the safe and the type of lock it uses. From there, we choose the least destructive method that fits that model. Some safes can be opened by working the lock directly, while others need more involved service. If the safe is older or has been tampered with before, we may need to adjust the approach.

What should I have ready before you come out to open my safe?

Have the safe's brand, model, and any paperwork if you still have it. If it's a keyed safe, bring any spare keys or the main key. It also helps to know whether the safe is bolted down, where it sits, and whether it was recently moved or repaired. That gives us a better starting point.

Can drilling my safe make the damage worse?

Yes. Blind drilling can damage the lock, the relocker, the boltwork, or even the contents if the hole goes where it shouldn't. A bad drill job can also leave the safe harder to service later. We only use drilling when the safe calls for it, and we plan it so the opening and repair are as controlled as possible.

Should I try the keypad again or force the handle if the safe won't open?

No. Repeated forcing can trip the relocker or bend internal parts. If the keypad isn't responding, the batteries may be dead, the lock may have failed, or the safe may have a deeper mechanical issue. Leave it alone and have us look at it before the problem spreads beyond the lock itself.

What if my safe is an older office safe near the Claymont rail station?

Older office safes can have worn dials, stiff bolts, or past repairs that complicate the opening. We can usually work with that, but the condition of the safe matters. If the lock has been altered before or the door is already out of alignment, we may need to service it after opening so it closes and locks correctly again.
