Broken Key Extraction in Milford - common questions

How do you get a broken key out of my door lock in Milford?

We inspect how the key broke, then use extraction tools matched to the lock style to remove the blade without chewing up the cylinder. After that, we test the lock and keyway to see whether the lock can keep working as-is or whether it needs a repair, rekey, or replacement.

What should I have ready before you come out for a snapped key?

Have the door accessible, and if it's a home or rental, be ready to show that you're allowed to be there. If part of the key is still in your hand, bring that too. A picture of the lock can help us bring the right tools, especially if the lock is older or a less common type.

Can forcing the key make the lock worse?

Yes. If a key is twisted, yanked, or turned after it starts to bind, the cylinder can get damaged along with the key. That can make extraction harder and can leave the lock rough or stuck afterward. We always check whether the lock is still safe to use before telling you to keep it.

Should I rekey the lock or replace it after a broken key?

If the lock still turns smoothly and the key broke because of the key itself, rekeying may be enough, especially if you want the old key to stop working. If the cylinder is worn, sticky, or scarred from the break, replacement is usually the cleaner choice. We'll tell you which path fits the hardware.

What if my key snapped off and I still have the door open near the Mispillion Riverwalk?

That can actually make the job simpler, because we can inspect the lock from both sides before we start. We still need to see whether the cylinder was damaged when the key broke. If the lock is binding, we may recommend rekeying or replacement instead of forcing a repair that won't last.
