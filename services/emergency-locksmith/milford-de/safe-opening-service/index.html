Safe Opening Service in Milford - common questions

How does a safe opening service usually work when the combination is lost?

First we identify the safe and the lock style, then choose the least invasive way to open it. Some safes can be opened by working the lock or servicing the mechanism. Others need more involved access. After it's open, we can often repair or adjust the lock so the safe closes and locks correctly again.

What should I have ready before you come to open a safe in Milford?

Have the safe's brand, model, and any serial number if you can find it. If there's paperwork or a key, keep that nearby. It also helps to clear space around the safe and make sure someone authorized to open it is present. Don't keep trying the lock while waiting.

Could trying the dial or handle too many times damage the safe?

Yes. Repeated forcing can bend parts, wear the lock, or make a simple issue harder to fix. If the dial feels gritty or the handle won't move normally, stop there. The safest approach is to leave the mechanism alone until it's examined, especially on older home and office safes.

If the safe is stuck, can it be opened without drilling?

Sometimes, yes. It depends on the safe, the type of lock, and what went wrong. A lost combination, a worn dial, or a jammed bolt may be handled without drilling. If the lock is badly damaged or the safe has failed in a way that blocks normal access, drilling may be the practical option.

What if the safe opens but won't close or relock after service?

That usually means there's still a problem in the bolt work, latch, or lock body. It's not something to ignore, because a safe that won't lock is not doing its job. We can inspect the mechanism, correct what's binding, and let you know if a repair or replacement part is needed.
