Broken Key Extraction in Cecilton - common questions

How do you get a broken key out of a lock in Cecilton?

We use extraction tools that grip the broken blade without pushing it deeper into the cylinder. The lock has to be handled carefully so the pins and springs stay in place. After the piece comes out, we test the lock and key it back to normal if the cylinder is still in good shape.

What should I do before you get to my place if my key snapped in the lock?

Don't keep trying to turn the remaining piece. That can bend the lock parts or push the broken blade farther in. Leave the keyway alone, keep any loose key pieces you still have, and make sure the door, gate, or vehicle is accessible so we can work on it when we arrive.

Could forcing the key make the lock unusable?

Yes. Forcing it can damage the cylinder, break a key extractor off inside, or leave the lock binding after the broken piece is removed. If the lock already feels stiff or gritty, that's a sign it needs careful handling. We check for that before we try to turn it again.

Should I replace the lock or just get a new key after a break like this?

It depends on what caused the break. If the key snapped but the lock is clean and smooth, a new key may be enough. If the cylinder is worn, sticky, or chewed up, replacement may make more sense. We look at the lock itself, not just the broken blade.

What if the broken key is stuck in a boat trailer lock or farm gate lock near Cecilton?

That's a common kind of call out here. Those locks often deal with weather, dirt, and heavy use, so the blade can break flush with the face of the lock. We can still usually extract it on site, then check whether the lock needs cleaning, rekeying, or replacement.
