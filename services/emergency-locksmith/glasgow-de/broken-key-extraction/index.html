Broken Key Extraction in Glasgow - common questions

How do you get a broken key out without wrecking the lock?

We first look at how much of the blade is exposed and whether the lock is still in a usable position. Then we use extraction tools that grip the fragment instead of forcing it. The goal is to remove the broken piece cleanly, check the cylinder for damage, and avoid turning a simple jam into a lock replacement.

What should I leave alone if my key snapped in my door or car lock in Glasgow?

Don't keep jamming the key slot with the rest of the key, paperclips, blades, or glue. That usually drives the fragment deeper or leaves debris inside the lock. If you can, stop using the lock and keep the area clear so we can work on the keyway as it is.

Can the lock still be saved if the broken key was already hard to turn?

Sometimes, yes. A hard-turning lock may only need the broken piece removed and the cylinder checked for wear or debris. Other times the key snapped because the lock was already failing. We'll tell you if the lock can be kept in service or if it's too worn to trust.

Should I ask for a new key or just have the broken piece removed?

If the key broke because it was worn, bent, or cracked, a replacement key is usually the smarter move after extraction. If the key was fairly new and the lock is the real issue, the key alone may not solve it. We look at both sides so you're not stuck with the same problem again.

What if my key broke off in an older house lock versus a newer car?

Older house locks often have more wear, so the broken piece may come out easier but the mechanism may still need attention. Newer car locks are tighter and less forgiving, so extraction has to be more exact. Either way, we focus on removing the blade, protecting the hardware, and confirming the lock still functions properly afterward.
