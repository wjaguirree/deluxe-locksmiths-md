When a key snaps in a lock, we do not jump straight to replacing the whole thing. We start by looking at what failed: the key, the lock cylinder, the keyway, or a worn internal part. If the blade broke because the key was worn down, the lock body is still sound, and the mechanism turns cleanly, we can usually extract the broken piece and make a new key from the remaining working pattern. That keeps the hardware in service and gets the lock back to normal without changing more than we need to.

If the lock is usable but the key broke because the cylinder is tired, binding, or full of wear, rekeying may be the better call. Broken Key Extraction in Smyrna - common questions

How do you get a broken key out of a lock without wrecking the cylinder?

We use thin extraction tools made for the lock type, line up the broken blade, and pull it out without grinding on the plug. If the piece is exposed, that's usually straightforward. If it's deep inside, the lock may need more careful work, especially on older hardware that already has wear.

What should I have ready when I call about a snapped key in Smyrna?

Have the lock location, whether it's a house, vehicle, or business entry, and any key or spare you still have. If it's a car, the make, model, and year help. If it's a rental or business door, let us know who controls the hardware so we can plan the next step cleanly.

Can trying to turn the key harder make the problem worse?

Yes. Extra force can push the broken piece deeper, bend the plug, or damage worn pins and springs. That's common with older locks and with keys that were already sticking. If the key breaks, stop using the lock and let it be handled as-is.

Should I repair the lock after extraction or replace it?

If the lock turns smoothly after the fragment is removed, repair may be enough. If the key broke because the cylinder is rough, loose, or misaligned, replacement can be the smarter move. On older doors, especially, repeated breakage usually means the hardware is telling you something.

What if the broken key is in a car ignition or a door that's badly worn?

That can still be handled, but the approach changes. Ignitions and heavily worn doors may need more careful extraction, and sometimes the lock's condition is the real problem. We'll look at whether the broken piece can be removed cleanly and whether the lock is still safe to keep using.
