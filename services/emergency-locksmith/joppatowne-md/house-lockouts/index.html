House Lockouts in Joppatowne - common questions

What do you do first when you arrive for a house lockout in Joppatowne?

We start by looking at the door, the lock, and any signs of damage or a broken key. That tells us whether the safest move is to work the latch, deal with a broken key, or address a deadbolt issue first. The order matters because it helps protect the door, frame, and hardware.

Do I need to have proof that I live there before you open the door?

If possible, have some way to show you belong at the property, like an ID with the address, mail, or a neighbor who can confirm it. If you're locked out and the proof is inside, tell us what's available and we'll work through it with you. The goal is to help the right person get back in.

My key snapped off in the lock on the way home. Can that be fixed without replacing the whole lock?

Often, yes. A broken key doesn't always mean the lock is done. If the cylinder isn't badly worn or damaged, we can usually clear the broken piece and check how the lock turns afterward. If the inside parts are failing too, we'll tell you that before doing more work.

Should I try to force the deadbolt myself if it's stuck?

Usually not. Forcing a stuck deadbolt can bend the bolt, twist the door, or break the key off inside the cylinder. In a waterfront area like Joppatowne, corrosion can be part of the problem, and brute force tends to make that worse. It's better to stop once the lock feels wrong.

My house has a smart lock and the battery died. Is that handled the same way as a regular lockout?

Not exactly. A dead battery can leave you locked out, but the hardware has to be handled with care so the electronics and backup parts aren't damaged. We look for the manual override, backup entry point, or the least invasive way to open the door, then make sure the lock still functions after.
