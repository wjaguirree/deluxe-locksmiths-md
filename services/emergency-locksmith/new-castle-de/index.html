Emergency locksmith in New Castle - common questions

My key broke off in the front door lock in New Castle. What should I do first?

Stop turning the key or pushing on the lock. If part of the key is still visible, leave it alone and let us handle the extraction. Pulling on it with pliers often pushes the broken piece deeper or damages the cylinder. If the door is open, don't close it until you know the lock still works properly.

What should I have ready when I call about a house lockout?

Have the address, the type of door or lock, and a way to show that you live there. If there's a deadbolt, a smart lock, or an older mortise lock, mention that too. In many cases, a little detail up front helps us choose the right tools and avoid unnecessary damage to the door.

I'm worried about damaging an old lock in the Historic District. Is that a real concern?

Yes, older hardware can be more delicate than newer locks. Many older doors use parts that wear differently and don't respond well to force. Tell us if the lock is original or if the door has been sticking. That helps us work carefully and decide whether opening, repair, or replacement makes the most sense.

Should I try another key or keep jiggling the one that won't turn?

No. If a key is sticking, bent, or only turning partway, more force can shear it off or damage the lock pins and springs inside. If you have a spare key, use that only if it turns smoothly. Otherwise, leave it alone and call us before the problem gets worse.

Can you help if I lost my car key and the spare is inside the vehicle?

Yes, that's a common call. We can open the car and discuss replacement options based on the make and model. If the original key is gone, have the vehicle info ready, and if possible, your registration and ID. That keeps the process straightforward and helps us match the right key or fob.
