Safe Opening Service in New Castle - common questions

Can you open a safe in my New Castle home if the combination is gone?

Often, yes. We first identify the safe, the lock type, and whether there's a mechanical issue or just a lost combination. Some safes can be opened with non-destructive methods, while others need careful drilling and repair. Either way, we work to open it cleanly and explain what the lock needs after that.

What should I have ready before you come to open my office safe?

If you have the make, model, old paperwork, or any key that came with the safe, have it nearby. It also helps to know what changed before the lockout started, such as a dead battery, a forgotten code, or a handle that suddenly got stiff. Clear space around the safe so we can reach it safely.

I tried the dial a few times and now it feels tighter. Did I make it worse?

It might be more loaded up than before, especially if the bolt work is under pressure or the door is out of alignment. Stop forcing it and leave it alone until we can look at it. Repeated hard turns can damage the lock or make opening more involved, especially on older safes.

Should I try drilling the safe if it's locked and I need what's inside?

Usually not. Drilling without the right plan can destroy the lock, the relocker, or the contents inside. It can also turn a service call into a repair job. We use a safer method first whenever the safe and the situation allow it, then decide what the lock needs after opening.

What if the safe is an older one in a historic house near the Court House Museum?

That can change the approach. Older safes often have worn parts, and older buildings can add settling or alignment issues. We take that into account before opening anything. The age of the building doesn't stop the service, but it does mean we work carefully and avoid quick force.
