Car Lockouts in New Castle - common questions

If I'm locked out of my car in New Castle, what do you actually do first?

We start by checking the lock style, the key situation, and whether the door is already showing a fault. Then we use non-destructive entry methods suited to that vehicle. The goal is to open the car without harming the door, glass, or trim, and then figure out whether anything needs attention beyond the lockout itself.

What should I have ready when you come to unlock my vehicle?

Have the vehicle location, make, model, and year ready, plus proof that the car is yours. If there's a broken key, a spare, or a key fob with it, that helps us judge whether the issue is only a lockout or part of a larger problem. If the car is stuck in a tricky spot, tell us that too.

Could trying to unlock my car myself damage the lock or window?

Yes. A lot of damage comes from bending the door frame, scratching glass, or tearing weather stripping while trying to pry the door open. That risk is higher on tighter modern vehicles, but older doors can be damaged too. We use methods meant to avoid those problems and keep the door working afterward.

When does a car lockout turn into a repair, rekey, or replacement job?

If the lock itself is fine and the key was just left inside, it stays a lockout job. If the cylinder is worn but still serviceable, repair may be enough. If the key or cylinder no longer matches what you need, rekeying can help. If the hardware is failing or damaged, replacement is usually the better choice.

What if my key broke off in the door or ignition while I was locked out?

That's a different kind of problem, but it can happen at the same time. We can usually deal with the lockout and then address the broken key. If the cylinder is damaged, we'll tell you whether the part can be repaired or whether replacement makes more sense than trying to keep a worn lock in service.
