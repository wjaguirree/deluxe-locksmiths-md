Business Lockouts in New Castle - common questions

How do you get a business door open in New Castle without wrecking the lock?

We start by looking at the type of hardware on the door and how it's failing. A modern commercial lock may call for a different approach than an older mortise lock in a historic building. Our goal is to open the entry with as little damage as possible and then check whether the lock still needs repair.

What should I have ready when we're locked out of our office or shop?

Have proof that you're allowed to access the property, the business name, and any details about the door or lock if you know them. If there's a landlord, property manager, or another decision-maker involved, let us know early. That helps us avoid delays once we're on site.

Can a broken key in an old New Castle storefront lock be taken care of without replacing the whole door hardware?

Often, yes. If the key snapped and the rest of the lock is still sound, we can usually work on the lock itself rather than the door. If the hardware is worn, damaged, or no longer reliable, we'll point that out so you can decide whether repair or replacement makes more sense.

If the owner and tenant disagree about the lockout, what happens next?

We need clear authorization before opening a business entry. If ownership, lease access, or control of the suite is in question, we may have to wait until the right person gives permission. That protects everyone involved and keeps the situation from turning into a bigger dispute.

What if the lockout is happening on a historic door near The Green?

Older doors can settle, shift, and bind in ways newer doors don't. We treat those jobs carefully because the lock, the door, and the frame may all be working against each other. In some cases the lockout is only part of the problem, and the door may also need adjustment after it's opened.
