House Lockouts in New Castle - common questions

My front door is locked and the key is inside. What do you do first?

We start by checking the lock type, the door gap, and how the latch sits in the strike. That tells us whether the door can be opened cleanly or whether the hardware needs a different approach. The goal is to get you back inside without turning a simple lockout into a damaged door or frame.

Do I need to have anything ready when you arrive at my house in New Castle?

It helps to have some way to show that you live there, since we're opening a home, not just a door. If the lock broke a key, keep any broken piece you have. If there's a back door, side door, or garage entry that works differently, point that out right away so we can choose the right approach.

Can trying to force the door make the problem worse?

Yes. Pushing harder can bend the latch, crack trim, or break the key off deeper in the lock. That's especially true on older doors where the hardware has already shifted. If the lock is already sticking, forcing it can turn a lockout into a repair job that should have been avoidable.

If I live in the historic part of New Castle, should I ask for the lock to be replaced instead of opened?

Not always. If the lock is sound, opening it and checking how it works afterward may be all that's needed. If the hardware is worn, original, or badly out of line, replacement might make more sense. We look at the door and the lock first, then talk through the practical option.

What if my key snapped off in an old mortise lock?

That changes the job. We have to clear the broken piece and work with the lock style you have, not a modern deadbolt routine. In some cases the lock can still be opened and used again; in others, the wear in the mechanism means it should be repaired or replaced after entry.
