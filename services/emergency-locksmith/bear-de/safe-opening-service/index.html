Safe Opening Service in Bear - common questions

How do you tell if my safe is locked up, jammed, or just the combination is wrong?

We start with the symptoms. If the keypad or dial works but the handle is loose, that often points to a bolt or linkage issue. If the lock accepts input and still won't release, it may be in a lockout or jammed state. If there's no clear code history, we treat it like an access problem and inspect before forcing anything.

What should I have ready before you come open my safe in Bear?

Have the safe model, the brand if you know it, and any old paperwork, keys, or code records you still have. If it's in a home or office with limited access, make sure someone can show us the safe and the room it's in. Even a small detail, like whether the handle moved strangely, helps us narrow it down.

I'm worried the safe will be damaged. Can it usually be opened without ruining it?

Often, yes, but not always. It depends on the lock type, the condition of the door, and whether someone already tried drilling or prying it. We choose the least invasive method that fits the problem. If the safe is already damaged or the lock has failed internally, we'll explain what can be saved before going further.

Should I keep trying the keypad, the dial, or the handle if the safe won't open?

Usually no. Repeated attempts can make a simple problem worse, especially if the safe is in lockout or the bolt is under pressure. If the handle feels wrong, stops early, or spins freely, stop there. That kind of clue helps us identify the fault faster than a long series of guesses.

My office safe opens partway, then sticks. Is that a lock problem or a door problem?

It can be either. A safe that opens partway and then hangs up may have a binding bolt, a shifted door, or internal damage to the mechanism. We look at how the door moves before and after the latch clears. If the safe was bumped, moved, or overloaded, that history matters just as much as the lock itself.
