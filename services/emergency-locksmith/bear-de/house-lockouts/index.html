House Lockouts in Bear - common questions

How do you open a house door in Bear without damaging it?

We start with the lock type, the door fit, and whether the latch or deadbolt is what's actually holding it shut. Then we use the least invasive method that fits the hardware. If the lock is worn or damaged, we may need to repair or replace parts after the door is open so it secures correctly.

What should I have ready when I'm locked out of my house in Bear?

Have your address, a way to confirm you're associated with the property, and any information about the lock if you know it. If there's a spare key with a neighbor, family member, or landlord, that can help too. Clear access to the door area also makes the job smoother.

Could my door or lock get worse if I keep forcing it?

Yes. Forcing the key or handle can bend the key, damage the cylinder, or make the latch bind harder. If the door is swollen or the hardware is already worn, pushing harder usually creates a bigger repair. It's better to stop before the lock or frame gets chewed up.

If my deadbolt and knob don't match, which one matters for a lockout?

Usually the deadbolt is the main security lock, but we look at both because either one can be the part keeping the door shut. In some Bear homes, the knob is loose but the deadbolt is fine, and in others the deadbolt is the problem. We assess the full setup before opening the door.

Can you help if I'm locked out because a key broke in the lock?

Yes, if the broken piece is in the cylinder, we can often remove it and see whether the lock still works well enough to keep. If the key broke because the lock is worn or gritty, we may recommend replacing the cylinder or rekeying it so the same problem doesn't keep coming back.
