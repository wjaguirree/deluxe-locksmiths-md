Business Lockouts in Churchville - common questions

How do you open a business door without damaging the lock if the key just stopped working?

We start by looking at the lock type and the condition of the door. If the cylinder, latch, or panic hardware is still usable, we use methods that open the door without forcing the whole assembly apart. If the lock is already damaged, we may need to remove and replace the failed part after entry.

What should I have ready when our office in Churchville is locked out?

Have someone on site who can show the door, the lock, and proof that the business is yours or that you're authorized to be there. If there's a master key, spare key, or an alarm contact tied to the door, that helps too. The more we know about the hardware, the cleaner the job usually goes.

Is it a bad sign if the key was sticking before the lockout happened?

Yes, that usually means the hardware was already wearing out. A key that drags can point to a dirty cylinder, a damaged key, a misaligned latch, or worn internal pins. If it's ignored, the lock can bind harder, trap the key, or fail completely. We can inspect it and replace the part that's actually causing the problem.

Should we rekey the lock or replace it after a business lockout?

Rekeying makes sense when the lock body is still in good shape and the issue is mainly with control over the keys. Replacement makes more sense when the cylinder is worn, the body is damaged, or the door hardware no longer works smoothly. We'll look at the condition of the lock and tell you which option fits the hardware.

What if the lockout is on a storefront with a panic bar or an older commercial lock?

Those doors can be opened, but the hardware matters. A panic bar may be out of adjustment, the latch may be worn, or the device may need a new part before it will reset correctly. Older commercial locks can have parts that are no longer holding up. We check the whole entry so it doesn't fail again right away.
