Business Lockouts in Felton - common questions

If my shop door is locked and the key is inside, what happens first?

The first step is figuring out what kind of lock you're dealing with and whether the door is only latched or the hardware is actually failing. That tells the locksmith the safest way to open it. The goal is to get you back inside without adding damage that turns a simple lockout into a bigger repair.

What should I have ready when I call about a business lockout in Felton?

Have the business name, the street address, and a way to confirm you're allowed to open the space. If there's a back door, side door, or a known problem with the lock, mention it. That kind of detail helps the job move more smoothly once someone is on site.

Could forcing the door be a bad idea if it's just a small office lockout?

Yes. Forcing a door can bend the latch, crack the frame, or ruin a cylinder that might otherwise still work. If the lock is already worn, the damage can spread fast. A careful opening is usually better than creating a second problem while trying to solve the first one.

Should I replace the lock right away, or can it just be opened and used again?

That depends on why it locked you out. If it was a simple latch issue, an adjustment may be enough. If the key broke, the cylinder binds, or the lock shows wear, replacement may be the better call. The right answer is the one that keeps the door reliable after the lockout is over.

What if the lockout is at a locked office near Killens Pond and the door has already been messed with?

That changes the job because the hardware may not be the only problem. A pry mark, a shifted strike plate, or a broken latch can make the door harder to open and harder to secure after. In that case, the locksmith has to deal with both access and the condition of the door before leaving it in service.
