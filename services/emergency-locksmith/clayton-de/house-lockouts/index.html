House Lockouts in Clayton - common questions

If I'm locked out of my house in Clayton, what do we need to have ready before you come?

Have your ID ready and, if possible, something that links you to the home, like mail, a lease, or a utility bill. If the lock is key-related, let us know whether the key is inside, lost, or broken off in the lock. That helps us come prepared for the right kind of entry and any follow-up repair.

Will opening my front door leave the lock or door damaged?

It depends on the lock and what shape it's in, but our first goal is to open it without turning a lockout into a repair job. If the door, latch, or deadbolt has already been forced or the lock is failing, we'll explain what needs attention so you know the door will still secure properly afterward.

My key snapped in the lock on Main Street. Can that be fixed, or does the whole lock have to be replaced?

Sometimes the broken piece can be removed and the lock kept in service. Other times the cylinder is worn, bent, or already sticking, and replacement is the safer choice. We look at the condition of the lock itself, not just the broken key, because a worn lock can keep causing the same problem.

Should I try to pick or force the lock myself if I'm stuck outside in Clayton?

It's usually a bad idea. That's how a simple lockout turns into a bent latch, damaged cylinder, or broken trim around the door. If the lock is jammed, forcing it can make it harder for us to open cleanly later. It's better to leave it alone and let us handle the entry the right way.

What if the door opens, but the deadbolt still feels rough or the door won't latch right?

That usually means the lockout was only part of the problem. The strike plate may be out of line, the deadbolt may be worn, or the door may have shifted in the frame. We can check that before we leave so you're not stuck with a door that opens easily but won't secure the way it should.
