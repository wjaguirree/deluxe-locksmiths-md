Broken Key Extraction in Clayton - common questions

How do you get a broken key out of a lock in Clayton?

We first look at how far the key blade sits in the cylinder and whether the lock is under tension. Then we use the right extractor tools to pull the broken piece without dragging the pins or scratching the plug. After that, we test the lock and make sure the keyway is clear before anyone tries another key.

What should I do before you get to my house if my key snapped in the front door lock?

Don't keep turning the stub, and don't put glue or anything sticky into the lock. If the door is closed, leave it closed. If you still have the other half of the key, keep it handy. The main thing is to leave the lock alone so the broken piece doesn't settle deeper.

Is my lock ruined if the key broke off inside it?

Not always. A clean extraction often leaves the lock usable, especially if the break happened because the key was worn. If the cylinder is damaged, loose, or gritty, the lock may need repair or replacement. We check that before we leave so you know whether the lock is dependable or just temporarily usable.

Should I keep using the same lock after you remove the broken key, or replace it?

That depends on why the key broke in the first place. If the lock turns smoothly and the key was the weak point, you may be fine with a new key. If the lock binds, sticks, or catches in the same spot, replacement is usually the smarter call because the same break can happen again.

What if the key broke in a weathered exterior lock on an older house in Clayton?

That's common enough around town. Rust, dirt, and weather can wear down both the key and the lock. We can usually extract the broken piece and tell you whether the cylinder can still be trusted. If the lock is badly worn, we'll explain the options clearly so you can decide what makes sense next.
