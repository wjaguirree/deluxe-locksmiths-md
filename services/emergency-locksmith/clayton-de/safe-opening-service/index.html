Safe Opening Service in Clayton - common questions

How do you open a safe in Clayton when the combination is lost?

We first identify the safe type and lock style, then choose the least intrusive opening method that fits that model. Some safes can be opened and serviced without major damage. Others need lock work after the door is opened. If the lock is too worn or the mechanism is damaged, we'll explain whether repair, rekeying, or replacement makes the most sense.

What should I have ready before you come to my home or office?

Have proof that the safe is yours, and know anything you can about the brand, size, or lock style. If there's paperwork, a model tag, or an old combination record, that helps. Please don't force the dial or keypad if it's stuck. That can turn a workable safe into a bigger repair.

Can forcing a safe open make the damage worse?

Yes. Drilling in the wrong place, prying the door, or repeatedly spinning a damaged dial can affect the lock and the boltwork. It can also make later repair harder. If the safe is already jammed, it's better to stop and let us assess it before anything else is tried.

Should I repair, rekey, or replace the lock on my safe?

If the lock is still sound and the problem is limited to wear or a lost combination, repair or rekeying is often the better path. If the lock has failed, the keypad is unreliable, or the safe has been damaged, replacement may be the practical choice. We look at the condition of the safe, not just the lock by itself.

Can you open a safe that's in an older house near the rail line and hasn't been used in years?

Often, yes. Older safes can have stiff boltwork, worn dials, or locks that were never serviced. We handle both the opening and the follow-up service. If rust, age, or prior damage has changed how the door operates, we'll tell you whether the safe can be put back into regular use or should be replaced.
