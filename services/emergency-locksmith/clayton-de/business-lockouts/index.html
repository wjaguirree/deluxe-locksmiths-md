Business Lockouts in Clayton - common questions

What should I tell you first if my shop door in Clayton is locked and I can't get in?

Start with the door type, the lock type, and what happened right before the lockout. A snapped key, a stuck deadbolt, or a latch that won't catch are different problems. If it's a storefront, office, or back entrance, that matters too. Clear details help us choose the right method and keep the door in good shape.

Do I need to have the building owner or manager there for a business lockout?

Usually, we need someone who can confirm you're allowed entry, especially in a shared building or rental space. If you're the owner, manager, or an authorized tenant, say that when you call. If the property has a landlord, alarm company, or building supervisor involved, it helps to have those contacts ready in case they need to be looped in.

What if the key broke off in the lock on Main Street or the latch is jammed?

That's common with older exterior hardware. We can often remove a broken key and work the lock from there, but if the cylinder or latch has failed, the repair may need more than a simple opening. Tell us whether the key still turns partway, whether the door is swollen, and whether the hardware has been sticking for a while.

Should I have the lock rekeyed after you open the business door?

If the key is missing, stolen, or in the wrong hands, rekeying is worth discussing right away. If it was just a lockout and all keys are accounted for, opening the door may be enough. The decision usually comes down to whether access is still secure after the lockout is resolved.

Can you help if my office has a keypad, smart lock, or panic bar instead of a regular key lock?

Yes, but the approach depends on the hardware. A keypad issue can be a dead battery, a code problem, or a failed part. A panic bar or commercial exit device needs different handling than a standard knob or deadbolt. Let us know the brand or style if you can, and we'll plan the job around that equipment.
