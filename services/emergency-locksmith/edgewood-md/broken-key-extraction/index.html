Why Keys Snap

A key is just a shaped blade doing a lot of small work inside the lock. Each cut on the key lines up the pin stacks or tumblers so the plug can turn. When the blade is worn down, bent, or weakened at the shoulder, it doesn't line up cleanly anymore. You may feel the key catch, need extra force, or turn only part way before it binds. That stress is what often leaves the broken end stuck in the cylinder.

From the outside, the warning signs are usually simple to spot. The key may wobble in the slot, the head may feel loose from the blade, or the lock may work only if you jiggle it just right. On apartment doors, mailbox locks, and commercial entries around the Route 40 corridor, we see this a lot because those locks get used hard and the key gets worn along with them. Even a straight key can snap if the lock is dirty, the cylinder is dry, or the wrong key has been forcing its way in for too long.

Broken Key Extraction in Edgewood - common questions

How do you get a snapped key out of a lock in Edgewood without breaking the cylinder?

We use extraction tools made for broken blades, not makeshift tools that can scar the keyway. First we check how the piece is sitting in the lock. Then we work it loose from the grooves or the cut edge of the blade. If the lock is already damaged, we'll say so before we try anything that could make it worse.

What should I have ready if my car key broke near the MARC station?

Have the vehicle make, model, and year ready, plus whether the key broke in the door, ignition, or trunk. If the car is locked, tell us whether the doors are still secure and whether any part of the key is visible. That helps us decide what tools to bring and whether a replacement key is likely to be needed.

Can leaving the broken key in my apartment lock make the door easier to force open?

It can, especially if the key fragment keeps the lock from latching or turning fully. A damaged cylinder may also let the bolt sit wrong. In multi-unit buildings, that's a real concern because the door may look shut when it isn't secured the way it should be. We treat that as more than a convenience issue.

If the key snapped, is it better to extract it or replace the whole lock?

It depends on the lock's condition. If the cylinder still turns smoothly after the broken piece comes out, extraction and a new key may be enough. If the lock was already sticky, worn, or damaged by forcing the key, replacement can be the safer choice. We'll inspect the lock and explain what makes sense.

What if part of my mailbox key is still stuck and I need the mail today?

We can often remove the broken piece and check whether the mailbox lock still works afterward. If the cylinder is bent, badly worn, or has been damaged by repeated trying, the lock may need to be changed. In that case, we'll walk through the next step so you're not left guessing about access.
