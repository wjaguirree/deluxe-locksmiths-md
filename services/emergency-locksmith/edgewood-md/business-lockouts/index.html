Business Lockouts in Edgewood - common questions

How do you usually handle a business lockout in Edgewood?

First, the lock and door are checked so the entry method fits the hardware instead of forcing it. If the key is lost, stuck, or broken, the goal is to open the door cleanly and then see whether the cylinder still works. If the lock is damaged, a repair or rekey may be the better next step.

What should I have ready before I call about my office lockout?

Have the exact business name, the address, and a clear description of the door if you can give it. It also helps to know whether it's a storefront, office, or suite door, and whether the key is missing, broken, or locked inside. If someone else has authorized access, tell us that too.

Could forcing the door make the security problem worse?

Yes. A door that gets pried or kicked can end up with frame damage, a bent strike plate, or a lock that no longer lines up right. That can leave the business easier to break into later. Clean opening methods are better for keeping the door secure after the lockout is solved.

If the key turns but the door still won't open, do I need a new lock?

Not always. Sometimes the latch is stuck, the door is out of alignment, or the lock is worn but still salvageable. The right answer depends on what failed. If the cylinder is slipping or the internal parts are worn, replacing the hardware may be the safest long-term fix.

What if this happened to a business with shared access or a back entrance?

That's common in mixed commercial spaces. We can focus on the door that's actually blocking entry and then look at whether other access points are secure enough for the rest of the day. If a shared lock or master key system is involved, it may need a rekey or hardware check after the door is opened.
