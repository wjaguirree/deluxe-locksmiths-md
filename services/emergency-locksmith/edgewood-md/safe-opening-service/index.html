Safe Opening Service in Edgewood - common questions

How do you open a safe when the combination is lost?

We start by identifying the safe type and lock style, then use the least invasive method that fits it. Some safes can be opened through the existing lock setup, while others need careful drilling or internal access. After opening, we check whether the lock can be repaired or reset so the safe stays usable.

What should I have ready before you come out for a safe opening in Edgewood?

Have the safe model if you know it, plus any old paperwork, keys, or combination records you still have. If the safe is in a shared home or office, be ready to show that you're allowed to access it. Clear the area around the safe so the work can be done cleanly.

Is there a risk the safe will be damaged during opening?

There can be some risk, especially with damaged locks, older fire safes, or units that have been altered before. The goal is always to keep the safe usable if possible. If non-destructive opening won't work, the next step is usually a controlled method that limits damage to the lock area rather than the whole safe.

Should I repair the safe, rekey it, or replace it?

If the safe is structurally sound and the problem is limited to the lock, repair or rekeying is often enough. If the combination is lost, rekeying or resetting access may solve it. Replacement makes more sense when the safe is too worn, badly damaged, or no longer secure for the kind of use you need.

Can you help if my office safe was left closed after a tenant change or staff change?

Yes, that's a common kind of call. Office safes sometimes end up with no working combination, missing keys, or access tied to someone who's gone. We can open the safe, identify whether the lock can be reset, and help you decide if the current unit should stay in service or be replaced.
