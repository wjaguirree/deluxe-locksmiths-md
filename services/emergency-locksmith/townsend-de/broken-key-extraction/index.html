Broken Key Extraction in Townsend - common questions

How do you get a broken key out without ruining the lock?

We inspect how the blade broke and how far it sits in the keyway. Then we use extraction tools matched to the lock type and avoid forcing the plug. If the cylinder is already damaged or badly worn, we'll say so before we try anything that could make the problem worse.

What should I have ready when we call about a key stuck in a lock in Townsend?

Have the lock location, the type of lock if you know it, and proof that you live there or have permission to access it. If the break happened on a car, truck, gate, or shed, tell us that too. That helps us bring the right tools and avoids extra back-and-forth once we arrive.

Can a broken key extraction make my lock stop working later?

It can if the lock was already worn, dirty, or damaged before the key broke. Extraction itself is usually straightforward, but a rough cylinder may still bind afterward. We check the lock after removing the blade and tell you if it needs repair or replacement to stay reliable.

Should I try to pull the broken piece out with pliers or a blade?

Usually not. Pliers, knives, and improvised picks often push the fragment deeper or scratch the cylinder. If the tip is already loose and visible, a careful pull might seem easy, but it can still worsen the lock. It's better to leave it alone and let us handle the extraction cleanly.

What if the key broke off in a lock out near the edge of town and the door still won't open?

That can happen when the lock is damaged, the keyway is blocked, or the cylinder is seized. We'll first remove the broken piece if we can. If the lock still won't turn after that, we'll look at whether the problem is the cylinder, the deadbolt, or the door hardware itself.
