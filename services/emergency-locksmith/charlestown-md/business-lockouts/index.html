Business Lockouts in Charlestown - common questions

What should I have ready before I call about a business lockout in Charlestown?

Have the exact address, the best door to work on, and a quick description of the lock if you can. It also helps to know whether the building is occupied, whether there's a manager or owner on site, and whether this is a storefront, office, storage room, or gate. That information helps the job go smoothly.

If the key is inside my office, can the door still be opened without changing the lock?

Often, yes. If the door and lock are in decent shape, the goal is usually to open it cleanly and keep the hardware usable. If the key is broken, the lock is damaged, or the cylinder is worn out, opening the door may be only part of the job. Once access is restored, the next step depends on the condition of the hardware.

What's the risk if someone tries to force a locked business door open?

Forcing the door can bend the frame, damage the latch, break the lock body, or leave the door out of alignment. That can turn a simple access problem into a repair problem. It can also make it harder to secure the building afterward. A clean opening is usually the safer path when you need back in.

Should I ask for the lock to be opened or rekeyed after a lockout?

If the key is lost or unaccounted for, rekeying may make sense after the door is open. If the key was simply left inside and you still trust the hardware, opening the lock may be enough. The right choice depends on who had access before, how many copies of the key exist, and whether the lock is still in good shape.

What if this is a slip-side storage box or gate lock near the waterfront?

That's still a lockout, but the hardware can be different from a normal office door. Wet weather, corrosion, and padlock wear can all matter. It helps to know whether the lock is a padlock, cam lock, or keyed gate lock, and whether salt air or rust has been an issue. That gives a better picture of what may be needed.
