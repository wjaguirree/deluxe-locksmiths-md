Broken Key Extraction in Charlestown - common questions

How do you get a broken key out without ruining the lock?

We look at how much of the blade is left in the keyway and choose the tool that fits the lock, not the other way around. If the piece is reachable, it can often be pulled without disassembly. If the lock is already worn or packed with debris, we may need to take it apart, clear the keyway, and check whether the cylinder is still usable.

What should I have ready when you come out for a snapped key in Charlestown?

Have the lock open if you can, and keep any remaining key pieces. It also helps to know whether the lock is on a house door, storage box, gate, or dock hardware. If the key broke because it was sticking, tell us that too. That usually points to wear inside the lock, not just a bad key.

Can the lock still be used after the key breaks off inside it?

Sometimes, yes. If the key comes out cleanly and the cylinder is still in good shape, the lock may work with a new key. But if the plug is rough, the pins are damaged, or the keyway is bent, using it again can make the problem worse. In those cases, rekeying or replacing the cylinder is the safer choice.

Should I try to pull the broken piece out myself before calling?

Only if the piece is sticking out far enough to grab without forcing it. Pushing too hard can drive it deeper or scratch the keyway. A snapped key in a salt-air town like Charlestown can also be corroded in place, which makes the metal brittle. If it doesn't move easily, stop and leave it alone.

What if the key broke in a padlock or slip-side storage box instead of a house lock?

That changes the job a little. Padlocks and storage boxes often have tighter keyways and more wear from weather, so extraction can be trickier. In some cases the lock can be opened and serviced on site; in others, replacement makes more sense than forcing a tired lock back into use. The hardware type decides the best path.
