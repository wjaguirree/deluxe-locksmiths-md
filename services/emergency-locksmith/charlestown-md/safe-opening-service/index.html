Safe Opening Service in Charlestown - common questions

My safe opens with a keypad but not the handle. What does that usually mean?

That often means the lock released but the boltwork is still hung up. It could be a binding bolt, a damaged linkage, or something inside pressing against the door. Don't force the handle. If the keypad accepts the code but the door won't swing free, the safe may need opening and service, not just a new battery.

What should I have ready before someone comes to open my safe in Charlestown?

Have the safe's make, model, and any paperwork you still have. If it's a keyed safe, bring the key you do have, even if it doesn't work. It also helps to know whether the problem started after a battery change, a move, or a wrong-code lockout. That sort of detail can narrow the job right away.

Is there a risk of damaging the safe if I keep trying the code?

Yes. Repeated wrong tries can trigger lockout on electronic safes, and forcing the handle can bend parts inside the door. If the safe is already making grinding, sticking, or beeping in a strange pattern, stop trying for the moment. More attempts can turn a workable opening into a repair job.

How do I know if I need the safe opened or if I just need it serviced?

If the safe still responds and you know the code, but the door is sticking or the handle feels rough, service may be enough. If the combination is lost, the keypad is dead, or the lock never releases, it needs opening first. Sometimes both are needed, especially on older safes that haven't been used in a while.

Can you open a safe that's been sitting in a storage box near the water?

Often, yes, but moisture can change the problem. Corrosion may make the lock, bolts, or hinges stick more than they would in a dry house. In those cases we look at whether the issue is the lock, the boltwork, or both. A safe that's been exposed to damp air may also need service after it's opened.
