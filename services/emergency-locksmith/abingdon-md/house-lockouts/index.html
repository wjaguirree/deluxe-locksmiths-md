House Lockouts in Abingdon - common questions

How do you open a house door in Abingdon without damaging it?

We start by looking at the lock, the latch, and how the door sits in the frame. If the hardware is working normally, we use the least invasive method that fits the situation. If something is already broken, we explain that before we go any farther. The goal is to open the door and keep the rest of the hardware usable.

What should I have ready when you come out for a house lockout?

Have some form of ID with the address if you can, and be ready to show that the home is yours or that you live there. If there are pets, a security system, or a tricky gate or storm door, let us know when you call. That helps us plan the job and avoid extra back-and-forth once we're there.

Can a lockout turn into a bigger repair after you get the door open?

Yes. If the key won't turn smoothly, the deadbolt drags, or the door has been slammed out of alignment, opening it may expose a problem that was already there. We'll point out what we see so you can decide whether it's just a one-time lockout or something that needs a proper repair before it causes another shutout.

Should I try the side door or the garage first if I'm locked out of my house?

Only if you're sure those doors are actually unlocked and safe to use. Forcing a window, screen, or garage setup usually creates more damage than the lockout itself. If there's another legitimate entry point, we can talk through it with you. Otherwise, opening the main door is usually the cleaner path.

What if my key is inside, but the deadbolt is turned and the knob won't budge?

That's a common lockout pattern, and it often means the latch or deadbolt is binding, not just that the key is missing. We can open the door and check whether the lock is worn, misaligned, or just hard to operate. If the hardware is failing, we'll tell you what needs to be fixed so the same thing doesn't happen again.
