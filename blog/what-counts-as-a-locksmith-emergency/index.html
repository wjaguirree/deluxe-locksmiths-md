What Counts as a Locksmith Emergency?

16,000 lockouts a day nationwide. Most aren't emergencies. Here's how to tell which pile you're in.

Call (443) 545-2775 Request service

By the Deluxe Locksmith team September 11, 2026

16,000 lockouts a day nationwide. Most aren't emergencies. Here's how to tell which pile you're in.

16,000 lockouts a day. That's the U.S. average, home and car combined. AAA alone handles around 4 million of those calls a year, according to the Locksmith Industry Statistics report (Locksmith Plus, 2025). Most aren't emergencies. A few are. The trick is telling them apart before you're standing on a porch at 9pm deciding what to do next.

Skimming? Here's the gist. Real emergency means safety risk, or no safe way to just wait it out. Not inconvenience. Break-ins, unsafe lockouts, snapped keys jamming a lock, those count. Sticky deadbolts don't. Deluxe Locksmith runs same-day, Sunday through Friday, across Cecil/Harford County MD and New Castle/Kent County DE. Closed Saturdays, plan around it.

What Actually Qualifies as a Locksmith Emergency?

Short version: a lock failure that creates a safety risk, or leaves you with no safe way to secure your property right now. A damaged door after a break-in. A lock that won't hold overnight. Being stuck somewhere unsafe. Not a jammed key that's just annoying.

People treat "urgent" and "emergency" as the same word. They're not. Mix them up and you either pay extra for an after-hours rush you didn't need, or you wait on something you shouldn't have. Sticky deadbolt? Fine till Tuesday. Door that won't close after someone kicked it in? Not fine, and if you're staring at that situation right now, here's what not to do when you're locked out before help arrives.

Break-Ins and Damaged Entry Points

779,542. That's how many burglaries the FBI recorded nationwide in 2024, and 72% of them hit single-family homes (Home Invasion Statistics, Get Safe and Sound, citing FBI Crime Data Explorer, 2024). About 62% involved forced entry, someone prying, kicking, or drilling their way through a door or lock. Door damaged? Frame splintered? That's same-day, full stop. A house sitting open overnight isn't a "we'll get to it" problem.

Call the police first. Always. Once the scene's cleared, then call a locksmith. And don't try to force a busted lock shut yourself, it almost never holds, and now you've made the actual repair harder.

Being Locked Out Somewhere Unsafe

Not every lockout is an emergency. Locked out of your car in a well-lit driveway at 8pm? Annoying, not urgent. Locked out of your house at night with nobody around to let you in, or stuck outside a vehicle somewhere isolated? Different story. The location changes the math.

This is exactly why dispatch routes by location, not by who picks up the phone first. Whoever's closest gets the call. When someone's standing outside in the dark, that difference is minutes, not an afterthought.

A Broken Key in the Lock or Ignition

A snapped key is its own kind of emergency. No safety angle needed, just a door that won't lock or a car that won't start. Don't reach for pliers. Digging at a broken key with the wrong tool almost always pushes it deeper or chews up the cylinder, and then you're paying for two problems instead of one. If it's the only copy you had, what to do when you lose your only car key walks through the rest of it.

Storefront and Commercial Break-Ins

Commercial break-ins run on their own clock. An open storefront overnight isn't just a repair job, it's an invitation for round two, plus inventory loss and an insurance headache waiting to happen. Damaged door, cracked frame, busted commercial-grade lock, even if nothing was actually stolen: fix it same-day. Not next week, and not because we'd rather bill you sooner, but because storefront doors fail in predictable ways once they've been forced once.

While You're Waiting

A few things help while you wait. Stay somewhere lit and populated if you're locked out. Just don't stand in a dark parking lot scrolling your phone for twenty minutes. If a break-in happened, leave the damaged door alone, both for the evidence and because touching it rarely helps. Broken key in a lock? Same rule: leave it.

One more thing for homes specifically. If the door won't fully secure, staying put until the technician shows up beats leaving the place open all night.

The Stuff That Can Actually Wait

Most lock issues aren't emergencies. A sticky deadbolt. A lock that's gotten slow to turn. Swapping in a smart lock, or rekeying after you've moved in but the old locks still work fine, which is really a question of rekey or replace, how to tell which one you need. Real needs, sure, but not urgent ones. Book the next available slot and move on with your day.

Worn-out storefront hardware fits here too, as long as it still latches at closing time. The moment it stops latching, though, that's same-day again.

Inside those hours, though, dispatch sends the nearest available technician, which is why same-day response actually holds up across Elkton and roughly 38 surrounding towns in Cecil County MD, Harford County MD, New Castle County DE, and Kent County DE.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a car lockout always an emergency?

Mostly, no. AAA handles roughly 4 million car lockout calls a year and most of them are just annoying, not urgent (Locksmith Industry Statistics, Locksmith Plus, 2025). The location's what flips it, unsafe surroundings, or nowhere safe to just wait it out.

Should I try to force open a lock myself during a break-in?

Don't. You'll likely wreck the cylinder or frame worse than it already is. Secure the scene. Call police. Locksmith comes after, once it's actually safe.

Does Deluxe Locksmith offer 24-hour emergency service?

No. Hours are Sunday-Thursday 7am-10pm, Friday 7am-6pm. Closed Saturdays, full stop. Same-day still works inside that window because dispatch sends whoever's closest.

What should I do if my key breaks inside the ignition?

Skip the household tools, that's the short version. You need actual extraction tools or you'll damage the cylinder further. A mobile locksmith pulls it out on-site, no extra damage.

Can a locksmith get into my home without damaging the door?

Usually. Non-destructive methods come first, whenever the lock and door allow it. Covers most homes.
