What Is a Transponder Key and Can You Copy One?

A transponder key has a chip your car must recognize. Learn how to spot one, why copying takes programming, and what to ask a locksmith before you book.

A transponder key has a chip your car must recognize. Learn how to spot one, why copying takes programming, and what to ask a locksmith before you book.

A transponder key is a car key with a small chip hidden in the plastic head, and the car only starts for a chip it knows. So yes, you can copy one. The catch is that cutting the metal blade is half the work. If the chip isn't programmed to your car, the key turns in the ignition and the engine still won't run.

Short version. A thick plastic head usually means a chip. A hardware store copy will open the door and won't start the car. A locksmith cuts the blade and programs the chip right at your vehicle. Get the spare now, while you still have a key that works.

Key Takeaways

  • The chip in a transponder key talks to the car's immobilizer. No match, no start.
  • Electronic immobilizers were standard on 96 percent of model year 2022 vehicles from brands other than Hyundai and Kia, according to the Insurance Institute for Highway Safety.
  • A blade-only copy falls short. The key needs a cut and programming.
  • Copying from a working key is far easier than replacing keys after the last one is gone.

What is a transponder key?

A transponder key is a car key with a built-in chip that sends a coded signal to the vehicle. When you turn the key or press start, the car's immobilizer reads that code. A match the car has stored lets the engine run. No match, and the car stays put, even if the blade turns the ignition without a hitch.

That's the design. Someone holding a copied blade can't drive off, and neither can you. The Insurance Institute for Highway Safety reports that electronic immobilizers were standard equipment on 92 percent of 2011 models and 96 percent of model year 2022 models from brands other than Hyundai and Kia. Its data also showed Hyundai and Kia vehicles that received an immobilizer software upgrade had theft claim frequencies 53 percent lower than those that did not (IIHS, August 7, 2024).

How do you know if your car key has a chip?

You can't see it, so go by clues. Most keys made since the late 1990s have a chip, and the plastic head tends to give it away.

What you seeWhat it usually means
Thick plastic head on the keyA chip is likely inside
Plain all-metal key, older vehicleProbably no chip
Key turns the lock but the car will not startThe chip is not being recognized
Security light blinks on the dash with the key inThe immobilizer is not accepting the key
Key with lock and unlock buttonsA remote and usually a chip

The owner's manual will settle it. If you're unsure, give us the year, make and model on the phone and we'll tell you what the car uses before anyone drives out.

Can you copy a transponder key?

Yes, though a copy is really two jobs. One is cutting the blade to match the original. The other is getting the chip to talk to your car. Consumer Reports says the days of copying a blank key at a hardware store are "pretty much over" for modern keys (Consumer Reports, May 4, 2019). A cut-only copy unlocks the door and turns the ignition. The engine stays off.

Programming needs equipment a hardware counter doesn't have. We handle transponder key programming and car key duplication at your vehicle, so nobody tows anything or drives to a shop. A few cars, mostly newer and European ones, are dealer-only. Consumer Reports says the same of certain makes. If yours is one, we'll tell you on the phone rather than send someone out who can't fix it.

What is the Chip Check for a spare key?

Before you order a spare, run the Chip Check. It's four questions, and the answers change what the job looks like.

  1. Do you have a working key? If so, it's a copy. If not, it's a replacement, and that's a bigger job.
  2. Does the key have a remote? Lock and unlock buttons may need pairing on their own. Our key fob checklist covers that part.
  3. Does the car start with it today? If the car already refuses the key, sort that out first. Copy a failing key and you get a failing copy.
  4. Where is the car? At home or work inside our service area, we come to you.

Once you've answered those, you can describe the job in a sentence. That's usually all a locksmith needs to say what's possible and when.

Copy or replace?

People mix these up, but they're different jobs. A copy starts from a key that already works. A replacement starts from nothing, which is the situation in what to do when you lose your only car key.

SituationJobWhat it involves
One working key, you want a spareCopyCut the blade, program the chip
Every key lostReplaceCut a key by code, enroll a new chip in the car
Key worn, car starts hardCopy now, then check the ignitionA worn key can hide an ignition fault

Consumer Reports has plain advice for anyone down to one key: order a second one as soon as possible. We see why. A spare made while a working key exists is the easy version of this job. Think of car key replacement as the backup plan, not the first one.

What happens when we copy a transponder key at your car?

The visit is short and happens wherever the car is parked. This is the order we work in.

  1. Identify the key. From your year, make and model we work out the keyway and chip family.
  2. Cut the blade. We cut it to match, then make sure it turns the door lock and ignition smoothly.
  3. Program the chip. The new key gets paired to the immobilizer so the engine accepts it.
  4. Test everything. Start the car, work the door locks, press every remote button.

Step four matters more than people think. A key that starts the car but drops the remote, or a remote that works while the engine won't run, is half a job. We stay until the whole key works, and nothing gets drilled or forced along the way. Car or house, that's how we handle every lock and key job.

If the ignition feels rough or the old key is badly worn, we'll say so. A worn key can hide a tired ignition cylinder, and copying it just hands the problem to the new key. That's a separate job, ignition repair, and it's better to hear about it before you order a second key.

What should you ask before you book?

Whoever you call, ask the same things. The Maryland Department of Labor advises customers to ask for a quote for the service call and for the work itself, and to confirm anything extra before the work starts. The same advisory says the invoice should list the vehicle's VIN for automobile locks serviced (Maryland Locksmith Consumer Advisory).

  • Get the total before work starts. Our pricing is quoted per job.
  • Find out whether the key will be cut and programmed, or only cut.
  • Request an invoice that lists your VIN.
  • Keep the year, make, model and your current key handy.

Frequently asked questions

What is a transponder key in simple terms?

It is a car key with a chip inside. The car reads the chip, and the engine only starts when the chip matches. A plain metal key cannot do that.

Can a transponder key be copied?

Yes. The blade is cut to match, and the chip is programmed to the car. A cut-only copy opens the door but will not start the engine.

Can you copy a transponder key without the original?

Often, yes, but it becomes a replacement job instead of a copy. The locksmith cuts a new key and enrolls a new chip in the car. It takes more work than copying from a key that runs the car today.

Why won't my car start with a new key?

The chip is probably not programmed to your car. The blade may be cut correctly, and the immobilizer still will not accept the key until it is paired.

Do all car keys have a transponder?

No. Many older vehicles use plain metal keys. Most vehicles from the late 1990s on use a chip, and many newer cars use a smart key instead. Check your manual.

Is it better to get a spare before I lose my key?

Yes. A copy from a working key is the simplest version of the job. Waiting until every key is gone makes it a replacement.
