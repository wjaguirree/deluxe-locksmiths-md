Key Sticking in Ignition? What to Try First

Key sticking in ignition? Check the shifter, release the steering wheel lock and test your spare before you call. Here is when it needs a locksmith visit.

By the Deluxe Locksmith team November 5, 2026

Key sticking in ignition? Check the shifter, release the steering wheel lock and test your spare before you call. Here is when it needs a locksmith visit.

Key sticking in ignition? Most of the time it's the steering wheel lock, a shifter that isn't quite in Park, or a key that's seen better days. The first two take a minute to rule out. The third sneaks up on you, because a worn key chews up the cylinder and a worn cylinder chews up the key. So here's the order we'd check things in, and where a driveway fix runs out.

In a hurry? Brake on. Shifter shoved all the way into Park. Rock the wheel a little while you turn the key. Then peek at the key. Bent, cracked or rounded off? Stop right there. And a key that only works after a wiggle, day after day, means the ignition's on its way out. Fix it when you pick the time, not when the car does.

Key Takeaways

  • A locked steering wheel is a common reason a key won't turn. Rocking the wheel while you turn the key often releases it, and forcing the key can damage the mechanism (NAPA Know How).
  • Worn keys don't always engage the lock tumblers, so try your spare before you blame the ignition.
  • A key that needs a wiggle every single time is a warning, not a quirk.
  • Maryland says every locksmith must be licensed, and the state's advisory says to ask for an estimate (Maryland Department of Labor).

Why does a key stick in the ignition?

A key sticks when something blocks the cylinder from turning, or blocks the key from sliding out. Start with the steering wheel lock. Then the shift position. Then the key, and last the cylinder.

What you noticeLikely causeFirst move
Key will not turn, wheel feels stiffSteering wheel lock is engagedRock the wheel while turning the key
Key will not come outShifter not fully in Park, or a shift lock problemFoot on the brake, reseat the shifter in Park
Key turns only after wigglingWorn key or worn cylinderTry the spare key
Key catches or feels gritty going inDebris or a damaged keyInspect the key, stop if it is bent
Spare works, main key does notMain key is wornHave a new key cut from the working one

The wheel locks on purpose. NAPA's guide says that deters theft and makes a disabled car easier to tow. So a locked wheel isn't a defect. It just feels like one when your key can't talk it into letting go.

What is the Stick Check?

Here's what we run through, and we call it the Stick Check. Four steps, no tools. By the end you'll know whether you're headed for a driveway fix or a phone call.

  1. Check the shifter. Press the brake, shove the shifter firmly into Park, and try again. Sitting a hair short of Park is enough to keep a key from turning or coming out.
  2. Release the wheel. Rock it gently side to side while you turn the key. Details just below.
  3. Inspect the key. Hunt for a bend, a crack, or edges rubbed smooth. Spot one? Stop and don't push it.
  4. Try the spare. Spare turns smoothly, main key doesn't? The main key's the culprit. Neither works? Then it's the car.

When the Stick Check points at the car and not the key, the fix is on the ignition side. Jump to the repair or replace section to see what a visit looks like.

How do you release a locked steering wheel?

Don't force the key. NAPA's guide warns that brute force can damage the internal mechanism. Steady, light pressure on the key is the way. Let the wheel do the work.

  1. Seat the key fully.
  2. Lean on it lightly, toward on.
  3. Rock the wheel back and forth in small movements.
  4. Once the wheel gives, the key turns.

Key won't reach on at all? NAPA says to pull it out a bit and try again, or slide it in and out a few times. The same guide says to keep other objects out of the cylinder, since they cause more damage.

Here's the part people skip. If the wheel only frees up after a wiggle, and it happens most mornings, that's a pattern. A lock that needs a trick is a lock wearing out.

What should you never do?

A few moves turn an annoying problem into an expensive one. Skip these.

  • Pry at it, hammer it, or twist the key hard. A snapped key inside the ignition makes a small problem bigger. Our automotive locksmith team handles broken key extraction, though skipping it altogether is nicer.
  • Spray heavy oil into the cylinder. It grabs dust and grit and can leave things worse a month on.
  • Poke a screwdriver or any tool into the keyway.
  • Keep driving on a key that fights you every time. It sticks today. It strands you next week.

Repair or replace the ignition?

Plenty of sticking ignitions are a worn cylinder, plain and simple. How worn is the question. Sticks now and then? Maybe repairable. Fails often, or fails even with a fresh key? Probably time to replace it.

SituationUsual route
Main key worn, spare works smoothlyCut a new key, no ignition work
Sticks now and then, key is in good shapeIgnition repair
Sticks daily, or fails with a new keyIgnition replacement
Key snapped off in the cylinderExtraction first, then repair or replacement
Key lost and the ignition is also failingCar key replacement alongside the ignition work

Frequently asked questions

Why is my key sticking in the ignition?

Usually it's a locked steering wheel, a shifter that isn't fully in Park, a worn key, or a worn ignition cylinder. Work through the Stick Check in that order and one of them will show itself.

How do I get a stuck key out of the ignition?

Press the brake, make sure the car is fully in Park, and rock the wheel gently as you ease the key out. No yanking. Still stuck after that? Stop and call us, since a snapped key is a bigger job.

Can a worn key damage my ignition?

It can. A worn key doesn't always engage the tumblers properly, and over months it wears the cylinder too. NAPA's guide suggests trying a spare and getting a new key cut once the old one is worn.

Is it safe to keep driving with a sticking ignition?

Not a great idea. A sticking ignition can quit without warning, and then the car won't start or the key won't come out. Have it looked at before it picks its own moment.
