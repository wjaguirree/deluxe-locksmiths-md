Locksmith in Claymont - common questions

If my front door in Claymont is sticking, do you replace the lock or try to repair it first?

We look at what's failing before we suggest a replacement. Sometimes the problem is a worn key, a loose strike plate, or a lock that needs to be serviced. If the cylinder or hardware is damaged beyond a solid repair, we'll walk through the replacement options and fit the new hardware to the door.

What should I have ready when you come to rekey my house near the rail station?

Have the doors and the current keys ready if you still have them. If you want certain locks to stay the same and others changed, point those out first. If the property is a rental or recently changed hands, having the relevant paperwork or permission on hand helps keep the job straightforward.

Can a worn apartment key cause the lock to jam, or is that usually the lock itself?

It can be either one, and often it's a mix of both. Worn keys stop lining up the pins cleanly, which makes the lock feel rough or get stuck. If the key is in bad shape, we can make a better copy or cut a new one, and we can also service the lock if the cylinder is the real problem.

Should I rekey or replace the locks after moving into a house in Claymont?

Rekeying is often the first step if the locks are still in good shape. It changes which key works without changing the whole set of hardware. Replacement makes more sense if the lock is damaged, mismatched, or too worn to trust. We can look at the setup and help you choose the right path for each door.

If my car key is broken off, can you still help if I'm parked at home and not at the vehicle?

Usually, yes. If the broken piece is still usable for identification or the vehicle can be accessed safely, we can often work from there. If the key is missing entirely, the make, model, and proof of ownership matter more. We'll tell you what we can do once we know the situation.
