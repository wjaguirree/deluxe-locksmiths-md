Locksmith in Bear - common questions

My townhouse key still works in the door, but I just moved in. Should I rekey or replace the locks?

In most cases, rekeying is the first thing to look at. If the hardware is solid and you just want old keys to stop working, rekeying keeps the existing locks in place. If the lock is worn, damaged, or doesn't match the door well, replacement may make more sense. We can look at the setup and explain the options.

What should I have ready before you come to my place in Bear?

Have the address, a working contact number, and any proof that you live there, lease there, or own the vehicle or property. If it's a lock change or rekey, gather every key you can find and any matching hardware you want included. The more the locks, keys, and access needs are together, the smoother the work goes.

I'm worried the key broke off inside the lock. Should I keep trying to turn it?

No. If a key is broken or binding, repeated turning can push the broken piece deeper or damage the cylinder. Stop using that key and leave the lock as it is. If you still have part of the key, keep it with the lock or bring it when you call, because that helps us see what failed.

For a rental in Bear, is it better to change all the locks or just rekey them?

That depends on the condition of the hardware and how many doors need to match. If the locks are in good shape, rekeying is often the cleaner choice for a move-in, tenant turnover, or key control issue. If the locks are damaged, mismatched, or outdated, replacing the hardware can be the better long-term fix.

Can you handle a mailbox lock or keypad lock if the old key or code doesn't work?

Often, yes, but the exact fix depends on the hardware. A cluster mailbox, keypad deadbolt, or smart lock each has its own limits. Don't try to reset or force it first, because that can make the problem harder to sort out. Keep any model name, key, code card, or paperwork with the unit if you have it.
