Locksmith in Newark - common questions

If I just moved into a rental near the University of Delaware, should I rekey the locks or replace them?

If the hardware is in good shape, rekeying usually makes more sense than replacing the whole lock. It changes old keys out and keeps the existing parts on the door. If the lock is worn, damaged, or doesn't operate smoothly, replacement may be the better option. We can talk through that on site.

What should I have ready before you work on my apartment or house lock in Newark?

Have proof that you live there, like a lease, ID, or utility information that matches the address. If it's a rental, it also helps to know whether the landlord wants the current lock kept in place or changed. Clear access to the door and a short explanation of the problem help us get started.

Can a stuck key in my front door become a bigger problem if I keep trying to turn it?

Yes. More force can bend the key, damage the cylinder, or break the key off inside the lock. If the key is binding, stop using it and leave the lock alone. That gives us a better chance of repairing it without adding extra damage. Same goes for a door that feels jammed.

For a Newark storefront, when does rekeying make more sense than changing all the locks?

If the locks work properly and the goal is to change who has access, rekeying is often the cleaner choice. If the hardware is worn out, mixed up with different lock types, or not holding up to daily use, replacement may be worth it. The right answer depends on the door, the key system, and how the business uses the space.

My car key is locked in the vehicle near I-95. Should I try to open it myself first?

It's better not to pry the door or try to wedge it open. That can bend the frame, damage weather stripping, or trigger a worse repair than the original lockout. Keep the car closed, make sure the vehicle is in a safe spot, and have your ownership information ready. We can handle the lockout without forcing the door.
