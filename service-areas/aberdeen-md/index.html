Locksmith in Aberdeen - common questions

If I just moved into a place in Aberdeen, should I rekey or replace the locks?

Rekeying is usually the first thing to look at if the hardware still works well. It changes which key opens the lock without changing the whole set. Replacement makes more sense if the lock is worn out, damaged, or not worth keeping.

What should I have ready before a locksmith comes to my house or car?

Have your ID, proof that you live there or own the vehicle, and any key you still have. If it's a rental or company vehicle, be ready to show permission from the owner, landlord, or manager. It also helps to know whether the lock is sticking, broken, or simply locked.

Can a key that's stuck in the lock be pulled out without changing the whole lock?

Sometimes, yes. If the key is bent, worn, or only partly broken, the lock may still be salvageable. Don't keep twisting it, because that can push the broken piece deeper or damage the cylinder. Once the key snaps cleanly, the repair is often simpler than people expect.

I'm deciding between rekeying and getting new hardware. How do I choose?

Choose rekeying when the lock works, but you want different keys. Choose new hardware when the lock is loose, corroded, mismatched, or no longer fitting the door well. If the door itself sags or drags, hardware alone may not solve the problem, so the condition of the door matters too.

What if my storefront or rental has a lock that works, but not everyone should have access anymore?

That's a rekeying job, and it's common after staff changes, tenant turnover, or a move. If there are multiple doors, a locksmith can sort out which ones should match and which should stay on separate keys. That keeps access tighter without changing every piece of hardware.
