Locksmith in Milford - common questions

Can you handle a lock change for a rental or moved-into house in Milford?

Yes. A rekey is often the first step when someone moves in or changes tenants, because it keeps the existing locks and makes old keys useless. If the hardware is worn, bent, or not matching the door well, replacing the lock may make more sense. The right choice depends on the condition of the door and cylinder.

What should I have ready before you come out to a house or business on the edge of Milford?

Have the address, a way to confirm you're allowed to request the service, and any working key you still have. If the lock has been acting up, it helps to know whether the door sticks, the key turns poorly, or the latch won't catch. That information helps narrow down whether the problem is the lock, the door, or both.

My key broke in the lock. Is that a bigger problem than it looks?

It can be. Sometimes the broken piece can be removed and the lock is still usable. Other times the key broke because the cylinder is worn, dirty, or binding internally. If the lock is forcing the key, taking it apart the wrong way can make things worse, so it is better to leave it alone until it's checked.

Should I rekey my locks or replace them on an older Milford property?

Rekeying makes sense when the locks still work well and you just want new keys to fit. Replacement is the better call when the hardware is loose, corroded, mismatched, or failing to latch cleanly. Older properties often need a mix of both, especially if one door sees much more use than the others.

What if my car key works sometimes and then stops, especially in a newer vehicle?

That can point to a worn key, a failing remote, a weak battery, or a programming issue. In newer vehicles, the problem is not always the door lock itself. If the lock turns but the car still won't recognize the key, the issue may be electronic rather than mechanical, and that changes the fix.
