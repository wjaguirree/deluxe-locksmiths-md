Towns we serve

Harford County, MD

Bel Air, MD

Bel Air is the Harford County seat and the county's center of gravity — the courthouse and the law offices around it, a genuinely walkable Main Street, and the Ma & Pa Trail running out the back of town.

Aberdeen, MD

Aberdeen runs on the Proving Ground.

Havre de Grace, MD

Havre de Grace stands exactly where the Susquehanna empties into the Chesapeake, and the whole town points at the water — the Concord Point Lighthouse, the boardwalk Promenade out over the marsh, the Decoy Museum, and a downtown of antique shops and restaurants along Washington and St.

Locksmith in Havre de Grace →

Edgewood, MD

Edgewood grew up around the Edgewood Area of Aberdeen Proving Ground and the Route 40 corridor, and it is one of the busiest, most mixed parts of Harford County — apartment communities, townhouse rows, single-family streets and commercial strips all packed close together.

Locksmith in Edgewood →

Joppatowne, MD

Joppatowne was built as a planned waterfront community on the Gunpowder River, and it still has that layout — curving streets, lagoons cut back from the water, and boat slips at the ends of yards.

Locksmith in Joppatowne →

Fallston, MD

Fallston is where Harford County stops being suburban and starts being country — larger lots, long driveways, horse fencing and the crossroads of MD-152 and MD-165 as the closest thing to a town center.

Locksmith in Fallston →

Forest Hill, MD

Forest Hill has grown fast along MD-24 and MD-23, filling in with subdivisions between what used to be open farmland north of Bel Air.

Locksmith in Forest Hill →

Jarrettsville, MD

Jarrettsville sits at the MD-165 and MD-23 crossroads in the northern Harford farm belt, surrounded by horse pastures, cornfields and the Deer Creek valley.

Abingdon, MD

Abingdon is Harford County's busy middle — I-95 exit 77, the Constant Friendship shopping district, Box Hill and a steady wall of townhouse and apartment communities along MD-24.

Churchville, MD

How it works

How a call actually goes

  1. Call or send the details

    Tell us what happened, what you are locked out of and where you are. No account, no forms to chase.

  2. We confirm what is needed

    We work out what the job actually takes and what to bring, so the right tools and blanks are on the van.

  3. We come to you

    Most work is done on-site, at the kerb or at the door. In-store visits are by appointment only.

  4. You test it before we leave

    Every key gets tried, every lock gets worked, and nothing is signed off until it operates the way it should.
